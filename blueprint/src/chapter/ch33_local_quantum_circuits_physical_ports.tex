\section{Fixed physical ports and bounded local registers}

The intersite gates of~\cite[Supplement, pp.~7--8]{Piroli2021LOCC}
act on fixed-dimensional physical qudits. Local ancillas are separate:
free onsite operations may mix the physical qudit with its local ancillas,
but a counted intersite gate does not act on an arbitrarily enlarged pair
of sites. This section derives bounded-register channel simulations and
unitary realizations with fresh local environments in that convention.
It does not identify the unrestricted local-channel model with the source's phase relation; see
\cite{gap:psc21_physical_port_simulation_scope}.

\begin{definition}[Physical-port channel protocols]
    \label{def:qc_physical_port_protocol}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.mem_unitary,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.conjTranspose,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.one,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.onsite_swap,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.isKrausCPTP,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.one}
    \leanok
    Every wire carries a $d$-level qudit. A layout assigns its wires to spatial
    sites and selects one physical port at each site. Free operations are
    unitaries supported inside one spatial site, wire permutations preserving
    each spatial site, and normalized onsite Kraus channels. A counted layer
    is an existing nearest-neighbor unitary layer placed on the physical ports,
    with identity on every memory wire. Products add physical depth bounds;
    a larger upper bound remains valid. Reversing a unitary circuit preserves
    its depth. Every resulting channel protocol is CPTP on all input operators.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Register codes and completed decoding]
    \label{lem:qc_register_code_completion}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.registerEncoding,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncoding_isIsometry,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncoding_isKrausCPTP,
        QuantumCircuit.registerDecoding,
        QuantumCircuit.registerDecoding_isKrausCPTP,
        QuantumCircuit.registerDecoding_encoding,
        QuantumCircuit.registerDecoding_comp_encoding,
        QuantumCircuit.registerDecoding_encoding_reference,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncodedMap,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncodedMap_isKrausCPTP,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncodedMap_encoding,
        QuantumCircuit.le_registerDimension,
        QuantumCircuit.registerBasisEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.pairRegisterBasisEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.pairRegisterBasisEmbedding_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncoding_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.registerEncoding_pair}
    \leanok
    An injection $e$ of local basis labels into register words defines an
    isometry $E$. The encoding is $X\mapsto EXE^\dagger$. For a fixed density
    matrix $\rho$, the completed decoder is
    \begin{align}
        D(Y) & =E^\dagger YE+
        \operatorname{tr}[(I-EE^\dagger)Y(I-EE^\dagger)]\rho.
    \end{align}
    It is CPTP and satisfies $D(EXE^\dagger)=X$ for every operator $X$.
    The same identity holds after tensoring with the identity on any finite
    reference. Encoding the two endpoints separately gives the tensor product
    of their local encoding matrices. If $m\le B$ and $d\ge2$, then
    $k=\lceil\log_d B\rceil$ qudits suffice. A channel $\Phi$ extends to the
    full input register by $\widehat\Phi=\mathcal E_{\rm out}\Phi D_{\rm in}$;
    this extension is CPTP and agrees with $\Phi$ on encoded inputs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    The inclusion has orthonormal columns. Complete the compression outside its
    range using the existing support-completion channel; the complementary
    term vanishes on $EXE^\dagger$. Matrix multiplication gives the exact left
    inverse, and tensoring preserves composition. The paired basis inclusion
    factors entrywise into two local inclusions. The ceiling-logarithm bound
    is $B\le d^k$.
\end{proof}

    \leanok
    A matching of ring bonds defines an involutive site permutation: each
    selected bond exchanges its endpoints and every other site is fixed.

\begin{proof}

    finite product therefore has the stated action. Applying the permutation
    matrix homomorphism identifies that product with the layer operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Selected-memory routing at unchanged physical depth]
    \label{thm:qc_port_register_routing}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.selectionPermutation,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.selectionPermutation_site,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.selectionPermutation_port,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.selectionPermutation_symm,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.selected_layer,
        QuantumCircuit.embedOp_permOp_viaEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.selected_permutation_layer,
        QuantumCircuit.registerPermutation,
        QuantumCircuit.registerPermutation_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.registerPermutation_apply_of_notMem,
        QuantumCircuit.registerPermutation_involutive,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.register_matching}
    \leanok
    Select one memory wire at each site. Swap it with the site's physical port,
    apply a physical layer, and undo the onsite swaps. This acts exactly as the
    layer on the selected wires and as identity on all other wires, at physical
    depth one. Repeating this for $k$ disjoint register digits implements the
    same matching permutation on each digit at depth at most $k$, independently
    of the number of selected bonds. All unselected wires are restored on the
    whole operator space, without assuming they were initialized or uncorrelated.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \uses{lem:qc_port_matching_swaps, lem:qc_placed_operators}
    \leanok
    The selection permutation preserves each spatial site, hence is free.
    Conjugating the port layer by it changes only the wire placement. Extend
    each digit's site permutation by identity outside that digit's selected
    wires. Disjoint ranges ensure the finite product has the desired action
    on each digit; every factor has a one-layer implementation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One shared routing for a channel family]
    \label{thm:qc_port_shared_channel_routing}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol, lem:qc_placed_operators}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.sum_conjTranspose_embedOp_mul,
        QuantumCircuit.routingMatrixEquiv,
        QuantumCircuit.routingMatrixEquiv_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.routingMatrixEquiv_symm_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.routingChannelConj,
        QuantumCircuit.routingChannelConj_apply,
        QuantumCircuit.routingChannelConj_embeddedKraus,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.of_routed_map,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.of_register_routing,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.of_routed_family}
    \leanok
    Suppose an actual wire routing of physical depth $T$ moves every channel
    in a commuting family into a single spatial site, possibly a different
    site for each member. Execute all those conjugated channels locally,
    then reverse the routing. The result is exactly the original family
    product, with physical depth at most $2T$. The number of family members
    does not multiply this depth bound. The identities hold for every input
    operator, including arbitrary correlations with unused registers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Wire reindexing is a linear equivalence on matrices. Its conjugation on
    linear maps is multiplicative, so it preserves the commuting family
    product. Placing Kraus operators preserves their completeness relation.
    Each routed map is a zero-depth onsite channel; only the one forward and
    one backward routing contribute to the depth.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform bounded-register matching simulation]
    \label{thm:qc_bounded_physical_port_simulation}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol, lem:qc_register_code_completion, lem:qc_port_data_scratch}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.pairChannel,
        QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.pairChannel_commute,
        QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.matchingChannel,
        QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.matchingChannel_isPhysicalPortProtocol,
        QuantumCircuit.boundedPairRegisterCode,
        QuantumCircuit.exists_bounded_matching_simulation,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.list_prod_uniform}
    \leanok
    Fix $d\ge2$, $B$, and a local input dimension $m\le B$, and choose a
    fallback density matrix on a pair of local inputs. Set
    $k=\lceil\log_d B\rceil$, independently of the chain length.
    For every ring of $N\ge2$ sites and every matching of CPTP pair channels,
    there are normalized Kraus maps on the two $k$-qudit data registers that
    agree with the prescribed code extensions on the entire register space,
    and intertwine exactly with the original pair channels on encoded inputs.
    Their actual placed matching channel has a physical-port protocol of depth
    at most $2k$. A product of $L$ such certified layers has depth at most $2kL$.
    For $B=1$ the register width and this bound are zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \uses{lem:qc_register_code_completion, lem:qc_port_data_scratch, thm:qc_port_shared_channel_routing}
    \leanok
    Encode the two endpoints independently, then complete each pair channel
    outside its joint code. The resulting map is CPTP, so choose its normalized
    finite Kraus representation. The common data-to-scratch routing has depth
    $k$ and puts every pair channel onsite. Apply
    Theorem~\ref{thm:qc_port_shared_channel_routing} once to the complete
    matching. Protocol composition gives the final linear-in-$L$ bound.
    The off-code joint decoder need not factor onsite; it is only executed
    after colocation. This theorem is not an assertion about protocols with
    an unrestricted intermediate dimension depending on $N$.
\end{proof}

    the representation-independent placement identity.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Compiling bounded native protocols}

The intermediate local dimensions of a native channel protocol need a common
bound before a family-level physical-depth comparison is available. The next
construction records that budget explicitly, compiles into one fixed register
alphabet, and then realizes those operations on the fixed physical ports.

\begin{definition}[Dimension-bounded native protocols]
    \label{def:qc_dimension_bounded_protocol}
    \uses{def:qc_local_channel_conversion}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol,
        QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol.toIsLocalChannelProtocol,

        QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol.comp,
        QuantumCircuit.OnsiteChannel.output_dimension_pos,
        QuantumCircuit.IsLocalChannelProtocol.exists_dimension_bound}
    \leanok
    Every input, output, and intermediate local dimension is positive and at most
    $B$. The constructors are the existing onsite channel and native pair-channel
    layer operations. Forgetting the certificates gives an ordinary native protocol;
    increasing $B$ preserves them, and composition adds depths at a common budget.
    Every finite native protocol with positive input dimension admits some finite
    budget. This last assertion does not give a budget uniform in system size.
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}
    \uses{lem:qc_native_register_wires, lem:qc_register_channel_lift}
    \leanok
    Reindexing and tensoring with an identity preserve CPTP. The split and
    its inverse cancel, as do the native encoding and completed decoding.
    Tensor the native intertwining equation with the identity on the
    complementary configuration space and conjugate by the same split.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Encoded physical-port simulation of bounded native protocols]
    \label{thm:qc_bounded_physical_protocol_compilation}
    \uses{thm:qc_fixed_register_word, lem:qc_physical_register_encoding, def:qc_physical_port_protocol}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.dataChannelLift_word,
        QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol.exists_physicalPortSimulation}
    \leanok
    A fixed-register operation word with $T$ pair layers has physical depth at
    most $2kT$. In particular, fix $d\geq2$, $N\geq2$, and one positive
    intermediate-dimension bound $B$.
    Set $k=\lceil\log_d B\rceil$. Every bounded native protocol $\Psi$ of depth
    $T$ has an actual physical-port realization $\Gamma$ of depth at most
    $2kT$, with
    \begin{align}
        \Gamma\,\mathcal E_{\mathrm{in}}
         & =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{out}}(\Psi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{R}),
    \end{align}
    where $R$ is the complete port/scratch reference and the physical encodings
    are constructed from independent site codes. This equality holds on all
    input-reference operators. A uniform family overhead requires fixing $d,B$
    before $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \uses{thm:qc_fixed_register_word, thm:qc_native_port_layer, lem:qc_physical_register_encoding}
    \leanok
    Compile to the fixed alphabet $d^k$, apply the zero-cost onsite and $2k$-cost
    layer implementations along the explicit word, then transport the native
    intertwiner through the physical encoding. No physical simulation equation
    is assumed. This encoded statement does not itself supply the source's
    original physical-port input/output initialization convention or a complete
    QCcc dilation and protocol-block witness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Original physical-port input and output}

The source treats onsite local unitaries and ancillas as free
resources~\cite[main text, p.~1]{Piroli2021LOCC}. The following bound counts
only intersite layers through the fixed physical ports; it is not a count
that also charges every onsite operation. The local CPTP primitives retain
a separate unitary-dilation representation obligation.

\begin{lemma}[Channels on complete spatial-site wire blocks]
    \label{lem:qc_whole_site_channels}
    \uses{lem:qc_native_register_wires, def:qc_physical_port_protocol}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PortRegisters.blockDigits,

    \leanok
    Grouping all digits of one spatial site preserves placement of native
    operators on selected sites. Independent channels on the complete physical
    site blocks therefore have actual zero-depth physical-port implementations.
    Their Kraus operators are supported within the named spatial sites; no
    free global regrouping operation is assumed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    \uses{lem:qc_native_register_wires, lem:qc_onsite_channel_product}
    \leanok
    Cancel the injective all-data placement in the native selected-register
    identity to obtain placement on whole spatial-site blocks. Decompose the
    onsite channel into its single-site factors. Each factor has normalized
    Kraus operators supported within one site, so composition retains depth zero.
\end{proof}

    \leanok

\begin{proof}

    Their product isometries agree with the onsite product encoders under
    sitewise digit coordinates. Complete each local decoder outside its
    code, and compose it with the other encoder. The onsite intertwining
    identity gives exact code conversion, while whole-site locality gives
    the zero-depth certificates.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Concrete data initialization and the zero reference]
    \label{lem:qc_data_initialization}

    The product data initializer equals the physical encoder composed with

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    \uses{thm:qc_physical_port_codes, lem:qc_physical_register_encoding}
    \leanok

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded simulation on the original physical ports]
    \label{thm:qc_bounded_physical_port_io}
    \uses{def:qc_dimension_bounded_protocol, thm:qc_physical_port_codes}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol.exists_physicalPortIO}
    \leanok
    Let the native input and output dimensions both equal the physical
    qudit dimension $d\geq2$, let $N\geq2$, and suppose every intermediate
    local dimension is positive and at most one bound $B$. For
    $k=\lceil\log_d B\rceil$, an actual physical-port protocol satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \Gamma_{\mathrm{port}} J_{\mathrm{port}}
         & = J_{\mathrm{port}}\Psi,
         & \operatorname{depth}_{\mathrm{intersite}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{port}})
         & \leq 2kT.
    \end{align}
    Here $J_{\mathrm{port}}$ places the input on its original physical ports
    and initializes all designated data/scratch memory in a product zero state.
    The output occupies the same ports, and that designated memory is reset
    to zero and uncorrelated with it. The identity holds on arbitrary operators
    and remains exact with an external reference.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \uses{thm:qc_bounded_physical_protocol_compilation, thm:qc_physical_port_codes, lem:qc_data_initialization}
    \leanok
    Preprocess locally into the data code, use the bounded encoded compiler,
    and postprocess locally back into the port code. The explicit initialization
    identity identifies the compiler's reference with the product-zero memory
    state. Both extra channel maps have physical depth zero. This theorem does
    not assert reset of discarded dilation environments or construct a full
    source QCcc protocol-block witness. The following unitary construction
    supplies the explicit local environments and final discard.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unitary circuits with one final environment discard}

The finite reduced-channel protocols have unitary realizations with the
same physical-port intersite depth. Every extra environment wire has a
specified spatial owner, is initialized in the zero basis state at the
start, and is discarded only after the circuit has finished.

\begin{definition}[Port-preserving wire embeddings]
    \label{def:qc_physical_port_embedding}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.append,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.appendEmbedding}
    \leanok
    An embedding of two layouts is an injection of wires that preserves
    their spatial owners and sends each designated physical port to the
    corresponding port in the larger layout. Given an assignment of owners
    to $A$ new wires, appending them to a layout gives such an embedding of
    its $W$ original wires. None of the new wires is a communication port.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Preservation of physical depth under wire embeddings]
    \label{thm:qc_physical_port_embedding}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_embedding}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortEmbedding.unitary}
    \leanok
    If $U$ has a physical-port unitary circuit of intersite depth at most
    $T$, its placement along a port-preserving wire embedding has a
    physical-port unitary circuit of depth at most $T$ in the enlarged layout.
    It acts as the identity on all additional wires.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol, lem:qc_placed_operators}
    Induct on the unitary circuit. An onsite operator remains supported at
    the same spatial site. An onsite wire permutation extends by the
    identity outside the old wires. Each counted layer still acts on the
    designated ports. Placement preserves multiplication of operators.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Environment input and discard]
    \label{def:qc_appended_environment}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.appendConfigurationSplit,
        QuantumCircuit.appendConfigurationSplit_append,
        QuantumCircuit.appendMatrixSplit,
        QuantumCircuit.appendEnvironmentInput,
        QuantumCircuit.discardAppendedEnvironment}
    \leanok
    Write $\mathcal H_W=(\C^d)^{\otimes W}$, with $d\geq1$.
    Restricting a configuration of $W+A$ wires to its two blocks identifies
    $\mathcal H_{W+A}$ with $\mathcal H_W\otimes\mathcal H_A$.
    In these coordinates, the initialization and discard maps are
    $\mathcal I_A(X)=X\otimes Z_A$ and $\mathcal D_A(Y)=\tr_A Y$, where
    $Z_A=\ketbra{0^A}{0^A}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Operators on original wires with an appended environment]
    \label{thm:qc_appended_environment_operators}
    \uses{def:qc_appended_environment, lem:qc_register_channel_lift}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.appendMatrixSplit_embedOp,
        QuantumCircuit.discardAppendedEnvironment_conj,
        QuantumCircuit.discardAppendedEnvironment_lift}
    \leanok
    In the original/environment coordinates, placement of a matrix $U$
    on the original wires is $U\otimes I_A$. For an arbitrary matrix $V$
    on all $W+A$ wires, the map
    $\mathcal D_A\circ\operatorname{Ad}_V\circ\mathcal I_A$ is exactly
    the partial-trace formula in these coordinates, with
    $\operatorname{Ad}_V(X)=VXV^\dagger$. In particular, placement of $U$
    followed by this reduction gives $\operatorname{Ad}_U$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:qc_register_channel_lift}
    Apply the register split with the complementary wires enumerated in
    their original order. Matrix reindexing preserves multiplication and
    adjoints. The environment factor $Z_A$ has trace one.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Disjoint appended environments]
    \label{def:qc_disjoint_appended_environments}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_embedding, def:qc_appended_environment}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortEmbedding.appendEnvironment,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortEmbedding.appendEnvironment_wires_old,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortEmbedding.appendEnvironment_wires_new,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.appendLeftEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.PhysicalPortLayout.appendRightEmbedding,
        QuantumCircuit.appendEnvironmentConfigurationSplit,
        QuantumCircuit.appendEnvironmentConfigurationSplit_symm_apply}
    \leanok
    An owner-preserving injection between environment wires extends by the
    identity on all original wires. Thus circuits on $W+A$ and $W+B$ wires
    embed into the common layout on $W+(A+B)$ wires, using disjoint
    environment blocks and the same original ports. The configuration split
    for the common layout orders the factors as
    $(\mathcal H_W\otimes\mathcal H_A)\otimes\mathcal H_B$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coordinates of the two environment extensions]
    \label{thm:qc_two_environment_coordinates}
    \uses{def:qc_disjoint_appended_environments}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.reindex_appendLeft_embedOp,
        QuantumCircuit.reindex_appendRight_embedOp,
        QuantumCircuit.discardAppendedEnvironment_eq_iterated,
        QuantumCircuit.reindex_appendEnvironmentInput}
    \leanok
    Let $U$ act on the original system and first environment, and let $V$
    act on the original system and second environment. In the three-block
    coordinates, their placed operators are $U\otimes I_B$ and
    $\widetilde V=\Sigma^\dagger(V\otimes I_A)\Sigma$, where $\Sigma$
    sends $(s,a,b)$ to $(s,b,a)$. Initialization is
    $(X\otimes Z_A)\otimes Z_B$, and discarding the combined environment
    equals $\tr_A\tr_B$ in these coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:qc_register_channel_lift}
    The two wire-sum bijections enumerate the selected wires and exactly
    their complements. Reindex the register-placement identity by these
    bijections. The zero configuration separates as $(0^A,0^B)$, and the
    two finite partial-trace sums combine into the trace over $A+B$ wires.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Composition with disjoint fresh environments]
    \label{thm:qc_appended_environment_composition}
    \uses{def:qc_disjoint_appended_environments}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortUnitary.appendEnvironment_comp,
        QuantumCircuit.appendEnvironment_comp_trace,
        QuantumCircuit.appendEnvironment_comp_channel}
    \leanok
    Let $\overline U,\overline V$ be the two placed operators in the common
    layout and set $C=\overline V\overline U$. Then
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal D_{A+B}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_C\circ\mathcal I_{A+B}
         & =(\mathcal D_B\circ\operatorname{Ad}_V\circ\mathcal I_B)
        \circ(\mathcal D_A\circ\operatorname{Ad}_U\circ\mathcal I_A).
        \label{eq:qc_appended_environment_composition}
    \end{align}
    The reduction identity also holds with arbitrary initial environment
    matrices in place of $Z_A,Z_B$. If $U,V$ have certified physical-port
    depths at most $T,S$, then $C$ has certified depth at most $T+S$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:qc_two_environment_coordinates, thm:qc_physical_port_embedding}
    In the three-block coordinates, the first operation sends
    $(X\otimes\sigma)\otimes\tau$ to
    $[U(X\otimes\sigma)U^\dagger]\otimes\tau$.
    The second operation acts identically on the first environment, so
    tracing that environment commutes with this operation, even when it
    is correlated with the system. Taking both partial traces gives
    (\ref{eq:qc_appended_environment_composition}). Both layout embeddings
    preserve ports and spatial owners; multiplication adds the depth bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Selected local wires and their fresh environment]

\begin{theorem}[Placement of a local environment reduction]
    \label{thm:qc_selected_environment_reduction}
    \uses{def:qc_selected_environment, def:qc_appended_environment}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.environmentKraus_selectedEnvironment,
        QuantumCircuit.reduced_selectedEnvironment}
    \leanok
    For an arbitrary matrix on the selected register and its fresh
    environment, placing the matrix and then reducing the environment
    gives the placement of its local reduced map. Every unselected
    original wire remains an identity reference. The initialized
    environment-column blocks of the placed matrix are the placements
    of the corresponding local blocks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:qc_register_channel_lift}
    The selected/environment configuration is the restriction to the
    selected original wires together with the complete environment word.
    Each column block is therefore the corresponding local matrix tensored
    with the identity on unselected wires. Sum the resulting Kraus maps
    and use representation-independent register placement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fresh onsite unitary dilations]
    \label{thm:qc_fresh_onsite_unitary_dilation}
    \uses{def:qc_appended_environment, def:qc_selected_environment}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.exists_qudit_freshEnvironment_unitary,
        QuantumCircuit.exists_selected_onsite_dilation,
        QuantumCircuit.exists_onsite_appended_dilation}
    \leanok
    A CPTP map on $m$ $d$-level qudits has a unitary dilation with $2m$
    fresh $d$-level environment qudits, initialized to zero. The original
    $m$-qudit system is retained. If the selected wires all belong to one
    spatial site, assigning every fresh wire to that site makes this an
    actual zero-intersite-depth circuit. Consequently every normalized
    Kraus channel supported within one site's wires has such a local
    appended-environment realization, independently of its Kraus count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:qc_selected_environment_reduction, lem:qc_placed_operators}
    The system dimension is $d^m$ and the environment dimension
    $d^{2m}=(d^m)^2$. The Choi-rank bound and unitary extension of the
    pure-input isometry give the retained-system dilation, following
    \cite[Theorems~2.2 and~2.5]{Wolf2012Quantum}.
    Place that unitary on the selected wires and their fresh environment.
    Its support lies in one spatial site. For a supported Kraus family,
    extract its matrices on the site register; injectivity of operator
    placement transfers their normalization to that register.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Same-depth unitary realization with one final discard]
    \label{thm:qc_physical_port_unitary_dilation}
    \uses{def:qc_physical_port_protocol, def:qc_appended_environment}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.exists_appended_unitary_dilation,
        QuantumCircuit.IsPhysicalPortProtocol.exists_appended_unitary_dilation_reference}
    \leanok
    Fix $d\geq1$ and a layout on a nonempty set of ring sites.
    For every finite physical-port channel protocol $\Phi$ of depth at
    most $T$, there are finitely many fresh environment wires with assigned
    spatial owners and an actual unitary $U$ on the port-preserving
    extension such that
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal D_A\circ\operatorname{Ad}_U\circ\mathcal I_A & =\Phi,
                                                              & \operatorname{depth}_{\mathrm{intersite}}(U) & \leq T.
        \label{eq:qc_physical_port_dilation}
    \end{align}
    All fresh wires start in a product-zero state and are discarded only
    at the end. The equality holds on all original-wire operators and
    remains exact after tensoring every map with an arbitrary reference
    identity. No reset of the discarded environment is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:qc_appended_environment_operators, thm:qc_fresh_onsite_unitary_dilation,
        thm:qc_appended_environment_composition}
    Induct on the channel protocol. A unitary primitive needs no fresh
    wires. Dilate an onsite Kraus primitive using fresh wires at its site.
    At composition, combine the two disjoint environment blocks and apply
    (\ref{eq:qc_appended_environment_composition}). Increasing a depth upper
    bound preserves the same witness. Tensoring the resulting map equality
    with the reference identity preserves composition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded unitary simulation on the original ports]
    \label{thm:qc_bounded_unitary_port_io}
    \uses{def:qc_dimension_bounded_protocol, def:qc_appended_environment,
        thm:qc_physical_port_codes}
    \lean{QuantumCircuit.IsDimensionBoundedLocalChannelProtocol.exists_physicalPortUnitaryIO}
    \leanok
    Let $d\geq2$, $N\geq2$, and let $\Psi$ be a depth-$T$ native protocol
    whose input and output dimensions equal $d$ and whose positive
    intermediate local dimensions are all at most $B$. With
    $k=\lceil\log_d B\rceil$, an actual unitary circuit on the physical
    ports, designated memory, and fresh local environments satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal D_A\circ\operatorname{Ad}_U\circ\mathcal I_A\circ J_{\mathrm{port}}
         & =J_{\mathrm{port}}\circ\Psi,
         & \operatorname{depth}_{\mathrm{intersite}}(U) & \leq2kT.
        \label{eq:qc_bounded_unitary_port_io}
    \end{align}
    Both the designated data/scratch memory and the added environments
    start in product-zero states. After the environment discard, the
    output returns to its original ports and the designated memory resets
    to zero. The identity holds for arbitrary input operators. Uniform
    family overhead requires $B$ and $d$ to be fixed independently of $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:qc_bounded_physical_port_io, thm:qc_physical_port_unitary_dilation}
    Apply the same-depth unitary dilation to the original-port channel
    protocol and compose its map equality with the port initializer.
    The designated-memory reset follows from the original-port identity;
    the additional environments are discarded without a reset claim.
    The source's QCcc block/control and pure-output system--ancilla
    conditions remain separate, as recorded in
    \cite{gap:psc21_physical_port_simulation_scope}.
\end{proof}
