\section{Matching elementary sides under midpoint subdivision}
\label{sec:area_law_actual_side_matching}

When two fine cells share a side segment, their midpoint subdivisions
agree on that segment. The theorem below establishes this matching for
every elementary segment under the subdivision determined by all cell
corners at or above a fixed layer index $k_0$; see
\cite[Section~11]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.

\begin{theorem}[Every elementary segment with contact matches]
    \label{thm:area_law_actual_elementary_side_matching}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.fineLayer_elementary_contact_match}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:area_law_actual_side_mask,def:area_law_fine_layer_indices,
        def:area_law_fine_belt_scales}
    Fix a common origin $o\in\mathbb R^2$, finite endpoint set $Z$,
    integer width $C_0\ge2$ and natural indices $k_0,k,h$ with
    $k\ge50\,000\,000$ and $k_0\le k,h$.
    Let $z\in F_{k,\ell_k}$ and $w\in F_{h,\ell_h}$ be
    fine-cell indices with $(k,z)\ne(h,w)$, and write
    $Q_{k,z}=Q_{\ell_k}(z)$ and $Q_{h,w}=Q_{\ell_h}(w)$.
    Let $[a_i,b_i]$ be any elementary segment of $Q_{k,z}$
    under its midpoint subdivision relative to $k_0$.
    Suppose that
    $[a_i,b_i]\cap\overline{Q_{h,w}}$ contains two distinct points.
    Then there is an elementary segment $[a_j,b_j]$ of $Q_{h,w}$
    under its midpoint subdivision relative to $k_0$
    such that
    \begin{align}
        s(j) & =s(i)+2\pmod4,       \\
        a_i  & =b_j,\qquad b_i=a_j,
    \end{align}
    where $s(i)$ and $s(j)$ are their counterclockwise side indices.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:area_law_fine_cell_contact,thm:area_law_elementary_side_geometry,
        def:area_law_actual_side_mask,thm:area_law_elementary_corner_endpoint,
        thm:area_law_unsplit_side_endpoints,thm:area_law_adjacent_fine_meshes}
    The intersection $\overline{Q_{k,z}}\cap\overline{Q_{h,w}}$ is either
    a full side of each cell or a full side of the smaller cell equal to
    one half of a side of the larger cell. Because the contact contains
    two distinct points and two distinct sides of a square meet in at most
    one point, it determines the facing side of $Q_{k,z}$.

    When the two sides have equal length, they share the same midpoint
    and therefore the same incident corners at layers $\ge k_0$. If
    neither is subdivided, the whole sides match; if both are subdivided,
    the first half of one side matches the second half of the opposite
    side, and conversely.

    When the cells have unequal sizes, a corner of the smaller cell lies
    at the midpoint of the larger side. Since the smaller cell belongs to
    a layer $\ge k_0$, this corner forces the larger side to be subdivided.
    The smaller side is undivided: a corner at its midpoint would lie
    strictly inside the corresponding half of the larger side,
    contradicting the elementary-endpoint bound
    applied to the larger cell (whose layer index is at least $\min(k,h)$
    and whose scale is at least $t_k$, so the late-layer bound holds).

    Hence the whole smaller side matches the corresponding half of the
    larger side. When $Q_{k,z}$ is the larger cell, contact in two
    distinct points selects that half, since the two halves meet only at
    their midpoint. Counterclockwise orientation along opposite facing
    sides reverses the endpoints, giving $a_i=b_j$ and $b_i=a_j$.
\end{proof}
