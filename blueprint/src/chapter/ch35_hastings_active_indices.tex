\section{Counting the indices of a finite Trotter product}
\label{sec:hastings_active_indices}

Let $V$ be a finite set of sites, with a local dimension $d_j$ at each site
$j\in V$. The construction of~\cite[lines 1069--1077]{Hastings2006Solving}
assigns indices only to the sites in the support of each factor of a Trotter
product. This section counts these indices; the local dimension may vary
from site to site.

\begin{definition}[Active factor occurrences]
    \label{def:hastings_active_occurrences}
    \lean{TNLean.Lattice.trotterActiveOccurrences}
    \leanok
    Let $X_i\subseteq V$ be the support assigned to the factor centred at $i$.
    In $n$ sweeps, each containing one factor per centre, the active occurrences
    at $j$ form
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal A_j=\{(s,i):0\le s<n,\ j\in X_i\}.
        \label{eq:hastings_active_occurrences}
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local index cardinalities]
    \label{thm:hastings_active_cardinality}
    \lean{
        TNLean.Lattice.card_trotterActiveOccurrences,
        TNLean.Lattice.card_trotterActiveIndices,
        TNLean.Lattice.card_trotterActiveOccurrences_le
    }
    \leanok
    \uses{def:hastings_active_occurrences}
    Put $c_j=\#\{i:j\in X_i\}$. Then $|\mathcal A_j|=nc_j$, and assigning
    one pair of physical indices at $j$ to each occurrence gives an index set
    of cardinality $(d_j^2)^{nc_j}$. A zero exponent gives cardinality one,
    including when $d_j=0$.
    On a finite planar domain with walk distance, if $X_i$ lies in the
    radius-$R$ ball about $i$, then $|\mathcal A_j|$ is at most $n$ times
    the cardinality of the radius-$R$ ball about $j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    The set in (\ref{eq:hastings_active_occurrences}) is the product of the
    $n$ sweeps and the $c_j$ contributing centres. Each component has $d_j^2$
    choices. Reversing a walk from $i$ to $j$ places every contributing
    centre in the ball about $j$.
\end{proof}

These index sets are the site index sets of a matrix product form of the
Trotter product, for every choice of the local dimensions.

\begin{lemma}[Matrix product form with the counted index sets]
    \label{lem:hastings_active_index_product_form}
    \lean{
        TNLean.Lattice.exists_productForm_trotterProduct_card_index_eq,
        TNLean.Lattice.isSupportedOn_exp_smul
    }
    \leanok
    \uses{
        def:hastings_active_occurrences,
        thm:hastings_active_cardinality,
        def:hastings_product_form,
        thm:hastings_product_form_mul
    }
    Prescribe a neighborhood $N_i\subseteq V$ at every site $i$, and let each
    $H_i$ act on the sites of $X_i\subseteq N_i$. Let $i_1,\dots,i_m$ list
    every site of $V$ exactly once. For $\beta\in\mathbb R$ and
    $n\in\mathbb N$, with the zero-step product equal to $1$, the Trotter
    product
    \begin{align*}
        \Bigl[\prod_{k=1}^m\exp\bigl(-\tfrac\beta nH_{i_k}\bigr)\Bigr]^n
    \end{align*}
    has a matrix product form with neighborhoods $N_i$ whose index set at
    every site $j$ has as many elements as the set of maps assigning a pair
    of physical indices at $j$ to each occurrence in $\mathcal A_j$, namely
    $(d_j^2)^{nc_j}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    The exponential of an operator acting on $X_i$ again acts on $X_i$,
    since these operators form a closed algebra. Expand each factor in
    matrix units at the sites of its support, with a one-element index set
    elsewhere, and attach the matrix-element coefficient to its centre
    $i$; this coefficient reads only indices in $X_i\subseteq N_i$.
    Multiplying such forms pairs the indices at each site, so an ordered
    product carries one pair of physical indices at $j$ for every factor
    acting at $j$. A sweep contains $c_j$ such factors, and $n$ sweeps give
    $nc_j$ pairs, which is the cardinality of
    Lemma~\ref{thm:hastings_active_cardinality}. If some local dimension is
    zero there are no configurations, and index sets of these cardinalities
    with vanishing coefficients give a matrix product form.
\end{proof}

On a finite metric lattice in which every radius-$R$ ball contains at
most $S$ sites, every $c_j$ is at most $S$; for positive local dimensions
this recovers the bound $(d_j^2)^{nS}$ of
Lemma~\ref{thm:hastings_trotter_index_count}, which needs only distinct
centres.

This is the count asserted at lines 1069--1077 of
\cite{Hastings2006Solving}. It does not establish convergence to the Gibbs
operator or the separate approximation and percolation estimates for
$\rho(\beta,l_{\mathrm{proj}})$.
