\section{The actual corrected soft resampling tree}
\label{sec:physical_soft_resampling_tree}

The interval families below are obtained from the original Hamiltonian,
with one choice on every possible long interval before all regional centers
and seed sequences. Only the internal intervals of the actual balanced root
enter the probability estimate. The same regional minima, represented soft
matrices and spectral targets are retained for the energy argument.

The construction uses the corrected soft and cluster estimates in
\cite{gap:alvv16_soft_regional_residuals,gap:alvv16_cluster_approximation},
the corrected complex sphere count in
\cite{gap:alvv16_random_sampling_net_count}, and the terminal full-image
convention in~\cite{gap:alvv16_bootstrap_constants}. It is a corrected
auxiliary construction for~\cite[lines 749--755 and 1777--1817]{Arad2016RigorousRG}.
It is not identified with the printed fourth-order choice or its sharp
dependence on the gap, and no running-time estimate is asserted.

\begin{lemma}[Choosing the interval families while preserving their data]
    \label{lem:canonical_interval_resampling_selection}
    \lean{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_canonical_interval_resampling_tree_with_certificate}\leanok
    Fix $q\ge1$, a positive leaf width $w$, a common threshold $\theta\ge2$,
    a number of rounds $R$, a family parameter $D$, $0<\xi\le1$ and a
    common count $M_*$ such that
    \[
        q^w\le M_*,\qquad D^2\theta\le M_*.
    \]
    Fix a global target $G$. Suppose that, on every valid long interval
    $J$, there exists a family $F_J$ of $n_J\le D^2$ operators satisfying
    an additional assertion $C_J(n_J,F_J)$. Suppose its actual physical
    dimension is at most $2^R$, and that every fixed $0.04$-viable
    candidate satisfies
    \[
        \mu_J(H_J(W)^c)\le\beta,
    \]
    with the same literal original-target history $H_J(W)$ implying
    $3/200$-viability of the complete parent. Then all families may be
    selected simultaneously, preserving every assertion $C_J$.

    For their same canonical balanced tree, the exact root/list event
    $\mathcal G_{0,L}$ of Definition~\ref{def:canonical_interval_resampling_good}
    is measurable and
    \[
        \mathbb P(\mathcal G_{0,L}^c)\le(L-1)_+\beta.
    \]
    It is the whole seed space if $L\le w$. For every seed and every
    valid retained interval, the space has dimension at most $M_*$
    and at most $M_*$ spanning generators satisfying
    \[
        \xi^2\operatorname{SR}_j(u_i)\le M_*
    \]
    at every internal cut $j$. This bound concerns the recorded generators.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:actual_interval_original_target_joint_probability,
        lem:canonical_interval_resampling_good_measurable,
        lem:actual_interval_resampling_tree_profile}
    Choose one of the assumed families on each interval, retaining its
    additional data. Apply the existing joint-history estimate and the
    unconditional recorded-rank estimate to those same families.
    A short full root is the full local space and has an empty merge
    list, so its joint event is the whole seed space.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A physical corrected soft resampling tree]
    \label{thm:physical_soft_resampling_tree}
    \lean{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ALVV.exists_physical_soft_resampling_tree}\leanok
    Fix $q\ge2$ and $\gamma>0$, and put $g=\min\{\gamma,1\}$.
    There are natural numbers $u\ge2$, $K,C_{\rm count}\ge1$, a positive
    real $C_\xi$ and a natural $P_\xi$, before every chain length and
    Hamiltonian, with the following properties. Use the numerical
    quantities in (\ref{eq:calibrated_soft_parameters}), evaluated at $g$,
    and denote their approximation precision by $\xi_{\rm MPO}$.
    All residual, positivity, scale and closeness inequalities, and
    the bounds on the contraction in
    Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform_soft_parameters}, are retained.

    Let $H=\sum_i h_i$ on $L$ sites, with nearest-neighbour supports and
    $0\le h_i\le I$. Suppose $\mu\in\operatorname{spec}H$ and every
    spectral value equals $\mu$ or is at least $\mu+\gamma$.
    Put $G=\ker(H-\mu I)$, $r=\dim G$ and $Z=(L+1)(r+1)$. Set
    \begin{align}
        w              & =4\ell,                 & \theta & =K(r+1)(L+2)^2, & R & =qL,\notag \\
        M_{\rm common} & =\max\{q^w,D^2\theta\}.
        \label{eq:physical_soft_tree_schedule}
    \end{align}
    One may choose $0<\xi_{\rm init}\le1$ and one native family $F_J$
    for every valid long interval $J$, before all center estimates,
    seed sequences and final approximation accuracies, such that
    \begin{align}
        \xi_{\rm init}^{-2} & \le C_\xi Z^{P_\xi},  &
        M_{\rm common}      & \le C_{\rm count}Z^2, & n_J & \le D^2.
        \label{eq:physical_soft_tree_size}
    \end{align}

    Each selected family retains the geometry, five attained regional
    minima, common minimizing vector, and represented-target conclusions
    of Lemma~\ref{lem:physical_soft_interval_history}. In particular the
    native matrices $W_{J,i}$ satisfy $F_{J,i}=W_{J,i}$ as operators.
    For every admissible triple of center estimates, its own Hermitian
    represented matrix $\widehat G_J$ is within $\xi_{\rm MPO}$ of the
    same centered five-region soft matrix and has norm at most
    $M+\xi_{\rm MPO}$. Its spectral spaces at $E+g/4$ and $E+3g/4$
    coincide, have dimension $r$, and are mutually $3\tau/200$-close to
    the original $G$. The relation $E+\sum_j e_j=\mu$ is retained.
    For every $0.04$-viable initial candidate, on its same literal
    original-target history the same terminal untrimmed image is $\Delta/(91/100)^2$-viable for every one of
    these targets. Neither a new family nor another target approximant
    is selected for that assertion.

    Form the actual complete-parent balanced tree using these fixed
    families, threshold, radius and finite sequences. Its exact joint
    history event $\mathcal G_{0,L}$ is measurable and
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb P(\mathcal G_{0,L}^c)\le\frac1{(L+1)^2}.
        \label{eq:physical_soft_tree_probability}
    \end{align}
    On this event the actual root and every listed internal interval
    are $3/200$-viable for the original target, and every listed parent
    has its same original-target history. No success at other overlapping
    intervals is asserted. If $L\le w$, this event is the whole seed space.

    For every seed, independently of success, every actual retained
    interval has dimension at most $M_{\rm common}$ and a spanning family
    of at most $M_{\rm common}$ vectors with
    \begin{align}
        \xi_{\rm init}^2\operatorname{SR}_j(u_i)\le M_{\rm common}
        \label{eq:physical_soft_tree_recorded_ranks}
    \end{align}
    at every internal cut. The statement includes empty whole chains
    and flat spectra and assumes neither a reference space nor an
    initial matrix product representation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:physical_soft_sampling_data,lem:physical_soft_interval_history,
        lem:canonical_interval_resampling_selection}
    Choose the uniform numerical data and the independent original-ground
    reference once. Its common radius supplies every interval's exact
    weighted trimming budget. Apply the physical interval-history
    construction once at each long interval, preserving its complete
    minimum and target data. The original gap implies the same gap with
    $g=\min\{\gamma,1\}$. Every long interval belongs to a nonempty whole
    chain, so the one-interval construction applies without restricting
    the full theorem's chain length.

    Apply the selection lemma to these proven physical assertions.
    The finite interval law has failure coefficient
    \[
        \beta=R\frac{1/q}{(L+2)^4}.
    \]
    The common scalar schedule gives
    $(L-1)_+\beta\le(L+1)^{-2}$. The same selection lemma supplies
    measurability, the short-root identity and the unconditional recorded
    profiles. The reference inverse-square estimate and common-count
    estimate give (\ref{eq:physical_soft_tree_size}). All center-dependent
    target and terminal-image conclusions are those of the same selected
    physical families.
\end{proof}
