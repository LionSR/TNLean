\section{Hermitian parts of operator chains}
\label{sec:hermitian_operator_chains}

The approximation to a Hamiltonian may be taken Hermitian while preserving
its norm error. The following tensor identities supply this property for
actual site-dependent matrix product operators. They support the operator
approximation in~\cite[lines 1262--1268 and 1412--1420]{Arad2016RigorousRG}.

\begin{lemma}[The adjoint of an operator chain]
    \label{lem:trace_chain_adjoint}
    \lean{MPOTensor.traceChainOperator_conjTranspose}
    \leanok
    Let
    \begin{align}
        T_{\sigma,\tau}
           =\operatorname{tr}\bigl(CA_0^{\sigma_0,\tau_0}
                                  \cdots A_{L-1}^{\sigma_{L-1},\tau_{L-1}}\bigr).
    \end{align}
    Define $B_v^{i,j}=\overline{A_v^{j,i}}$, where the conjugation is
    entrywise on the virtual matrix, and replace $C$ by $\overline C$.
    These tensors represent $T^*$ at the same bond dimension.
    The chain, physical dimension and virtual dimension may be zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    Exchanging the physical indices and conjugating the scalar trace gives
    the adjoint entry. Complex conjugation preserves the order of matrix
    multiplication and commutes with the trace. Applying these identities
    to the finite product proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A Hermitian approximation with actual tensors]
    \label{lem:hermitian_trace_chain_approximation}
    \lean{MPOTensor.exists_traceChainOperator_selfAdjointPart,
        MPOTensor.exists_hermitian_traceChainOperator_approximation}
    \leanok
    On a nonempty chain, an operator $T$ with actual tensors of virtual
    dimension $D$ has a Hermitian part $(T+T^*)/2$ with actual tensors of
    virtual dimension $2D$. If $H=H^*$ and $\|H-T\|\leq\epsilon$, this
    constructed Hermitian operator satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \|H-(T+T^*)/2\|\leq\epsilon.
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    Use the preceding tensors for $T^*$ and put the two operator chains
    on diagonal blocks, with coefficients $1/2$ in the closing matrix.
    Absorb that closing matrix into the first site. The resulting operator
    is the Hermitian part. Its error is at most the original error because
    the Hermitian-part map is real-linear, fixes $H$, and has norm at most one.
\end{proof}
