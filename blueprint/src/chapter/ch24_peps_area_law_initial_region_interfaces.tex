\section{Colors across initial interfaces}
\label{sec:area_law_initial_region_interfaces}

Retain the actual initial identifiers, closed birth regions $X_i^0$ and
colors $\lambda(i)$ of \cite[Section~11]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}. The origin,
finite endpoint set and valid residue choices are arbitrary, with
$C\ge 2$ and $k_0\ge 50{,}000{,}000$.

\begin{theorem}[Opposite colors along a shared segment]
    \label{thm:area_law_initial_interface_colors}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.initialRegionColor_ne_of_segment_subset_inter}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:area_law_initial_birth_regions,def:area_law_initial_region_colors}
    Let $i\ne j$ be actual initial identifiers. If $u,v\in\mathbb R^2$
    are distinct and their entire closed segment satisfies
    \begin{align}
        [u,v] & \subseteq X_i^0\cap X_j^0,
    \end{align}
    then $\lambda(i)\ne\lambda(j)$.
    No nonemptiness condition on the endpoint set is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:area_law_initial_run_color,
        thm:area_law_belt_run_primary_interface,
        thm:area_law_belt_runs_interface_colors,
        thm:area_law_belt_run_dummy_interface,
        thm:area_law_fan_run_geometric_contact,
        thm:area_law_local_layer_indices,
        thm:area_law_dummy_contact_index,
        thm:area_law_primary_diameter_separation}
    For an interface involving a belt run and a primary or the dummy,
    the corresponding run-interface theorem gives opposite colors.
    The color of a run agrees with every constituent triangle.
    The same argument handles runs in different indexed cells.
    If two distinct runs lie in one cell, the shared segment supplies
    two distinct common points; the fan-run contact theorem gives
    opposite colors.

    Two contacting primaries lie in neighboring closed layers.
    Distinct primaries in the same layer have positive separation,
    so contact forces consecutive layer indices. Their parities,
    and hence their colors, differ. A primary contacting the dummy
    lies in layer $k_0$; its parity differs from the dummy parity
    $k_0-1$. Two dummy identifiers cannot be distinct. These cases
    exhaust the actual identifier family.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis retains the whole segment. Two isolated common corners of
possibly disconnected regions do not constitute such an interface.
