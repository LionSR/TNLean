\section{Actual initial regions in eight sectors}
\label{sec:area_law_actual_sector_assignment}

Fix an arbitrary origin $o\in\mathbb R^2$, a finite endpoint set
$Z\subset\mathbb Z^2$, an integer width $C\ge2$ and an initial layer
$k_0\ge50{,}000{,}000$. Choose arbitrary valid pitch residues at every
layer, and retain the resulting actual initial identifiers $\mathcal I$.
Write $X_i^0$ for their closed birth regions and
$U_i=\operatorname{int}(X_i^0)$ for their open interiors.
Let $k\ge k_0$, let $z\in F_{k,\ell_k}$ be an actual fine-cell index,
and let $v$ be any of the nine marks of its defining cell. Put
\begin{align}
    \ell & =\ell_k-5,                        \\
    r    & =\frac{2^\ell}{2}=\frac{t_k}{64}.
\end{align}
The late-layer bound implies $\ell_k\ge5$. Use the maximum norm on
$\mathbb R^2$. Center two fans at $v$, with origins
$o_{\rm S}=v-r(1,1)$ and $o_{\rm L}=v-2r(1,1)$, exponents $\ell$ and
$\ell+1$, and cell index $(0,0)$. Divide every side at its midpoint.
Let $\mathcal J$ index the eight corresponding elementary bases, and
write $P_s^{\rm S}$ and $P_s^{\rm L}$ for the corresponding closed
triangles. Their half-sides are $r=t_k/64$ and $2r=t_k/32$.
This local construction is part of
\cite[Section~11]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.

\begin{theorem}[Unique sector assignment and closed restriction]
    \label{thm:area_law_actual_sector_assignment}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_unique_initialRegion_sector_assignment}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:area_law_initial_region_indices,
        def:area_law_initial_birth_regions,def:area_law_initial_open_regions,
        def:area_law_fine_layer_indices,def:area_law_fine_belt_scales,
        def:area_law_belt_cell_marks,def:area_law_cell_fan}
    There is a unique function $\sigma:\mathcal J\to\mathcal I$
    satisfying both assertions below. Every working triangle satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \operatorname{int}(P_s^{\rm L}) & \subseteq U_{\sigma(s)}.
    \end{align}
    For every initial identifier $i\in\mathcal I$,
    \begin{align}
        X_i^0\cap\overline B_\infty(v,r)
         & =\bigcup_{\substack{s\in\mathcal J \\\sigma(s)=i}}P_s^{\rm S}.
    \end{align}
    The union is empty when no triangle is assigned to $i$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:area_law_belt_marks_closed_cell,
        thm:area_law_fine_cell_containment,thm:area_law_fine_belt_scales,
        thm:area_law_dyadic_layer_distance,
        thm:area_law_initial_closed_cover,thm:area_law_initial_birth_regularity,
        thm:area_law_initial_open_disjoint,
        thm:area_law_initial_frontier_near_mark,
        thm:area_law_cell_fan_regularity,thm:area_law_cell_fan_cover,
        thm:area_law_cell_fan_sector_directions,
        thm:area_law_concentric_fan_restriction,
        thm:area_law_dyadic_closures,thm:area_law_adjacent_fine_meshes,
        def:area_law_cell_fan}
    The mark belongs to the closed reference fine cell and hence to
    the closed actual layer. The layer's endpoint-distance assertion
    supplies an element of $Z$, so the actual closed birth regions
    cover the plane. The late-layer bound and monotonicity of the fine
    exponent give $\ell_k\ge5$, and exponent arithmetic gives
    $2r=t_k/32$.

    Put $S_s=\operatorname{int}(P_s^{\rm L})$ and
    $W=B_\infty(v,2r)$. The coordinate interval description of the
    centered square and the fan cover give
    $\bigcup_sP_s^{\rm L}=\overline B_\infty(v,2r)$.
    Taking interiors of the inclusion of each triangle into this
    square shows $S_s\subseteq W$. Each $S_s$ is nonempty by triangle
    regularity, and is preconnected by convexity.

    No $S_s$ meets the frontier of any $X_i^0$. Indeed, a frontier point
    $x\in S_s$ would satisfy $\|x-v\|_\infty<t_k/32$, so the nearby
    frontier theorem would give an allowed direction for $x-v$.
    The all-midpoint triangle assertion excludes that direction.
    Choose a point of $S_s$ and an actual birth region containing it.
    Frontier exclusion puts this point in the region's interior.
    Since $X_i^0=\overline{U_i}$, the same exclusion gives
    $\overline{U_i}\cap S_s\subseteq U_i$. Preconnectedness then
    propagates membership to all of $S_s$. Its nonemptiness and the
    pairwise disjointness of the $U_i$ make its identifier unique.
    Choosing these unique identifiers defines $\sigma$.

    Let $D=\bigcup_sS_s$. Triangle regularity and the finiteness of
    $\mathcal J$ give
    $\overline D=\bigcup_sP_s^{\rm L}=\overline B_\infty(v,2r)$.
    For an arbitrary identifier $i$, set
    $E_i=\bigcup_{\sigma(s)=i}P_s^{\rm L}$.
    This is a closed finite union, including when it is empty.
    The assignment and disjointness give $U_i\cap D\subseteq E_i$.
    Since $U_i$ and $W$ are open and $W\subseteq\overline D$,
    \begin{align}
        U_i\cap W   & \subseteq\overline{U_i\cap D}\subseteq E_i, \\
        X_i^0\cap W & =\overline{U_i}\cap W
        \subseteq\overline{U_i\cap W}\subseteq E_i.
    \end{align}
    Conversely, taking closures of the assigned interiors gives
    $E_i\subseteq X_i^0$. The smaller closed square lies inside $W$
    because $r<2r$, and hence
    $X_i^0\cap\overline B_\infty(v,r)=E_i\cap\overline B_\infty(v,r)$.
    Distribute this intersection through the finite union and apply
    the concentric restriction to each assigned triangle. This yields
    the asserted union of smaller triangles for every identifier.
    Finally, any other assignment has the same identifier on each
    nonempty working sector, and thus equals $\sigma$.
\end{proof}
