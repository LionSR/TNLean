\section{A fixed local family improving ground-space viability}

Closeness and viability have the projection meanings of
\cite[``Closeness and viability of subspaces'']{QICLean2026Blueprint}.
For an interval $J$, identify the full Hilbert space with
$\mathcal H_J\otimes\mathcal H_{J^c}$ by restriction of configurations,
with the interval factor first.

\begin{theorem}[The operator-family conclusion in the gapped case]
    \label{thm:physical_dg_viable_family}
    \lean{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_physical_dg_viable_family}
    \uses{def:chain_model}
    There is a universal real constant $A\ge21$ with the following property.
    Let $H=\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_L}h_i$ be an original nearest-neighbour
    Hamiltonian on $(\C^q)^{\otimes L}$, where each $h_i$ is supported on
    $\{i,i+1\}$ and satisfies $0\le h_i\le\Id$.
    Suppose that $\mu,\nu\in\operatorname{spec}(H)$, $\mu<\nu$,
    $\gamma>0$, and
    \begin{align}
        \operatorname{spec}(H)\subseteq\{\mu\}\cup[\mu+\gamma,\infty).
    \end{align}
    Thus $\mu$ is the original ground energy and the excited spectrum is
    nonempty. Let $G=\ker(H-\mu\Id)$ be the full original ground space;
    its dimension is unrestricted. Put
    \begin{align}
        g&=\min(\gamma,1),&u&=1+\log q+\log(1/g),\notag\\
        \ell&=\lceil Au^2/g\rceil,&\mathcal D&=(q\ell^2)^{22\ell},\notag\\
        c_A&=6+2\log(A+1).
    \end{align}
    Then $\log\mathcal D\le22(A+1)c_Ag^{-1}u^3$.
    For every interval $J=\{p,\ldots,r-1\}$ with $0\le p\le r\le L$,
    there are a subspace $T$ of the full Hilbert space, a number $\Delta>0$,
    and a family of operators $W_0,\ldots,W_{\mathcal D^2-1}$ on
    $\mathcal H_J$ such that $T,G$ are mutually $1/200$-close and
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal D^{12}\Delta\le10^{-5}.
    \end{align}
    The same $T,\Delta,(W_j)$ satisfy the following assertion for every
    subspace $S\subseteq\mathcal H_J$ and every real $\delta<1$:
    if $S$ is $\delta$-viable for $T$, then
    \begin{align}
        S'=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal D^2-1}W_jS
        \quad\text{is }\frac{\Delta}{(1-\delta)^2}\text{-viable for }T.
    \end{align}
    The constant $A$ is chosen before the chain, Hamiltonian, gap and interval;
    the family, $T$ and $\Delta$ are chosen before $S,\delta$.
    No upper bound on $\gamma$, rank-one ground-space condition or boundary
    margin for $J$ is assumed. Empty intervals are included.
    This is the existence and viability part of
    \cite[Theorem t:ais, lines 1010--1024]{Arad2016RigorousRG}.
    The comparison with the full ground space and the restriction $\delta<1$
    follow the source clarification in
    \cite{gap:alvv16_spectral_projector_boundary}.
    An efficient matrix-product representation, running-time estimate,
    entropy consequence and approximation theorem are separate assertions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \uses{thm:short_actual_region_improving_family,thm:inward_effective_cuts,
        thm:five_region_dg_cluster_parameters,thm:actual_clipped_polynomial_family,
        thm:physical_dg_collar_budget}
    The two distinct spectral values imply $q\ge2$. Replace the gap lower
    bound by $g=\min(\gamma,1)$ without changing $H$ or $G$.
    Choose the universal scalar constant used for the five-region cluster.
    If $|J|\le4\ell$, take $T=G$ and apply the short-region matrix-unit
    family; this also covers an empty interval.
    Otherwise move the two cuts inward by at most $\ell$ each, leaving an
    interval $J_0\subseteq J$ with at most $2\ell$ removed sites.
    The actual five-region clipping construction gives $T$, its closed
    spectral gap, the mutual closeness and the positive Chebyshev parameter
    $\Delta$. At degree $\ell^2$, its fixed polynomial family on $J_0$
    has at most $((q\ell^2)^{20\ell})^2$ members. Transfer that same family
    to $J$ and absorb the collar factor into $\mathcal D^2$.
    The actual-cluster Chebyshev estimate of
    \cite[``An actual matrix cluster and a fixed polynomial family'']
    {QICLean2026Blueprint} gives the improvement simultaneously for every
    $S,\delta<1$. Extend the family by zero to exactly $\mathcal D^2$
    members; its sum of image subspaces is unchanged.
\end{proof}
