\section{Primitive frontiers of actual fans}
\label{sec:area_law_fan_frontiers}

The local mesh argument of \cite[Section~11]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw} needs the
actual endpoints of boundary segments. Retain any origin, dyadic exponent,
integer cell index and optional midpoint subdivisions. Write $Q$ for the
half-open square, $c$ for its center, $\mathcal E$ for its cyclic fan slots,
$P_e$ for the corresponding closed triangle and $v_e$ for its final base
endpoint.

\begin{theorem}[Fan frontiers lie on outer or radial edges]
    \label{thm:area_law_fan_frontier_primitives}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.cellFanPolygon_frontier_subset_outer_and_radials}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:area_law_cell_fan}
    For every actual fan slot $e\in\mathcal E$,
    \begin{align}
        \partial P_e & \subseteq\partial Q\cup
        \bigcup_{f\in\mathcal E}[c,v_f].
    \end{align}
    No layer, contact or positive-exponent condition is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:area_law_cell_fan_cover,
        thm:area_law_fan_triangle_contact,thm:area_law_cell_fan_intersections}
    A boundary point of a member of a finite closed cover lies on the
    outer frontier or belongs to another member. Indeed, away from the
    outer frontier and all other members, the complement of their
    finite closed union gives an open neighborhood contained in the
    selected member, contradicting boundary membership.

    Apply this observation to the actual closed fan triangles, whose
    union is $\overline Q$. A shared point is either the center or,
    together with the center, gives two distinct common points. In the
    latter case the triangle-contact classification places it on an
    actual radial segment. The center itself belongs to every such
    segment. This proves the inclusion.
\end{proof}

The inclusion retains primitive edges, including seams that may disappear
when neighboring triangles are merged. Active boundaries are determined
by distinct region identifiers in the subsequent local analysis.
