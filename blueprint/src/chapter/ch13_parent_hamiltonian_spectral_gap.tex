
\section{Spectral gap}\label{sec:spectral_gap}

A frustration-free Hamiltonian is \emph{gapped} if its smallest nonzero
eigenvalue, or the spectral gap above the ground-state space, is bounded away from
zero uniformly in the chain length $N$.
For parent Hamiltonians of matrix product states, the martingale method of
Fannes, Nachtergaele, and Werner~\cite{Fannes1992Finitely, Nachtergaele1996Spectral},
as formulated by Cirac et al.~\cite[Section~IV.C]{Cirac2021Matrix}
and Kastoryano and Lucia~\cite{Kastoryano2018Martingale}, reduces a uniform
spectral gap to an anticommutator estimate for overlapping local terms.
Principal angles between local ground spaces provide a geometric interpretation
of this criterion, and norm-compression bounds for cyclic-window local terms
yield uniform spectral gap lower bounds on periodic chains.
