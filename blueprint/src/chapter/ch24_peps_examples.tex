\section{Examples of PEPS on the torus}\label{sec:peps_examples}

Two-dimensional PEPS on the discrete torus are defined by a four-leg tensor
$A^i_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ placed at every site of a square lattice, with
virtual indices ordered top, right, down, left
\cite[Appendix~A, ``Two dimensions: PEPS'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. On a torus, the
four legs of site $v=(x,y)$ correspond to the edges from $v$ to $(x,y+1)$
(top), from $v$ to $(x+1,y)$ (right), from $(x,y-1)$ to $v$ (down), and from
$(x-1,y)$ to $v$ (left).

\begin{definition}[Torus PEPS of a four-leg tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.torusDownEdge, TNLean.PEPS.torusLeftEdge,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusTopLeg, TNLean.PEPS.torusRightLeg,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusDownLeg, TNLean.PEPS.torusLeftLeg,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusLeftEdge_add_one, TNLean.PEPS.torusDownEdge_add_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusSiteTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_tensor, def:peps_torus_uniform_bond_dim}
    Let $a^i_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ be a tensor with physical dimension
    $d$ and bond dimension $D$. Its torus PEPS on the torus of width $w$ and
    height $h$ has bond dimension $D$ on every edge and, at every site $v$,
    the local tensor $a$ with its four virtual indices read on the top,
    right, down and left legs of $v$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``Two dimensions: PEPS'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. The right
    leg of $v$ is the left leg of $v+(1,0)$, and the top leg of $v$ is the
    down leg of $v+(0,1)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Shift-invariant functions on the torus are constant]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_shift_const}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.torusVertex_apply_eq_apply_zero_of_shift}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_geometry_translation}
    If a function $f$ on the sites of the torus satisfies $f(x+1,y)=f(x,y)$
    and $f(x,y+1)=f(x,y)$ for all $(x,y)$, then $f$ is constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Induction on the representatives $0,\ldots,w-1$ of the first coordinate
    and $0,\ldots,h-1$ of the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Contraction of a torus PEPS]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_torusSiteTensor_eq_sum_edge,
        TNLean.PEPS.isHorizontalTorusEdge_torusRightEdge,
        TNLean.PEPS.isVerticalTorusEdge_torusUpEdge,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusRightEdge_injective, TNLean.PEPS.torusUpEdge_injective,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusRightEdge_ne_torusUpEdge, TNLean.PEPS.torusEdgeEquiv,
        TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_torusSiteTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor, def:peps_stateCoeff}
    Let $w,h\ge 3$. The right edges and the up edges of the sites are
    pairwise distinct and exhaust the edges of the torus, and the coefficient
    of the torus PEPS of $a$ is
    \begin{align}
        \Coeff(\sigma)=\sum_{h_\bullet,v_\bullet}\ \prod_{(x,y)}
        a^{\sigma(x,y)}_{v_{(x,y)}\,h_{(x,y)}\,v_{(x,y-1)}\,h_{(x-1,y)}},
        \notag
    \end{align}
    the sum running over all assignments of bond indices $h_{(x,y)}$ to the
    horizontal edges from $(x,y)$ to $(x+1,y)$ and $v_{(x,y)}$ to the
    vertical edges from $(x,y)$ to $(x,y+1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Every edge is horizontal or vertical, and every horizontal edge is the
    right edge of one of its endpoints and every vertical edge the up edge
    of one of its endpoints. For $w\ge 3$ the right edges of distinct sites
    are distinct: otherwise $p=q+(1,0)$ and $q=p+(1,0)$, so $2=0$ in the
    horizontal coordinate. The same holds vertically for $h\ge 3$. A
    horizontal edge is never vertical. Reindexing the sum over edge
    labellings along this bijection gives the formula.
\end{proof}

On a torus of width two the right and left neighbours of a site coincide and
the two edges joining them are one edge of the lattice graph, so the four
legs of a site are not four distinct bonds. The contraction formula, and the
cluster, CZX, AKLT, RVB and classical-model statements below, therefore require
$w,h\ge 3$; the
source constructions are local and apply to every torus
\cite{gap:rmp_peps_examples_small_torus}.

A virtual string on a torus PEPS acts on the bonds it crosses, diagonally in
the bond basis: a bond carrying the index $\alpha$ contributes an element
$X_\alpha$ of a monoid $M$, and the contributions are multiplied in the order
in which the string crosses the bonds. For a matrix algebra $M$ this is a
matrix product operator on the virtual level.

\begin{lemma}[Pulling a virtual string through a rectangle]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_string_pull_through}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.gridPath_column_prod_eq, TNLean.PEPS.gridPath_prod_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusWestSouthString, TNLean.PEPS.torusNorthEastString,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusWestSouthString_eq_torusNorthEastString,
        TNLean.PEPS.sum_smul_torusWestSouthString_eq}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    Let $a$ be a four-leg tensor and $\alpha\mapsto X_\alpha$ a map from the
    bond indices to a monoid $M$ with $X_tX_r=X_lX_b$ whenever
    $a^{s}_{trbl}\neq0$. Fix a rectangle of $m$ columns and $n$ rows of sites
    of the torus. Consider the string from its upper left to its lower right
    corner that crosses the left legs of the leftmost column, from top to
    bottom, and then the down legs of the bottom row, from left to right, and
    the string that crosses the top legs of the top row and then the right
    legs of the rightmost column. On every labelling of the bonds whose term
    in the contraction of the torus PEPS of $a$ is nonzero the two strings
    have the same product, and so, for an algebra $M$ over $\C$, the
    contracted network with the first string inserted equals the contracted
    network with the second string inserted. This is the pulling-through
    equation of a matrix product operator symmetry
    \cite[Section~``MPO and PEPO'']{Cirac2021Matrix} for strings that are
    diagonal in the bond basis.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Write $P_{ij}$ for the contribution of the left leg of the site in
    column $i$ and row $j$ of the rectangle, counted from its upper left
    site, and $Q_{ij}$ for that of its top leg. The right leg of that site is
    the left leg of the site in column $i+1$, and its down leg is the top leg
    of the site in row $j+1$, so the hypothesis at a site with nonzero entry
    reads $Q_{ij}P_{i+1,j}=P_{ij}Q_{i,j+1}$. Induction on $n$ gives
    $P_{i0}\cdots P_{i,n-1}\,Q_{in}=Q_{i0}\,P_{i+1,0}\cdots P_{i+1,n-1}$
    for one column, and induction on $m$, moving one column at a time, gives
    the identity for the rectangle. A nonzero term of the contraction has a
    nonzero entry at every site, and the terms that vanish contribute zero
    to both sides.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The GHZ state}

\begin{definition}[Two-dimensional GHZ tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_ghz_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.ghzSiteTensor, TNLean.PEPS.ghzPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    The GHZ tensor has $D=d$ and
    $A^i_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\delta_{i=\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\delta}$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The GHZ state'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The GHZ PEPS is the GHZ state]%
    \label{thm:peps_ghz_state}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_ghzPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_ghz_tensor, def:peps_stateCoeff}
    On every torus with $w,h\ge 2$ the GHZ PEPS generates the unnormalized
    GHZ state $\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{i,i,\ldots,i}$: its coefficient at
    $\sigma$ is $\sum_{i}\prod_{v}\delta_{\sigma(v),i}$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The GHZ state'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_shift_const}
    A virtual configuration contributes only if every leg of every site
    carries the physical index of that site. The right leg of $v$ is the left
    leg of $v+(1,0)$ and the top leg of $v$ is the down leg of $v+(0,1)$, so
    $\sigma$ is invariant under both unit shifts and is constant by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_shift_const}. Every edge is then labelled by
    that constant, so exactly one virtual configuration contributes when
    $\sigma$ is constant and none otherwise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The cluster state}

\begin{definition}[Two-dimensional cluster tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_cluster_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.clusterSiteTensor, TNLean.PEPS.clusterPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    The cluster tensor is $A=\ket{0}(00{+}{+}|+\ket{1}(11{-}{-}|$ with
    $(\pm|=[(0|\pm(1|]/\sqrt{2}$: the top and right legs copy the physical
    index $s$, and the down and left legs carry
    $(-1)^{sb}/\sqrt{2}$ at bond index $b$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The cluster state'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The cluster PEPS]%
    \label{thm:peps_cluster_state}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_clusterPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_cluster_tensor, def:peps_stateCoeff}
    On a torus with $w,h\ge 3$ and $N=wh$ sites the cluster PEPS has
    coefficient
    \begin{align}
        \Coeff(\sigma)=2^{-N}\prod_{v}(-1)^{\sigma_v\sigma_{v+(1,0)}
                +\sigma_v\sigma_{v+(0,1)}}.
        \notag
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction} the top and right
    legs force every bond index to equal the physical index of the site
    below or to the left of it. The remaining factor at $v$ is
    $\tfrac12(-1)^{\sigma_v\sigma_{v-(0,1)}+\sigma_v\sigma_{v-(1,0)}}$;
    shifting the product over $v$ by one site in each direction gives the
    formula.
\end{proof}

Up to the factor $2^{-N/2}$, the coefficient in
Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_cluster_state} is that of the state obtained by
applying controlled-$Z$ gates on all nearest-neighbor bonds to
$\ket{+}^{\otimes N}$ \cite[Appendix~A, ``The cluster state'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.

\subsection{The CZX model}

In the CZX model of \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX} every site of the square lattice
carries four qubits, one at each corner, and the ground state on a closed
surface is the product over plaquettes of the states $\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}$
on the four qubits around each plaquette. Blocking the four qubits of a site,
\cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX model'']{Cirac2021Matrix} writes this state as a
PEPS of bond dimension four.

\begin{definition}[CZX tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_czx_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czxBond, TNLean.PEPS.czxQubits, TNLean.PEPS.czxTopLeft,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxTopRight, TNLean.PEPS.czxBottomRight,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBottomLeft, TNLean.PEPS.czxSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBondSwap, TNLean.PEPS.czxBondSwap_czxBond,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    Label the four qubits of a site $\ket{ijkl}$, with $i$ at the top left,
    $j$ at the top right, $k$ at the bottom right and $l$ at the bottom left,
    and label the four basis vectors of a bond by pairs of qubits. The CZX
    tensor is
    \begin{align}
        \sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{1}\ket{ijkl}\bigl((i,j),(j,k),(k,l),(l,i)\bigr|,
        \notag
    \end{align}
    so each leg carries the pair of corner qubits on its side, listed
    clockwise around the site
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX model'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. The CZX PEPS
    places this tensor at every site with its down and left labels read in
    reverse, so that each bond identifies the pair $(a,b)$ at one end with
    the pair $(b,a)$ at the other.
\end{definition}

The reversal corrects the printed formula. The review contracts every bond
with equal labels at its two ends
\cite[Section~``MPS and PEPS'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. Contracted in this way,
the printed tensor sets the top-right qubit of a site equal to the
bottom-left qubits of its right and upper neighbours, and the resulting state
is a product of GHZ states on diagonal loops that wind around the torus, not
the plaquette state. Two neighbouring sites list the qubits of their common
side in opposite orders, and the plaquette state is obtained by identifying
the pair $(a,b)$ at one end of a bond with $(b,a)$ at the other
\cite{gap:rmp_peps_czx_bond_orientation}.

\begin{theorem}[The CZX PEPS is a product of plaquette GHZ states]%
    \label{thm:peps_czx_plaquette_product}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czx_bond_iff_plaquette, TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_czxPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_tensor, def:peps_stateCoeff}
    On a torus with $w,h\ge 3$ the CZX PEPS has coefficient
    \begin{align}
        \Coeff(\sigma)=\prod_{v}\delta\bigl[j_v=i_{v+(1,0)}=k_{v+(0,1)}
            =l_{v+(1,1)}\bigr],
        \notag
    \end{align}
    where $(i_v,j_v,k_v,l_v)$ are the four qubits of $\sigma(v)$ and the
    factor at $v$ is the coefficient of $\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}$ on the four
    qubits around the plaquette at the upper right corner of $v$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX model'']{Cirac2021Matrix},
    \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction} the top and right
    legs force the vertical bond above $v$ to carry $(i_v,j_v)$ and the
    horizontal bond to its right to carry $(j_v,k_v)$. The down and left legs
    then require $(i_{v-(0,1)},j_{v-(0,1)})=(l_v,k_v)$ and
    $(j_{v-(1,0)},k_{v-(1,0)})=(i_v,l_v)$ at every site, and these
    conditions, shifted by one site, are equivalent to the plaquette
    conditions.
\end{proof}

The on-site $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry of the CZX model is generated by
$U_{CZX}=U_XU_{CZ}$, where $U_X=X_1X_2X_3X_4$ flips the four qubits of a site
and $U_{CZ}=CZ_{12}CZ_{23}CZ_{34}CZ_{41}$ applies controlled-$Z$ gates to its
four pairs of neighbouring qubits \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}. On the state
$\ket{ijkl}$ it acts by
$U_{CZX}\ket{ijkl}=(-1)^{ij+jk+kl+li}\ket{\bar\imath\bar\jmath\bar k\bar l}$.

\begin{definition}[CZX on-site symmetry]%
    \label{def:peps_czx_on_site}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator, TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator_monomial, TNLean.PEPS.czxSiteFlip,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxTopLeft_czxSiteFlip, TNLean.PEPS.czxTopRight_czxSiteFlip,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBottomRight_czxSiteFlip,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBottomLeft_czxSiteFlip, TNLean.PEPS.czxSiteFlip_czxQubits,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxSiteFlip_czxSiteFlip, TNLean.PEPS.czxSiteFlip_symm,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxCZExponent, TNLean.PEPS.czxOnSite,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxOnSite_eq_monomial}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_tensor}
    The operator $U_{CZX}=U_XU_{CZ}$ on the sixteen states of a site, and
    its product $U_{CZX}^{\otimes V}$ over the sites $V$ of the lattice, whose
    entry at $(\sigma',\sigma)$ is $\prod_v(U_{CZX})_{\sigma'_v\sigma_v}$
    \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The CZX PEPS is invariant under the on-site symmetry]%
    \label{thm:peps_czx_invariant}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.prod_neg_one_pow_czxCZExponent,
        TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator_czxOnSite_mulVec_stateCoeff_czxPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_on_site, thm:peps_czx_plaquette_product}
    On a torus with $w,h\ge 3$ the CZX PEPS is invariant under
    $U_{CZX}^{\otimes V}$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX model'']{Cirac2021Matrix},
    \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_czx_plaquette_product}
    Flipping every qubit preserves the plaquette constraints of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_czx_plaquette_product}. On a configuration
    satisfying them, let $P_v$ be the value of the plaquette at the upper
    right corner of $v$. The sign of $U_{CZX}$ at $v$ is
    $(-1)^{e_v}$ with
    $e_v=P_{v-(1,0)}P_v+P_vP_{v-(0,1)}+P_{v-(0,1)}P_{v-(1,1)}
        +P_{v-(1,1)}P_{v-(1,0)}$. Shifting the summation variable, the sums
    over $v$ of the third and fourth terms equal those of the first and
    second, so $\sum_ve_v$ is even: each controlled-$Z$ gate between two
    neighbouring plaquettes appears twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[CZX leg operator]%
    \label{def:peps_czx_leg_operator}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czxLegFlip, TNLean.PEPS.czxLegFlip_czxBond,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxLegPhase, TNLean.PEPS.czxLegPhase_czxBond,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxLegOperator}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_tensor}
    A virtual leg carries two qubits $(a,b)$. The operator
    $V=(X\otimes X)\,CZ$ acts on a leg by
    $V\ket{ab}=(-1)^{ab}\ket{\bar a\bar b}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Pulling the symmetry through a site]%
    \label{thm:peps_czx_pull_through}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czxOnSite_mul_czxSiteTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_on_site, def:peps_czx_leg_operator}
    Applying $U_{CZX}$ to the physical index of the CZX tensor equals applying
    $V$ to each of its four virtual legs
    \cite[Section~III.B, ``Non-injective PEPS: SPT phases'']{Cirac2021Matrix}:
    writing $A^s_{trbl}$ for the entry of the CZX tensor with physical index $s$
    and top, right, bottom and left legs $t,r,b,l$,
    \begin{align}
        \sum_s(U_{CZX})_{s's}\,A^s_{trbl}
         & =\sum_{t',r',b',l'}A^{s'}_{t'r'b'l'}\,V_{t't}V_{r'r}V_{b'b}V_{l'l}.
        \label{eq:peps_czx_pull_through}
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Both sides are supported on the flipped legs of the flipped qubits, and
    the sign $(-1)^{ij+jk+kl+li}$ of $U_{CZX}$ is the product of the signs
    of $V$ on the four legs $(i,j)$, $(j,k)$, $(k,l)$, $(l,i)$.
\end{proof}

On the boundary of a region, neighbouring boundary legs carry the same qubit
of their common plaquette, one effective spin per boundary plaquette
\cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}.

\begin{definition}[Boundary legs of the CZX tensor network]%
    \label{def:peps_czx_boundary_embedding}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czxBoundaryLegs, TNLean.PEPS.czxBoundaryEmbedding}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_tensor}
    For $N>0$ and effective spins $c\in\{0,1\}^N$, the closed chain of $N$
    boundary legs carrying $c$ has the pair $(c_m,c_{m+1})$ on leg $m$, indices
    modulo $N$. The map $\iota$ from $N$ effective spins into the legs is
    \begin{align}
        \iota\ket{c} & =\ket{(c_0,c_1),(c_1,c_2),\dots,(c_{N-1},c_0)}.
        \label{eq:peps_czx_boundary_embedding}
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The boundary action is the CZX matrix product unitary]%
    \label{thm:peps_czx_boundary_mpu}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.czxBoundaryLegs_injective,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBoundaryEmbedding_conjTranspose_mul_self,
        TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator_czxLegOperator_mul_czxBoundaryEmbedding,
        TNLean.PEPS.czxBoundaryEmbedding_conjTranspose_mul_onSiteOperator_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.onSiteOperator_czxLegOperator_mul_czxBoundaryEmbedding_review}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_czx_leg_operator, def:peps_czx_boundary_embedding,
        thm:asymex_czx_kernel, def:asymex_czx_review_tensor}
    For every $N>0$, the chain of boundary legs determines $c$, so $\iota$ is an
    isometry, $\iota^\dagger\iota=\Id$. It satisfies $V^{\otimes N}\iota=\iota\,U_N$ and
    $\iota^\dagger V^{\otimes N}\iota=U_N$, where
    $U_N\ket{c}=(-1)^{\sum_mc_mc_{m+1}}\ket{\bar c}$ is the CZX matrix product
    unitary of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymex_czx_kernel}; in terms of the
    review's operator $O(A)$ with the printed matrices,
    $V^{\otimes N}\iota=(-1)^N\iota\,O(A)$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX MPU'']{Cirac2021Matrix},
    \cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}. The identification of the contraction of a
    region of the torus with a vector in the range of $\iota$ is not
    formalized \cite{gap:rmp_peps_czx_boundary_chain}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:asymex_czx_kernel, thm:asymex_czx_review_kernel}
    $V^{\otimes N}$ maps the legs of $c$ to the legs of $\bar c$ with the sign
    $\prod_m(-1)^{c_mc_{m+1}}$, which is the kernel of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:asymex_czx_kernel}; the relation to $O(A)$ is
    Theorem~\ref{thm:asymex_czx_review_kernel}.
\end{proof}

The review states that the CZX model lies in the non-trivial
symmetry-protected sector of its on-site $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry
\cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX model'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. This classification is
not formalized for the two-dimensional state. Its boundary symmetry is a
matrix product unitary; the three-cocycle attached to a group of matrix
product unitaries is constructed in
Theorems~\ref{thm:mpug_anomaly_three_cocycle}
and~\ref{thm:mpug_anomaly_three_cocycle_gauge}, and for the CZX boundary
representation its class is computed and shown to be non-trivial in
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymex_czx_decorated_anomaly_class}.

\subsection{The toric code and quantum double models}

Let $G$ be a finite group. The quantum double model of $G$
\cite{Kitaev2003FaultTolerant} carries a spin with basis
$\{\ket{g}\}_{g\in G}$ on every edge of a square lattice; for
$G=\mathbb{Z}_2$ it is the toric code. The review gives two PEPS
representations \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code and quantum double
    models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. Their symmetries are phrased through the map
$\mathcal{P}(A)=\sum A^i_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\ket{i}\bra{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$
from the virtual to the physical system
\cite[Section~``MPS and PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.

\begin{definition}[$G$-injective and $G$-isometric tensors]%
    \label{def:peps_g_injective}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective, TNLean.PEPS.IsGIsometric,
        TNLean.PEPS.siteMap, TNLean.PEPS.siteMap_apply}
    \leanok
    Let $g\mapsto U_g$ be a representation of a finite group $G$ on the
    virtual system of a tensor $A$, and let $\Pi_U$ be the projector onto the
    $U_g$-invariant subspace. The tensor is $G$-injective if
    $\mathcal{P}(A)U_g=\mathcal{P}(A)$ for every $g$ and $\mathcal{P}(A)$ is
    injective on the invariant subspace
    \cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
    It is $G$-isometric if moreover
    $\mathcal{P}(A)$ restricted to the invariant subspace is a multiple of an
    isometry, $\langle\mathcal{P}(A)x,\mathcal{P}(A)y\rangle
        =c\,\langle x,y\rangle$ for invariant $x,y$ and one constant $c>0$
    \cite[Section~``Isometric PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Left-inverse form of $G$-injectivity]%
    \label{thm:peps_g_injective_left_inverse}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.apply_averageMap_of_forall_comp_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.isGInjective_iff_exists_leftInverse}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective}
    A tensor is $G$-injective if and only if
    $\mathcal{P}(A)U_g=\mathcal{P}(A)$ for every $g$ and $\mathcal{P}(A)$ has a
    left inverse $L$ with $L\,\mathcal{P}(A)=\Pi_U$
    \cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    An invariant map satisfies $\mathcal{P}(A)\Pi_U=\mathcal{P}(A)$, since
    $\Pi_U$ is the average of the $U_g$. If $\mathcal{P}(A)$ is injective on
    the invariant subspace, choose a linear map $L$ into the invariant
    subspace with $L\,\mathcal{P}(A)x=x$ for invariant $x$; then
    $L\,\mathcal{P}(A)=L\,\mathcal{P}(A)\Pi_U=\Pi_U$. Conversely, if
    $L\,\mathcal{P}(A)=\Pi_U$ and $\mathcal{P}(A)x=0$ for an invariant $x$, then
    $x=\Pi_Ux=L\,\mathcal{P}(A)x=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$G$-injectivity for the trivial representation]%
    \label{thm:peps_g_injective_trivial}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.isGInjective_trivial_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.siteMap_injective_iff}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective}
    For the trivial representation, $G$-injectivity is injectivity of
    $\mathcal{P}(A)$, that is, linear independence of the physical vectors
    $A^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ indexed by the virtual
    configurations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Every vector is invariant under the trivial representation, and
    $\mathcal{P}(A)$ is the linear map whose columns are the vectors
    $A^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$.
\end{proof}

The group-theoretic input to $G$-injectivity is the averaging over the group
and an operator $\Delta$ that inverts the expansion of an operator in the
$U_g$'s \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
Throughout, $g\mapsto U_g$ is a representation of a finite group $G$ on a
finite-dimensional complex space.

\begin{theorem}[Group averaging]%
    \label{thm:peps_group_averaging}
    \lean{Representation.averageMap_linHom_apply,
        Representation.mem_invariants_linHom_iff,
        Representation.isProj_averageMap_linHom,
        Representation.averageMap_linHom_apply_of_adjoint,
        Representation.trace_adjoint_comp_averageMap_linHom}
    \leanok
    The map $\sigma(X)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_g U_gXU_g^{-1}$ is a projection onto
    the operators commuting with every $U_g$. If the representation is unitary,
    then $\sigma(X)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_g U_gXU_g^\dagger$ and
    $\tr[Y^\dagger\sigma(X)]=\tr[\sigma(Y)^\dagger X]$, so $\sigma$ is the
    orthogonal projector onto these operators for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner
    product \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    The map $\sigma$ is the average of the conjugation action
    $X\mapsto U_gXU_g^{-1}$, so it is a projection onto the operators fixed
    by every conjugation, and these are the operators commuting with every
    $U_g$. For a unitary representation,
    $\tr[Y^\dagger U_gXU_g^\dagger]=\tr[(U_g^\dagger YU_g)^\dagger X]$, and
    summing over $g$, with $g$ replaced by $g^{-1}$, gives the symmetry of
    $\sigma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Isotypic projectors and the operator $\Delta$]%
    \label{def:peps_delta_operator}
    \lean{Representation.charProjector,
        Representation.irreducibleCharacters,
        Representation.characterMultiplicity,
        Representation.deltaOperator}
    \leanok
    Let $\chi_U$ be the character of $U$, and let $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_r$ be the
    distinct characters of the irreducible subrepresentations of $U$, with
    $d_i=\chi_i(1)$ and multiplicities
    $m_i=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_g\chi_U(g)\chi_i(g^{-1})$. The isotypic projectors
    are $P_i=\frac{d_i}{|G|}\sum_g\chi_i(g^{-1})U_g$, and
    \begin{align*}
        \Delta=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{d_i}{m_i}P_i
    \end{align*}
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Isotypic projectors from characters]%
    \label{thm:peps_isotypic_projectors}
    \lean{Representation.sum_character_inv_smul_eq,
        Representation.exists_isInternal_isAtom,
        Representation.charProjector_apply_of_mem,
        Representation.irreducibleCharacters_finite,
        Representation.characterMultiplicity_eq_finrank,
        Representation.sum_charProjector_irreducibleCharacters,
        Representation.trace_comp_charProjector}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_delta_operator}
    For irreducible representations $D^i$ and $D^j$ of $G$,
    $\sum_g\chi_i(g^{-1})D^j(g)$ equals $\frac{|G|}{d_i}\Id$ if $D^i$
    and $D^j$ are equivalent and $0$ otherwise. Consequently $P_i$ acts as
    the identity on every irreducible subrepresentation of $U$ with
    character $\chi_i$ and as zero on every other irreducible
    subrepresentation, the number $m_i$ is the dimension of the space of
    intertwining maps from $D^i$ to $U$, and
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_{i=1}^{r}P_i & =\Id, & \tr[U_kP_i] & =m_i\chi_i(k)
    \end{align*}
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    The operator $\sum_g\chi_i(g^{-1})D^j(g)$ commutes with every $D^j(h)$,
    because characters are constant on conjugacy classes, so by Schur's lemma
    it is a multiple of the identity; its trace is
    $\sum_g\chi_i(g^{-1})\chi_j(g)$, which orthogonality of characters
    evaluates. By Maschke's theorem $U$ is a direct sum of irreducible
    subrepresentations. On each summand the projectors act as just
    described, which gives $\sum_iP_i=\Id$, and the trace of
    $U_kP_i$ is the sum of $\chi_i(k)$ over the summands with character
    $\chi_i$. Orthogonality of characters counts these summands as $m_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Block form of $\Delta$]%
    \label{thm:peps_delta_block_form}
    \lean{Representation.deltaOperator_apply_of_mem}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_delta_operator}
    On every irreducible subrepresentation of $U$ with character $\chi_i$,
    $\Delta$ acts as the scalar $\frac{d_i}{m_i|G|}$. Thus, in a basis in which
    $U_g\cong\bigoplus_iD^i(g)\otimes\Id_{m_i}$,
    \begin{align*}
        \Delta\cong\frac{1}{|G|}\bigoplus_i\frac{d_i}{m_i}\,
        \Id_{d_i}\otimes\Id_{m_i}
    \end{align*}
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_isotypic_projectors}
    Only the projector of $\chi_i$ acts nontrivially on such a
    subrepresentation, and it acts as the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Expansion in the representation]%
    \label{thm:peps_delta_expansion}
    \lean{Representation.trace_comp_deltaOperator,
        Representation.sum_trace_comp_deltaOperator_smul,
        Representation.sum_trace_inv_comp_deltaOperator_comp_smul}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_delta_operator}
    For every $g\in G$,
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_h\tr[U_{h^{-1}}\Delta U_g]\,U_h
        =\sum_h\tr[U_{gh^{-1}}\Delta]\,U_h=U_g .
    \end{align*}
    For a unitary representation $U_{h^{-1}}=U_h^\dagger$
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_isotypic_projectors}
    By the trace formula for the projectors,
    $\tr[U_k\Delta]=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_i d_i\chi_i(k)$, so
    $\sum_k\tr[U_{k^{-1}}\Delta]U_k=\sum_iP_i=\Id$. Substituting
    $h=kg$ gives
    $\sum_h\tr[U_{gh^{-1}}\Delta]U_h=\big(\sum_k\tr[U_{k^{-1}}\Delta]U_k\big)U_g
        =U_g$, and the first expression equals the second by cyclicity of
    the trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Semi-regular representation]%
    \label{def:peps_semiregular}
    \lean{Representation.IsSemiRegular,
        Representation.isSemiRegular_leftRegular}
    \leanok
    A representation of $G$ is semi-regular if it contains every irreducible
    representation of $G$, that is, if every irreducible representation
    admits a nonzero intertwining map into it
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. The
    left-regular representation is semi-regular.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Linear independence of a semi-regular representation]%
    \label{thm:peps_semiregular_trace}
    \lean{Representation.character_leftRegular,
        Representation.deltaOperator_leftRegular,
        Representation.sum_irreducibleCharacters_mul_of_isSemiRegular,
        Representation.trace_inv_comp_comp_deltaOperator_of_isSemiRegular}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_delta_operator, def:peps_semiregular}
    If $U$ is semi-regular, then $\tr[U_{g^{-1}}U_h\Delta]=\delta_{g,h}$ for
    all $g,h\in G$, with $U_{g^{-1}}=U_g^\dagger$ for a unitary
    representation. For the left-regular representation
    $\Delta=\frac{1}{|G|}\Id$
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_delta_expansion, thm:peps_isotypic_projectors}
    The character of the left-regular representation is $|G|\delta_{k,1}$,
    so every character $\chi_i$ has multiplicity $d_i$ in it, and
    $\Delta=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_iP_i=\frac{1}{|G|}\Id$. Every
    irreducible representation occurs in the left-regular representation,
    and, since $U$ is semi-regular, in $U$; hence $U$ and the left-regular
    representation have the same irreducible characters. Comparing the two
    expressions for $\tr[U_k\Delta]$ in the left-regular representation gives
    $\sum_id_i\chi_i(k)=|G|\delta_{k,1}$, and therefore
    $\tr[U_{g^{-1}}U_h\Delta]=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_id_i\chi_i(g^{-1}h)
        =\delta_{g,h}$.
\end{proof}

A matrix product tensor is a family of operators $A^i$ on a bond space
carrying the representation $U$. Its virtual system is the space of
operators $X$ on the bond space, on which $G$ acts by conjugation
$X\mapsto U_gXU_g^{-1}$, and identifying the virtual vector
$\sum x_{\alpha\beta}\ket{\alpha,\beta}$ with the operator
$X=\sum x_{\alpha\beta}\ket{\beta}\bra{\alpha}$ gives
$\mathcal{P}(A)X=\sum_i\tr[A^iX]\ket{i}$
\cite[Section~``MPS and PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.

\begin{definition}[$G$-injective matrix product tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_mps_g_injective}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.mpsSiteMap, TNLean.PEPS.mpsSiteMap_apply}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective}
    A matrix product tensor $A$ is $G$-injective if the map
    $\mathcal{P}(A)X=\sum_i\tr[A^iX]\ket{i}$ is $G$-injective for the
    conjugation action of $U$ on the operators on the bond space.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[$G$-injective matrix product tensors]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_source_form}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.mpsSiteMap_comp_linHom,
        TNLean.PEPS.comp_smulRight_dual,
        TNLean.PEPS.eq_of_forall_trace_mul_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.mpsSiteMap_comp_linHom_eq_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.isGInjective_mpsSiteMap_iff}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_mps_g_injective}
    A matrix product tensor $A$ is $G$-injective if and only if
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $U_gA^iU_g^{-1}=A^i$ for all $i$ and $g$, and
        \item $\mathcal{P}(A)$ has a left inverse on the commutant
              $\mathcal{S}=\{X\mid [X,U_g]=0\}$: there is a linear map $L$
              with $L\,\mathcal{P}(A)X=X$ for every $X\in\mathcal{S}$.
    \end{enumerate}
    For a unitary representation $U_g^{-1}=U_g^\dagger$, and this is the
    source's definition
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_group_averaging}
    Since $\tr[A^iU_gXU_g^{-1}]=\tr[U_g^{-1}A^iU_gX]$, invariance of
    $\mathcal{P}(A)$ under conjugation by $U_g$ says that $U_g^{-1}A^iU_g$
    and $A^i$ have the same trace pairing with every operator $X$; pairing
    with the rank-one operators $\ket{v}\bra{f}$ shows that they are equal.
    The operators fixed by conjugation are the commutant, and a linear map
    is injective on a subspace if and only if it has a left inverse there.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stability under concatenation]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_concatenation}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.concatTensor, TNLean.PEPS.concatTensor_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.concatLeftInverse,
        TNLean.PEPS.apply_mpsSiteMap,
        TNLean.PEPS.averageMap_linHom_apply_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.sum_leftInverse_mul_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.concatLeftInverse_comp_mpsSiteMap,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.mpsSiteMap_concatTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_mps_g_injective, def:peps_delta_operator}
    Let $A$ and $B$ be $G$-injective matrix product tensors for the same
    representation, with left inverses $L_A$ and $L_B$ satisfying
    $L_A\mathcal{P}(A)=L_B\mathcal{P}(B)=\sigma$. Then $C^{ij}=A^iB^j$ is
    $G$-injective, and the map
    \begin{align*}
        L_C\ket{i,j}=|G|\,\big(L_B\ket{j}\big)\,\Delta\,\big(L_A\ket{i}\big)
    \end{align*}
    satisfies $L_C\mathcal{P}(C)=\sigma$
    \cite[Section~``MPS: the $G$-injective case'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_source_form,
        thm:peps_g_injective_left_inverse, thm:peps_delta_expansion}
    Invariance of $C$ follows from
    $U_gA^iB^jU_g^{-1}=(U_gA^iU_g^{-1})(U_gB^jU_g^{-1})$. For the left
    inverse, first $\sum_i\tr[A^iY]\,L_A\ket{i}=\sigma(Y)$ for every $Y$.
    Second, for every operator $M$,
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_j\big(L_B\ket{j}\big)\,M\,B^j
        =\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_h\tr[U_{h^{-1}}M]\,U_h ;
    \end{align*}
    writing $M$ in a basis $\{b_k\}$ with coordinate functionals $b^*_k$,
    the left-hand side applied to $w$ is
    $\sum_k\sigma\big(\ket{w}\bra{b^*_k}\big)Mb_k$, which is the right-hand
    side applied to $w$. Taking $Y=B^jX$ in the first identity and
    $M=\Delta U_g$ in the second,
    \begin{align*}
        L_C\mathcal{P}(C)X
        =|G|\sum_j\big(L_B\ket{j}\big)\,\Delta\,\sigma(B^jX)
        =\sum_g\Big(\sum_j\big(L_B\ket{j}\big)\,\Delta U_g\,B^j\Big)XU_g^{-1}
        =\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_gU_gXU_g^{-1},
    \end{align*}
    the last step by the expansion
    $\sum_h\tr[U_{h^{-1}}\Delta U_g]U_h=U_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Blocking a $G$-injective tensor]%
    \label{cor:peps_mps_g_injective_blocking}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.mpsSiteMap_reindex,
        TNLean.PEPS.wordTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.mpsSiteMap_wordTensor,
        MPSTensor.toLin'_evalWord,
        MPSTensor.isGInjective_mpsSiteMap_blockTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_mps_g_injective, def:blocked_tensor}
    If $A$ is $G$-injective, then the tensor obtained by blocking $L\ge1$
    sites, $(i_1,\dots,i_L)\mapsto A^{i_1}\cdots A^{i_L}$, is $G$-injective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_concatenation}
    Induction on $L$: the blocked tensor on $L+1$ sites is the concatenation
    of $A$ with the blocked tensor on $L$ sites, up to relabelling the
    physical index, and relabelling preserves $G$-injectivity.
\end{proof}

For $G$-injective tensors the parent-Hamiltonian argument runs as in the
injective case, with the left inverses replaced by left inverses up to the
twirl $\sigma$
\cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. In the next two
theorems $A$ and $B$ are $G$-injective for the same representation $U$; three-leg
tensors $M$, $N$ carry one physical and two virtual legs, and the operators $X$,
$M$, $N$, $U_g$ sit on a bond.

\begin{lemma}[Twirling the closing operator]%
    \label{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_twirl}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.mpsSiteMap_averageMap,
        TNLean.PEPS.averageMap_mul_of_conj_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.mul_comm_of_conj_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.concatTensor_conj_eq}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_mps_g_injective}
    If $U_gC^iU_g^{-1}=C^i$ for all $i$ and $g$, then
    $\tr[C^i\sigma(X)]=\tr[C^iX]$ for every operator $X$: an operator closing
    an invariant tensor may be replaced by its twirl without changing the
    state. Moreover $\sigma(YZ)=\sigma(Y)Z$ whenever $Z$ commutes with every
    $U_g$, and the concatenation of two invariant tensors is invariant
    \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_source_form, thm:peps_group_averaging}
    Invariance of $C$ is invariance of the map $X\mapsto(\tr[C^iX])_i$ under
    conjugation, and an invariant map does not see the average over the
    group. For the second claim, $U_gYZU_g^{-1}=(U_gYU_g^{-1})Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The concatenated left inverse on traces]%
    \label{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_left_inverse_traces}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.concatLeftInverse_apply_trace_mul}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_concatenation}
    Let $L_A$ be any linear map and let $L_B\mathcal{P}(B)=\sigma$. The map
    $L_C\ket{j,k}=|G|\,(L_A\ket{k})\,\Delta\,(L_B\ket{j})$ of the concatenation
    $C^{jk}=B^jA^k$ sends the vector $(\tr[B^jY_k])_{j,k}$ to
    $|G|\sum_k(L_A\ket{k})\,\Delta\,\sigma(Y_k)$, for every family of
    operators $Y_k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Summing over $j$ first, $\sum_j\tr[B^jY_k]\,L_B\ket{j}
        =L_B\mathcal{P}(B)Y_k=\sigma(Y_k)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Intersection property for $G$-injective tensors]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_intersection}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.intersection_property}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_source_form,
        thm:peps_mps_g_injective_concatenation}
    \begin{align*}
        \Big\{\sum_{ijk}\tr[A^iB^jM^k]\ket{ijk}\ \Big|\ M\Big\}\cap
        \Big\{\sum_{ijk}\tr[N^iB^jA^k]\ket{ijk}\ \Big|\ N\Big\}
        =\Big\{\sum_{ijk}\tr[A^iB^jA^kX]\ket{ijk}\ \Big|\ X\Big\}
    \end{align*}
    \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_twirl,
        lem:peps_mps_g_injective_left_inverse_traces}
    The right-hand side lies in both sets, with $M^k=A^kX$ and $N^i=XA^i$.
    Conversely, let $\tr[A^iB^jM^k]=\tr[N^iB^jA^k]$ for all $i,j,k$. Fix
    left inverses with $L_A\mathcal{P}(A)=L_B\mathcal{P}(B)=\sigma$, and let
    $L_C$ be the left inverse of $C^{jk}=B^jA^k$, so that
    $L_C\mathcal{P}(C)=\sigma$. For fixed $i$ the vector
    $(\tr[B^jA^kN^i])_{j,k}$ equals $(\tr[B^jM^kA^i])_{j,k}$, and applying
    $L_C$ gives
    \begin{align*}
        \sigma(N^i)=|G|\sum_k(L_A\ket{k})\,\Delta\,\sigma(M^kA^i)=XA^i,
        \qquad X=|G|\sum_k(L_A\ket{k})\,\Delta\,\sigma(M^k),
    \end{align*}
    because $A^i$ commutes with every $U_g$. Since $C$ is invariant,
    $\tr[N^iB^jA^k]=\tr[B^jA^k\sigma(N^i)]=\tr[A^iB^jA^kX]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closure property for $G$-injective tensors]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_closure}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.eq_sum_of_closure,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.closure_property}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_source_form,
        thm:peps_mps_g_injective_concatenation, def:peps_delta_operator}
    \begin{align*}
        \Big\{\sum_{ij}\tr[B^iA^jM]\ket{ij}\ \Big|\ M\Big\}\cap
        \Big\{\sum_{ij}\tr[B^iNA^j]\ket{ij}\ \Big|\ N\Big\}
        =\Big\{\sum_g\lambda_g\sum_{ij}\tr[B^iA^jU_g]\ket{ij}\ \Big|\
        \lambda_g\in\C\Big\}.
    \end{align*}
    A vector in the intersection, given by $M$ and $N$, has
    $\lambda_g=\tr[U_{g^{-1}}\sigma(N)\Delta]$
    \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_twirl,
        lem:peps_mps_g_injective_left_inverse_traces}
    The right-hand side lies in both sets, with $M=N=\sum_g\lambda_gU_g$,
    since $U_g$ commutes with $A^j$. Conversely, let
    $\tr[B^iA^jM]=\tr[B^iNA^j]$ for all $i,j$, and let $L_C$ be the left
    inverse of $C^{ij}=B^iA^j$. Applying $L_C$ to the vector
    $(\tr[B^iNA^j])_{i,j}$, and then the identity
    $\sum_j(L_A\ket{j})\,Y\,A^j=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_h\tr[U_{h^{-1}}Y]\,U_h$ with
    $Y=\Delta\sigma(N)$,
    \begin{align*}
        \sigma(M)=|G|\sum_j(L_A\ket{j})\,\Delta\,\sigma(N)\,A^j
        =\sum_h\tr[U_{h^{-1}}\Delta\sigma(N)]\,U_h .
    \end{align*}
    Since $C$ is invariant, $\tr[B^iA^jM]=\tr[B^iA^j\sigma(M)]$, and
    $\tr[U_{h^{-1}}\Delta\sigma(N)]=\tr[U_{h^{-1}}\sigma(N)\Delta]$ because
    $\sigma(N)$ commutes with $U_{h^{-1}}$.
\end{proof}

The two properties give the parent Hamiltonian of a $G$-injective tensor, as
the intersection and closure properties of injective tensors give Theorem 3.5
of the source \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. For a
tensor $A$ and a ring of $N$ sites, write $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$ for the space
of Definition~\ref{def:chain_ground_space}, the common kernel of the parent
interactions on all windows of $L$ consecutive sites of the ring, and write
$\ket{\mathcal M(A|K)}=\sum_{\sigma}\tr[A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_N}K]
    \ket{\sigma}$ for the matrix product state with closure $K$. The source's
two-site interaction is $L=2$. In the rest of this subsection $A$ is
$G$-injective for the representation $U$.

\begin{lemma}[The open chain of a $G$-injective tensor]%
    \label{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_open_chain}
    \lean{MPSTensor.mem_groundSpace_add_two_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.chainGroundSpace_le_groundSpace_of_isGInjective}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_intersection,
        cor:peps_mps_g_injective_blocking, def:chain_ground_space}
    Let $K\ge1$. A vector on $K+2$ sites whose restrictions to the first and to
    the last $K+1$ sites lie in
    $\{\sum_{\sigma}\tr[A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_{K+1}}X]\ket{\sigma}\mid X\}$
    lies in the corresponding space on $K+2$ sites. Consequently, if
    $2\le L\le N$, every vector of $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$ has the form
    $\sum_{\sigma}\tr[A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_N}X]\ket{\sigma}$
    \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Fixing the last letter $k$ writes the vector as $\tr[A^iB^wM^k]$ and fixing
    the first letter $i$ as $\tr[N^iB^wA^k]$, where $B$ is the tensor of $K$
    blocked sites, which is $G$-injective by
    Corollary~\ref{cor:peps_mps_g_injective_blocking}. The intersection
    property gives the form $\tr[A^iB^wA^kX]$. Iterating along the
    non-wrapping windows of the ring gives the second claim, as in the proof of
    Theorem 3.5 of the source.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Parent Hamiltonians of $G$-injective tensors]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_parent_hamiltonian}
    \lean{MPSTensor.groundSpaceMap_mem_span_closure_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.groundSpaceMap_rep_mem_chainGroundSpace,
        MPSTensor.chainGroundSpace_eq_span_closure_of_isGInjective}
    \leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_open_chain,
        thm:peps_mps_g_injective_closure}
    If $2\le L\le N$, then
    \begin{align*}
        \mathcal G_{N,L}(A)=\spn_{\C}\bigl\{\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}\ \big|\
        g\in G\bigr\}.
    \end{align*}
    In particular, for the two-site interaction on a ring of $N\ge2$ sites
    the frustration-free ground space of the parent Hamiltonian is spanned by
    the states $\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}$
    \cite[Theorem~4.11]{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Each $U_g$ commutes with every $A^i$, so it can be moved to any bond, and
    every window of $\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}$ lies in the local ground space.
    Conversely, by Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_mps_g_injective_open_chain} a vector
    of $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$ is $\tr[A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_N}X]$, and
    its translate by one site is $\tr[A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_N}Y]$. Hence
    $\tr[A^aB^wX]=\tr[A^aYB^w]$, where $B$ is the tensor of the last $N-1$
    blocked sites. The closure property, applied to $A$ and $B$, writes the
    vector as a combination of the states $\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Twirled representation operators]%
    \label{lem:peps_twirled_representation}
    \lean{Representation.rep_mul_charProjector,
        Representation.apply_conj_of_mem_irreducibleCharacters,
        Representation.sum_rep_conj_apply_of_mem,
        Representation.averageMap_linHom_rep,
        Representation.linearIndependent_charProjector,
        Representation.linearIndependent_of_isSemiRegular,
        Representation.linearIndependent_averageMap_conjClasses}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_isotypic_projectors, thm:peps_group_averaging,
        thm:peps_semiregular_trace}
    The isotypic projectors $P_i$ commute with every $U_g$, are linearly
    independent, and
    \begin{align*}
        \sigma(U_g)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_hU_{hgh^{-1}}
        =\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\chi_i(g)}{\chi_i(1)}\,P_i .
    \end{align*}
    If $U$ is semi-regular, then the operators $U_g$ are linearly independent,
    and so are the twirls $\sigma(U_g)$ of one representative $g$ of each
    conjugacy class \cite[Section~``Parent Hamiltonians'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    On an irreducible subrepresentation with character $\chi_i$ the operator
    $\sum_hU_{hgh^{-1}}$ commutes with the representation, so by Schur's lemma
    it is a scalar, and its trace $|G|\chi_i(g)$ fixes the scalar as
    $|G|\chi_i(g)/\chi_i(1)$; the projector $P_i$ acts there as the identity
    and every other projector as zero. Evaluating $\sum_i\mu_iP_i$ on a nonzero
    vector of such a subrepresentation gives $\mu_i$ times the vector. For
    semi-regular $U$, the functional $X\mapsto\tr[U_{g^{-1}}X\Delta]$ takes the
    value $\delta_{g,h}$ on $U_h$ by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_semiregular_trace}; on $\sigma(U_k)$ it is
    $\frac{1}{|G|}$ times the number of $h$ with $hkh^{-1}=g$, which vanishes
    unless $k$ is conjugate to $g$ and is positive for $k=g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structure of the ground space of a $G$-injective tensor]%
    \label{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_ground_space_structure}
    \lean{MPSTensor.groundSpaceMap_toMatrix'_averageMap,
        MPSTensor.chainGroundSpace_eq_span_charProjector_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.linearIndependent_groundSpaceMap_charProjector_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.finrank_chainGroundSpace_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.chainGroundSpace_eq_span_conjClasses_of_isGInjective,
        MPSTensor.linearIndependent_groundSpaceMap_conjClasses_of_isSemiRegular,
        MPSTensor.finrank_chainGroundSpace_of_isSemiRegular,
        MPSTensor.card_irreducibleCharacterFinset_of_isSemiRegular}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_parent_hamiltonian,
        lem:peps_twirled_representation, def:peps_semiregular}
    Let $2\le L\le N$, and let $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_I$ be the distinct
    characters of the irreducible representations occurring in $U$, with
    isotypic projectors $P_i$. The states $\ket{\mathcal M(A|P_i)}$ are
    linearly independent and span $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$, so this space has
    dimension $I$. If $U$ is semi-regular, then $I$ is the number of conjugacy
    classes of $G$, and for any choice of one representative $g$ of each
    conjugacy class the states $\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}$ are linearly
    independent and span $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$
    \cite[Theorem~4.12]{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Since $A$ is invariant, $\ket{\mathcal M(A|K)}=\ket{\mathcal M(A|\sigma(K))}$
    for every closure $K$; in particular the closures of one conjugacy class
    give the same state. By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_twirled_representation} each
    $\ket{\mathcal M(A|U_g)}$ is a combination of the states
    $\ket{\mathcal M(A|P_i)}$, and conversely each $P_i$ is a combination of
    the $U_g$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_mps_g_injective_parent_hamiltonian}
    gives the spanning claims. The tensor of $N$ blocked sites is
    $G$-injective, so $K\mapsto\ket{\mathcal M(A|K)}$ is injective on the
    operators commuting with every $U_g$. These contain the projectors $P_i$
    and the twirls $\sigma(U_g)$, and
    Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_twirled_representation} gives the linear independence
    of both families. Computing the dimension of $\mathcal G_{2,2}(A)$ in the
    two ways gives $I$ equal to the number of conjugacy classes. The source
    instead quotes that the numbers of conjugacy classes and of irreducible
    representations agree, and passes from the $U_g$ to the $P_i$ through the
    completeness of the characters in the class functions.
\end{proof}

The source asks for a tensor that is $G$-isometric with respect to the
left-regular representation. In the definition above the representation is a
parameter; the dual quantum-double tensor below is $G$-isometric for its
color-shift representation, and the constant $c$ absorbs the
normalization of the tensor \cite{gap:rmp_peps_quantum_double_g_isometry}.

The invariance of a $G$-injective tensor lets a string of $U_g$'s on the
virtual bonds be deformed across the sites of a network
\cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. To
state this on the torus, orient the horizontal edges to the right and the
vertical edges downwards, as in the source, and let the bonds carry
operators.

\begin{definition}[Torus network with operators on the bonds]%
    \label{def:peps_torus_bond_network}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork, TNLean.PEPS.torusLegKernel,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusLegKernel_apply, TNLean.PEPS.torusLegKernel_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusLegMatrix, TNLean.PEPS.torusLegRep,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusLegRep_apply, TNLean.PEPS.torusDress}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    Let every site $v$ of the torus carry a tensor $A_v$ with four virtual
    indices, ordered top, right, down, left, and let every bond carry a
    $D\times D$ matrix $O$: $O^{\mathrm h}_v$ on the horizontal bond from $v$
    to $v+(1,0)$ and $O^{\mathrm v}_v$ on the vertical bond from $v+(0,1)$ to
    $v$. Split every bond into its two ends, and give a labelling of the ends
    the weight $O_{ij}$ on each bond whose head reads $i$ and whose tail
    reads $j$. The network is the sum over all labellings of the ends of the
    product of these weights and of the entries of the site tensors, each
    reading the ends of its four legs. For a representation $g\mapsto U_g$ on
    one bond, $U_g$ acts on the virtual system of a site by $U_g$ on the
    incoming legs (top and left) and $U_g^{-1}$ on the outgoing legs (right
    and down), each along the orientation of its edge
    \cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Diagonal operators on the bonds]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_bond_network_diagonal}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork_diagonal,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_torusSiteTensor_eq_torusBondNetwork}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_bond_network, lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    If every bond carries a diagonal matrix, the network is the sum over one
    index per bond of the product of the diagonal entries and of the site
    tensors. In particular, with the identity on every bond it is the
    contraction of the site tensors, and for $w,h\ge3$ and $A_v=a^{\sigma(v)}$
    it is the coefficient of the torus PEPS of $a$ at $\sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    The weight of a labelling vanishes unless the two ends of every bond
    carry the same index, and the labellings with equal ends correspond to
    the assignments of one index per bond.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moving bond operators into the site tensors]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_bond_network_gauge}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork_gauge}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_bond_network}
    Let $P_v$ and $Q_v$ be matrices at every site. Replacing the matrix $O$
    of every bond by $P_{\text{head}}\,O\,Q_{\text{tail}}$ gives the same
    network as keeping $O$ and replacing every site tensor $A_v$ by the
    tensor that has absorbed $P_v$ into its incoming legs and $Q_v$ into its
    outgoing legs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Expand each product $P\,O\,Q$ into its entries. The resulting sum runs
    over the labellings of the bond ends and over new intermediate indices;
    exchanging the roles of the two families, the intermediate indices
    become the labelling of the bond ends and the old labels become the
    summation indices of the absorbed matrices at the sites. The factors
    $P_{v+(1,0)}$ and $Q_{v+(0,1)}$ are reindexed by a unit shift of the
    torus.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Deformation of a string of $U_g$'s]%
    \label{thm:peps_torus_operator_string_deformation}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.vecMul_torusLegMatrix_of_comp_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusWestSouthOperatorString,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusNorthEastOperatorString,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork_westSouthOperatorString_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsGInjective.torusBondNetwork_westSouthOperatorString_eq}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective, lem:peps_torus_bond_network_gauge}
    Let $a$ be a four-leg tensor with $\mathcal{P}(a)U_g=\mathcal{P}(a)$ for
    every $g$, the invariance clause of $G$-injectivity for the action of
    Definition~\ref{def:peps_torus_bond_network}. Fix a rectangle of $m<w$
    columns and $n<h$ rows of sites. A string of $U_g$'s carries $U_g$ on
    the vertical bonds, which it crosses going right, and $U_g^{-1}$ on the
    horizontal bonds, which it crosses going down. In the torus network of
    $a$, the string along the left and bottom sides of the rectangle and the
    string along its top and right sides give the same network
    \cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'', eq.~(2d:move-strings)
        and Lemma~``Deformations of the string'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. Only the
    invariance is used: the source assumes that $U_g$ contains every
    irreducible representation, and its argument, like this one, does not
    use it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Let $\Phi_v=g^{-1}$ on the sites of the rectangle and $\Phi_v=1$
    elsewhere, and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_bond_network_gauge} with
    $P_v=U_{\Phi_v}$ and $Q_v=U_{\Phi_v}^{-1}$ to the second string. A bond
    with both ends inside or both outside the rectangle carries the identity
    in both strings and keeps it. A
    bond entering the rectangle across its top side is multiplied by
    $U_g^{-1}$ at its head, which cancels the $U_g$ of the second string; a
    bond leaving it across its right side is multiplied by $U_g$ at its
    tail, which cancels its $U_g^{-1}$. A bond entering across the left side
    acquires $U_g^{-1}$ and a bond leaving across the bottom side acquires
    $U_g$, which is the first string. By the invariance every site tensor
    absorbs $U_{\Phi_v}$ on its incoming and $U_{\Phi_v}^{-1}$ on its
    outgoing legs unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Dual quantum-double tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_quantum_double_dual}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.colorDiag, TNLean.PEPS.colorDiag_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.colorDiag_mul, TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualSpins,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualTensor, TNLean.PEPS.colorShiftRep,
        TNLean.PEPS.colorShiftRep_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.mem_invariants_colorShiftRep_iff}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective}
    The virtual legs carry colors $h_1$ (left), $h_2$ (top), $h_3$ (right)
    and $h_4$ (down) in $G$, and the dual tensor is $1$ at the physical
    configuration
    $(h_2h_1^{-1},\,h_3h_2^{-1},\,h_3h_4^{-1},\,h_4h_1^{-1})$ of differences
    of adjacent colors and $0$ elsewhere \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code
        and quantum double models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. The group acts on the
    virtual system by shifting all four colors,
    $(U_gx)(h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4)=x(h_1g,h_2g,h_3g,h_4g)$, the right-regular
    representation on each leg, which the relabeling $h\mapsto h^{-1}$
    turns into the left-regular one
    \cite{gap:rmp_peps_quantum_double_g_isometry}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The dual tensor is $G$-isometric]%
    \label{thm:peps_quantum_double_dual_g_isometric}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualSpins_mul_colorDiag,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualSpins_eq_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.siteMap_quantumDoubleDualTensor_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.siteMap_quantumDoubleDualTensor_apply_spins,
        TNLean.PEPS.siteMap_quantumDoubleDualTensor_apply_spins_of_mem_invariants,
        TNLean.PEPS.isGInjective_quantumDoubleDualTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.isGIsometric_quantumDoubleDualTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.ToricCodeGroup,
        TNLean.PEPS.isGIsometric_toricCodeDualTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_g_injective, def:peps_quantum_double_dual}
    For every finite group $G$ the dual quantum-double tensor is
    $G$-injective and $G$-isometric, with constant $c=|G|$, for the
    color-shift representation
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code and quantum double
        models'']{Cirac2021Matrix},
    \cite[Section~``Examples'', ``The double models'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
    In particular the toric code tensor, $G=\mathbb{Z}_2$, is
    $\mathbb{Z}_2$-isometric.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    Two colorings have the same differences exactly when they differ by one
    global shift $h_i\mapsto h_ig$: the shift of the left color determines
    $g$, and the other three differences then fix the remaining colors.
    Hence $(\mathcal{P}x)(\text{differences of }h)=\sum_{g}x(hg)$. Invariance
    follows by reindexing the sum over colorings by a shift. For an
    invariant $x$ the sum equals $|G|\,x(h)$, so $\mathcal{P}x=0$ forces
    $x=0$, and
    $\langle\mathcal{P}x,\mathcal{P}y\rangle
        =\sum_h\overline{(\mathcal{P}x)(\text{differences of }h)}\,y(h)
        =|G|\langle x,y\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strings of $R_g$'s on the dual quantum-double network]%
    \label{cor:peps_quantum_double_dual_string_deformation}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.rightRegularMatrix,
        TNLean.PEPS.colorShiftRep_eq_torusLegRep,
        TNLean.PEPS.torusBondNetwork_quantumDoubleDual_westSouthOperatorString_eq}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_quantum_double_dual_g_isometric,
        thm:peps_torus_operator_string_deformation}
    Let $R_g$ be the permutation matrix of $h\mapsto hg$. The color shift of
    the dual tensor is the action of $R_g$ on the incoming and $R_g^{-1}$ on
    the outgoing legs. Hence, in the torus network of the dual tensor with
    colors in $G$ on the bonds, a string of $R_g$'s along the left and bottom
    sides of a rectangle of $m<w$ columns and $n<h$ rows of sites gives the
    same network as the string along its top and right sides
    \cite[Section~``Two dimensions: $G$-injective PEPS'',
        eq.~(2d:move-strings)]{Schuch2010PEPS}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    On the incoming legs $R_g$ sends $x$ to $h\mapsto x(hg)$, and on the
    outgoing legs $R_g^{-1}$ acting along the edge does the same, so the
    action on the four legs is the color shift. The dual tensor is invariant
    under it by Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_quantum_double_dual_g_isometric}, and
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_torus_operator_string_deformation} applies.
\end{proof}

Placed at every site of the torus, the dual tensor gives a network whose
bonds carry colors in $G$ and whose physical spins are the differences of the
colors of adjacent bonds; its state is the superposition over all color
patterns \cite[Section~``Examples'', ``The double models'']{Schuch2010PEPS}.
The review identifies it with the equal-weight superposition of the
configurations obeying Gauss' law \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code and
    quantum double models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. On the torus not every such
configuration is a difference of colorings: the transports of the spins
around the two non-contractible cycles must be trivial.

\begin{lemma}[Parallel sections of transports on the torus]%
    \label{lem:peps_torus_parallel_section}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.zmodTransport, TNLean.PEPS.zmodTransport_zero,
        TNLean.PEPS.zmodTransport_succ,
        TNLean.PEPS.apply_natCast_eq_zmodTransport_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.zmodTransport_self_eq_one_of_apply_add_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.zmodTransport_val_add_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection, TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusFlat,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection.mul_right,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection.isTorusFlat,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection.zmodTransport_row,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection.zmodTransport_column,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusFlat.zmodTransport_column_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusParallelSection.eq_mul_right,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusFlat.zmodTransport_column_eq_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.exists_isTorusParallelSection_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.card_isTorusParallelSection}
    \leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_shift_const}
    Let $G$ be a finite group and assign to every site $v$ of the torus two
    elements $a_v,b_v\in G$. Call $f$ from the sites to $G$ a parallel section
    if $f_{v+(1,0)}=a_vf_v$ and $f_{v+(0,1)}=b_vf_v$ for every $v$. Parallel
    sections exist if and only if
    $b_{v+(1,0)}a_v=a_{v+(0,1)}b_v$ for every $v$ and the ordered products
    $a_{(w-1,0)}\cdots a_{(0,0)}$ and $b_{(0,h-1)}\cdots b_{(0,0)}$ are the
    identity. If they exist, there are exactly $|G|$ of them, and any two
    differ by right multiplication with one group element.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    If $f$ and $f'$ are parallel sections, $f_v^{-1}f'_v$ is invariant under
    both unit shifts and hence constant, so $f'=fg$; conversely $fg$ is a
    parallel section for every $g$. The two paths from $v$ to $v+(1,1)$ give
    the flatness condition, and going once around a row or a column gives
    the trivial products. Conversely, for flat $a,b$ with trivial products
    set $f_{(x,y)}=b_{(x,y-1)}\cdots b_{(x,0)}\,a_{(x-1,0)}\cdots a_{(0,0)}$
    for the representatives $0\le x<w$, $0\le y<h$. Flatness moves the
    product up a column one column to the right, conjugated by the
    horizontal transports at its ends, so the product around every column is
    trivial; this and the trivial product around the bottom row give the
    two transport rules also where the representatives wrap around.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The dual quantum-double network is the Gauss-law superposition]%
    \label{thm:peps_quantum_double_dual_gauss_law}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualPEPS,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualRowTransport,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoubleDualColumnTransport,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsQuantumDoubleGaussLaw,
        TNLean.PEPS.IsQuantumDoubleTrivialHolonomy,
        TNLean.PEPS.isTorusFlat_quantumDoubleDualTransport_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.forall_eq_quantumDoubleDualSpins_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_quantumDoubleDualPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_quantum_double_dual, lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction,
        lem:peps_torus_parallel_section}
    Let $w,h\ge3$ and place the dual quantum-double tensor at every site of
    the torus. Write $(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4)$ for the top left, top right, bottom
    right and bottom left spins of a site. A spin configuration obeys Gauss'
    law if at every site $s_2s_1=s_3s_4$, and around the corner shared by
    the sites $v$, $v+(1,0)$, $v+(1,1)$, $v+(0,1)$ the spins satisfy
    $s_1(v+(1,0))\,s_2(v)=s_4(v+(1,1))\,s_3(v+(0,1))$, the local and the
    plaquette terms of \cite[Section~``Examples'', ``The double
        models'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. It has trivial holonomy if the ordered
    products of $a_v=s_2(v)s_1(v)$ along the row $y=0$ and of
    $b_v=s_4(v+(0,1))^{-1}s_1(v)$ along the column $x=0$ are the identity.
    Then the coefficient of the contracted network is $|G|$ at every
    configuration obeying Gauss' law with trivial holonomy, and $0$ at every
    other configuration. So the state is the equal-weight superposition of
    the Gauss-law configurations of trivial holonomy; those of nontrivial
    holonomy do not occur on the torus
    \cite{gap:rmp_peps_quantum_double_g_isometry}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction} the coefficient
    counts the colorings of the horizontal and vertical bonds whose
    differences are the given spins. Write such a coloring through the left
    color $f_v$ and the top color $u_v$ of each site. The top left spin gives
    $u_v=s_1(v)f_v$, the top right spin gives $f_{v+(1,0)}=a_vf_v$, the
    bottom left spin of the site above gives $f_{v+(0,1)}=b_vf_v$, and the
    bottom right spin is then equivalent to $s_2s_1=s_3s_4$ at $v$. So the
    colorings are counted by the parallel sections of $a,b$ when the local
    rule holds, and there are none otherwise. Using the local rule,
    flatness of $a,b$ is the plaquette rule, and
    Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_parallel_section} counts the sections.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Primal quantum-double tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_quantum_double_primal}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalLabels,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalTensor}
    \leanok
    A tensor carries the four edge spins $g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4$ of a plaquette (top
    left, top right, bottom right, bottom left), and is $1$ when its virtual
    labels are $g_1g_2$ (top), $g_2^{-1}g_3$ (right), $g_4g_3$ (down) and
    $g_1g_4^{-1}$ (left), and $0$ otherwise \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code
        and quantum double models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The primal virtual labels fuse to the identity]%
    \label{thm:peps_quantum_double_primal_fusion}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalTensor_ne_zero_mul_eq_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalTensor_ne_zero_map_mul_eq_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalTensor_smul_map_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.character_mul_quantumDoublePrimalTensor}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_quantum_double_primal}
    On the support of the primal tensor the virtual labels satisfy
    $t\,r\,b^{-1}l^{-1}=1$. Hence $\pi(t)\pi(r)\pi(b)^{-1}\pi(l)^{-1}=1$ for
    every representation $\pi$ of $G$, in particular every irreducible one,
    so that each entry of the tensor times the operator
    $\pi(t)\pi(r)\pi(b)^{-1}\pi(l)^{-1}$, acting by $\pi$ on the top and
    right legs and by $\pi^{-1}$ on the down and left legs, equals the same
    entry times the identity. For a one-dimensional representation $\chi$
    the tensor is invariant under this virtual action
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code and quantum double
        models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. This is a statement about one tensor; the
    resulting symmetry of the contracted network is
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_quantum_double_primal_network}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    $(g_1g_2)(g_2^{-1}g_3)(g_4g_3)^{-1}(g_1g_4^{-1})^{-1}
        =g_1g_3g_3^{-1}g_4^{-1}g_4g_1^{-1}=1$.
    Since $\pi$ is multiplicative,
    \[
        \pi(t)\pi(r)\pi(b)^{-1}\pi(l)^{-1}=\pi(t\,r\,b^{-1}l^{-1})=\pi(1)=1
    \]
    on the support, which gives the operator identity entrywise. A
    one-dimensional representation is the case of a character $\chi$, where
    the identity reads $\chi(t)\chi(r)\chi(b)^{-1}\chi(l)^{-1}=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Virtual symmetry of the primal quantum-double network]%
    \label{thm:peps_quantum_double_primal_network}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalTensor_ne_zero_mul_eq_mul,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.quantumDoublePrimalPEPS,
        TNLean.PEPS.sum_smul_torusWestSouthString_quantumDoublePrimalPEPS_eq}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_quantum_double_primal, lem:peps_torus_string_pull_through}
    Place the primal quantum-double tensor at every site of the torus, and
    let $\pi$ be a representation of $G$, in particular an irreducible one.
    The virtual string that multiplies $\pi(g)$ for each bond with label $g$
    it crosses can be pulled through every rectangle of sites of the
    contracted network: inserted along the left and bottom sides of the
    rectangle it gives the same network as inserted along its top and right
    sides \cite[Appendix~A, ``The Toric Code and quantum double
        models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. Around one site the two paths give
    $\pi(l)\pi(b)$ and $\pi(t)\pi(r)$, so the closed string around a site is
    the operator $\pi(t)\pi(r)\pi(b)^{-1}\pi(l)^{-1}$ of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_quantum_double_primal_fusion}. The statement holds
    on every torus of width and height at least two; the source's network,
    with four distinct bonds at each site, is the case of width and height
    at least three \cite{gap:rmp_peps_examples_small_torus}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    On the support of the primal tensor $t\,r\,b^{-1}l^{-1}=1$, so
    $tr=lb$ and $\pi(t)\pi(r)=\pi(l)\pi(b)$. This is the hypothesis of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_string_pull_through} for $X_g=\pi(g)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The AKLT state}

The two-dimensional AKLT state places singlets on the edges of the lattice and
projects the four spin-$\tfrac12$ at each site onto their symmetric subspace
\cite[Appendix~A, ``The AKLT model'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. Label the basis of
four spin-$\tfrac12$ by $s=(s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3)\in\{0,1\}^4$, write $|s|$ for the
number of entries equal to $1$, and let $P_{\pi}$ be the permutation of the four
factors by $\pi\in S_4$.

\begin{definition}[Symmetric projector on four spin-$\tfrac12$]%
    \label{def:peps_aklt_sym_projector}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.legKronecker, TNLean.PEPS.legPermMatrix,
        TNLean.PEPS.symProjector}
    \leanok
    The projector onto the symmetric subspace of $(\C^2)^{\otimes 4}$ is
    $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{\pi\in S_4}P_{\pi}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The symmetric subspace has dimension five]%
    \label{lem:peps_aklt_sym_projector}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.legKronecker_mul, TNLean.PEPS.legKronecker_one,
        TNLean.PEPS.legKronecker_transpose,
        TNLean.PEPS.legPermMatrix_mul, TNLean.PEPS.legPermMatrix_conjTranspose,
        TNLean.PEPS.legPermMatrix_mul_legKronecker,
        TNLean.PEPS.symProjector_mul_self, TNLean.PEPS.symProjector_conjTranspose,
        TNLean.PEPS.symProjector_mul_legKronecker, TNLean.PEPS.legWeight,
        TNLean.PEPS.card_perm_comp_eq, TNLean.PEPS.symProjector_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.choose_legWeight_dvd, TNLean.PEPS.sum_div_choose_legWeight,
        TNLean.PEPS.trace_symProjector, TNLean.PEPS.finrank_range_symProjector}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_aklt_sym_projector}
    $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is a Hermitian idempotent commuting with $U^{\otimes 4}$
    for every $2\times 2$ matrix $U$. Its entries are
    $(\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}})_{s,t}=\binom{4}{|s|}^{-1}$ if $|s|=|t|$ and $0$
    otherwise, and its range, the spin-$2$ subspace, has dimension five.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    $P_{\pi}P_{\rho}=P_{\rho\pi}$ and $P_{\pi}^{\dagger}=P_{\pi^{-1}}$, so the average
    over $S_4$ is a Hermitian idempotent, and
    $P_{\pi} U^{\otimes 4}=U^{\otimes 4}P_{\pi}$ by reordering the tensor
    factors. The number of $\pi$ with
    $s=t\circ\pi$ is $24/\binom{4}{|t|}$ when $|s|=|t|$ and $0$ otherwise, by
    a finite check. The dimension of the range of an idempotent is its trace,
    $\sum_{s}\binom{4}{|s|}^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom4k\binom4k^{-1}=5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[AKLT tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_aklt_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.akltLegs,
        TNLean.PEPS.akltSiteTensorFun, TNLean.PEPS.akltSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.akltPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_aklt_sym_projector, def:peps_torus_site_tensor,
        def:aklt_tensor_review}
    With the singlet matrix
    $Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, the AKLT
    tensor on the square lattice is
    $A=\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes Y\otimes Y)$,
    with physical index $s\in\{0,1\}^4$ (physical dimension $16$) and virtual
    indices ordered top, right, down, left
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``The AKLT model'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Entries and physical span of the AKLT tensor]%
    \label{lem:peps_aklt_entries}
    \lean{Matrix.singletY_apply, Matrix.singletY_mul_transpose,
        TNLean.PEPS.akltLegs_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.legWeight_vec, TNLean.PEPS.akltSiteTensorFun_apply,
        TNLean.PEPS.akltLegs_mul_transpose, TNLean.PEPS.span_akltSiteTensorFun}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_aklt_tensor, lem:peps_aklt_sym_projector}
    The entries of the AKLT tensor are
    \begin{align}
        A^{s}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=
        \frac{(-1)^{\gamma+\delta}}{\binom{4}{k}}\,\delta_{|s|,k},\qquad
        k=\alpha+\beta+(1-\gamma)+(1-\delta),
        \notag
    \end{align}
    and the span of the vectors $s\mapsto A^s_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ over
    all virtual indices is the range of $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    $Y\ket{0}=\ket{1}$ and $Y\ket{1}=-\ket{0}$, so the column
    $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ of $\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes
        Y\otimes Y$ is $(-1)^{\gamma+\delta}$ times the basis vector
    $(\alpha,\beta,1-\gamma,1-\delta)$. The matrix
    $L=\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes Y\otimes Y$ satisfies $LL^{T}=\mathbb{1}$,
    so the columns of $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}L$ span the range of
    $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$SU(2)$ covariance of the AKLT tensor]%
    \label{thm:peps_aklt_su2}
    \lean{Matrix.mul_singletY, TNLean.PEPS.akltSiteTensor_covariant,
        Matrix.adjugate_eq_star_of_mem_specialUnitaryGroup,
        TNLean.PEPS.akltSiteTensor_su2_covariant}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_aklt_tensor, lem:peps_aklt_sym_projector}
    Regard $A$ as the map from the virtual space $(\C^2)^{\otimes 4}$ to the
    physical space. For every $U\in SU(2)$,
    \begin{align}
        U^{\otimes 4}A=A\,(U\otimes U\otimes\bar U\otimes\bar U).
        \notag
    \end{align}
    For an arbitrary $2\times 2$ matrix $U$ the same identity holds with $\bar U$ replaced by
    $(\operatorname{adj}U)^{T}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    For a $2\times2$ matrix, $UY=Y(\operatorname{adj}U)^{T}$ by direct
    computation. Since $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}$ commutes with $U^{\otimes 4}$,
    $U^{\otimes4}\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}L=\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}(U\otimes U\otimes
        UY\otimes UY)=\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}L\,(U\otimes U\otimes V\otimes V)$ with
    $V=(\operatorname{adj}U)^{T}$. For $U\in SU(2)$, $U^{\dagger}U=\mathbb{1}$
    and $U\operatorname{adj}U=(\det U)\mathbb{1}=\mathbb{1}$, so
    $\operatorname{adj}U=U^{\dagger}U\operatorname{adj}U=U^{\dagger}$ and
    $V=\bar U$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The AKLT PEPS as projected singlets]%
    \label{thm:peps_aklt_state}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.akltLegs_vec, TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_akltPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_aklt_tensor, lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    On a torus with $w,h\ge 3$ the AKLT PEPS has coefficient
    \begin{align}
        \Coeff(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau}\prod_{v}(\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}})_{\sigma_v,\tau_v}
        \prod_{v}Y_{\tau_{v+(1,0),3},\,\tau_{v,1}}\,Y_{\tau_{v+(0,1),2},\,\tau_{v,0}},
        \notag
    \end{align}
    the sum running over the spin-$\tfrac12$ labels $\tau_v\in\{0,1\}^4$ of the
    four legs of every site. The factor $Y_{b,a}$ is the coefficient of
    $\ket{a}\ket{b}$ in the singlet $\ket{01}-\ket{10}$, so the state is
    $\bigotimes_v\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}$ applied to a singlet on every edge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    Expand each $A=\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}L$ as a sum over the intermediate label
    $\tau_v$. The identity factors of $L$ force the bond index on the top edge
    of $v$ to be $\tau_{v,0}$ and on the right edge to be $\tau_{v,1}$, and the
    $Y$ factors on the down and left legs then give
    $Y_{\tau_{v,2},\tau_{v-(0,1),0}}Y_{\tau_{v,3},\tau_{v-(1,0),1}}$; shifting
    the product by one site gives the formula.
\end{proof}

The tensor $A$ takes values in $(\C^2)^{\otimes 4}$ with physical support on
the five-dimensional symmetric subspace, on which the physical action
$U^{\otimes 4}$ acts as the irreducible spin-$2$ representation
\cite[Appendix~A, ``The AKLT model'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.

\subsection{The RVB state}

The nearest-neighbour resonating valence bond state is the superposition of
all coverings of the lattice by nearest-neighbour singlets. Its PEPS of bond
dimension three combines, at each site, the projector
\begin{align}
    P=\ket{0}\bigl[(0222|+(2022|+(2202|+(2220|\bigr]
    +\ket{1}\bigl[(1222|+(2122|+(2212|+(2221|\bigr]
    \notag
\end{align}
with singlet matrices on the edges; on the square lattice with a
translation-invariant orientation of the singlets the tensor is
$A=P(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes Y\otimes Y)$
\cite[Appendix~A, ``The RVB state'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. On the bond of
dimension three the matrix $Y$ acts on the spin states $0,1$ and is extended by
$1$ on the vacuum state $2$, so that the bond state is
$\ket{01}-\ket{10}+\ket{22}$. The extension by $0$ would make every entry of
$A$ vanish, since a leg carrying $Y$ could then never be in the vacuum state
\cite{gap:rmp_peps_rvb_bond_vacuum}.

\begin{definition}[RVB tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_rvb_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.rvbSingletY, TNLean.PEPS.rvbProjector,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensorInt, TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    The RVB tensor is $A=P(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes Y\otimes Y)$ with
    $Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
    virtual indices ordered top, right, down, left.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local dimer constraint]%
    \label{lem:peps_rvb_local}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.rvbFlip, TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensorInt_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensor_apply, TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensor_ne_zero_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbBondLabel, TNLean.PEPS.rvbBondLabel_ne_two_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensorInt_ne_zero,
        TNLean.PEPS.rvbSiteTensorInt_bondLabel}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_rvb_tensor}
    $A^{s}_{urdl}\neq 0$ if and only if exactly one of $u,r,d,l$ differs from
    $2$, that index being $s$ if it is $u$ or $r$ and $1-s$ if it is $d$ or
    $l$. The nonzero entries equal $-1$ when the down or left index is $1$ and
    $+1$ otherwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    $Y$ maps the bond index $d$ to $1-d$ on the spin states, with sign $-1$
    at $d=1$, and fixes the vacuum. The statement is a finite check over the
    $162$ entries.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The RVB PEPS is the superposition of singlet coverings]%
    \label{thm:peps_rvb_state}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.IsTorusDimerCovering, TNLean.PEPS.rvbCoveringBonds,
        TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_rvbPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_rvb_tensor, def:peps_stateCoeff}
    Let $w,h\ge3$. A dimer covering of the torus is a set $c$ of edges such
    that every site lies on exactly one edge of $c$. The RVB PEPS has
    coefficient
    \begin{align}
        \Coeff(\sigma)=\sum_{c}\ \prod_{(v,v+e)\in c}Y_{\sigma_{v+e},\sigma_v},
        \notag
    \end{align}
    the sum running over all dimer coverings, and each edge of $c$ written as
    $(v,v+e)$ with $e\in\{(1,0),(0,1)\}$. The factor $Y_{\sigma_{v+e},\sigma_v}$
    is the coefficient of the singlet $\ket{01}-\ket{10}$ on the two sites, so
    the state is the superposition of all nearest-neighbour singlet coverings
    with the translation-invariant orientation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction, lem:peps_rvb_local}
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction} the coefficient is a
    sum over bond indices $h_v$, $v_v$ on the right and up edges. By
    Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_rvb_local} a configuration contributes only if the
    edges carrying a non-vacuum index form a dimer covering and the index on
    each such edge is the physical index of the site below or to the left of
    it. Conversely every dimer covering determines one such configuration,
    and its term is the product over sites of the singlet factors on the down
    and left legs; shifting the product by one site gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{PEPS from classical models}

Let $q\ge 1$, let $h\colon\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a
nearest-neighbour interaction and $\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ an inverse temperature.

\begin{definition}[Classical energy]%
    \label{def:peps_classical_energy}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.classicalEnergy}
    \leanok
    On the torus a configuration $\sigma$ has energy
    \begin{align}
        H(\sigma)=\sum_{v}\bigl(h(\sigma_{v},\sigma_{v-(1,0)})
        +h(\sigma_{v},\sigma_{v-(0,1)})\bigr),
        \notag
    \end{align}
    each nearest-neighbour bond counted once. For symmetric $h$ this is the
    sum of $h$ over unordered bonds.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Classical-model tensor]%
    \label{def:peps_classical_tensor}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.classicalBondMatrix, TNLean.PEPS.classicalSiteTensor,
        TNLean.PEPS.classicalPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_torus_site_tensor}
    Let $M=\sum_{i,j}e^{-\beta h(i,j)/2}|i)(j|$. The classical-model tensor is
    \begin{align}
        A=\Bigl[\sum_{i}\ket{i}(i\,i\,i\,i|\Bigr]
        (\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes M\otimes M):
        \notag
    \end{align}
    the top and right legs copy the physical index $s$, and the down and left
    legs carry $M_{sb}$ and $M_{sl}$ at bond indices $b$ and $l$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``PEPS from classical models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}. The
    classical-model PEPS places this tensor at every site of the torus.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coefficients of the classical-model PEPS]%
    \label{thm:peps_classical_coeff}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.stateCoeff_classicalPEPS,
        TNLean.PEPS.conj_mul_stateCoeff_classicalPEPS,
        TNLean.PEPS.normSq_stateCoeff_classicalPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_classical_tensor, def:peps_classical_energy,
        def:peps_stateCoeff}
    On a torus of width and height at least three the classical-model PEPS
    has coefficient
    $\Coeff(\sigma)=e^{-\beta H(\sigma)/2}$, so
    $|\Coeff(\sigma)|^{2}=e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}$ is the Gibbs weight of
    $\sigma$ \cite{gap:rmp_peps_examples_small_torus}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction}
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_torus_site_tensor_contraction} the top and right
    legs force every bond index to equal the physical index of the site
    below or to the left of it. The remaining factor at $v$ is
    $M_{\sigma_{v}\sigma_{v-(0,1)}}M_{\sigma_{v}\sigma_{v-(1,0)}}$, one factor
    $e^{-\beta h/2}$ for each bond at $v$ read towards its lower and left
    neighbours, and the product over $v$ is $e^{-\beta H(\sigma)/2}$. The
    coefficient is real, so its squared modulus is $e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}$.
\end{proof}

No bond carries a product of two copies of $M$: the entry
$e^{-\beta h/2}$ is not a square root of a transfer matrix, and no symmetry
of $h$ is used.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal expectation values are Gibbs averages]%
    \label{thm:peps_classical_expectation}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.expectation_classicalPEPS}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_classical_tensor, def:peps_classical_energy,
        def:peps_stateCoeff}
    Let the torus have width and height at least three and let
    $O=\sum_{\sigma}O(\sigma)\ket{\sigma}\bra{\sigma}$ be a diagonal observable.
    For the classical-model PEPS state
    $\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\sigma}\Coeff(\sigma)\ket{\sigma}$,
    \begin{align}
        \frac{\sum_{\sigma}\overline{\Coeff(\sigma)}\,O(\sigma)\Coeff(\sigma)}
        {\sum_{\sigma}\overline{\Coeff(\sigma)}\Coeff(\sigma)}
        =\frac{\sum_{\sigma}O(\sigma)e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}}
        {\sum_{\sigma}e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}},
        \notag
    \end{align}
    that is, $\bra{\psi}O\ket{\psi}/\braket{\psi}{\psi}$ is the expectation
    value of $O$ in the Gibbs state $e^{-\beta H}/Z$
    \cite[Appendix~A, ``PEPS from classical models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_classical_coeff}
    Replace $\overline{\Coeff(\sigma)}\Coeff(\sigma)$ by
    $e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}$ in both sums, by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_classical_coeff}.
\end{proof}

In particular, classical correlation functions are expectation values of
the PEPS. The review adds that at the critical inverse temperature
$\beta_{c}$ the PEPS has correlations decaying as a power law, so its parent
Hamiltonian must be gapless
\cite[Appendix~A, ``PEPS from classical models'']{Cirac2021Matrix}, since a
gapped local Hamiltonian has exponentially decaying correlations
\cite{Hastings2006Spectral,Nachtergaele2006LiebRobinson}.
