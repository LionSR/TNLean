For a normal PEPS, the tensors become injective after blocking around every edge,
and each site separates two injective regions whose complements are also injective.
With union closure in hand, these hypotheses yield the Fundamental
Theorem for normal PEPS (Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_connected})
on a connected graph \cite[Section~4]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, with gauges unique
modulo balanced edge scalars.
Corollaries specialize the result to closed chains, translation-invariant matrix
product states, and the square lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental Theorem for normal PEPS]%
    \label{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS}
    \notready
    \uses{thm:peps_normalEdgeBlockingHypotheses_injectiveChain, def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses, thm:peps_oneVertexComplementComparison, def:GaugeEquivPEPS}
    Suppose two normal PEPS generate the same state, can be blocked into
    three-site injective chains around every edge, and for every site admit
    two injective regions with injective complements that differ exactly at
    that site. Then their defining tensors are related by a local gauge, and
    the gauges are unique up to the scalar freedom allowed by the graph. This
    is the general normal-PEPS theorem in
    \cite[Section~4]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, following its Theorem~3.
    A connected-graph form, with the blocking hypothesis stated as the
    source's proof uses it (one blocking shared by the two states, each
    distinguished edge the entire bond of its chain), is
    Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_connected}; its per-edge
    uniqueness clause is
    Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_gauge_unique}.
    The source's remark following the theorem, that for a translationally
    invariant system the gauges are also translationally invariant when the
    proportionality constants are not absorbed into them, is realized on the
    torus by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalTorusPEPS_unconditional}: its
    gauge family is carried onto every edge by the translations from one
    reference matrix per orientation class, and its scalar $\lambda$ is kept
    separate rather than absorbed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Doubly injective regions]%
    \label{def:peps_regionInjectivityDataPair}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.regionInjectivityDataPair}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_regionInjectivityData}
    For two region-injectivity assignments on the same vertex set, a region is
    doubly injective when it is injective for each of the two. The general
    normal theorem blocks two states by one shared geometry, and its final
    comparison contracts both tensors over the same regions, so its blocking
    hypotheses are instantiated at the doubly injective assignment of the two
    tensors.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Two normal PEPS tensors]%
    \label{def:peps_normalPEPSPairHypotheses}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.NormalPEPSPairHypotheses}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses, def:peps_regionInjectivityDataPair}
    Two PEPS tensors $A$ and $B$ on a finite simple graph are a
    \emph{pair of normal PEPS tensors} if they satisfy the normal blocking
    hypotheses at the doubly injective assignment of the two tensors and every
    virtual bond space of each tensor is nonzero, that is, $D_A(e)>0$ and $D_B(e)>0$ for every edge
    $e$. Normality is blockability into injective regions, as defined in
    \cite[Section~3]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}. The nonzero bond spaces are the
    standing convention of that section. Region injectivity does not imply
    them: a zero-dimensional boundary bond leaves a region with no boundary
    configurations, so its injectivity holds vacuously. The convention is
    recorded in \cite{gap:peps_injective_ft_section3_route}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Equal bond dimensions from the normal blocking hypotheses]%
    \label{thm:peps_bondDim_eq_of_normalBlocking}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.bondDim_eq_of_normalBlocking}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.bondDim_apply_eq_of_blockingData}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_normalPEPSPairHypotheses, def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses,
        def:peps_regionInjectivityDataPair}
    Let $A$ and $B$ be a pair of normal PEPS tensors on a finite simple graph
    with the same positive physical dimension, generating the same state,
    with each distinguished edge the only edge of the graph joining its two
    adjacent blocks. Then the bond dimensions of $A$ and $B$ agree on every
    edge. The one-edge form assumes only the blocking datum at a single edge,
    and takes the positive bond dimensions as explicit hypotheses.
    In the source the equality is part of the isomorphism lemma of
    \cite[Section~3]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}: the insertion correspondence
    $X\mapsto Y$ on the chosen bond is an algebra isomorphism between the two
    full bond matrix algebras, so the bond dimensions on the two sides agree;
    the normal case inherits the argument through the blocked three-site
    chains. The single-crossing condition is the source's blocking around an
    edge made explicit: the chosen edge is the entire bond between the first
    two parties of the blocked chain.
    \begin{proof}
        \leanok
        \uses{thm:peps_edgeBlockedThreeSiteInjective, thm:peps_edgeBlockedInsertionAlgebraIsomorphism}
        Fix an edge. Blocking both networks into the shared three regions
        around it produces two three-site chains on the three-vertex complete
        graph whose super-sites carry the blocked-region weight families,
        injective by hypothesis. Each coarse state coefficient is a positive
        merge-collapse constant, a product of bond dimensions over the edges
        not incident to each region, times the corresponding original state
        coefficient, so the two coarse states are proportional with ratio the
        two constants. Multiplying one super-site of each chain by the other
        chain's constant yields chains with equal states, unchanged bond
        dimensions, and the super-site injectivities preserved. The matched
        matrix insertions on the distinguished coarse bond of the two scaled
        chains form an algebra isomorphism between the two full bond matrix
        algebras
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_edgeBlockedInsertionAlgebraIsomorphism}), and
        an isomorphism of full matrix algebras forces equal matrix sizes.
        Under the single-crossing hypothesis the coarse bond carries the
        original bond dimension of the distinguished edge for each tensor, so
        the two original bond dimensions agree.
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental Theorem for normal PEPS on a connected graph]%
    \label{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_connected}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS}
    \leanok
    % Reading note: this is the general normal theorem stated after Theorem 3
    % of \cite{Molnar2018NormalPEPS} on a connected graph.  The shared
    % blockings and the single-crossing hypothesis are the source's blocking
    % hypothesis made explicit, not restrictions; both readings are recorded
    % in \path{docs/paper-gaps/peps_normal_ft_section3_route.tex}.
    \uses{def:peps_normalPEPSPairHypotheses, def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses,
        def:peps_regionInjectivityDataPair, def:GaugeEquivPEPS}
    Let $A$ and $B$ be a pair of normal PEPS tensors on a connected finite
    simple graph with the same positive physical dimension, generating the
    same state. Thus the normal blocking hypotheses hold at the doubly
    injective assignment of the two tensors: around every edge the network
    blocks into three doubly injective regions with the edge endpoints in the
    first two, and every site admits two doubly injective regions, with doubly
    injective complements, differing exactly at that site. Suppose also
    that at every edge the distinguished edge is
    the only edge of the graph joining its two adjacent blocks. Then the
    defining tensors are related by a local gauge: there are invertible
    matrices, one on each edge, whose endpoint actions transform $A_v$ into
    $B_v$ at every vertex, with no leftover scalar.
    The shared blocking gives a common tripartition for the two tensors, with
    all three regions injective for both. For an edge $e$ between the first
    two regions, the coefficient transfer $T_e$ given by the three-site
    isomorphism argument satisfies
    \begin{align}
        C_A(R_1,e,X;\sigma,\tau)
         & =C_B(R_1,e,T_e(X);\sigma,\tau),
        \notag                             \\
        T_e(X)
         & =Z_e\,\iota_e(X)\,Z_e^{-1},
        \notag
    \end{align}
    for an invertible $Z_e$ and every virtual insertion $X$ on $e$.
    The single-crossing hypothesis identifies $e$ with the entire bond between
    $R_1$ and $R_2$, so $Z_e$ acts on the original edge.
    The equality of the bond dimensions is not assumed; it follows from the
    blocking hypotheses by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_bondDim_eq_of_normalBlocking}. Following the
    blocking argument of \cite[Section~4]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, the nonzero bond
    spaces of the pair are used in the injectivity argument, while
    connectivity permits the per-vertex constants to be absorbed into the edge
    gauges (see Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_PEPS}).
    % Source-faithful reading: Section 4 of \cite{Molnar2018NormalPEPS} uses
    % one common blocking of the two states and enlarges the endpoint regions
    % so that they meet only along the distinguished edge.
    \begin{proof}
        \leanok
        \uses{thm:peps_bondDim_eq_of_normalBlocking, lem:peps_injectiveRegion_union_pos, thm:peps_region_edge_gauge_of_coeff_transfer, thm:gaugeConsistency}
        The bond dimensions of the two tensors agree on every edge by
        Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_bondDim_eq_of_normalBlocking}.
        The union closure of injective regions for either tensor follows from
        the positive bond dimensions and
        Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_injectiveRegion_union_pos}. At every edge the
        shared blocking datum restricts to a datum for each tensor over the
        same three regions, so the per-edge gauge of the region insertion
        transfer (Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_region_edge_gauge_of_coeff_transfer})
        and its orientation-adapted absorption give a family of invertible
        matrices realizing the absorbed inserted-coefficient equality at every
        edge. A gauge preserves blocked-region linear independence, so at
        every site the one-site comparison applies to the gauge-absorbed
        tensor: comparing the smaller region with its one-site completion
        yields two proportionality scalars whose ratio is a nonzero scalar
        $\lambda_v$ with $A_v$ equal to $\lambda_v$ times the gauge action on
        $B$ at $v$. When the smaller region is empty its block is the count
        of global virtual configurations, and when the completion is the full
        vertex set its block is the closed state coefficient; in these
        degenerate cases the proportionality holds with scalar one, and every
        other comparison region has a boundary edge by connectivity. The
        per-vertex relations against the nonzero closed state force
        $\prod_v \lambda_v = 1$, so on the connected graph a spanning-tree
        construction realizes the reciprocals of the scalars as oriented
        incidence products of edge scalars, which fold into the gauges as in
        the absorption step of the injective Fundamental Theorem
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:gaugeConsistency}).
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the normal-PEPS gauges up to a constant]%
    \label{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_gauge_unique}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_gauge_unique}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_normalPEPSPairHypotheses, def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses,
        def:peps_regionInjectivityDataPair, def:gaugeVertex}
    Let $A$ and $B$ be a pair of normal PEPS tensors on a finite simple graph
    with equal bond dimensions. Suppose two families of invertible matrices, one on each
    edge, each realize the scalar-free gauge relation of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_connected}: at every vertex
    the endpoint actions of the family transform $A_v$ into $B_v$. Then the
    two families are proportional at every edge: $X'_e = c\cdot X_e$ for a
    nonzero scalar $c$ depending on the edge. This is the uniqueness clause
    of the general normal-PEPS theorem in
    \cite[Section~4]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, following its Theorem~3, read
    against the scalar-free relation; neither equality of the two states nor
    connectivity is needed.
    \begin{proof}
        \leanok
        \uses{thm:peps_gaugeConj_eq_of_coeffIdentities, lem:peps_injectiveRegion_union_pos}
        The inserted-edge coefficient is multilinear in the site tensors, one
        factor per site, so each scalar-free per-vertex relation makes every
        inserted coefficient of $B$ equal the corresponding inserted
        coefficient of the gauge-absorbed $A$: each family realizes the
        absorbed inserted-coefficient equality at every edge, with the roles
        of the two tensors exchanged. On the first block of the edge's frame,
        whose blocked map and complement blocked map are injective for $A$ by
        the blocking hypotheses and union closure
        (Lemma~\ref{lem:peps_injectiveRegion_union_pos}), the region insertion
        transfer is determined by the absorbed equality
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_gaugeConj_eq_of_coeffIdentities}), so the two
        orientation-adapted absorbing gauges induce the same conjugation and
        differ by a nonzero scalar; unwinding the orientation adaptation gives
        the proportionality of the gauges themselves.
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Positive physical dimension from an injective region]%

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok

    on every boundary edge. Linear independence gives the nonzero blocked

    \end{align}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regional injectivity from the state and bond dimensions]%
    \label{thm:peps_regional_injectivity_from_state}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.regionBlockedTensorInjective_of_sameState_of_bondDim_eq}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:peps_regionInjectivityDataOf, def:peps_same_state}
    Let $A$ and $B$ have the same state and the same positive bond
    dimensions. If a region $R$ and its complement are injective for $A$,
    then $R$ is injective for $B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{def:peps_region_partial_state, thm:peps_region_partial_state_same_state}
    Write $F_{A,R}$ and $F_{B,R}$ for the blocked maps from the common
    boundary space to the physical space of $R$. Injectivity of the
    complement for $A$ identifies the range of $F_{A,R}$ with the span of
    the partial-state vectors across this cut:
    \begin{align}
        \operatorname{ran}F_{A,R}
         & =\operatorname{span}\{\psi^\tau_{A,R}\}_\tau.
        \notag
    \end{align}
    Since $A$ and $B$ have the same state, Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_region_partial_state_same_state}
    gives
    \begin{align}
        \psi^\tau_{A,R}
         & =\psi^\tau_{B,R}
        \notag
    \end{align}
    for every complement configuration $\tau$. Splitting the contraction of
    $B$ along the boundary of $R$ writes each $\psi^\tau_{B,R}$ as a linear
    combination of the blocked vectors of $B$, because every bond dimension
    is positive. Hence
    \begin{align}
        \operatorname{ran}F_{A,R}
         & =\operatorname{span}\{\psi^\tau_{B,R}\}_\tau
        \subseteq\operatorname{ran}F_{B,R}.
        \notag
    \end{align}
    Thus $\dim\operatorname{ran}F_{B,R}\ge
        \dim\operatorname{ran}F_{A,R}=\dim\operatorname{dom}F_{B,R}$.
    The two domains have the same dimension because their bond dimensions
    agree. Consequently the columns of $F_{B,R}$ are linearly independent.
\end{proof}

    Suppose $A$ admits the normal blocking regions of
    Definition~\ref{def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses}, with nonzero
    virtual spaces. If $B$ has the same state and bond dimensions, the
    inherited pair blockings use exactly these regions for both tensors.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_regional_injectivity_from_state, lem:peps_injectiveRegion_union_pos}
    For each edge blocking, the complement of each of the three regions
    is the disjoint union of the other two, hence is injective for $A$.
    The one-site comparison regions already have injective complements.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_regional_injectivity_from_state} therefore proves
    injectivity of each selected region for $B$. All regions and their
    incidence properties are retained, and the bond spaces stay nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Virtual gauges for a normal on-site symmetry]%
    \label{thm:peps_normal_onsite_symmetry_gauge}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.gaugeEquiv_physicalTensorAction_of_normal_symmetry}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:peps_physical_tensor_action, def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses,
        def:peps_normalPEPSPairHypotheses, def:GaugeEquivPEPS}
    Let $A$ be a normal tensor network on a connected finite simple graph
    with nonzero virtual spaces. Assume the edge blockings and one-site
    comparison regions of
    Definition~\ref{def:peps_normalPEPSBlockingHypotheses}; in each edge
    blocking, the distinguished edge is the entire bond between the first
    two regions. If $U\in\MN{d}$ satisfies
    $U^{\otimes V}\Coeff_A=\Coeff_A$, then there is an invertible matrix
    $X_e$ on every edge such that $(U\cdot A)_v=\operatorname{Gauge}_X(A)_v$
    at every vertex.
    This gives the virtual-gauge conclusion of the global-symmetry
    application in~\cite[lines~1696--1713]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}.
    Connectivity is needed to absorb the component scalars, as recorded
    in~\cite{gap:peps_gaugeConsistency_connectivity_gap}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{def:peps_physical_tensor_action, thm:peps_state_physical_tensor_action,
        def:peps_normal_pair_blockings_from_state,
        thm:peps_physical_dimension_pos_of_region,
        thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_connected}
    Put $B=U\cdot A$. Its state equals that of $A$, and its bond
    dimensions are unchanged. Definition~\ref{def:peps_normal_pair_blockings_from_state}
    gives the required shared injective regions for $A$ and $B$, retaining
    the single-crossing property of each distinguished edge.
    A one-site comparison region containing its distinguished site is
    nonempty, so Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_physical_dimension_pos_of_region}
    gives $d>0$. Apply the connected normal Fundamental Theorem to this pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplication of endpoint gauge matrices]%
    \label{thm:peps_endpoint_gauge_mul}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.edgeGaugeAt_mul}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:edgeGaugeAt}
    Let $X$ and $Y$ be invertible edge-matrix families, and write
    $M_{v,e}(X)$ for the endpoint action of $X_e$ at $v$. Then
    $M_{v,e}(XY)=M_{v,e}(X)M_{v,e}(Y)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    At the first endpoint this is matrix multiplication. At the second,
    $(XY)^{-\mathsf T}=X^{-\mathsf T}Y^{-\mathsf T}$, where
    $X^{-\mathsf T}=(X^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Composition of virtual gauge actions]%
    \label{thm:peps_gauge_action_composition}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.gaugeVertex_applyGauge}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:gaugeVertex, def:applyGauge}
    For invertible edge-matrix families $X$ and $Y$, the vertex tensors
    satisfy $(A^X)^Y_v=A^{YX}_v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_endpoint_gauge_mul}
    Expand both gauge actions and exchange the finite sums. On each
    incident leg the resulting matrix is
    $M_{v,e}(Y)M_{v,e}(X)=M_{v,e}(YX)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Composition of physical tensor actions]%
    \label{thm:peps_physical_action_composition}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.physicalTensorAction_mul_component}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:peps_physical_tensor_action}
    For physical matrices $U,W\in\MN{d}$, the vertex tensors satisfy
    $(U\cdot(W\cdot A))_v=((UW)\cdot A)_v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    Exchange the finite sums over the two physical indices and use
    $(UW)_{\sigma\kappa}=\sum_\tau U_{\sigma\tau}W_{\tau\kappa}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Physical and virtual tensor actions commute]%
    \label{thm:peps_physical_virtual_commute}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.physicalTensorAction_applyGauge_component}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:peps_physical_tensor_action, def:gaugeVertex, def:applyGauge}
    For a physical matrix $U$ and an invertible edge-matrix family $X$,
    the vertex tensors satisfy $(U\cdot A^X)_v=(U\cdot A)^X_v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    Write $M_{v,e}=M_{v,e}(X)$ and let $\eta,\eta'$ range over the
    virtual indices of the legs incident to $v$. Exchanging the sums gives
    \begin{align}
        (U\cdot A^X)_v(\eta,\sigma)
         & =\sum_\tau U_{\sigma\tau}\sum_{\eta'}
        \Bigl(\prod_{e\ni v}(M_{v,e})_{\eta_e\eta'_e}\Bigr)
        A_v(\eta',\tau)\notag                                               \\
         & =\sum_{\eta'}\Bigl(\prod_{e\ni v}(M_{v,e})_{\eta_e\eta'_e}\Bigr)
        \sum_\tau U_{\sigma\tau}A_v(\eta',\tau)
        =(U\cdot A)^X_v(\eta,\sigma).\notag
    \end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projective bond representations of a normal symmetry group]%
    \label{thm:peps_normal_virtual_representation}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.exists_virtualRepresentation_of_normal_symmetry}
    \leanok

    \uses{def:projective_rep, thm:peps_normal_onsite_symmetry_gauge}
    Let $A$ satisfy all the graph and blocking hypotheses of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_normal_onsite_symmetry_gauge}. Let $H$ be a group
    and $U:H\to\GL_d(\C)$ a representation such that
    $U(g)^{\otimes V}\Coeff_A=\Coeff_A$ for every $g\in H$.
    Then each edge $e$ admits a homomorphism
    $\overline\rho_e:H\to\operatorname{PGL}_{D_e}(\C)$ and invertible lifts
    $\rho_e(g)\in\GL_{D_e}(\C)$ with
    $\overline\rho_e(g)=[\rho_e(g)]$. Here
    $\operatorname{PGL}_{D_e}(\C)=\GL_{D_e}(\C)/\C^\times$.
    The lifts have a factor system $\omega_e:H\times H\to\C^\times$
    such that, for all $g,h,k\in H$,
    \begin{align}
        \rho_e(g)\rho_e(h)          & =\omega_e(g,h)\rho_e(gh),\notag     \\
        \omega_e(g,h)\omega_e(gh,k) & =\omega_e(g,hk)\omega_e(h,k).\notag
    \end{align}
    At every vertex, $(U(g)\cdot A)_v=A^{X(g)}_v$, where
    $X(g)_e=\rho_e(g^{-1})$.
    This proves the projective-representation assertion
    in~\cite[Applications, line~1713]{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, with the
    connectedness correction in~\cite{gap:peps_gaugeConsistency_connectivity_gap}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:peps_normal_onsite_symmetry_gauge,
        def:peps_normal_pair_blockings_from_state,
        thm:peps_physical_action_composition, thm:peps_physical_virtual_commute,
        thm:peps_gauge_action_composition,
        thm:fundamentalTheorem_normalPEPS_gauge_unique, lem:cocycle_of_assoc}
    Choose gauges $Z(g)$ from
    Theorem~\ref{thm:peps_normal_onsite_symmetry_gauge}.
    Composition and commutation give
    $(U(gh)\cdot A)_v=A^{Z(h)Z(g)}_v$.
    The same transformed tensor is also $A^{Z(gh)}_v$.
    Gauge uniqueness therefore gives nonzero scalars $c_e(g,h)$ with
    $Z_e(h)Z_e(g)=c_e(g,h)Z_e(gh)$.
    Set $\rho_e(g)=Z_e(g^{-1})$ and
    $\omega_e(g,h)=c_e(h^{-1},g^{-1})$.
    Since $(gh)^{-1}=h^{-1}g^{-1}$, this is the stated projective
    multiplication law. Passing to projective classes removes its scalar
    factor and gives the homomorphism $\overline\rho_e$.
    The bond dimension $D_e$ is positive, so
    Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle_of_assoc} gives the cocycle identity.
\end{proof}
