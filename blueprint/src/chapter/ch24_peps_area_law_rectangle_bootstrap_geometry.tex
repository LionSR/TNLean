\subsection{Scanner geometry for weakly safe rectangles}
\label{sec:rectangle_bootstrap_geometry}

Let $Q$ be a nonempty integer rectangle, with maximal side length $s$,
measured in numbers of lattice sites.
The geometric argument includes one-site-wide rectangles and singleton
rectangles.
For a cut $A$ in an arbitrary finite induced domain, the physical target
is $A\cap Q$; it may be empty even though the ambient rectangle is not.

\begin{definition}[The rectangle scanner]
    \label{def:rectangle_bootstrap_scanner}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Scan.rectangleBootstrapScanner}
    \leanok
    Fix scanner exponents and real coefficients $C_r,C_1$.
    Put $T=Q\cap\mathbb Z^2$ and $n=\lceil24s\rceil=24s$.
    Use the induced domain graph, the given cut $A$, and
    ambient integer sup-norm depth from $T$.
    Anchor each interaction support at one chosen member of that same
    nonempty support.
    Assign the original rounded scales
    \begin{align}
        m & =\lfloor n^\mu\rfloor,
          & K                      & =\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor n^{1-\ell}\rfloor}{8m}\right\rfloor,
          & D                      & =\lceil n^\kappa\rceil,
          & r_0                    & =\lceil C_r\log^2n\rceil_+.
        \label{eq:rectangle_bootstrap_scales}
    \end{align}
    These are the data of the original finite-history collar scanner.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Weak-rectangle scanner geometry]
    \label{thm:rectangle_bootstrap_scanner_geometry}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Scan.exists_rectangleBootstrapScanner_geometry}
    \leanok
    Fix scanner exponents and $C_r\geq0$.
    There is a threshold $N\geq1$ before the finite domain, cut,
    rectangle, interaction range, charge coefficient and safety
    parameter, with the following property.
    Let $s\geq N$ and $D_0\geq1$, and suppose the rectangle has
    strict clearance greater than $D_0s$ from every endpoint of an
    edge crossing $A$.
    For the scanner above and $L=\lfloor n^{1-\ell}\rfloor$,
    \begin{align}
        |T|                    & \leq n^2,
                               & 1         & \leq L\leq s,
                               & D_0L+L    & \leq D_0s,\nonumber               \\
        |T_r\setminus T_{r-1}| & \leq n
                               &           & (1\leq r\leq L),
                               & 2L+10r_0  & <\operatorname{dist}_\infty(t,z).
        \label{eq:rectangle_bootstrap_geometry}
    \end{align}
    The last inequality holds for every $t\in T$ and every cut
    endpoint $z$.
    Every chosen anchor lies in its original support, and at most
    $2^{(1+2R(R+1))-1}$ labels have a given anchor.
    The target is exactly $A\cap Q$, the positive-depth prefix at
    every nonnegative integer $j$ is exactly $A\cap(Q^{+j}\setminus Q)$,
    and the truncation set at every $L'$ is exactly $A\cap Q^{+L'}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    The lower-left corner belongs to $T$, and the product cardinality
    gives $|T|\leq s^2\leq n^2$ independently of aspect ratio.
    For $1\leq r\leq L\leq s$, the native rectangle layer bound gives
    $|T_r\setminus T_{r-1}|\leq4(s+2r)\leq12s\leq n$.
    The rounded-clearance theorem gives $1\leq L\leq s$,
    $D_0L+L\leq D_0s$, and $2L+10r_0\leq D_0s$ at one threshold
    uniform in $D_0$.
    Combine its last inequality with the assumed weak safety.
    Unpacking an ambient cut endpoint gives its crossing edge
    and physical endpoint; the natural sup-norm distance equals the
    integer sup-norm distance after casting.
    The existing support count bounds the fibers of the chosen anchors.
    Finally, ambient depth sublevel sets are exactly ambient dilations,
    and the dilation of the native rectangle is exactly $Q^{+j}$.
    These equalities give all three physical-region identities.
\end{proof}

No template with stronger clearance is used.
The same original physical entropy and dyadic shell estimates can now be
applied to these exact regions.
The entropy improvement, uniform current-exponent coefficient and box
supremum are separate steps in the proof of
\cite[Proposition~9.5]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.
