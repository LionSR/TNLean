\chapter{Proof of the Fundamental Theorem}\label{ch:ft_proof}

For BNT canonical forms $P$ and $Q$, write the weighted direct sums over
normal-tensor sectors as $P^i=\bigoplus_{j,q}\mu_{j,q}P_j^i$ and
$Q^i=\bigoplus_{k,q}\nu_{k,q}Q_k^i$. When the generated matrix product vectors
are proportional, the normal sectors are matched by a bijection $\beta$, unit
phases $\zeta_k$, and invertible matrices $X_k$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    Q_k^i
     & =\zeta_kX_kP_{\beta(k)}^iX_k^{-1}.
    \notag
\end{align}
In the equal-MPV case, eventual linear independence yields the coefficient
identities
\begin{align}
    c_N^{(\beta(k))}(P)
     & =\zeta_k^N c_N^{(k)}(Q).
    \notag
\end{align}
Finite power-sum comparison determines the copy weights,
$\nu_{k,q}=\zeta_k^{-1}\mu_{\beta(k),\tau_k(q)}$ for a copy permutation
$\tau_k$. Each sector phase cancels against the inverse phase in its matched
copy weight, and the block conjugacies combine into
$X=\bigoplus_k(\mathbf 1_{r_k}\otimes X_k)$, giving global gauge equivalence.

Throughout, ``eventually'' means ``for all sufficiently large $N$''. At a
fixed length in that range, BNT linear independence detects exact coefficient
equalities even when a weight of modulus less than $1$ makes its powers decay.
