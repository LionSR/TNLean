\chapter{Physics Examples of the Multi-Block Compression Theorem}%
\label{ch:asymmetric_examples}

Matrix product states and operators describe periodic spin chains by
contracting local tensors around a ring~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}. At every
positive chain length $N$, an MPS tensor $A$ and an MPO tensor $M$ give
\begin{align}
	\ket{V^{(N)}(A)}
	 & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\tr(A^{i_1}\cdots A^{i_N})
	\ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}, \notag                        \\
	O_N(M)
	 & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_N}
	\tr(M^{i_1j_1}\cdots M^{i_Nj_N})
	\ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\bra{j_1,\ldots,j_N}.
	\notag
\end{align}
The physical indices $i,j$ range over $\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$.
Each local matrix acts on its tensor's virtual bond space, with rows
and columns indexed by $\{0,\ldots,D-1\}$ for bond dimension $D$.
The periodic boundary contraction is the ordinary matrix trace, with
no boundary insertion. The first MPO physical index is the output and
the second is the input; operator products act from right to left.
Thus the local tensor $\sum_jM^{ij}\otimes P^{jk}$ represents
$O_N(M)O_N(P)$. MPS vectors need not be normalized. A nonzero positive
periodic MPDO operator gives a density operator after division by its
trace.

The examples begin with elementary GHZ and product-state families,
then turn to the CZX operator and its sum with the identity.
Kramers--Wannier duality maps between product and ferromagnetic states;
its square gives an Ising-chain operator identity involving translation.
The Fibonacci example acts on admissible label paths, whose projector
is the unit of its operator algebra rather than the identity on the
full physical space. The parity examples distinguish physical parity,
an operator on the chain, from length parity in a coefficient such as
$1+(-1)^N$. The renormalization fixed-point examples distinguish
physical mixed states built from Bell bonds from the vertical tensor
components used to compute their operator products. Later, separate
sections treat Ising coefficients and the groups
$\Z_2\times\Z_2$ and $\Z_3$, distinguishing coefficient and cocycle
identities from physical tensor realizations.

The multi-block compression theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:asym_multiblock}) explains what these periodic
identities imply for local tensors. It identifies normal target blocks
along an invariant flag of a source that need not be in canonical form
and gives compression maps recovering each target's products for every
word. Periodic traces record contributions from the blocks and their
scalar weights but can miss extensions between them. Compression
exposes those extensions and separates cases admitting sitewise
intertwiners from cases where only word-level compression is available.
Boundary insertions can detect information lost by the ordinary trace.
The examples use exact identities in the number rings appropriate to
their tensors, without restricting all entries to integers or to a
single algebraic extension.
