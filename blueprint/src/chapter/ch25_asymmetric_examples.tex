\chapter{Physics Examples of the Multi-Block Compression Theorem}%
\label{ch:asymmetric_examples}

This chapter works out explicit tensors to which the multi-block
asymmetric compression theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:asym_multiblock}) applies
with a source tensor not assumed to be in canonical form. Each example fixes a target
family of normal blocks, an arbitrary source tensor with the same periodic
data, an explicit change of bond coordinates, and the resulting compression
pairs; it then records the number of zero slots, the dimension count, the
nilpotency order of the remainder, and whether the extension splits into
sitewise intertwiners.

The examples fall into three groups. Two warm-ups isolate the two features
that the classical theory cannot see: inequivalent blocks hidden at
non-adjacent positions, and a repeated block carried by a nontrivial
extension. Four fusion examples treat periodic operator algebras: the
square of an anomalous $\Z_2$ symmetry, its non-simple companion with a
length-dependent structure coefficient, the square of the Kramers--Wannier
duality of the Ising chain, and the Fibonacci fusion
$\tau\otimes\tau=1\oplus\tau$. The remaining examples treat the duality
acting on ground states, a parity-graded pair of weights, and two
renormalization fixed points whose structure coefficients depend on the chain
length.

Every tensor and every change of bond coordinates occurring below is a
fixed scalar multiple of a matrix family with entries in $\Z$, or, for the
Fibonacci example, with entries in the ring $\Z[\sigma]$ generated by
$\sigma=\varphi^{-1/2}$; the scalars are the weights and the normalizations
of the example, and clearing them reduces each verification to a finite
computation with exact arithmetic in that ring, transported to the complex
matrices along an entrywise embedding.
