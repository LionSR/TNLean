\chapter{Physical examples of the multi-block compression theorem}%
\label{ch:asymmetric_examples}

Matrix product states and operators describe periodic spin chains by
contracting local tensors around a ring~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}. At every
positive chain length $N$, an MPS tensor $A$ and an MPO tensor $M$ give
\begin{align}
    \ket{V^{(N)}(A)}
     & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\tr(A^{i_1}\cdots A^{i_N})
    \ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}, \notag                        \\
    O_N(M)
     & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_N}
    \tr(M^{i_1j_1}\cdots M^{i_Nj_N})
    \ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\bra{j_1,\ldots,j_N}.
    \notag
\end{align}
The physical indices $i,j$ range over $\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$, while each
local matrix acts on a virtual bond space of dimension $D$ with rows
and columns indexed by $\{0,\ldots,D-1\}$. Closing the ring by the
ordinary matrix trace without a boundary insertion, and reading the
first MPO physical index as the output and the second as the input so
that operator products act from right to left, the stacked local tensor
$\sum_jM^{ij}\otimes P^{jk}$ generates the product $O_N(M)O_N(P)$.
Whereas MPS vectors need not be normalized, a nonzero positive
periodic MPDO operator yields a density operator upon division by its
trace.

Physical examples of matrix product operators, topological symmetries, and
fixed points illustrate the multi-block asymmetric compression theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:asym_multiblock}). The cases treated here, in chapter
order, are the GHZ and product
states~\cite{GreenbergerHorneZeilinger1989,Greenberger1990Bell} in the
conventions of Ref.~\cite[Appendix~A]{Cirac2021Matrix};
the CZX symmetry of Chen, Liu, and Wen~\cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}, whose
matrix product unitary is displayed in
Ref.~\cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX MPU'']{Cirac2021Matrix}, together with its
decorated form~\cite{GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall}, whose anomaly class is
shown here to be nontrivial;
Kramers--Wannier
duality~\cite{KramersWannier1941,Aasen2016Topological,Seiberg2024Majorana,Lootens2023Dualities};
the Fibonacci~\cite[Appendix~D.1]{Bultinck2017Anyons} and
Ising~\cite[Appendix~D.2]{Bultinck2017Anyons} fusion categories and their
anyon chains~\cite{Feiguin2007GoldenChain}, together with the review's
Fibonacci operator~\cite[Appendix~A, ``The MPO for the Fibonacci model'']{Cirac2021Matrix};
the periodic group matrix product operators built from a normalized
three-cocycle~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym}, evaluated for the group of order
two, for the cyclic groups with the cocycles
of~\cite{GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall}, and for $\Z_2\times\Z_2$;
symmetry tensors for $\Z_2\times\Z_2$ and $\Z_3$ with their condensation
defects, following the anomalous group symmetries of
Refs.~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym,GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall,Roumpedakis2023Condensation}
(the anomaly class is nontrivial for $\Z_3$ and for the full dressed
$\Z_2\times\Z_2$ representation, where the invariant detectors match the
type-II class~\cite{gap:glm23_z2z2_anomaly_detector_scope});
and mixed Bell-bond fixed points addressing the length-dependence question
of Ref.~\cite[Theorem~4.14]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. Building on the general
definitions of Chapter~\ref{ch:mpo_symmetry_basics} (fusion rules, units,
invertible and non-invertible labels, Perron--Frobenius dimensions,
symmetric matrix product states with their nonnegative integer
representations, and the zipper condition), each section below constructs
the explicit local tensors, evaluates their fusion tables, and computes
their periodic compressions and anomaly invariants.
