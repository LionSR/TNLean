\chapter{Physics Examples of the Multi-Block Compression Theorem}%
\label{ch:asymmetric_examples}

Matrix product states and operators describe periodic spin chains by
contracting local tensors around a ring~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}. At every
positive chain length $N$, an MPS tensor $A$ and an MPO tensor $M$ give
\begin{align}
    \ket{V^{(N)}(A)}
     & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\tr(A^{i_1}\cdots A^{i_N})
    \ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}, \notag                        \\
    O_N(M)
     & =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_N}
    \tr(M^{i_1j_1}\cdots M^{i_Nj_N})
    \ket{i_1,\ldots,i_N}\bra{j_1,\ldots,j_N}.
    \notag
\end{align}
The physical indices $i,j$ range over $\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$.
Each local matrix acts on its tensor's virtual bond space, with rows
and columns indexed by $\{0,\ldots,D-1\}$ for bond dimension $D$.
The periodic boundary contraction is the ordinary matrix trace, with
no boundary insertion. The first MPO physical index is the output and
the second is the input; operator products act from right to left.
Thus the local tensor $\sum_jM^{ij}\otimes P^{jk}$ represents
$O_N(M)O_N(P)$. MPS vectors need not be normalized. A nonzero positive
periodic MPDO operator gives a density operator after division by its
trace.

Physical examples of matrix product operators, topological symmetries, and
fixed points illustrate the multi-block asymmetric compression theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:asym_multiblock}). The cases treated here, in chapter
order, are the GHZ and product
states~\cite{GreenbergerHorneZeilinger1989,Greenberger1990Bell} in the
conventions of Ref.~\cite[Appendix~A]{Cirac2021Matrix};
the CZX symmetry of Chen, Liu, and Wen~\cite{ChenLiuWen2011CZX}, whose
matrix product unitary is displayed in
Ref.~\cite[Appendix~A, ``The CZX MPU'']{Cirac2021Matrix};
Kramers--Wannier
duality~\cite{KramersWannier1941,Aasen2016Topological,Seiberg2024Majorana,Lootens2023Dualities};
the Fibonacci~\cite[Appendix~D.1]{Bultinck2017Anyons} and
Ising~\cite[Appendix~D.2]{Bultinck2017Anyons} fusion categories and their
anyon chains~\cite{Feiguin2007GoldenChain}, together with the review's
Fibonacci operator~\cite[Appendix~A, ``The MPO for the Fibonacci model'']{Cirac2021Matrix};
the periodic group matrix product operators built from a normalized
three-cocycle~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym}, evaluated for the group of order
two, for the cyclic groups with the cocycles
of~\cite{GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall}, and for $\Z_2\times\Z_2$;
symmetry tensors for $\Z_2\times\Z_2$ and $\Z_3$ with their condensation
defects, constructed here after the anomalous group symmetries discussed
in Refs.~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym,GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall,Roumpedakis2023Condensation},
whose anomaly classes this chapter determines for $\Z_3$ and for the
diagonal subgroup of order two of $\Z_2\times\Z_2$, where they are
nontrivial;
and mixed Bell-bond fixed points addressing the length-dependence question
of Ref.~\cite[Theorem~4.14]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The general notions used
by the symmetry examples, namely fusion rules, units, invertible and
non-invertible labels, Perron--Frobenius dimensions, symmetric matrix
product states with their nonnegative integer representations, and the
zipper condition, are defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:mpo_symmetry_basics}; this
chapter supplies the tensors, fusion tables, and computations.
