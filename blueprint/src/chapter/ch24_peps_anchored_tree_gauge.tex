\begin{lemma}[A tree gauge fixed on an untwisted component]
    \label{lem:peps_anchored_tree_gauge}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.exists_regularRegionTreeGauge_fixed_on_untwisted_component}\leanok
    In a connected finite region, let a subgraph have identity directed
    bond transport and fix a root. There is a spanning tree and a vertex
    gauge equal to the identity throughout the root component of that
    subgraph. The transformed operator on every internal tree edge is
    the identity. The group may be nonabelian.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    Take a spanning forest of the untwisted subgraph, preserving all its
    reachable pairs, and extend this forest to a spanning tree of the
    full region graph. Normalize the tree gauge at the root. On a forest
    edge its endpoint gradient is the identity operator, so the endpoint
    gauges coincide. Propagation along forest walks fixes the entire
    root component. The defining tree gradient removes all tree operators.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A complementary gauge fixing incident operators]
    \label{lem:peps_anchored_complement_gauge}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.exists_regularComplementGauge_fixed_on_incident}\leanok
    Suppose the complement is connected, the original operators on all
    bonds incident to the observation region are the identity, and every
    complementary boundary endpoint belongs to one rooted untwisted
    component. Then a global vertex gauge fixes the observation region,
    preserves identity on every incident bond, and removes all operators
    on a complementary spanning tree. Local regular invariance makes
    the actual closed physical vector unchanged by this gauge.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{lem:peps_anchored_tree_gauge}
    Apply the preceding construction in the complement and extend its
    gauge by the identity over the observation region. The boundary
    component condition fixes both endpoints of every crossing bond.
    The native tree residual gives the complementary assertion, and
    local invariance with a bijective change of bond labels preserves
    the closed contraction.
\end{proof}
