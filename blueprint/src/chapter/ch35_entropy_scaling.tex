\chapter{Entropy scaling and approximation by matrix product states}
\label{ch:entropy_scaling}

Schuch, Wolf, Verstraete and Cirac~\cite{Schuch2008EntropyScaling} relate
the entropies of the regions of a chain to the bond dimension needed to
approximate its state by a matrix product state. If the R\'enyi entropies of
some order below one grow at most logarithmically in the length, the states
can be approximated with polynomial bond dimension; conversely, efficiently
approximable states have R\'enyi entropies of every order above one that grow
at most logarithmically. A bound on the von Neumann entropy alone does not
suffice: a family of states on qutrit chains has entropy at most $4\log2$
on every interval, yet admits no approximation with polynomial bond
dimension. This chapter formalizes these statements.
