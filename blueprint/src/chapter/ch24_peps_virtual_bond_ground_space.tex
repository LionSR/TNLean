\section{The virtual Bell-bond ground space}%
\label{sec:peps_virtual_bond_ground_space}

The virtual part of the injective-parent uniqueness argument in
\cite[Section~IV.C.1]{Cirac2021Matrix}, local source lines 2017--2044,
concerns a product of independent maximally entangled bonds.
We first establish this part without physical maps.

\begin{definition}[Bell-bond slice conditions]%
    \label{def:peps_virtual_bond_ground_space}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.VirtualBondConfig,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondBell,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondProduct,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondSlice,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondGroundSpace,
        TNLean.PEPS.mem_virtualBondGroundSpace_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondDiagonalConfig}
    \leanok
    Let $E$ be a finite set of bonds with dimensions $D_e\ge0$.
    Write $\zeta_e=(a_e,b_e)$ for their endpoint labels and put
    \[
        \omega_e(a_e,b_e)=\delta_{a_e,b_e},\qquad
        \Omega(\zeta)=\prod_{e\in E}\omega_e(\zeta_e).
    \]
    The common virtual ground space consists of the vectors whose
    slice on each bond, for every fixed configuration of all
    other bonds, belongs to $\operatorname{span}\{\omega_e\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Independent virtual-bond uniqueness]%
    \label{thm:peps_virtual_bond_ground_space}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondProduct_update,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondProduct_mem_virtualBondGroundSpace,
        TNLean.PEPS.eq_zero_of_mem_virtualBondGroundSpace_off_diagonal,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondGroundSpace_diagonal_update_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondGroundSpace_diagonal_eq,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondGroundSpace_eq_span,
        TNLean.PEPS.virtualBondProduct_ne_zero_iff,
        TNLean.PEPS.finrank_virtualBondGroundSpace_eq_one}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:peps_virtual_bond_ground_space}
    The common virtual ground space is
    $\operatorname{span}\{\Omega\}$.
    The vector $\Omega$ is nonzero exactly when every $D_e$ is
    positive; in this case the common ground space is one-dimensional.
    If a bond dimension vanishes, the entire virtual configuration
    space is empty and the vector space is zero.
    The assertion also includes an empty bond collection, whose
    product vector is the scalar vacuum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
    The product vector satisfies each slice condition by separating
    its corresponding Bell factor. Conversely, the slice condition
    forces a coefficient to vanish whenever the two labels of some
    bond differ. On diagonal configurations, replacing one common
    endpoint label by another leaves the coefficient unchanged.
    Finitely many such replacements join any two diagonal configurations.
    Thus every common ground vector is a scalar multiple of $\Omega$.
    Its coefficient at any diagonal configuration is one, proving
    nonvanishing when all dimensions are positive. A zero-dimensional
    bond makes the configuration space empty, giving the stated
    degenerate case.
\end{proof}
