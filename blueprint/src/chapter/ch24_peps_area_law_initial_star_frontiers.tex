\section{Initial boundaries near a mark}
\label{sec:area_law_initial_star_frontiers}

Fix an origin, a finite endpoint set, and valid pitch residues
for the initial region construction, with $C\ge2$ and
$k_0\ge50{,}000{,}000$. The theorem below establishes the local boundary
direction constraint from the mesh argument of \cite[Section~11]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.

\begin{lemma}[Nearby initial boundary points lie on allowed lines]
    \label{thm:area_law_initial_frontier_near_mark}
    \lean{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.initialBirthRegion_frontier_near_mark_allowed}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:area_law_initial_birth_regions,def:area_law_belt_cell_marks,
        def:area_law_fine_layer_indices}
    Let $k\ge k_0$, let $z$ be a fine-cell index in layer $k$,
    and let $v$ be one of its nine marks. Set $t=2^{\ell_k}$.
    For any initial region identifier $i$ and any
    $x\in\partial X_i^0$ satisfying $\|x-v\|_\infty<t/32$, the vector
    $x-v$ has one of the four allowed slopes, so $x$ lies on one
    of the four allowed lines through $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:area_law_primary_fine_cell_cover,def:area_law_fan_runs,
        thm:area_law_belt_marks_quarter_mesh,thm:area_law_square_frontier_side,
        thm:area_law_fan_frontier_primitives,
        thm:area_law_near_mark_quarter_mesh,
        thm:area_law_dummy_fine_scale_separation,
        thm:area_law_affine_mesh_line_clearance}
    The boundary of a finite union is contained in the union of the boundaries
    of its pieces. For a primary region, $x$ lies on a side of one of its
    closed fine cells; for a fan run, $x$ lies on an outer square side or a
    radial segment of one of its triangles. In either case, $x$ lies on a
    segment whose endpoints are cell marks and whose supporting line has one of
    the four allowed slopes.

    Since $\|x-v\|_\infty<t/32$, the closed fine cell containing that segment
    meets the $(t/32)$-ball around $v$, so the nearby-cell mesh lemma places
    both segment endpoints and $v$ on the quarter mesh of scale $t/4$. Any line
    of allowed slope through two points of this quarter mesh that misses $v$
    has sup-norm distance at least $t/8$ from $v$, contradicting
    $\|x-v\|_\infty<t/32$. Thus $v$ lies on the segment's supporting line.

    For the dummy region $\overline{N_{k_0}}$, separation by $16$ fine-cell
    sides rules out $k>k_0$, so $k=k_0$. Then $x$ lies on a side of a
    constituent coarse cell of scale $2^{k_0}$, whose endpoints also lie on the
    quarter mesh since $\ell_{k_0}\le k_0$, and the same line-clearance
    bound applies.
\end{proof}
