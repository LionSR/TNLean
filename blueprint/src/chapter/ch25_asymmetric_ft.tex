\chapter{The Multi-Block Asymmetric Fundamental Theorem}%
\label{ch:asymmetric_ft}

The fundamental theorem of matrix product states classically compares two
tensors that have both been placed in a normal form, whether canonical
\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}, irreducible \cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible},
normal \cite{Molnar2018NormalPEPS}, or semi-injective
\cite{Molnar2018SemiInjective}. Here we consider the asymmetric comparison in which one tensor is a
weighted direct sum of normal tensors and the other is arbitrary. This situation occurs naturally for stacked matrix product
operators: even when two normal tensors are stacked into a product, the
result need not be in any normal form, while its generated periodic
operators still determine the traces of every word.

The multi-block compression theorem proved here is an extension of the
single-block reduction result discussed below.
Equality of all positive-length periodic vectors determines every nonzero
simple composition factor of the arbitrary tensor, including its
multiplicity; the only undetectable factor is the one-dimensional tensor on
which every physical matrix vanishes. The remaining freedom is an
upper-triangular, globally nilpotent extension between the factors, which is
why periodic equality alone does not imply a sitewise intertwiner
(Remark~\ref{rem:asym_zipper}). Specializing to a single normal block
recovers, with three repairs, the reduction theorem of
Molnár--Ge--Schuch--Cirac (\S\ref{sec:asym_single_block}).

The argument runs through the free unital associative algebra
$F=\C\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\rangle$ on the physical alphabet, realized so
that a tensor becomes an $F$-module (\S\ref{sec:asym_word_algebra}). A
normal tensor's module is simple (Lemma~\ref{lem:asym_normal_simple}), and a
finite-dimensional module whose word traces match those of a family of
simple blocks embeds a copy of one block as a subquotient
(\S\ref{sec:asym_separation}); iterating this identification along a
composition series produces a labelled invariant flag
(\S\ref{sec:asym_flag}), which a block-triangular coordinate change turns
into the compression statement (\S\ref{sec:asym_main}).
