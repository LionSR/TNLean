\section{Mixed Bell bonds and renormalization fixed points}%
\label{sec:asymex_physical_models}

A renormalization fixed point of a matrix product density operator is a
tensor whose one-site and two-site closures with an arbitrary boundary
matrix are carried into each other by trace-preserving completely
positive maps, in both
directions~\cite[Definition~4.1, Example~4.12, Proposition~4.13,
    Theorem~4.14]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The characterization there expresses
the products of the vertical components of such a tensor through
structure coefficients, and the question left open after its
Theorem~4.14 is whether these coefficients can depend on the length of
the chain. The mixed Bell-bond tensor, the flagged tensor, the one-label
tensor, and the dimer twist below address that question. They are not taken
from the literature; only the flag factor of the flagged tensor is, namely the
normalized tensor of \cite[Example~4.12]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The first two are mixed states built
from Bell bonds: in the first, incoming bonds carry
the mixed state $\beta_R=(25/32)P_++(7/32)P_-$; in the second, each site also
carries a flag qubit $\ket{f}$ ($f\in\{0,1\}$), with a controlled flip coupling
the Bell sectors to an even-parity flag state. The vertical components
of these two tensors yield the one-label tensor and the dimer twist, whose
structure coefficients are computed explicitly by the multi-block compression
theorem. These results establish tensor decompositions and periodic trace
identities for the examples, not a classification of length-dependent fixed
points.

Write $E_{ij}=\ketbra{i}{j}$ and
$\ket{\Phi_\pm}=(\ket{00}\pm\ket{11})/\sqrt2$, with
$P_\pm=\ketbra{\Phi_\pm}{\Phi_\pm}$.
For an MPO tensor $M^{ij}\in\MN{D}$, its periodic physical operator is
\begin{align}
    \rho^{(N)}(M)
     & =\sum_{\boldsymbol i,\boldsymbol j}
    \tr(M^{i_0j_0}\cdots M^{i_{N-1}j_{N-1}})
    \ketbra{\boldsymbol i}{\boldsymbol j}.
    \label{eq:asymex_physical_closure}
\end{align}
An MPDO requires positivity for every $N>0$; trace-one normalization is a
separate condition. Throughout the physical state formulas below, $N>0$
and site indices are cyclic.

\subsection{Independent mixed Bell bonds}

The local physical space is
$\mathcal H_R=\C^2_L\otimes\C^2_R$, with basis index $2l+r$.
On the incoming bond $(R_{n-1},L_n)$, put
$\beta_R=(25/32)P_++(7/32)P_-$.
Let $Q_N$ regroup sites into incoming bonds:
\begin{align}
    Q_N\bigotimes_{n=0}^{N-1}\ket{l_n,r_n}
     & =\bigotimes_{n=0}^{N-1}\ket{r_{n-1},l_n}.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_regroup}
\end{align}
Thus $Q_N^\dagger\beta_R^{\otimes N}Q_N$ is a normalized density operator
of rank $2^N$.

An MPO representation uses four real matrices:
\begin{align}
    A^0 & =\tfrac45E_{00}, \nonumber \\
    A^1 & =\tfrac45E_{11}, \nonumber \\
    A^2 & =\tfrac35E_{01}, \nonumber \\
    A^3 & =\tfrac35E_{10}.
    \label{eq:asymex_one_label_matrices}
\end{align}
The auxiliary indices $a,b\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ label these matrices; their
ordering is not the physical pair ordering $2l+r$. Define
\begin{align}
    R^{(l,r),(l',r')}_{ab}
     & =\frac{25}{32}A^a_{ll'}A^b_{rr'}.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_tensor}
\end{align}
To contract this tensor, introduce
$w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}16/25&9/25\\9/25&16/25\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The matrix-unit entries give
\begin{align}
    \sum_a A^a_{uv}A^a_{xy} & =\delta_{ux}\delta_{vy}w_{uv}.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_contraction}
\end{align}
For physical strings $p_n=(l_n,r_n)$ and $q_n=(l'_n,r'_n)$, the auxiliary
trace pairs the right half of site $n-1$ with the left half of site $n$.
Consequently,
\begin{align}
    \rho^{(N)}(R)_{p,q}
     & =\left(\frac{25}{32}\right)^N
    \prod_{n=0}^{N-1}
    \delta_{r_{n-1},l_n}\delta_{r'_{n-1},l'_n}w_{l_nl'_n}.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_entries}
\end{align}
This also gives a support-isometry description. Define
$B_N\ket{a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1}}=\bigotimes_n\ket{a_n,a_{n+1}}$.
Distinct bit strings have distinct images, so $B_N^\dagger B_N=\Id$.
Set $h=(25/32)w=
    \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&9/32\\9/32&1/2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Formula~(\ref{eq:asymex_R_entries}) becomes
\begin{align}
    \rho^{(N)}(R) & =B_Nh^{\otimes N}B_N^\dagger.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_isometry}
\end{align}
The eigenvectors of $h$ are $(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$, with eigenvalues $25/32$
and $7/32$. For $J\ket a=\ket{a,a}$, one has
$JhJ^\dagger=\beta_R$ and $Q_NB_N=J^{\otimes N}$. Hence
\begin{align}
    Q_N\rho^{(N)}(R)Q_N^\dagger & =\beta_R^{\otimes N}.
    \label{eq:asymex_R_density}
\end{align}
This proves positivity, trace one, and rank $2^N$ directly. At $N=1$ the
bond closes within the site, giving $\rho^{(1)}(R)=\beta_R$. At $N\ge2$
the two qubits at a site belong to distinct independent bonds. Since both
one-qubit marginals of $\beta_R$ are $\Id_2/2$, the one-site marginal is
$\Id_4/4$.

\subsection{Mixed Bell bonds with an even-parity flag}

The second model has local space
$\mathcal H_T=\C^2_L\otimes\C^2_R\otimes\C^2_F$, with basis index
$4l+2r+f$. Put $x=7/8$, $y=1/8$, and define the bond state
$\sigma=xP_++yP_-$. The flag state is the classical even-parity mixture
\begin{align}
    \eta_N
     & =2^{-N}(\Id_2^{\otimes N}+Z^{\otimes N})
    =2^{1-N}\sum_{\substack{\boldsymbol f\in\{0,1\}^N \\
            \sum_n f_n\equiv0\pmod2}}
    \ketbra{\boldsymbol f}{\boldsymbol f},
    \label{eq:asymex_even_flags}
\end{align}
where $Z=E_{00}-E_{11}$. There are $2^{N-1}$ even strings, so
$\eta_N$ is positive and has trace one. For $N\ge2$ its rank is
$2^{N-1}$; it is not a pure GHZ state. At $N=1$ it is $\ketbra{0}{0}$.

A cell consists of the incoming bond and its receiving flag. With
$X_F=E_{01}+E_{10}$ on the flag, define
\begin{align}
    V & =(\Id_4-P_-)\otimes\Id_2+P_-\otimes X_F.
    \label{eq:asymex_flag_gate}
\end{align}
This gate fixes the flag outside the minus Bell sector and flips it
inside that sector. Orthogonality of $P_-$ and $\Id_4-P_-$ gives
$V^\dagger=V$ and $V^2=\Id_8$, including on the complement of the two
Bell vectors. Let $\widetilde Q_N$ regroup sites into incoming cells by
$\bigotimes_n\ket{l_n,r_n,f_n}\mapsto
    \bigotimes_n\ket{(r_{n-1},l_n),f_n}$.
The physical state is obtained by applying $V$ on each cell to
$\sigma^{\otimes N}\otimes\eta_N$. Here and below, the separated bond
and flag factors are identified with cell coordinates by the canonical
permutation of tensor factors.

For its MPO representation, set $\tau_k(f)=(-1)^{kf}$ and
$C_k(u,v)=\tfrac12(x+(-1)^{k+u+v}y)$. Explicitly,
\begin{align}
    C_0 & =\begin{pmatrix}1/2&3/8\\3/8&1/2\end{pmatrix}, \nonumber \\
    C_1 & =\begin{pmatrix}3/8&1/2\\1/2&3/8\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:asymex_dimer_C}
\end{align}
An auxiliary index is $(p,p',k)$, encoded as $4p+2p'+k$. Define
\begin{align}
    T^{(l,r,f),(l',r',g)}_{(p,p',k),(q,q',k')}
     & =\delta_{kk'}\delta_{fg}\delta_{lp}\delta_{l'p'}
    \delta_{rq}\delta_{r'q'} \frac12C_k(p,p')\tau_k(f).
    \label{eq:asymex_T_tensor}
\end{align}
Both the physical and auxiliary dimensions are eight.

\begin{theorem}[Closed density of the twisted dimer]
    \label{thm:asymex_T_density}
    \leanok
    For $N>0$, write $W_N=V^{\otimes N}$ in incoming-cell coordinates.
    Then
    \begin{align}
        \widetilde Q_N\rho^{(N)}(T)\widetilde Q_N^\dagger
         & =W_N(\sigma^{\otimes N}\otimes\eta_N)W_N^\dagger.
        \label{eq:asymex_T_density}
    \end{align}
    In particular, $\rho^{(N)}(T)$ is positive and has trace one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:mpdo_twisted_dimer_unitary_factorization,
        lem:mpdo_twisted_dimer_controlled_flip,
        thm:mpdo_twisted_dimer_factor_normalization,
        thm:mpdo_twisted_dimer_flag_factor}
    Put $\sigma'=xP_+-yP_-$. The entries of
    $xP_++(-1)^kyP_-$ on the matched bond subspace are $C_k(u,v)$.
    In a positive-length auxiliary trace of~(\ref{eq:asymex_T_tensor}),
    the label $k$ is constant around the ring. The remaining constraints
    require matching neighboring half-bonds and equal ket and bra flags.
    Therefore
    \begin{align}
        \widetilde Q_N\rho^{(N)}(T)\widetilde Q_N^\dagger
         & =2^{-N}\bigl[(\sigma\otimes\Id_2)^{\otimes N}
            +(\sigma'\otimes Z)^{\otimes N}\bigr].
        \label{eq:asymex_T_contraction}
    \end{align}
    The gate fixes $\sigma\otimes\Id_2$ under conjugation and sends
    $\sigma\otimes Z$ to $\sigma'\otimes Z$, since $X_FZX_F=-Z$.
    Applying these identities in each cell proves~(\ref{eq:asymex_T_density}).
    Positivity follows from the positive factor states; their traces are
    $\tr\sigma=x+y=1$ and $\tr\eta_N=1$. Unitary conjugation preserves trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The flag factor as a normalized fixed-point state]
    \label{thm:asymex_flag_rfp}
    \leanok
    The flag family is represented by the density-normalized tensor
    $\widehat M^{fg}=\tfrac12\delta_{fg}\diag(1,(-1)^f)$ of
    Example~4.12 of Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}.
    This tensor is an MPDO and satisfies the channel fixed-point
    equations defined below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:mpdo_twisted_dimer_flag_factor}
    The two diagonal auxiliary sectors contribute
    $2^{-N}\Id_2^{\otimes N}$ and $2^{-N}Z^{\otimes N}$, respectively.
    Their sum is~(\ref{eq:asymex_even_flags}). The channel fixed-point
    property is that of the normalized example. The normalization
    includes a factor $1/2$ in every local tensor, absent from the
    unnormalized tensor printed in the example.
\end{proof}

The factorization~(\ref{eq:asymex_T_density}) is an identity of closed
operators, not an on-site tensor equivalence or an auxiliary gauge
equivalence. The positive-length restriction matters for both models:
at length zero the auxiliary trace gives $\rho^{(0)}(R)=4$ and
$\rho^{(0)}(T)=8$, whereas the corresponding empty factor expressions
give $1$ and $2$.

\subsection{Trace-preserving fixed-point maps}

For a tensor $M$ and an auxiliary boundary matrix $X$, define the
one-site and two-site physical closures by
\begin{align}
    M_1(X) & =\sum_{i,j}\tr(M^{ij}X)E_{ij}, \nonumber               \\
    M_2(X) & =\sum_{i,j,k,l}\tr(M^{ij}M^{kl}X)E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}.
    \label{eq:asymex_boundary_closures}
\end{align}
The channel fixed-point condition of
\cite[Definition~4.1]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv} requires trace-preserving
completely positive maps, abbreviated tpCPMs, $\mathcal S$ and
$\mathcal T$ such that, for every $X$,
\begin{align}
    \mathcal S(M_2(X)) & =M_1(X), \nonumber \\
    \mathcal T(M_1(X)) & =M_2(X).
    \label{eq:asymex_channel_equations}
\end{align}
The symbol $T$ denotes the physical dimer tensor, whereas $\mathcal T$
denotes a refinement channel. Closed density factorizations alone do
not establish these equations for arbitrary boundary matrices.

\begin{theorem}[Channel fixed points of the two physical tensors]
    \label{thm:asymex_physical_rfp}
    \leanok
    Both $R$ and $T$ satisfy~(\ref{eq:asymex_channel_equations}) with tpCPMs
    on the full physical matrix spaces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:mpdo_bnt_label_rescaling_stable_is_rfp_via_ts,
        thm:mpdo_twisted_dimer_is_rfp_via_ts}
    For $R$, set $h=(25/32)w$. Its spectral probabilities are
    $p_0=25/32$ and $p_1=7/32$. The refinement Kraus operators act by
    \begin{align}
        K_s\ket{l,r}
         & =\sqrt{\frac{p_s}{2}}\sum_{b=0}^1(-1)^{sb}
        \ket{l,b}\otimes\ket{b,r}.
        \label{eq:asymex_R_refine}
    \end{align}
    Thus $\sum_sK_s^\dagger K_s=\Id_4$. Direct contraction of
    (\ref{eq:asymex_R_tensor}) gives, for every boundary matrix $X$,
    \begin{align}
        R_2(X)_{(l,b),(c,r);(l',b'),(c',r')}
         & =\delta_{bc}\delta_{b'c'}h_{bb'}R_1(X)_{(l,r),(l',r')}.
        \label{eq:asymex_R_boundary}
    \end{align}
    The spectral identity
    $\sum_s(p_s/2)(-1)^{s(b+b')}=h_{bb'}$ proves refinement.
    Coarse-graining contracts the inner bond against the four
    orthonormal vectors $\Phi_+,\Phi_-,01,10$, retaining the outer bits.
    The four Kraus operators resolve the identity on the full two-site
    space. The last two annihilate~(\ref{eq:asymex_R_boundary}); summing
    the first two returns $R_1(X)$ because $\tr h=1$.

    For $T$, coarse-graining reads the flags $f_1,f_2$ and measures the
    inner bond in the basis $\Phi_+,\Phi_-,01,10$. It retains the outer
    qubits and outputs $f_1+f_2+s$ modulo two, where $s=1$ only for
    outcome $\Phi_-$. Completeness of the bond basis gives the Kraus
    resolution. On the matched support, the two Bell outcomes have
    weights $x$ and $(-1)^ky$ in block $k$. Their flag shifts therefore
    yield the required factor $\tau_k(f_1+f_2+s)$, and $x+y=1$ gives
    the coarse-graining equation.

    Refinement inserts $\Phi_+$ or $\Phi_-$ with probabilities $x,y$.
    For outcome $s$, it splits an input flag $f$ uniformly among the
    two pairs satisfying $f_1+f_2+s=f$. Its boundary equation follows
    from
    \begin{align}
        xP_++(-1)^kyP_- & =JC_kJ^\dagger.
        \label{eq:asymex_T_refine_identity}
    \end{align}
    Each inserted Bell vector is normalized, and the two flag choices
    each have half the outcome probability, so refinement is trace
    preserving. These are the coarse-graining and refinement
    constructions for the respective tensors.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A necessary physical-trace identity]
    \label{thm:asymex_trace_obstruction}
    \leanok
    If $M$ satisfies~(\ref{eq:asymex_channel_equations}), then
    $F_M=\sum_iM^{ii}$ satisfies $F_M^2=F_M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:rfp_via_ts_physical_trace_idempotent}
    Trace preservation of $\mathcal T$ gives
    $\tr(F_M^2X)=\tr M_2(X)=\tr M_1(X)=\tr(F_MX)$ for every $X$.
    Nondegeneracy of the matrix trace pairing gives $F_M^2=F_M$.
\end{proof}

This is a statement about the physical-trace transfer matrix $F_M$,
not the doubled-index completely positive transfer map.
For $R$, the identity also follows directly from
$F_R=(25/32)tt^{\mathsf T}$, where
$t=(4/5,4/5,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and $t^{\mathsf T}t=32/25$.

\subsection{Vertical components and their multiplication}%
\label{sec:asymex_vertical_dictionary}

Rotation exchanges the physical and auxiliary pairs:
$\mathcal V(M)^{ab}_{ij}=M^{ij}_{ab}$.
For a vertical component $K$, its periodic operator at length $L>0$ is
\begin{align}
    O_L(K)
     & =\sum_{\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b}
    \tr(K^{a_0b_0}\cdots K^{a_{L-1}b_{L-1}})
    \ketbra{\boldsymbol a}{\boldsymbol b}.
    \label{eq:asymex_vertical_closure}
\end{align}
It acts on strings of the original auxiliary indices, rather than on
the original physical chain.

For $R$, the normalized vertical component is
$K^{ab}=A^a\otimes A^b$, so
$\mathcal V(R)=(25/32)K$. This rotation and scalar factor are essential:
$K$ is not the physical tensor $R$. If its indices $a,b$ are instead
treated as physical indices, then
\begin{align}
    F_K & =\sum_aA^a\otimes A^a
    =\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}
                   16 & 0 & 0 & 9 \\0&0&0&0\\0&0&0&0\\9&0&0&16
               \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:asymex_K_transfer}
\end{align}
Its $(00,00)$ entry satisfies
$(F_K^2-F_K)_{00,00}=(337-400)/625=-63/625$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymex_trace_obstruction} therefore excludes the
channel fixed-point property for $K$ in this physical reading.

Writing $\ket{\psi_L}=\sum_{\boldsymbol a}
    \tr(A^{a_0}\cdots A^{a_{L-1}})\ket{\boldsymbol a}$ gives
$O_L(K)=\ketbra{\psi_L}{\psi_L}$.
A nonzero cyclic product of the matrix-unit letters specifies a cyclic
bit path, and its squared amplitude is the product of the corresponding
entries of $w$. Thus $\braket{\psi_L}{\psi_L}=\tr(w^L)$, and
\begin{align}
    O_L(K)^2 & =\tr(w^L)O_L(K)
    =\left(1+\left(\frac7{25}\right)^L\right)O_L(K).
    \label{eq:asymex_K_product}
\end{align}
The eigenvalue $7/25$ controls vertical multiplication; it is not the
physical Bell probability $7/32$.

For $T$, restrict the physical ket and bra flags to the same value $f$.
The unnormalized vertical component $D_f$ is defined by
\begin{align}
    (D_f^{ab})_{(l,r),(l',r')}
     & =T^{(l,r,f),(l',r',f)}_{ab}.
    \label{eq:asymex_dimer_dictionary}
\end{align}
It satisfies $D_f=\nu\widehat K_f$, where $\widehat K_f$ is the
normalized flag component, $\nu=5/8$, and
$\nu^2=(x^2+y^2)/2=25/64$.
For $a=(p,p',k)$ and $b=(q,q',k')$, its normalized letter is
\begin{align}
    \widehat K_f^{ab} & =\frac{\delta_{kk'}C_k(p,p')\tau_k(f)}{2\nu}
    (E_{pp'}\otimes E_{qq'}).
    \notag
\end{align}
The nonzero coefficient magnitudes of $D_f$ are $1/4,3/16$,
whereas those of $\widehat K_f$ are $2/5,3/10$.
Neither component is the physical tensor $T$.

For an MPDO in CPSV canonical form, the fixed-point characterization
requires a vertical canonical decomposition with a basis of normal tensors
$K_\alpha$, positive diagonal multiplicity matrices $\mu_\alpha$, and
positive diagonal matrices $\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}$.
Set $m_\alpha=\tr\mu_\alpha$ and
$c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(L)}=\tr(\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^L)$.
The required multiplication identities, for every $L>0$, are
\begin{align}
    O_L(K_\alpha)O_L(K_\beta)
     & =\sum_\gamma c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(L)}O_L(K_\gamma).
    \label{eq:asymex_vertical_product}
\end{align}
The vector $m$ is an idempotent for the multiplication induced by
$c^{(1)}$:
\begin{align}
    m_\gamma
     & =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(1)}m_\alpha m_\beta.
    \label{eq:asymex_weight_idempotent}
\end{align}
For $R$, $\mu_0=(25/32)$ and $\chi_{000}=\diag(1,7/25)$;
indeed $(32/25)(25/32)^2=25/32$.
For the normalized dimer components, $m_0=m_1=5/8$.
The fusion calculation below gives unnormalized weights $7/16,1/16$,
which become $7/10,1/10$ after division by $\nu$. Each output sector
receives two contributions of each kind
in~(\ref{eq:asymex_weight_idempotent}), giving
$2(7/10+1/10)(5/8)^2=5/8$.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed points and vertical multiplication]
    \label{thm:asymex_rfp_equivalence}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:mpdo_cpsv_bnt_rfp_equivalence}
    Let $M$ generate MPDOs and suppose its doubled-physical-index tensor
    is in CPSV canonical form (CF), with normalized normal tensor blocks.
    The channel equations~(\ref{eq:asymex_channel_equations}) are equivalent
    to the existence of a vertical canonical decomposition of $M$ whose
    basis of normal tensors (BNT) satisfies
    (\ref{eq:asymex_vertical_product}) and
    (\ref{eq:asymex_weight_idempotent}) with positive diagonal $\chi$.
    They are also equivalent to the existence of active-sector fusion
    coisometries with exact reconstruction and the same idempotent relation,
    as specified in Theorem~\ref{thm:mpdo_cpsv_bnt_rfp_equivalence}.
    The first equivalence is Ref.~\cite[Theorem~4.14(i)--(ii)]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv};
    the second is the corrected active-support form of its fusion equivalence.
    The zero fusion multiplicity of absent product sectors, the empty active
    support of a fixed pair, and the coisometry orientation of the fusion map
    are recorded in \cite{gap:cpsv16_bnt_uniqueness_zero_coefficient},
    \cite{gap:cpsv16_figure11_per_pair_support}, and
    \cite{gap:cpsv16_figure11_fusion_coisometry}. The comparison of one-site
    and two-site prefixes and the completion of the physical maps on
    discarded complements are recorded in
    \cite{gap:cpsv16_two_site_sector_unitary_gauge_gap} and
    \cite{gap:cpgsv17_vertical_isometry_zero_sector}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{thm:mpdo_cpsv_bnt_rfp_equivalence}
    The fixed-point channels give an active-sector fusion decomposition,
    which implies the operator product identities and the idempotent relation.
    Conversely, these relations for the vertical components of $M$ yield
    coarse-graining and refinement channels by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:mpdo_cpsv_bnt_rfp_equivalence}.
    The comparison uses both one-site and two-site prefixes, and the
    physical maps are completed on discarded complements. These implications
    also prove the equivalence with the active-sector fusion description.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Representative rescaling}%
\label{sec:asymex_rescaling}

Scalar rescalings of the normal tensors in a basis change the structure
coefficients by geometric factors, so the length-dependence question
must be posed modulo this freedom; the entries below record the
rescaling rule for the structure coefficients of
\cite[Theorem~4.14]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}.

Consider the structure coefficients
$c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(L)}=\tr(\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{L})$
and trace scalars $m_\alpha$ of
\cite[Theorem~4.14(ii)]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}.
\begin{definition}[Rescaling the structure coefficients]
    \label{def:asymex_rescaling}
    \lean{MPOTensor.DiagonalChiFamily.rescale,
        MPOTensor.DiagonalChiFamily.rescale_dim,
        MPOTensor.DiagonalChiFamily.rescale_entry,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelCoefficientFamily.rescale,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelCoefficientFamily.rescale_coeff,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelTraceScalarFamily.rescale,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelTraceScalarFamily.rescale_traceScalar}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:mpdo_diagonal_chi_family,
        def:mpdo_bnt_label_coefficient_family,
        def:mpdo_bnt_label_trace_scalar_family}
    Let $\Lambda$ be a label set and let $s_\alpha\in\C$
    for every $\alpha\in\Lambda$. In the algebraic formulas below,
    division is extended by $z/0=0$. Their interpretation as changes
    of tensor representatives requires all scales to be nonzero.
    Rescale the diagonal entries, coefficients, and trace scalars by
    \begin{align}
        \chi'_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,k}
                  & =\frac{s_\alpha s_\beta}{s_\gamma}
        \chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,k},
        \label{eq:asymex_chi_rescale}                                 \\
        c'{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(L)}
                  & =\left(\frac{s_\alpha s_\beta}{s_\gamma}\right)^L
        c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(L)},
        \label{eq:asymex_c_rescale}                                   \\
        m'_\alpha & =\frac{m_\alpha}{s_\alpha}.
        \label{eq:asymex_m_rescale}
    \end{align}
    The multiplicity $r_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}$ is unchanged, and
    $0\leq k<r_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trace powers and positivity under rescaling]
    \label{thm:asymex_rescale_trace}
    \lean{MPOTensor.DiagonalChiFamily.tracePowerCoeff_rescale,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelCoefficientFamily.ofChi_rescale,
        MPOTensor.DiagonalChiFamily.PosEntries.rescale}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:asymex_rescaling}
    Taking trace-power coefficients commutes with rescaling: for every
    $L\in\N$, including $L=0$,
    \begin{align}
        \tr((\chi'_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})^L)
         & =
        \left(\frac{s_\alpha s_\beta}{s_\gamma}\right)^L
        \tr(\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^L).
    \end{align}
    If the original diagonal entries are positive real numbers and
    every $s_\alpha$ is positive real, the rescaled entries are positive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:asymex_rescaling}
    Expand the trace as the sum of the $L$th powers of the diagonal
    entries. Each summand acquires the factor
    $(s_\alpha s_\beta/s_\gamma)^L$, which factors out of the finite
    sum. For positive real scales, the ratio
    $s_\alpha s_\beta/s_\gamma$ is positive, so multiplication by it
    preserves positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Preservation of the idempotent coefficient equation]
    \label{thm:asymex_rescale_idempotent}
    \lean{MPOTensor.BNTLabelTraceScalarFamily.hasIdempotentCoefficientForm_rescale_iff,
        MPOTensor.BNTLabelTraceScalarFamily.rescale_coefficient_sum}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:asymex_rescaling,
        def:mpdo_bnt_label_idempotent_coefficient_form}
    Suppose $\Lambda$ is finite and every $s_\alpha$ is nonzero.
    The vector $m'$ is idempotent for $c'{}^{(1)}$ if and only if
    $m$ is idempotent for $c^{(1)}$. Explicitly, the equations
    \begin{align}
        m'_\gamma
         & =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
        c'{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(1)}m'_\alpha m'_\beta,
        \\
        m_\gamma
         & =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
        c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(1)}m_\alpha m_\beta
    \end{align}
    are equivalent when imposed for every $\gamma\in\Lambda$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:asymex_rescaling,
        def:mpdo_bnt_label_idempotent_coefficient_form}
    Substituting~(\ref{eq:asymex_c_rescale})
    and~(\ref{eq:asymex_m_rescale}) and cancelling the nonzero
    scales in each summand gives
    \begin{align}
        \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
        c'{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(1)}m'_\alpha m'_\beta
         & =
        \frac{1}{s_\gamma}
        \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
        c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{(1)}m_\alpha m_\beta.
    \end{align}
    The left side of the idempotent equation is
    $m'_\gamma=m_\gamma/s_\gamma$. Cancellation of $s_\gamma$
    proves both implications.
\end{proof}

Scaling a normal-tensor representative by a positive factor $c \ne 1$
alters the transfer map's spectral radius to $c^2$, though the rescaled
idempotent coefficient equations remain preserved.

\subsection{The one-label fixed point}%
\label{sec:asymex_one_label}

supporting results the proofs invoke,
Theorems~\ref{thm:asymex_stacked_pair} and~\ref{thm:asymex_normal_units}.

The vertical component $K$ of $R$ has letters

with eigenvectors $\Phi_+,\Phi_-,01,10$.

target, not two inequivalent targets. In this subsection write $M=K$;

\subsection{The graded quantum-dimer twist}%
\label{sec:asymex_dimer}

For the dimer, write $M_f=D_f$ for the unnormalized vertical
components of~(\ref{eq:asymex_dimer_dictionary}).
Their matrix space acts on $\C^2\otimes\C^2$; their physical letter
indices are the horizontal auxiliary pairs of $T$. The matrices $C_k$
are the two-by-two matrices in~(\ref{eq:asymex_dimer_C}), equivalently
the restrictions of $xP_++(-1)^kyP_-$ to the matched bond subspace.

Stacking sectors $f,f'$ gives targets $M_{f+f'}$ and $M_{f+f'+1}$,
with addition modulo two, and weights $x/2=7/16$ and $y/2=1/16$.
Unlike the one-label example, these contributions multiply different
targets. For sector zero multiplied by itself,
$c_{000}^{(L)}=(7/16)^L$ and $c_{001}^{(L)}=(1/16)^L$ in this
unnormalized component convention. These are the trace powers of
$\chi_{000}=(7/16)$ and $\chi_{001}=(1/16)$.
The normalized BNT coefficients instead use $7/10,1/10$, as explained
in Subsection~\ref{sec:asymex_vertical_dictionary}.

\begin{definition}[The sector tensors and their stacked product]
    \label{def:asymex_dimer}

    dimension four. The stacked product
    $B_{f,f'}^{ab}=\sum_cM_f^{ac}\otimes M_{f'}^{cb}$ has bond
    dimension sixteen. Its target tensors are $M_{f+f'}$ and
    $M_{f+f'+1}$, with weights $7/16$ and $1/16$.
\end{definition}

The inner idempotents select different outer target sectors.
The exact reordered letter identity is
\begin{align}

\begin{align}

\end{align}

$B_{f,f'}^a=\mathcal M^a\Sigma=\Sigma\mathcal M^a$.

these stacked letters. Its spectral factor $\Sigma$ is not the physical
Bell density $\sigma$ in~(\ref{eq:asymex_T_density}).
