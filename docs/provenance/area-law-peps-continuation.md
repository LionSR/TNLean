## Mathematical scope of the eighteenth contribution

For an arbitrary translated dyadic cell, exponent, signed index and optional
midpoint choices, each fan triangle meets a whole side of its own closed
square precisely where its elementary perimeter segment meets that side.
For any two distinct indexed actual fine cells, the intersection of a
reference fan triangle with the opposing closed cell equals the intersection
of its elementary perimeter segment with that closed cell.

Both equalities include empty intersections and contacts consisting of one
point. Layers and natural widths are arbitrary. No late-scale bound,
positive-width condition, nonempty endpoint-set assumption, supporting-face,
matching or positive-contact certificate is supplied. Convexity puts a
triangle's points away from its perimeter segment inside the open reference
cell. Own square sides exclude this interior; actual half-open fine-cell
disjointness excludes it from the closure of any distinct actual fine cell.
The two equalities follow from those exclusions.

The previous contributions already supply actual fine-cell plane cover,
whole and elementary side subdivisions, unique opposing cell or dummy tags,
reciprocal matching, the actual two-color assignment, contacts of triangles
and connected runs within one fan, and the exact finite nonbelt fine-cell
cover of every primary birth region. These are local ingredients. They do
not yet supply the global two-family proposition, isolated stars or repairs.
