# Assembly of evolutions on disjoint regions

The block approximation to one-dimensional propagation in
[Osborne, arXiv:quant-ph/0508031v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0508031v2),
equations (2)–(13), is an input to the Gaussian construction in
[Huang, arXiv:1406.6355v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6355v6), Section 5.4.
`TNLean/Circuit/DisjointRelativeEvolution.lean` proves the following auxiliary
analytic statement. It does not yet prove the block approximation theorem.

Let the sites be finite, with local dimension q ≥ 1. Let A and Bⱼ be Hermitian
operators, let the regions Kⱼ be pairwise disjoint, and write

H = A − ∑ⱼ Bⱼ,     Gⱼ(s) = E_{Kⱼ}(e^{isA} Bⱼ e^{-isA}),

where E_{Kⱼ} is normalized partial-trace expectation. The theorem
`exists_disjoint_projected_propagator_approximation` constructs unitary paths
Uⱼ, supported on Kⱼ, with Uⱼ(0) = 1 and Uⱼ′ = i Gⱼ Uⱼ. The same paths are
chosen before the time and error bounds. If

‖e^{isA} Bⱼ e^{-isA} − Gⱼ(s)‖ ≤ εⱼ

for every s between 0 and t, then

‖e^{-itH} − e^{-itA} U₀(t) ⋯ Uₘ₋₁(t)‖ ≤ |t| ∑ⱼ εⱼ.

The local evolutions are obtained from the existing supported projected-evolution
theorem, applied to A and A − Bⱼ. Their existence is not an added hypothesis.
Disjoint support implies the commutations needed for the ordered product rule.
The exact relative path e^{itA}e^{-itH} is generated by the sum of the unprojected
interaction-picture terms. Duhamel comparison and the triangle inequality give
the estimate. Left multiplication by e^{-itA} preserves the operator norm.

To obtain the full chain propagator approximation, the remaining argument must
choose an explicit block partition, express A as the sum of the within-block
Hamiltonians, group the omitted terms into the Bⱼ, and prove the displayed
generator errors from Lieb–Robinson localization on disjoint boundary regions.
The first factor e^{-itA} must then be expressed as the product of its disjoint
block evolutions. Polynomial simultaneous bond bounds and integration with the
finite Gaussian quadrature are additional obligations. The present theorem
assumes neither those conclusions nor a family of cut-dependent approximants.
