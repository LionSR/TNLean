# A fixed probability space for the interval recursion

A finite product supplies a sampling coordinate for every pair of endpoints between zero and L. The coordinate of an interval is itself the product of Haar unitary groups over every possible rank of its physical space. This fixes the probability space before any retained rank is known. The probability law and each interval marginal are the existing finite product constructions.

The balanced recursion will use only the coordinates of its actual internal intervals. Unused endpoint pairs contribute no failures to the sum; the previously proved merge count concerns only one specified root hierarchy.

These definitions support Arad, Landau, Vazirani and Vidick, arXiv:1602.08828v2, Figure fig:algo1 and lines 1777–1817. They are not a proof of measurable dependence of the retained spaces or of the algorithm's success probability.
