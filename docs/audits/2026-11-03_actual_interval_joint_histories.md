# A joint history bound on the actual interval construction

The actual finite-resampling tree now has a single outer-measure estimate
for original-ground viability at its root, viability at every listed
internal interval, and the literal original-target stopped history at
every listed parent. The multiplier is the original merge count
max(r-p-1,0), rather than the sum of two such counts.

The statement is a conditional analytic composition. Its fixed-candidate
premise requires that every 0.04-viable candidate have original-target
history failure at most beta and that this history imply 3/200-viability
of the same complete retained parent. These are intended to be supplied
by the fixed-input physical sampling theorem. They are not claimed to
follow from the recursive geometry alone.

The proof derives measurable guarded events and their exact actual
coordinate sections. Two viable children give a 3/100-viable adjacent
merge, hence a 0.04-viable candidate. A parent's sequence leaves both
shorter children unchanged. Its guarded section is empty when a baseline
child fails, and is otherwise contained in the literal fixed-candidate
history complement. The existing one-union theorem then gives the bound.
No second tree or arbitrary successful-event choice is introduced.

The original global ground space and all operator families, thresholds
and trimming parameters precede all samples. The event involves only
the original target. Terminal untrimmed-image estimates for all soft
centers are separate pointwise consequences of this same history; no
uncountable intersection is asserted measurable.

These statements support ALVV, arXiv:1602.08828v2, lines 749–755 and
1777–1817. They do not assert a complete physical construction or a
running-time bound. Ordinary integration and the compiled blueprint checks
remain pending.
