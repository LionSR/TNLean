## Result and exact scope

Finite oriented dual paths use Mathlib `Quiver.Path` on the four labelled
unit steps. Integer signed crossings retain multiplicities, reversal,
periodic seams, and separately labelled bonds at small periods. The
homotopy relation is generated by backtrack cancellation, a positive
east–north versus north–east square move, reversal, composition,
symmetry and transitivity. A supported integer potential is derived by
induction on those moves. Neither state equality, flatness, nor existence
of a gauge is a constructor or premise of the path-deformation theorem.

The potential exponentiates to the genuine native group gauge. Absorbing
that gauge in the actual finite bond-end sum proves physical coefficient
and state equality. The group may be nonabelian. Virtual invariance is
required only at the swept vertices, the exterior tensors may be arbitrary,
and physical spaces may depend on the vertex. The exterior-matrix theorem
keeps arbitrary fixed matrices on every bond with both ends outside the
swept set. Such matrices need not commute with the flux element. Bonds
incident to the swept set carry the specified string matrices and receive
the derived gauge change. This is an equality of closed contractions,
not an assumed equality of fixed open boundary columns.

The geometry and native contraction cover every pair of positive periods,
including one and two. The simple-graph measurement and local parent
statements require both periods at least three. The lifted integer
displacement is invariant under the generated homotopy, preventing an
identification of distinct winding classes merely because their periodic
endpoints agree. No completeness classification of these homotopies and
no equality of arbitrary same-endpoint torus states is claimed.

## Local parent consequences

Identity plaquette holonomy gives a four-vertex supported gauge removing
all internal insertions. The remaining crossing and exterior contractions
produce a boundary vector, so each complementary physical slice belongs
to the original plaquette PEPS range. This yields annihilation by every
positive parent interaction with that range as its kernel and by the
canonical orthogonal parent projector. The final dual-path consumers
substitute the derived endpoint identity, without asking for vanishing
curl or holonomy as an additional hypothesis. Closed loops are locally
flux-free and satisfy the local parent constraints; this does not make
their global states equal to the vacuum state. No equality of all reduced
densities away from endpoints is claimed.
