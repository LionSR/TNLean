\section{What a stronger example must establish}
\label{sec:intrinsic-frontier}

\subsection{Conclusions for the existing families}

The finite-group examples have exact RFP channels, nonproduct
ordered constraints, and literal length-dependent canonical
coefficients. Nevertheless, \cref{prop:group-separation} separates
every weight into independent bonds by an explicit bounded-range
circuit.

The Ising and Fibonacci examples have canonical categorical fusion
and states that are not identical independent-bond products. At their
certified register-crossing points, their length dependence survives
every positive representative rescaling. The fixed regrouping gives
exactly the normal representatives in \cref{eq:kappa}, with the
sector-dependent multiplicities in \cref{eq:category-spectra}.
Their shifted full-space RFP channels exist for all positive
weights. Coprime multiplicity ranks exclude a common mixed factor
within the stated sector-preserving one-site decomposition.
They do not exclude a more general bounded-depth separation.

The CZX example retains a nonunital vertical algebra and anomalous
horizontal symmetry. Its shifted multiplicities are identical, so
its positive weights separate at the tensor level. Anomaly alone
does not protect inseparable fusion weights.

Thus nontrivial algebra, nonproduct states, and inseparable
weight-intertwiner coupling are different properties. The first two
are established. The third has not been established by these
constructions under the stronger equivalence convention of
\cref{sec:intrinsic-question}.

\subsection{Necessary algebraic constraints and conditional obstructions}

Associativity supplies an exact necessary test. For sufficiently
large $L$, BNT independence gives
\begin{align}
	\sum_d c_{abd}(L)c_{dce}(L)
	 & =\sum_d c_{bcd}(L)c_{ade}(L).
	\label{eq:associativity}
\end{align}
Let $X_{abc}$ be the multiset of positive eigenvalues of
$\chi_{abc}$. Products of power sums are power sums of pairwise
products, so
\begin{align}
	\biguplus_dX_{abd}\cdot X_{dce}
	 & =\biguplus_dX_{bcd}\cdot X_{ade}.
	\label{eq:multiset}
\end{align}
Here the dot includes every pairwise product with multiplicity.
To justify the implication, subtract the two power sums and list
their distinct positive bases as $y_1>\cdots>y_k$. If
$\sum_ja_jy_j^L=0$ for all sufficiently large $L$, division by
$y_1^L$ and passage to the limit gives $a_1=0$. Iteration gives
$a_j=0$ for every $j$. Zero eigenvalues, invisible to positive-length
power sums, have already been omitted.

This constraint is not a realization theorem. It does not construct
fusion isometries, establish horizontal positivity, or provide
physical recovery channels.

There is also a conditional categorical obstruction. Suppose the
positive fusion data arise from a coherent natural tensorator of a
monoidal equivalence of finite unitary categories, and its positive
part is trivialized by a positive natural automorphism $\eta$.
This is the setting of categorical unitarization results, not an
automatic consequence of RFP~\cite{Reutter}. On a simple object,
$\eta_a=C_a\Id$ with $C_a>0$. For every copy inclusion
$v_{c,q}:c\to a\otimes b$, naturality gives
\begin{align}
	\eta_{a\otimes b}v_{c,q}
	 & =v_{c,q}\eta_c=C_cv_{c,q},     \\
	v_{d,r}^\dagger\eta_{a\otimes b}v_{c,q}
	 & =C_c\delta_{cd}\delta_{rq}\Id.
\end{align}
After absorbing the unitary tensorator into the fusion maps, the
positive component is therefore
\begin{align}
	\chi_{abc} & =\frac{C_aC_b}{C_c}\Id.
\end{align}
The rescaling $A_a\mapsto C_a^{-1}A_a$ removes it.
Naturality on every copy inclusion is what excludes a nonscalar
positive multiplicity operator. The argument additionally requires
the coherent tensorator and its categorical trivialization.
Recoverability of physical letter spaces does not supply these
hypotheses by itself.

A separate conditional obstruction applies to canonical-element
reconstructions. If $\{e_I\}$ and $\{e^I\}$ are dual bases and a
multiplicative dual representation $\psi$ is used in
$\widetilde A_a=\sum_I\phi_a(e_I)\otimes\psi(e^I)$, then
\begin{align}
	e^Ie^J & =\sum_K\Delta_K^{IJ}e^K,                  \\
	\widetilde A_a\widetilde A_b
	       & =\sum_K[(\phi_a\otimes\phi_b)\Delta(e_K)]
	\otimes\psi(e^K).
\end{align}
An isometric representation decomposition places the identity on
each multiplicity space. Subsequent positive sector normalization
again produces only a scalar ratio. This deduction assumes the
stated reconstruction and decomposition; it is not a classification
of arbitrary MPDO RFPs.

Unitless systems, nonnatural positive data, and other reconstruction
mechanisms may fall outside these arguments. Falling outside a
conditional theorem is necessary for some proposed mechanisms, but
is not evidence that a stronger example exists.

\subsection{A newer construction tested}

Correlated idempotent states provide a concrete test beyond independently
weighted bonds. Le-Nestour, P\'erez-Garc\'ia, and Ruiz-de-Alarc\'on
construct fixed tensors from such states with a Haar marginal and derive
a quantum Goursat reconstruction~\cite{Goursat}. On the reconstructed
Hopf supports, the induced dual representation is multiplicative and
faithful. The positive physical weight commutes with the represented
algebra; it need not be central in that algebra. Decomposing its
representations still gives
\begin{align}
 \chi_{ab}^{c}=\frac{s_as_b}{s_c}\Id_{N_{ab}^{c}},\qquad s_a>0.
\end{align}
Thus this route does not evade the fixed-rescaling obstruction just
described. A companion derivation, \emph{Correlated idempotent states and
removable length dependence in MPDO fixed points}, proves the channel and
canonical statements and checks the subgroup
$\{((a,b),(a,c)):a,b,c\in\Z_2\}\subset(\Z_2^2)^2$ explicitly.
Its density operator is an even-parity boundary tensored with independent
maximally mixed qubits. General faithful representations alone do not
ensure positive periodic contractions; the explicit example satisfies
that extra condition.

Likewise, the canonical-element construction of Liu et al. has scalar
ratio weights explicitly in its fixed-point proof~\cite{Bialgebra}.
These exclusions concern specified constructions, not every RFP.
Kato's exact-flow structure theorem instead retains positive matrices
conditioned on sectors and fusion paths~\cite{Kato}. Its common,
scale-independent coproduct is an assumption, and membership of the
positive factor in a commutant is not a proof of independent-register
factorization. This is a useful formulation of the remaining question,
not an existing intrinsically length-dependent example.

\subsection{The remaining gap}

Sector-dependent multiplicities are a meaningful unresolved case.
The categorical examples show that such multiplicities need not
possess a common mixed factor in their canonical one-site
decomposition. What is missing is an obstruction stable under the
specified blocking and bounded-depth circuits, rather than another
fixed-cut eigenvalue calculation.

Another possible source of stronger behavior is an operator
recoupling system whose positive multiplicities cannot be
simultaneously assigned to independent registers in all fusion
trees. Such a system must satisfy the full recoupling equations,
including off-diagonal output sectors. The spectral condition
\cref{eq:multiset} cannot by itself detect that coupling. Nonunital
or nonrigid systems remain relevant, but the CZX separation shows
that these properties alone are insufficient.

Any stronger claim consequently needs several distinct certificates.
The physical tensor must generate positive rings at every length
at one fixed raw scale. Its canonical representatives must be
normal and inequivalent in the relevant orientation, with every
transfer factor and multiplicity included. Fusion identities must
specify their support projections and zero complements. Recovery
maps must be trace preserving on complete physical spaces. Finally,
nonseparation must be proved against a clearly quantified class of
local operations. A symmetry-preserving version needs
symmetry-compatible operations; a bulk interpretation needs a
separate bulk tensor and a controlled boundary map.

These requirements do not demand a new bulk phase. They distinguish
a boundary spectral decoration from positive data that remain tied
to its intertwiner structure. The present assessment supports the
modest conclusion that length-dependent RFPs with genuinely
nontrivial canonical algebra and nonproduct states exist, while
inseparable weight-intertwiner coupling under bounded local
refactoring remains unproved for the examples considered here.
