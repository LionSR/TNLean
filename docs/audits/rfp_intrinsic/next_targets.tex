\title{Next tests for nonspectator length dependence in MPDO fixed points}
\date{16 September 2026}
\begin{abstract}
	The construction target is a nonproduct matrix product density operator
	renormalization fixed point with nontrivial fusion and length dependence
	that is not an independent spectator. We derive an all-length spectral
	diagnostic for the shifted Fibonacci family: distinct parameters cannot
	share a parameter-independent density-operator core with independent
	spectators in a fixed repeating cell, even under global unitaries.
	This is a family obstruction, not intrinsic pointwise fusion weighting,
	because a fixed regrouping already makes the raw fusion flat.
	For construction, we formulate a physical-dimension-five,
	bond-dimension-four ansatz with exact recovery channels and a local
	purification. Trace idempotence and coefficient-kernel compatibility
	provide inexpensive rejection tests. Three environment states suffice
	for local sector compatibility, but not for the trace-one, full-span
	stationary search considered here. A four-environment Pauli seed also
	fails, by an explicit kernel mismatch. Finally, Reutter's theorem
	excludes positive nonflat coherent weights on the rigid categorical
	locus. A subsequent coefficient-space obstruction excludes the proposed
	active-rank-one search, independently of scalar multiplicity. The
	remaining same-encoding frontier begins at Gram ranks $(2,1)$ and
	purification environment dimension at least five; its feasibility is open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Decision and meaning of nonseparation}
\label{sec:decision}

The boundary-theory discussion of Cirac, P\'erez-Garc\'ia, Schuch,
and Verstraete (CPSV)~\cite{CPSV} motivates separating local spectral
weights from global constraints. A constraint may disappear when even
one boundary site is traced out. Independent weighted dimers do not
address this mechanism. The target here is therefore a nonproduct
matrix product density operator (MPDO) fixed point with nontrivial
canonical fusion, followed by a test of whether its weights remain
inseparable from that structure under specified physical operations.
No new example meeting all these requirements is established below.

Let $M^{ab}\in\End(\mc H)$ be physical operator letters, with horizontal
indices $a,b=1,\ldots,D$. Define the periodic contraction and blocking by
\begin{align}
	\rho_N(M)
	          & =\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_N}
	M^{a_1a_2}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{a_Na_1},
	\nonumber                                \\
	(MM)^{ab} & =\sum_cM^{ac}\otimes M^{cb}.
	\label{eq:definition}
\end{align}
A physical renormalization fixed point (RFP) requires completely
positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) maps on the complete operator spaces,
\begin{align}
	\mc T(M^{ab})    & =(MM)^{ab}, &
	\mc S((MM)^{ab}) & =M^{ab}.
	\label{eq:rfp}
\end{align}
These are identities for every complex letter. Neither positivity of
a few periodic contractions nor an associative coefficient table
implies them. Canonical form must also be checked before applying the
CPSV characterization.

For vertical normal representatives $A_a$, that characterization uses
positive active multiplicity operators $\chi_{abc}$ in isometric
decompositions of $A_aA_b$. The vertical closures satisfy
\begin{align}
	[O_L(A)]_{\boldsymbol i,\boldsymbol j}
	 & =\tr\left(A^{i_1j_1}\cdots A^{i_Lj_L}\right),
	\nonumber                                        \\
	O_L(A_a)O_L(A_b)
	 & =\sum_c\tr(\chi_{abc}^{L})O_L(A_c).
	\label{eq:vertical}
\end{align}
An eigenvalue $x$ of a multiplicity operator contributes $x^L$ because
the same multiplicity coordinate passes through all $L$ factors.
Tracing sums these powers. This power-sum law is not the issue under
investigation. The issue is whether the weights can be separated
physically from a nontrivial fusion structure.

Three notions must remain distinct.
\begin{description}
	\item[Fixed-cut weighting.]
	      Unequal positive eigenvalues within a fusion channel prevent flattening
	      by compensated representative rescalings at that cut.
	\item[Uniform-family separation.]
	      Different parameter values admit the same sequence of density
	      operators as a core, with parameter dependence confined to independent
	      spectators.
	\item[Pointwise intrinsic weighting.]
	      At a fixed parameter, no allowed regrouping and bounded-depth unitary
	      circuit removes the weighted fusion structure into spectators.
\end{description}
The spectral result in \cref{sec:spectral} excludes uniform-family
separation for a precise spectator model. It does not establish
pointwise intrinsic weighting.

The spectators considered here are independent one-site or bond density
operators in a fixed finite repeating cell. Blocking a common multiple
of all cell and regrouping lengths makes each spectator moment
geometric in the number of cells. Pure ancillas and arbitrary unitaries
preserve nonzero spectra. Arbitrary site-dependent or length-dependent
spectator assignments are outside this convention. No invariance under
arbitrary quantum channels is claimed.

Kato's exact compression and recovery construction~\cite{Kato} motivates
the channel-first search: redundant density operators can be discarded
and prepared within direct-sum sectors without changing the encoded
state coordinates. Its algebraic RG construction additionally assumes
compatible faithful representations of the minimal sufficient algebras
at all block lengths and a block-length-independent inclusion map.
A single recoverable encoding does not establish these assumptions.
In particular, faithful representations alone do not guarantee positive
periodic contractions.

\section{A spectral test on the Fibonacci family}
\label{sec:spectral}

\subsection{Fusion data and all-length moments}

Write $\tau^2=1+\tau$, $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and $\phi^2=\phi+1$.
The shifted family has raw multiplicities
\begin{align}
	Z         & =(\phi+2)(1+\phi t),\qquad 0<t<1,
	\nonumber                                       \\
	\eta_1    & =Z^{-1}\diag(1,\phi t),           &
	\eta_\tau & =Z^{-1}\diag(\phi,t,\phi t).
	\label{eq:eta}
\end{align}
The relevant reviewed tensor identities~\cite{Reviewed} are
\begin{align}
	C      & \simeq(\eta_1\otimes A_1)\oplus(\eta_\tau\otimes A_\tau),
	\nonumber                                                          \\
	A_aA_b & \simeq\bigoplus_c\Id_{N_{ab}^c}\otimes A_c.
	\label{eq:fib-input}
\end{align}
Here $N_{ab}^c$ are the Fibonacci fusion coefficients. The symbol
$\simeq$ denotes an isometric direct-sum identity, including zero
operators on unused complements. The one-letter horizontal closures are
\begin{align}
	R_1    & =\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},    &
	R_\tau & =\phi^{-2}\ket{\tau,\tau}\bra{\tau,\tau}.
	\label{eq:fib-seeds}
\end{align}
Their nonzero eigenvalues are $\phi^2,\phi^{-2}$ and $\phi^{-2}$,
respectively: the characteristic polynomial of $R_1$ is $z^2-3z+1$.

For completeness, the base letters act on
$\mc P_a=\operatorname{span}\{\ket{x,y}:N_{ax}^y=1\}$ and are
\begin{align}
	A_a^{(y',\rho,y),(x',\rho,x)}
	 & =[F^{a x'\rho}_{y}]_{y'x}\ket{x',y'}\bra{x,y},
	\nonumber                                         \\
	F^{\tau\tau\tau}_\tau
	 & =\begin{pmatrix}
		    \phi^{-1}   & \phi^{-1/2} \\
		    \phi^{-1/2} & -\phi^{-1}
	    \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Other admissible scalar associators are one, inadmissible entries
vanish, and unequal external $\rho$ labels give zero letters.
The full-space path-recoupling identity in Ref.~\cite{Reviewed}
establishes \cref{eq:fib-input}; no positivity assumption about a
generic representation is used.

Repeated fusion gives the horizontal closure of any word:
\begin{align}
	R(a_1,\ldots,a_N)
	 & \simeq\bigoplus_c
	\Id_{N_{a_1\cdots a_N}^{c}}\otimes R_c.
	\label{eq:word}
\end{align}
To see why the identity holds at every length, fuse the first two
letters in \cref{eq:fib-input} and then fuse each output with the next
letter. The recoupling isometries are independent of the contracted
horizontal indices, so they pass through the finite contraction sums.
Their ranges remain orthogonal. Since both seeds in
\cref{eq:fib-seeds} are positive, this also proves positivity at every
word length.

For an integer $p\geq1$, the one-site multiplicity moments and seed
moments are
\begin{align}
	a_p           & =\tr(\eta_1^p)=\frac{1+\phi^pt^p}{Z^p}, &
	c_p           & =\tr(\eta_\tau^p)
	=\frac{\phi^p+t^p+\phi^pt^p}{Z^p},
	\nonumber                                                 \\
	\ell_{1,p}    & =\phi^{2p}+\phi^{-2p},                  &
	\ell_{\tau,p} & =\phi^{-2p}.
	\label{eq:moment-data}
\end{align}
Orthogonal copies contribute their weights to the $p$th power.
Writing $(a_p+c_p\tau)^N=u_{N,p}+v_{N,p}\tau$, multiplication by
$a_p+c_p\tau$ gives
\begin{align}
	                          & (u+v\tau)(a_p+c_p\tau)
	=a_pu+c_pu\tau+a_pv\tau+c_pv(1+\tau),
	\nonumber                                                                         \\
	\binom{u_{N+1,p}}{v_{N+1,p}}
	                          & =\begin{pmatrix}a_p&c_p\\c_p&a_p+c_p\end{pmatrix}
	\binom{u_{N,p}}{v_{N,p}}, &
	\binom{u_{0,p}}{v_{0,p}}  & =\binom10,
	\nonumber                                                                         \\
	P_{N,p}:=\tr(\rho_N^p)
	                          & =\begin{pmatrix}\ell_{1,p}&\ell_{\tau,p}\end{pmatrix}
	\begin{pmatrix}a_p&c_p\\c_p&a_p+c_p\end{pmatrix}^{N}
	\binom10.
	\label{eq:moment-recurrence}
\end{align}
This is an all-length recurrence derived from the tensor decomposition.

The two fusion-ring characters send $\tau$ to $\phi$ and
$-\phi^{-1}$. Define $h_{p,+}=a_p+\phi c_p$ and
$h_{p,-}=a_p-\phi^{-1}c_p$. Solving the two character equations gives
\begin{align}
	u_{N,p}+\phi v_{N,p}     & =h_{p,+}^{N},                                                &
	u_{N,p}-\phi^{-1}v_{N,p} & =h_{p,-}^{N},
	\nonumber                                                                                 \\
	v_{N,p}                  & =\frac{h_{p,+}^{N}-h_{p,-}^{N}}{\sqrt5},                     &
	u_{N,p}                  & =\frac{\phi^{-1}h_{p,+}^{N}+\phi h_{p,-}^{N}}{\sqrt5},
	\nonumber                                                                                 \\
	P_{N,p}
	                         & =\frac{\phi^{-1}\ell_{1,p}+\ell_{\tau,p}}{\sqrt5}h_{p,+}^{N}
	+\frac{\phi\ell_{1,p}-\ell_{\tau,p}}{\sqrt5}h_{p,-}^{N}.
	\label{eq:moments}
\end{align}
Here $\phi+\phi^{-1}=\sqrt5$.

The rings are already normalized. Indeed,
$a_1=1/(\phi+2)$, $c_1=\phi/(\phi+2)$, and
\begin{align}
	(1+\phi\tau)^2
	 & =1+2\phi\tau+\phi^2(1+\tau)
	\nonumber                      \\
	 & =(\phi+2)+(3\phi+1)\tau
	=(\phi+2)(1+\phi\tau).
\end{align}
Thus their trace at every positive length equals the seed trace
\begin{align}
	\frac{\tr R_1+\phi\tr R_\tau}{\phi+2}
	 & =\frac{3+\phi^{-1}}{\phi+2}=1.
\end{align}

\subsection{Purity and separation of distinct parameters}

For $p=2$, $\ell_{1,2}=7$ and $\ell_{\tau,2}=\phi^{-4}=5-3\phi$.
The two coefficients in \cref{eq:moments} reduce to
\begin{align}
	\frac{7\phi^{-1}+\phi^{-4}}{\sqrt5}
	 & =\frac{7\phi-7+5-3\phi}{\sqrt5}=2,
	\nonumber                             \\
	\frac{7\phi-\phi^{-4}}{\sqrt5}
	 & =\frac{7\phi-5+3\phi}{\sqrt5}=5.
\end{align}
Writing $h_\pm=h_{2,\pm}$, their numerators are
\begin{align}
	Z^2h_+
	 & =1+\phi^2t^2+\phi^3+\phi t^2+\phi^3t^2
	=2\phi^2(1+\phi t^2),
	\nonumber                                  \\
	Z^2h_-
	 & =1+\phi^2t^2-\phi-\phi^{-1}t^2-\phi t^2
	=-\phi^{-1}+\phi^{-2}t^2.
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
	P_N(t):=\tr(\rho_N(t)^2)
	 & =2h_+(t)^N+5h_-(t)^N,
	\nonumber                                        \\
	r(t):=\frac{h_-(t)}{h_+(t)}
	 & =-\frac{1-\phi^{-1}t^2}{2\phi^3(1+\phi t^2)}.
	\label{eq:purity}
\end{align}
The ratio lies in $(-1,0)$. With $x=t^2$, its derivative is
\begin{align}
	\frac{dr}{dx}
	 & =\frac{\phi^{-1}(1+\phi x)+\phi(1-\phi^{-1}x)}
	    {2\phi^3(1+\phi x)^2}
	=\frac{\phi+\phi^{-1}}{2\phi^3(1+\phi x)^2}>0.
	\label{eq:r-injective}
\end{align}
Thus distinct parameters have distinct $|r|$.

To eliminate geometric spectator contributions, define the blocked
purity curvature
\begin{align}
	K_n^{(k)}(t)
	 & =\frac{P_{kn}(t)P_{k(n+2)}(t)}{P_{k(n+1)}(t)^2},
	\qquad k,n\geq1.
\end{align}
Set $z=r(t)^k$ and $Q=5/2$. The geometric factor $h_+^{kn}$
cancels, and direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
	K_n^{(k)}-1
	                                    & =\frac{1+Qz^n+Qz^{n+2}+Q^2z^{2n+2}
		                                       -1-2Qz^{n+1}-Q^2z^{2n+2}}
	                                       {(1+Qz^{n+1})^2}
	\nonumber                                                                  \\
	                                    & =\frac{Qz^n(1-z)^2}{(1+Qz^{n+1})^2},
	\nonumber                                                                  \\
	\lim_{n\to\infty}
	\frac{K_{n+1}^{(k)}-1}{K_n^{(k)}-1} & =z.
	\label{eq:curvature}
\end{align}
The denominators are nonzero because the purities are positive.

Suppose two parameter values $t,s$ shared a density-operator core
$\omega_n$ after common blocking and finite-cell regrouping. Independent
spectators would contribute purities $b_t^n,b_s^n$, so that
\begin{align}
	P_{kn}(t) & =\tr(\omega_n^2)b_t^n, &
	P_{kn}(s) & =\tr(\omega_n^2)b_s^n.
\end{align}
Their curvature sequences would coincide. By \cref{eq:curvature},
$r(t)^k=r(s)^k$. Both ratios are negative, and \cref{eq:r-injective}
then implies $t=s$. This proves the claimed uniform-family obstruction.
The argument allows global, parameter-dependent and length-dependent
unitaries because it uses only spectra.

The first finite benchmark is $K_1^{(1)}$ at $t=1/2$ and $t=2/3$,
computed from \cref{eq:purity}. The exact script~\cite{Checks} checks
that these values differ and verifies the recurrence through length
six. Those finite tests do not replace the all-length argument or its
common-blocking conclusion.

This diagnostic does not discover intrinsic fusion weights.
In \cref{eq:fib-input}, the raw fusion multiplicities are already
identities. Normalizing the base tensors by $A_a/\phi$ introduces
only the common factor $\phi^{-1}$ in populated fusion channels,
removable by compensated representative rescaling. The parameter
dependence is in $\eta_a$. Failure of a parameter-independent common
core does not exclude a parameter-dependent pointwise core.

\section{A channel-first finite-dimensional search}
\label{sec:channels}

\subsection{Encodings, full-space recovery, and stationarity}

Choose the logical algebra and physical space
\begin{align}
	\mc A          & =M_2(\C)\oplus\C,           &
	\mc H          & =(\C^2\otimes\C^2)\oplus\C, &
	\tau           & =\diag(1/3,2/3),
	\nonumber                                      \\
	J_1(X\oplus z) & =(X\otimes\tau)\oplus z.
	\label{eq:J1}
\end{align}
The trace on $\mc A$ is $\tr X+z$. If $P_0,P_1$ project onto the
two physical summands, define
\begin{align}
	D_1(Y)
	 & =\tr_{\mathrm{red}}(P_0YP_0)\oplus\bra4Y\ket4.
\end{align}
Pinching and partial trace are CP,
$\tr D_1(Y)=\tr(P_0Y)+\tr(P_1Y)=\tr Y$, and
$D_1J_1=\mathrm{id}_{\mc A}$ because $\tr\tau=1$.

For integers $r,s\geq1$, $2r+s\leq25$, choose full-rank normalized
states $\sigma_0\in M_r$, $\sigma_1\in M_s$, and an isometry
\begin{align}
	V:(\C^2\otimes\C^r)\oplus\C^s & \longrightarrow\mc H^{\otimes2},
	\nonumber                                                                        \\
	J_2(X\oplus z)
	                              & =V[(X\otimes\sigma_0)\oplus z\sigma_1]V^\dagger.
	\label{eq:J2}
\end{align}
Let $V_0,V_1$ be its restrictions and $P=VV^\dagger$.
For a fixed density operator $\omega\in\mc A$, use the decoder
\begin{align}
	D_2(Y)
	 & =\left[\tr_{\C^r}(V_0^\dagger YV_0)
		    \oplus\tr(V_1^\dagger YV_1)\right]
	+\tr((\Id-P)Y)\omega.
	\label{eq:D2}
\end{align}
The last term is essential on the full input space. If
$\omega=\sum_jp_j\ket j\bra j$ and $\{\ket z\}$ spans $\ker P$,
it has Kraus operators $\sqrt{p_j}\ket j\bra z$ with squared sum
$\Id-P$. The two compression branches have squared sum $P$.
Hence $D_2$ is CPTP. Normalization of $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ gives
\begin{align}
	D_2J_2(X\oplus z)
	 & =(X\tr\sigma_0)\oplus(z\tr\sigma_1)=X\oplus z.
\end{align}
Both encodings are CP and preserve the direct-sum trace.

Take sixteen coefficients $X_{\alpha\beta}\in\mc A$,
$\alpha,\beta=0,\ldots,3$, spanning $\mc A$, and set
$M^{\alpha\beta}=J_1(X_{\alpha\beta})$. The equation to solve is
\begin{align}
	G_{\alpha\beta}:=
	\sum_{\gamma=0}^{3}
	J_1(X_{\alpha\gamma})\otimes J_1(X_{\gamma\beta})
	 & =J_2(X_{\alpha\beta}).
	\label{eq:stationarity}
\end{align}
Recovery of arbitrary encoded states does not imply this equation.
If it holds, however, the full-space channels
\begin{align}
	\mc T & =J_2D_1, & \mc S & =J_1D_2
\end{align}
give an exact fixed-point certificate:
\begin{align}
	\mc T(M^{\alpha\beta})
	 & =J_2(X_{\alpha\beta})=G_{\alpha\beta},
	\nonumber                                 \\
	\mc S(G_{\alpha\beta})
	 & =J_1(X_{\alpha\beta})=M^{\alpha\beta}.
	\label{eq:certificate}
\end{align}

Stationarity supplies some, but not all, algebraic structure.
For $\delta=(D_1\otimes D_1)J_2$, it implies
\begin{align}
	\delta(X_{\alpha\beta})
	 & =\sum_\gamma X_{\alpha\gamma}\otimes X_{\gamma\beta},
	\nonumber                                                \\
	(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(X_{\alpha\beta})
	 & =\sum_{\gamma,\nu}
	X_{\alpha\nu}\otimes X_{\nu\gamma}\otimes X_{\gamma\beta},
	\nonumber                                                \\
	(\mathrm{id}\otimes\delta)\delta(X_{\alpha\beta})
	 & =\sum_{\gamma,\nu}
	X_{\alpha\gamma}\otimes X_{\gamma\nu}\otimes X_{\nu\beta}.
	\label{eq:state-coproduct}
\end{align}
Renaming the summation indices proves coassociativity on the spanning
coefficients and hence on $\mc A$. Stationarity on the same spanning
set also gives
\begin{align}
	J_2 & =(J_1\otimes J_1)\delta,     \\
	\bigl[D_2(J_1\otimes J_1)\bigr]\delta
	    & =D_2J_2=\mathrm{id}_{\mc A}.
\end{align}
Thus $\delta$ has a CPTP left inverse. These are state coordinates.
There is no resulting proof that
$\delta(XY)=\delta(X)\delta(Y)$. Even $J_1$ is not multiplicative:
its two sides contain $XY\otimes\tau$ and $XY\otimes\tau^2$,
respectively. Observable algebra inclusions and categorical naturality
require additional reconstruction.

\subsection{All-length positivity and the environment bound}

Impose a bond-two local purification,
\begin{align}
	M^{(u,u'),(v,v')}
	 & =\sum_{e=1}^{r_E}
	\ket{a_{uv,e}}\bra{a_{u'v',e}},
	\qquad u,v,u',v'\in\{0,1\}.
	\label{eq:purification}
\end{align}
The horizontal MPDO bond dimension is $D=2^2=4$. For each environment
string, define
\begin{align}
	\ket{\Psi_{\boldsymbol e}}
	 & =\sum_{u_1,\ldots,u_N}
	\bigotimes_{n=1}^{N}\ket{a_{u_nu_{n+1},e_n}},
	\qquad u_{N+1}=u_1.
\end{align}
Substitution into \cref{eq:definition} gives
\begin{align}
	\rho_N
	 & =\sum_{\boldsymbol e,\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol u'}
	\bigotimes_{n=1}^{N}
	\ket{a_{u_nu_{n+1},e_n}}
	\bra{a_{u'_nu'_{n+1},e_n}}
	\nonumber                                             \\
	 & =\sum_{\boldsymbol e}
	\ket{\Psi_{\boldsymbol e}}\bra{\Psi_{\boldsymbol e}}\geq0.
	\label{eq:all-positive}
\end{align}
Thus positivity holds at every length, independently of stationarity.

Put $i=(u,v)$ and
$\ket{\mathfrak a_e}=\sum_i\ket i\otimes\ket{a_{i,e}}$.
The positive Gram operator
$B=\sum_e\ket{\mathfrak a_e}\bra{\mathfrak a_e}$ has letters
as its $(i,j)$ blocks, with $j=(u',v')$.
Compatibility with $J_1(\mc A)$ requires, after ordering summands,
\begin{align}
	B & =(B_0\otimes\tau)\oplus B_1, & B_0 & \geq0, & B_1 & \geq0.
	\label{eq:Gram}
\end{align}
The positivity conditions follow by compression and are also sufficient.
Rank additivity gives
\begin{align}
	r_E\geq\rank B
	 & =2\rank B_0+\rank B_1\geq3
	\label{eq:env-bound}
\end{align}
when both sectors occur. Spectral factorization of $B$ attains its rank
as the minimal environment size for the specified letters.

At rank three both $B_0,B_1$ have rank one. Their factors can be written
as $\sum_i\ket i\ket{v_i}$ and $\sum_i b_i\ket i$, giving
\begin{align}
	a_{i,1} & =\sqrt{1/3}\,v_i\otimes\ket0, &
	a_{i,2} & =\sqrt{2/3}\,v_i\otimes\ket1, &
	a_{i,3} & =b_i\ket4.
	\label{eq:rank-three}
\end{align}
This proves local compatibility with three environment states,
not existence of a stationary solution.

\subsection{Trace, kernel, and a stronger rank-three rejection}

For any proposed tensor define
\begin{align}
	T_{\alpha\beta} & =\tr M^{\alpha\beta},                               &
	F_1(c)          & =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}M^{\alpha\beta}, &
	F_2(c)          & =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}G_{\alpha\beta}.
\end{align}
Trace preservation and \cref{eq:rfp} imply
\begin{align}
	T_{\alpha\beta}
	    & =\tr G_{\alpha\beta}
	=\sum_\gamma T_{\alpha\gamma}T_{\gamma\beta},
	    & T^2                  & =T,
	\nonumber                                             \\
	F_2 & =\mc T F_1,          & F_1        & =\mc S F_2,
	    & \ker F_1             & =\ker F_2.
	\label{eq:cheap}
\end{align}
Each kernel inclusion follows by applying one channel to zero.
Equal ranks alone are weaker than equal kernels. In addition,
\begin{align}
	\tr\rho_N=\tr(T^N)=\tr T=\rank T.
	\label{eq:trace-scale}
\end{align}
A trace-one tensor search therefore imposes $\rank T=1$.
A nonzero tensor cannot be normalized by arbitrary positive rescaling:
replacing $M$ by $sM$ would require $s^2T=sT$, hence $s=1$.

There is a further inexpensive obstruction in the trace-one,
rank-three purification stratum. Write
$(A_j)_{uv}=(v_{uv})_j$ for $j=0,1$ and $B_{uv}=b_{uv}$.
The trace matrix is the matrix of the CP map
\begin{align}
	\mc E(Y) & =A_0YA_0^\dagger+A_1YA_1^\dagger+BYB^\dagger.
	\label{eq:trace-cp}
\end{align}
If $T^2=T$ and $\rank T=1$, positivity of this rank-one map gives
\begin{align}
	\mc E(Y) & =R\tr(LY), &
	R,L      & \geq0,     & \tr(LR) & =1.
\end{align}
Indeed, the image of the positive cone is a ray generated by $R$; the coefficient
functional is positive and therefore represented by $L\geq0$.
Idempotence then gives $\tr(LR)=1$.

In the output--input convention, its Choi matrix is
$R\otimes L^{\mathsf T}$. The three Kraus operators in
\cref{eq:trace-cp} imply
\begin{align}
	\rank R\,\rank L
	 & =\rank(R\otimes L^{\mathsf T})\leq3.
\end{align}
Since $R,L$ are nonzero $2\times2$ matrices, at least one has rank one.
If $\rank R=1$, every Kraus operator has a common one-dimensional
output range and can be written $K_j=\ket r\bra{w_j}$.
The horizontal coordinate matrices $K_j\otimes\overline{K_k}$
then have a common one-dimensional output range and span at most
four dimensions. If $\rank L=1$, every Kraus operator has a common
one-dimensional input support; their tensor products have a common
input support and again span at most four dimensions.
Physical coordinates of $M$ are linear combinations of these matrices.
Thus
\begin{align}
	\rank F_1\leq4,
\end{align}
contradicting the required five-dimensional span of $J_1(\mc A)$.

This rank-three argument alone gives an environment lower bound of four.
The companion obstruction~\cite{EnvironmentFour} strengthens it to five
and requires active Gram rank at least two. Neither argument classifies
idempotent trace ranks greater than one. An unnormalized fixed-point family could have
$\tr\rho_N=\rank T>1$ and be normalized at the state level by that
constant. Such sectors are not excluded by the rank-one argument.

\subsection{The Pauli seed and the next feasibility test}

The first environment-four stratum has
$\rank B_0=1$, $\rank B_1=2$. An explicit seed is
\begin{align}
	A_0 & =\Id/2,                                      & A_1 & =X/2, & C_0 & =Y/2, & C_1 & =Z/2,
	\nonumber                                                                                    \\
	X_{(u,u'),(v,v')}
	    & =\sum_{j,k}(A_j)_{uv}\overline{(A_k)_{u'v'}}
	\ket j\bra k
	\ \oplus\
	\sum_l(C_l)_{uv}\overline{(C_l)_{u'v'}}.
	\label{eq:Pauli}
\end{align}
Here $X,Y,Z$ are the Pauli matrices. In virtual order
$(00,01,10,11)$ its trace matrix is
\begin{align}
	T            & =\frac14\left(\Id\otimes\Id+X\otimes\overline X
	+Y\otimes\overline Y+Z\otimes\overline Z\right)
	=\frac12\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega},
	\nonumber                                                        \\
	\ket{\Omega} & =\ket{00}+\ket{11},                             &
	T^2          & =T.
\end{align}
The physical coordinate matrices span
\begin{align}
	\Id\otimes\Id,\quad \Id\otimes X,\quad
	X\otimes\Id,\quad X\otimes X,\quad
	Q_0:=Y\otimes\overline Y+Z\otimes\overline Z.
	\label{eq:span-one}
\end{align}
These five matrices are independent by Pauli orthogonality, so
$\rank F_1=5$.

Blocking introduces a sixth coordinate. The active--scalar coordinate
with active indices $(0,1)$ is
\begin{align}
	H & =\sum_l A_0C_l\otimes\overline{A_1C_l}
	\nonumber                                  \\
	  & =\frac1{16}\left(Y\otimes\overline{iZ}
	+Z\otimes\overline{-iY}\right)
	=-\frac{i}{16}(Z\otimes Y+Y\otimes Z).
	\label{eq:H}
\end{align}
The two active diagonal coordinates give $Q_0/16$; the reverse
off-diagonal coordinate gives $H$. Scalar--active coordinates give
the same span, with the off-diagonal sign reversed.
Active--active coordinates remain in the first four directions of
\cref{eq:span-one}. Finally,
\begin{align}
	\sum_{l,m}C_lC_m\otimes\overline{C_lC_m}
	 & =\frac1{16}\left(\Id\otimes\Id+\Id\otimes\Id
	+iX\otimes\overline{iX}
	+(-iX)\otimes\overline{-iX}\right)
	\nonumber                                       \\
	 & =\frac18(\Id\otimes\Id+X\otimes X).
\end{align}
Thus the blocked span is exactly six-dimensional.

A single-entry kernel witness is even cheaper.
The one-site letters obey $M^{10}=M^{01}$.
Order the physical basis as $(00,01,10,11,4)$ and flatten two-site
indices lexicographically, starting at zero. Then
\begin{align}
	(G_{10}-G_{01})_{4,14}
	 & =\frac13(H_{10}-H_{01})
	=\frac13\left(\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}\right)
	=\frac1{24}.
	\label{eq:seed-reject}
\end{align}
No linear refinement exists for this seed. Positivity, full one-site
span, and trace idempotence are not sufficient. The exact finite script
in Ref.~\cite{Checks} reproduces these ranks and this witness.

For a fixed surviving tensor, the next test is semidefinite channel
feasibility. Search for the Choi matrices of $\mc T$ and $\mc S$,
each of size $125\times125$, subject to positivity, their respective
partial-trace identities, and all sixteen letter equations in
\cref{eq:rfp}. These constraints are linear in the Choi entries.
Certified infeasibility rejects the tensor. Numerical feasibility
requires exact reconstruction before acceptance. To certify the
specific ansatz, additionally recover $V,\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ and check
\cref{eq:stationarity} entry by entry, including its zero complement.

\section{The rigidity gate for higher multiplicities}
\label{sec:rigidity}

Consider the near-group shape with labels $g\in\Z_3$ and $x$:
\begin{align}
	gh & =g+h, & gx & =xg=x, & x^2 & =\sum_{g\in\Z_3}g+2x.
\end{align}
The unit is the group label $0$. The proposed weights are
$\chi_{xx}^{x}=\diag(q,q^{-1})$, $q>1$, with identity weights
elsewhere. For a triple fusion with output $d$, operator coherence is
\begin{align}
	F^{abc}_d\Lambda_L^{abc;d}
	 & =\Lambda_R^{abc;d}F^{abc}_d,
	\nonumber                                           \\
	\Lambda_L^{abc;d}
	 & =\bigoplus_e\chi_{ab}^{e}\otimes\chi_{ec}^{d}, &
	\Lambda_R^{abc;d}
	 & =\bigoplus_f\chi_{bc}^{f}\otimes\chi_{af}^{d}.
	\label{eq:weight-coherence}
\end{align}
Matching weight spectra are necessary but do not solve the pentagon.

The compatible block count follows directly from the fusion rules.
There are $27$ triples with no $x$ and $27$ with one $x$, each
with one scalar output. The nine triples with two $x$ each have
three scalar outputs and one two-dimensional output. For three $x$,
the three group outputs have dimension two, while output $x$ has
dimension $3+2\cdot2=7$. Hence the counts are
\begin{align}
	27+27+9\cdot3 & =81
	              &     & \text{scalar blocks},
	\nonumber                                                                              \\
	9+3           & =12
	              &     & \text{two-dimensional blocks},
	\nonumber                                                                              \\
	1             &     &                                & \text{seven-dimensional block}.
\end{align}
The two-dimensional blocks have weights $q,q^{-1}$ and therefore
allow only diagonal phases. The seven-dimensional block has weights
$1,1,1,q^2,1,1,q^{-2}$, allowing
$U(5)\times U(1)\times U(1)$. There are
\begin{align}
	81+12\cdot2+5^2+1+1=132
\end{align}
compatible complex entries before coherent vertex gauge. This is
a count of permitted entries, not of pentagon solutions.

The standard $\operatorname{Rep}(A_4)$ associator is already excluded
for positive nonflat weights, including coherent multiplicity-basis
changes~\cite{A4report}. More importantly, the entire rigid locus of
the proposed coherent-weight construction is excluded.

To state the scope precisely, reconstruct a skeletal semisimple
category with objects $\bigoplus_a E_a\otimes a$ and morphisms
$\bigoplus_a f_a\otimes\Id_a$. Extend each simple-pair weight by
identities on the external multiplicity spaces. Naturality then follows
from
\begin{align}
	(\Id_{E'_a\otimes E'_b}\otimes\chi_{ab})
	(f_a\otimes g_b\otimes\Id)
	 & =(f_a\otimes g_b)\otimes\chi_{ab}
	\nonumber                            \\
	 & =(f_a\otimes g_b\otimes\Id)
	(\Id_{E_a\otimes E_b}\otimes\chi_{ab}).
\end{align}
With unit compatibility, \cref{eq:weight-coherence} makes these positive
invertible operators a tensorator of the identity functor.

Reutter's positive-auto-equivalence proposition~\cite{Reutter} applies
if the reconstructed category is a unitary fusion category: it is
rigid and semisimple, has finitely many simple objects and a simple
unit, and its monoidal coherence is unitary.
The positive tensorator is then a positive coboundary. A positive
natural automorphism of the identity is scalar $\epsilon_a>0$ on
each simple object. On every output channel this gives
\begin{align}
	\chi_{ab}^{c}
	 & =\frac{\epsilon_a\epsilon_b}{\epsilon_c}
	\Id_{N_{ab}^{c}}.
	\label{eq:coboundary}
\end{align}
For the two-copy $xx\to x$ channel, this requires
$q=\epsilon_x=q^{-1}$, hence $q=1$. Multiplicity two does not evade
the rigid categorical obstruction.

Two distinctions prevent overextending this conclusion.
Naturality is automatic after the specified skeletal reconstruction;
it is not an additional loophole there. In contrast, physical unary
recovery maps do not by themselves provide a tensor product on all
intertwiners or its naturality. In particular,
\cref{eq:state-coproduct} does not establish such a reconstruction.
Also, no assertion is made here that a bare unitary pentagon and this
fusion table force rigidity.

The first categorical computation should therefore test the vacuum
channels. Choose maps
$R:0\to\bar x\otimes x$ and
$\bar R:0\to x\otimes\bar x$, where $\bar x=x$.
With unitors understood, the conjugate equations are
\begin{align}
	(\bar R^\dagger\otimes\Id_x)\alpha^{-1}_{x,\bar x,x}
	(\Id_x\otimes R)            & =\Id_x,
	\nonumber                                    \\
	(R^\dagger\otimes\Id_{\bar x})\alpha^{-1}_{\bar x,x,\bar x}
	(\Id_{\bar x}\otimes\bar R) & =\Id_{\bar x}.
	\label{eq:snakes}
\end{align}
Their contractions are scalars because $x$ is simple. Compute both,
including their phases, and solve for compatible cup normalizations.
Nonzero contractions alone should not replace this check.
If the equations hold, the group objects have inverse duals and
direct sums inherit duals; the solution is on the excluded rigid
locus. If a contraction vanishes, retain that locus only for a bounded
coherence test. Its existence is not evidence of a physical MPDO.

The smaller backup $x^2=1+g+2x$ lacks a saturated positive integer
dimension assignment with $d_1=d_g=1$:
\begin{align}
	n^2=2+2n
	\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
	(n-1)^2=3
	\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n=1+\sqrt3.
\end{align}
This rejects a saturated Hilbert-space realization, not a physical
isometric ansatz with zero complements. The latter requires
$n^2\geq2+2n$, rather than equality.

\section{Ranked next experiments and stopping rules}
\label{sec:experiments}

The originally proposed environment-four stratum is now excluded by
an exact coefficient-space theorem~\cite{EnvironmentFour}. Under the
same trace-one and full-span hypotheses, active Gram rank one is
impossible for every scalar Gram rank. Priority A below records the
excluded experiment and its regression tests, not an open optimization
target. The first unexcluded pair is $(2,1)$, requiring environment
dimension at least five. No candidate in that larger class is asserted.

\begin{description}
	\item[Priority A: excluded channel-first stratum.]
	      Begin with environment dimension four,
	      $\rank B_0=1$, $\rank B_1=2$.
	      The data are eight complex active amplitudes
	      $(v_{uv})_j$ and eight scalar amplitudes $b_{uv,l}$,
	      with $j,l,u,v\in\{0,1\}$.
	      Set
	      \begin{align}
		      a_{uv,1} & =\sqrt{1/3}\,v_{uv}\otimes\ket0, &
		      a_{uv,2} & =\sqrt{2/3}\,v_{uv}\otimes\ket1,
		      \nonumber                                     \\
		      a_{uv,3} & =b_{uv,0}\ket4,                  &
		      a_{uv,4} & =b_{uv,1}\ket4.
	      \end{align}
	      Impose span rank five, $T^2=T$, and $\rank T=1$.
	      The first computation is exact kernel comparison in
	      \cref{eq:cheap}, before optimizing $V$.
	      A mismatch rejects the seed immediately. The Pauli seed is a
	      regression test for this rejection, not a stationary starting point.

	      No survivors exist in this excluded stratum. For a revised,
	      unexcluded ansatz, Choi feasibility and reconstruction of the
	      encoding data in \cref{eq:J2} remain separate tests. The bounds
	      $r,s\geq1$, $2r+s\leq25$ still apply at physical dimension five.
	      Construction-stage success requires exact letters, a Gram
	      factorization, full-space channels, and exact stationarity.
	      Numerical failure alone does not justify increasing dimensions.
	      The trace-one rank-three stratum is analytically excluded above;
	      other integer trace ranks are a separate, explicitly deferred search.

	      A certified RFP must next pass horizontal canonical analysis and
	      vertical fusion extraction. Trivial fusion or an explicit dimer
	      factorization rejects it for the present target. Nontrivial fusion
	      with an elementary spectator recut remains a useful benchmark, but
	      does not complete the intrinsic-weighting target.

	\item[Priority B: spectral benchmark.]
	      Compute the three purities entering $K_1^{(1)}$ at $t=1/2$ and
	      $t=2/3$, and compare them with
	      \cref{eq:purity,eq:curvature}. A mismatch rejects the implementation
	      or its normalization. Agreement supplies a finite benchmark for the
	      proved family obstruction.

	      For any later candidate family, derive an all-length moment recurrence
	      before interpreting curvature as an obstruction under fixed blocking.
	      Stop this calculation once the common-core question is answered:
	      further finite spectra do not establish pointwise intrinsic fusion
	      weights.

	\item[Priority C: categorical rigidity gate.]
	      Use the compatible blocks counted in \cref{sec:rigidity}, one coherent
	      basis per fusion space, and normalized unitors.
	      First compute \cref{eq:snakes} together with pentagon equations
	      involving vacuum channels.
	      Every rigid solution is excluded by \cref{eq:coboundary}.
	      Continue only on an explicitly specified remaining locus, with an
	      exact reason why the rigidity argument does not apply.

	      If that locus is empty, stop. If it survives, the next success
	      criterion is an exact coherent associator there, followed by a
	      separate physical realization with all-length positivity and
	      full-space recovery. A compatible seven-dimensional block, or matching
	      path-weight spectra, is not a success. An indiscriminate search over
	      all 132 compatible entries is not recommended.
\end{description}

Do not continue the excluded active-rank-one channel-feasibility
search. A continuation with the same encoding must instead examine
active Gram rank at least two, beginning with $(2,1)$, or explicitly
change the trace hypothesis. The Fibonacci calculation remains an
exact spectral benchmark. The categorical proposal should receive
only the bounded rigidity test before further construction effort.

The established results are the specified uniform-family spectral
separation, the conditional full-space channel certificate, the
purification rank bound, the trace-one rank-three obstruction, and
the Pauli rejection. They do not supply an intrinsically weighted
example, a universal no-go theorem, invariance under arbitrary channels,
or a new topological phase. For any successful tensor, pointwise
separation under the stated bounded-depth physical refactorings remains
a distinct final problem.
