\section{Source statement}

Section~IV.C of Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}
considers an invertible map $\Lambda$ acting on the physical space $\C^d$ of a
tensor network state $\ket\psi$ defined on a general graph $G=(V,E)$. Under the
action of $\Lambda^{\otimes V}$, the deformed state $\ket{\psi'}=\Lambda^{\otimes V}\ket\psi$
has deformed local parent interactions
\begin{align}\label{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_interaction}
    h'_e & = ({\Lambda^{-1}}^\dagger)^{\otimes \partial e}\,h_e\,(\Lambda^{-1})^{\otimes \partial e},
\end{align}
where $\partial e$ is the support of the interaction term. The source asserts
that the map $\Lambda^{\otimes V}$ establishes a one-to-one correspondence
between the ground spaces of the parent Hamiltonians of $\ket\psi$ and $\ket{\psi'}$.

\section{Graph correspondence}

The correspondence on an arbitrary finite graph is formalized. For a family
of regions $R_i\subseteq V$, let $\mathcal G(A)$ be the full physical vectors
whose complementary slices lie in the regional spaces $\mathcal G_{R_i}(A)$.
For arbitrary invertible on-site maps $F_v$, their product $F_V$ gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_graph_transport}
    \mathcal G(A^F) & = F_V\mathcal G(A).
\end{align}
It restricts to an isomorphism of these spaces and preserves their
dimensions. When the positive local interactions are transported by
inverse-adjoint congruence as in
(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_interaction}), the same
map identifies the kernels of their full Hamiltonians. No injectivity or
translation-invariance assumption is imposed on the tensor.\footnote{Formal
    declarations: \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.map_regionParentGroundSpace_physicalDeform},
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.regionParentGroundSpacePhysicalEquiv},
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.finrank_regionParentGroundSpace_physicalDeform}, and
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.map_ker_regionParentHamiltonian_physicalDeform}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/RegionPhysicalGroundSpaceTransport}.
    Local interactions are treated in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/RegionPhysicalDeformation}.}
Invertibility gives ground-space correspondence; the stronger statement
that the full spectra agree requires unitary on-site maps.\footnote{See
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/RegionPhysicalHamiltonianTransport}.}

\section{Periodic-chain comparison}

The periodic-chain theorem independently proves
\begin{align}\label{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_ground_space}
    \mathcal G_{N,L}(\Lambda A) & = \Lambda^{\otimes N}\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)
\end{align}
for every invertible $\Lambda$ and all $N$ and $L$, and the corresponding
Hamiltonian-kernel identity for $0<N$ and $L\le N$.\footnote{Formal
    declarations: \leanid{MPSTensor.chainGroundSpace_rotatePhysical} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.ker_parentHamiltonian_rotatePhysical}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PhysicalDeformation}.}

On the cycle with $N\ge3$ sites, let $T_A$ be the tensor whose component at a
vertex with physical index $i$ is the matrix entry $(A^i)_{ab}$, with $a$ on
the bond to the predecessor and $b$ on the bond to the successor, and let
$R_s$ be the arc of $L$ consecutive sites starting at $s$. For $0<L<N$,
ordering the sites of $R_s$ identifies its regional space with the local
matrix product space of length $L$. Every configuration outside $R_s$ is the restriction of a full
configuration, and the regional slice of a full vector at that background is
its cyclic restriction to $R_s$. Hence the common regional parent space of
$T_A$ over the arcs $R_0,\ldots,R_{N-1}$ is exactly $\mathcal G_{N,L}(A)$.
Applying the same matrix $\Lambda$ at every vertex turns $T_A$ into
$T_{\Lambda A}$, and the graph product map is $\Lambda^{\otimes N}$. The graph
transport (\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_graph_transport}) therefore
yields (\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_ground_space}) for
$N\ge3$ and $0<L<N$. None of these steps assumes injectivity or a positive
physical or bond dimension.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.map_regionGroundSpace_cycleTensorOfMPS}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/CycleGroundSpace};
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.regionParentGroundSpace_cycleTensorOfMPS}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/CycleParentGroundSpace}; and
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.physicalDeform_cycleTensorOfMPS},
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.globalPhysicalMap_const_eq_onSiteTensorPow} and
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.map_chainGroundSpace_physicalEquiv_of_cycle}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/ParentHamiltonian/CyclePhysicalDeformation}.}

The comparison of parent spaces is stated only for $N\ge3$ and $0<L<N$,
because outside that range the two constructions differ. For $L=N$ the graph
region is the entire closed cycle and has no crossing virtual bonds. Its
regional contraction has only a scalar boundary coefficient. In contrast, the
local matrix product space used by the chain theorem retains an arbitrary
boundary matrix $X$ and consists of
$\sigma\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(A^{\sigma_1}\cdots A^{\sigma_N}X)$. The two
spaces need not agree. For example, the two diagonal letters
$A^0=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $A^1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ on a
three-site ring give the closed-state line generated by
$|000\rangle+|111\rangle$, whereas arbitrary boundary matrices yield the
two-dimensional span of $|000\rangle$ and $|111\rangle$. The one- and
two-site rings are not simple cycles; a comparison there would require a
loop or a pair of parallel edges. In all these cases
(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_ground_space}) remains
valid by the chain argument, and the source assertion is covered on every
finite simple graph by
(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_graph_transport}).

\section{Conclusion}

The source correspondence of Section~IV.C is formalized on every finite simple
graph, for arbitrary invertible on-site maps. The periodic-chain identity
(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_physical_deformation_general_graph_ground_space}) is both
proved directly and, for $N\ge3$ and $0<L<N$, derived from the graph theorem.
The full-ring and short-ring cases differ only in their boundary and graph
conventions, as described above. No gap remains.
