\section{The source statement}

Lemma~11.5 of~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw} (\path{10-geometry.tex}, lines~573--582)
asserts a fixed constant $c_1>0$ with the following property. Let
$\mathcal B$ be the closed birth region either of an initial belt tile of side
scale $u=t_k$, or of a replacement tile whose removal square has half-side $u$.
Then $\mathcal B$ has distance at least $c_1u^{\rho_0}$ from the final actual
region of every earlier or peer identifier of the same label. Its proof,
lines~584--659, compares closed births with the older and peer regions after the
removal step, uses a compactness argument away from contact marks and an angular
estimate near them, and lets the following trim remove a square of radius
comparable with $u^{\rho_0}$ about every contact mark.

\section{The formal statement}

The formalization proves both halves of the lemma for the actual repair
stages, with one constant $c_1$ fixed before all data.

\emph{Initial belt tiles.} The closed birth of every initial belt tile of side
$u=t_k$ has distance at least $c_1u^{\rho_0}$ from the final actual region of
every primary and of every other initial belt tile with the same label, for
every domain, cut, origin, layer width $C_0\ge2$, lower layer index
$k_0\ge50{,}000{,}000$ and stopping scale; these are the standing restrictions
of the initial construction.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.IsInitialBirthSeparated},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_isInitialBirthSeparated}.} No premise
is used: near the reference cell the primary and belt closures are finite
unions of elementary triangles of one fixed line arrangement, so the finitely
many local patterns of the source become finitely many pairs of such
triangles, and the contacts are marks of the cell by the belt construction.

\emph{Replacements.} Its only premise beyond the choice of the stopping scale is
the conclusion of the uniform repair induction, Lemma~11.4
(lines~471--485), stated as a separate
proposition.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.IsRepairBirthSeparated},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_isRepairBirthSeparated_of_uniformRepairInductionStatement}.}
The separation is proved against every other identifier of the same
generation with the same label, which contains the earlier and peer
identifiers of the source.

Given that premise, one constant serves both
halves.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_birthSeparation_of_uniformRepairInductionStatement}.}
The conclusion of Lemma~11.4 is in turn derived from the conclusion of the
initial isolated-star lemma, Lemma~11.3 (lines~332--349), for the jointly
chosen initial stage, so the combined statement also follows from
that.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_birthSeparation_of_initialIsolatedStars}.}

\section{Resolution}

The conclusion of Lemma~11.3 for the jointly chosen initial stage is now
proved, with $a_0=1/128$.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.initialIsolatedStarsStatement_holds}.}
The combined statement therefore holds without premise: one constant $c_1>0$
and a threshold $T$ serve both halves for every stopping scale $S_*\ge T$,
every layer width and every sufficiently large lower layer index. These are the
standing choices of large $S_*$ and $k_0$ under which Lemma~11.4 and Lemma~11.5
are stated in the source.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.exists_birthSeparation}.}
This is Lemma~11.5 with the source's hypotheses, and the gap is resolved.
