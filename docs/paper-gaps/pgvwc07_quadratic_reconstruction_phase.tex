\section{The printed assertions}

Theorem~8 of P\'erez-Garc\'ia, Verstraete, Wolf, and Cirac considers a
translation-invariant state on a finite ring with unique open-boundary
canonical form and successive reduced-state ranks bounded by $D^2$. It forms a
quadratic system from a window of $D^4$ matrices in the open-boundary canonical
form. Its second conclusion states that every solution supplies a
translation-invariant $D$-dimensional MPS representation of the state and is
related to the canonical tensor by exact unitary conjugacy
\cite[Theorem~8, lines~1154--1165]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix}.

Write $A=(A_i)_i$ for the canonical translation-invariant tensor and
$B=(B_i)_i$ for the tensor component of a solution. The printed conclusion is
therefore
\begin{align}
    V^{(N)}(B)
     & =V^{(N)}(A),
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_printed_state} \\
    A_i
     & =UB_iU^\dagger
    \qquad\text{for every }i
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_printed_conjugacy}
\end{align}
for some unitary $U$. Here $V^{(N)}(B)$ denotes the length-$N$ trace-MPS
vector generated by $B$.

The preceding uniqueness theorem makes the same exact-conjugacy assertion for
two canonical translation-invariant representations of the same finite-ring
state \cite[Theorem~7, lines~1002--1015]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix}. Both
assertions overlook the scalar phase freedom at fixed ring length.

\section{Phase symmetry of the quadratic system}

Use the zero-based notation of the blueprint and the formal quadratic-system
structure. A window has matrices $A_i^{[j]}$ for $0\leq j<m$. A solution
consists of matrices $Y_j,Z_j$ and a tensor $B$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    B_i\otimes\Id
     & =Y_jA_i^{[j]}Z_j,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_system_gauge}   \\
    Y_{j+1}Z_j
     & =\Id
    \qquad(0\leq j<m-1),
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_system_inverse} \\
    \sum_iB_iB_i^\dagger
     & =\Id.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_system_normalization}
\end{align}
This is the shifted indexing of the system printed immediately before
Theorem~8 \cite[lines~1142--1152]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix}.

Let $\eta\in\C$ have unit modulus. Define
\begin{align}
    B_i^{(\eta)}
     & :=\eta B_i,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_rescaled_tensor}     \\
    Y_j^{(\eta)}
     & :=\overline{\eta}^{\,j}Y_j,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_rescaled_left_gauge} \\
    Z_j^{(\eta)}
     & :=\eta^{j+1}Z_j.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_rescaled_right_gauge}
\end{align}
Since $\overline\eta\eta=1$, the gauge equation transforms as
\begin{align}
    Y_j^{(\eta)}A_i^{[j]}Z_j^{(\eta)}
     & =\overline\eta^{\,j}\eta^{j+1}
    Y_jA_i^{[j]}Z_j
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauge_rescaling_step}   \\
     & =\eta(B_i\otimes\Id)
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauge_rescaling_tensor} \\
     & =B_i^{(\eta)}\otimes\Id.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauge_rescaling_result}
\end{align}
The internal inverse equation becomes
\begin{align}
    Y_{j+1}^{(\eta)}Z_j^{(\eta)}
     & =\overline\eta^{\,j+1}\eta^{j+1}Y_{j+1}Z_j
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_inverse_rescaling_step} \\
     & =\Id.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_inverse_rescaling_result}
\end{align}
Finally, the normalization is unchanged:
\begin{align}
    \sum_iB_i^{(\eta)}(B_i^{(\eta)})^\dagger
     & =\eta\overline\eta\sum_iB_iB_i^\dagger
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_normalization_rescaling_step} \\
     & =\Id.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_normalization_rescaling_result}
\end{align}
Thus every solution belongs to a full unit-modulus phase family of solutions.
This freedom is present in the quadratic system itself and is not a virtual
similarity gauge.

\section{Effect on a finite-ring state}

Every length-$N$ coefficient is homogeneous of degree $N$ in the local tensor.
Therefore
\begin{align}
    V^{(N)}(B^{(\eta)})
     & =\eta^N V^{(N)}(B).
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_finite_ring_effect}
\end{align}
Suppose $V^{(N)}(B)$ is the prescribed nonzero state. If $\eta^N\neq1$, then
(\ref{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_finite_ring_effect}) shows that
$B^{(\eta)}$ is another solution but does not represent that state. This
contradicts the unrestricted representation claim
(\ref{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_printed_state}).

If $\eta^N=1$, then $B^{(\eta)}$ does represent the same finite-ring state.
The exact-conjugacy claim can nevertheless fail. Take $d=D=1$, let the single
canonical matrix be
\begin{align}
    A_0
     & =1,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_product_tensor}
\end{align}
and choose $N>3$. This tensor represents a nonzero product state, satisfies
condition C1 with onset $L_0=1$, has rank-one reduced states, and has no
Schmidt-value degeneracy. Let $\eta\neq1$ be an $N$th root of unity and set
\begin{align}
    B_0
     & =\eta A_0.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_product_rephasing}
\end{align}
The phase construction above makes $B$ the tensor component of a solution, and
(\ref{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_finite_ring_effect}) gives
$V^{(N)}(B)=V^{(N)}(A)$. In bond dimension one, unitary conjugation acts
trivially:
\begin{align}
    UB_0U^\dagger
     & =B_0
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_scalar_conjugation} \\
     & \neq A_0.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_scalar_conjugation_failure}
\end{align}
Hence exact unitary conjugacy fails even after restricting to solutions that
represent the same fixed-length state. This also gives a finite-ring phase
counterexample to the exact conclusion printed in Theorem~7.

\section{Scope of the endpoint-cut argument}

The uniqueness argument compares open-boundary representations over the
$D^4$-site window. The complete dimension argument requires one intertwiner at
each of the $D^4+1$ cuts and one relation across each of the $D^4$ sites. The
off-by-one correction is recorded separately in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/pgvwc07_intertwiner_chain_off_by_one.tex}.

The inversion equations of the quadratic system constrain only the gauges at
the $D^4-1$ internal cuts. The two endpoint gauges are unknowns of the system,
but no equation states that they are invertible. There are two ways to supply
the endpoint intertwiners. If the solution tensor is known to represent the
prescribed state, comparison of the surrounding physical cut representations
supplies them; condition C1 and the fixed-length equality give that comparison
in the size regime $N>2L_0+D^4$. For an arbitrary solution over the canonical
window, the left endpoint gauge is forced to be invertible by the internal
equations themselves once the words of some length $1\leq\ell\leq D^4-2$ span
the matrix algebra; this argument is given in
Section~\ref{sec:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_endpoints} below.

Once the endpoint intertwiners are available, the corrected chain argument
gives a nonzero matrix $R$ and a nonzero scalar $x$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    RB_i
     & =x^{-1}A_iR
    \qquad\text{for every }i.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_intertwiner}
\end{align}
The normalization and fixed-point argument then gives
\begin{align}
    |x|
     & =1,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_intertwiner_modulus} \\
    RR^\dagger
     & =c\Id
    \qquad\text{for some }c>0.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_intertwiner_norm}
\end{align}
Thus $U=c^{-1/2}R$ is unitary and
\begin{align}
    A_i
     & =xUB_iU^\dagger
    \qquad\text{for every }i.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_unit_phase_conjugacy}
\end{align}
Unitary conjugation preserves traces of products, so
(\ref{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_unit_phase_conjugacy}) gives
\begin{align}
    V^{(N)}(A)
     & =x^NV^{(N)}(B).
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_same_state_phase_relation}
\end{align}
If $A$ and $B$ generate the same nonzero length-$N$ state, then
\begin{align}
    x^N
     & =1.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_root_condition}
\end{align}

\section{Corrected theorem}

The corrected statement keeps the hypotheses of Theorem~8 and weakens only its
conclusion by the unavoidable phase. Write $A$ for the canonical tensor,
normalized by $\sum_iA_iA_i^\dagger=\Id$; under condition C1 the canonical
form has a single block, whose weight only rescales the state. The system does
not see this weight: its normalization equation fixes the scale of every
solution tensor. The comparison is therefore with the unital block $A$, and a
canonical tensor of weight $\lambda\in(0,1]$ equals $\lambda A$. Let $B$ be the
tensor of any solution of the system over the canonical window. Then there are
a unitary $U$ and a scalar $\xi\in\C$ such that
\begin{align}
    |\xi|
     & =1,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_corrected_unit_modulus} \\
    A_i
     & =\xi UB_iU^\dagger
    \qquad\text{for every }i.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_corrected_conjugacy}
\end{align}
Equivalently, the explicitly rephased tensor
\begin{align}
    \widehat B_i
     & :=\xi B_i
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_corrected_rephasing}
\end{align}
satisfies
\begin{align}
    V^{(N)}(\widehat B)
     & =V^{(N)}(A),
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_rephased_same_state} \\
    A_i
     & =U\widehat B_iU^\dagger
    \qquad\text{for every }i.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_rephased_exact_conjugacy}
\end{align}
The solution tensor $B$ itself generates the vector $V^{(N)}(A)$ exactly when
\begin{align}
    \xi^N
     & =1.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_corrected_root_condition}
\end{align}
The first relation is unit gauge-phase equivalence between $B$ and $A$, while
the second is exact gauge equivalence after the displayed scalar
rephasing.\footnote{The corresponding formal predicates are
    \leanid{MPSTensor.UnitGaugePhaseEquiv} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.GaugeEquiv} in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Defs}.}

\section{Scope decision}

The following readings of Theorem~8 are adopted.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Nonvanishing of the state is a convention. A state is a nonzero
          vector, and the theorem's hypothesis that the state has an
          open-boundary canonical form already forces it: the faithful
          densities of that form exclude the zero vector.
    \item The size condition $N>2L_0+D^4$ is the source's own setup. It is
          hypothesis (iii) of Theorem~7 \cite[lines~1002--1015]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix},
          and the proof of Theorem~8 \cite[lines~1167--1176]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix}
          proceeds by reasoning as in that proof. The system itself
          \cite[lines~1139--1152]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix} also requires the
          open-boundary matrices at the sites $L_0+1,\ldots,L_0+D^4$ to have
          size $D^2\times D^2$, which by the proposition on condition C1
          \cite[lines~911--920]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix} holds when the whole
          window, with both endpoint cuts, lies between $L_0$ and $N-L_0$.
          Stating the size condition therefore adds no hypothesis.
    \item The existence clause is formalized under the source's hypotheses.
          It assumes condition C1 for some translation-invariant
          representation, the size condition, and the canonical equations of
          the given open-boundary representation; uniqueness of the
          open-boundary canonical form and the rank bound are not needed as
          hypotheses.
    \item The hypothesis that the solution tensor represents the prescribed
          state is a genuine restriction. It is part of the printed
          conclusion, not a hypothesis of the source, and the phase-corrected
          conclusion above holds for every solution by the argument of
          Section~\ref{sec:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_endpoints}.
          The source-labelled reconstruction entry of the blueprint states the
          corrected theorem for every solution, and its formal anchor carries
          no such hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The formalized parts}

The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.exists_quadraticSolution_canonicalOBCWindow}
is the existence clause. Its inputs are a translation-invariant tensor
satisfying condition C1 at $L_0$, the size condition, and an open-boundary
representation of the same length-$N$ vector satisfying the source canonical
equations: row normalization, the faithful density recursion, and the scalar
initial density. The density recursion identifies every original bond
dimension with the actual physical cut rank, the periodic representation
bounds these ranks by $D^2$, condition C1 forces rank exactly $D^2$ across the
window, and the physical cut bases supply the gauges. Nonvanishing of the
state is derived from faithfulness. The window entries are the original site
entries in the derived doubled coordinates.

The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.exists_unitary_phase}
is the corrected converse for every solution over the canonical window. Its
inputs are those of the existence clause, a canonical form for the
translation-invariant tensor, and a solution of the system over the original
canonical window. It returns the weight $\lambda\in(0,1]$ of the single
canonical block with $\sum_iA_iA_i^\dagger=\lambda^2\Id$, a unitary $U$ and a
unit-modulus $\xi$ with $A_i=\lambda\xi UB_iU^\dagger$, where $A$ now denotes
the canonical tensor itself. The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.exists_rephased_solution_exact_gauge}
gives the rephased solution of the same system, its equality of length-$N$
vectors with the unital block $\lambda^{-1}A$, exact unitary conjugacy, and
the criterion that $B$ itself represents that vector exactly when $\xi^N=1$.

The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.exists_unitary_phase_of_same_state}
is the converse for same-state solutions over an arbitrary window. It consumes a solution, a canonical
tensor satisfying condition C1 at $L_0$, the size condition, and the equality
of the actual nonzero length-$N$ vectors. The solution tensor is not assumed
canonical or injective. The weighted finite-length uniqueness theorem derives
that the canonical weight is one and gives the unitary and the $N$th-root
phase. The predicate form is
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.unitGaugePhaseEquiv_of_same_state}.
The same-system rephasing
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.rephase}
multiplies the tensor by $\xi$, $Y_j$ by $\xi^{-j}$ and $Z_j$ by $\xi^{j+1}$,
and
\leanid{MPSTensor.QuadraticReconstructionSolution.exists_rephased_solution_exact_gauge_of_same_state}
gives a solution of the same system with the same actual length-$N$ vector and
exact unitary gauge equivalence after rephasing.

\section{Endpoint invertibility for an arbitrary solution}
\label{sec:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_endpoints}

This argument removes the restriction in item~4 of the scope decision. Use
zero-based sites $0\leq j\leq m-1$ with $m=D^4$. The physical
cut bases of the canonical window give invertible matrices
$P_0,\ldots,P_m$ with
\begin{align}
    A_i^{[j]}
     & =P_j(A_i\otimes\Id)P_{j+1}^{-1},
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_window_gauges}
\end{align}
after a geometric rescaling that removes the canonical weight. For a solution
$(B,Y_j,Z_j)$ put $G_j=Y_jP_j$ and $H_j=P_{j+1}^{-1}Z_j$. Then
\begin{align}
    B_i\otimes\Id
     & =G_j(A_i\otimes\Id)H_j,
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauged_system} \\
    G_{j+1}H_j
     & =\Id
    \qquad(0\leq j\leq m-2).
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauged_inverse}
\end{align}
Hence $G_1,\ldots,G_{m-1}$ are invertible and $H_j=G_{j+1}^{-1}$ for
$j\leq m-2$, while $G_0$ and $H_{m-1}$ are unconstrained. A word $w$ of length
$\ell$ placed at the sites $a,\ldots,a+\ell-1$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    B_w\otimes\Id
     & =G_a(A_w\otimes\Id)H_{a+\ell-1}.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_gauged_words}
\end{align}
Suppose that the words of length $\ell$ in $A$ span $M_D(\C)$ for some
$1\leq\ell\leq m-2$. Choose $c_w$ with $\sum_wc_wA_w=\Id$ and put
$X=\sum_wc_wB_w$. Placing the words at the sites $1,\ldots,\ell$ and at
$0,\ldots,\ell-1$ gives
\begin{align}
    X\otimes\Id
     & =G_1G_{\ell+1}^{-1}
    =G_0G_\ell^{-1}.
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_endpoint_identity}
\end{align}
The first expression is invertible, so $G_0$ is invertible. The right endpoint
gauge $H_{m-1}$ is nonzero, because $B_i\otimes\Id=G_{m-1}(A_i\otimes\Id)H_{m-1}$
and some $B_i$ is nonzero by the normalization equation. With $K_0=G_0^{-1}$
and $K_{k+1}=H_k$, all $D^4+1$ cuts carry nonzero matrices with
\begin{align}
    K_k(B_i\otimes\Id)
     & =(A_i\otimes\Id)K_{k+1}
    \qquad(0\leq k\leq m-1),
    \label{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_full_chain}
\end{align}
which is the complete cut family of the off-by-one note. The dependence
argument uses only that these matrices are nonzero. Linear dependence, the
chain-combination lemma, the slice extraction, and the fixed-point argument
then give (\ref{eq:pgvwc07_quadratic_reconstruction_phase_corrected_conjugacy}).

The spanning length exists when $D\geq2$: the adjoint family of the unital
block is trace preserving and satisfies condition C1, so it is primitive; its
letters span a space of dimension $d'\geq2$, since a single matrix generates
word spans of dimension at most one. The quantum Wielandt inequality then
bounds the least spanning length by $(D^2-d'+1)D^2\leq D^4-D^2\leq m-2$. If
$D=1$, the window has one site and $G_0$ is a nonzero scalar: the gauge
equation at that site expresses a nonzero $B_i$ through $G_0$, so $G_0$ is
invertible without a spanning length.

\section{Verdict}

The phase family proves that the unrestricted conclusion of Theorem~8 and the
exact finite-ring conclusion of Theorem~7 are false as printed. The faithful
target is the corrected theorem above, for every solution over the canonical
window. Both its existence clause and its converse for every solution are
formalized with the source's hypotheses, so this note is resolved.
