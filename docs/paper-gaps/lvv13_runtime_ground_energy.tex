\section{The source statement}

Landau, Vazirani and Vidick~\cite{Landau2015Polynomial},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5143v1}{arXiv:1307.5143v1}, source
\path{algo53.tex}, consider $H=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}H_i$ on $n$ qudits of dimension
$d$, with $H_i$ acting on the qudits $i$ and $i+1$ and $0\le H_i\le1$, a unique
ground state $\Gamma$ of energy $\varepsilon_0$, and gap
$\varepsilon=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_0$ (lines 283--285). No bound on
$\varepsilon_0$ is assumed. The unused letter is the identity $P_n=I$. With $C=(1-\varepsilon_0/n)^{-1}$ and $P_i=1-H_i$,
the operator
\begin{align*}
    A=\Bigl(C\Bigl(1-\frac Hn\Bigr)\Bigr)^m
    =C^m\frac1{n^m}\sum_{I\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^m}P_I
\end{align*}
is approximated by the sampling operator
$K=C^m\ell^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^\ell P_{I_j}$ of $\ell$ independent uniform words
(Definition \texttt{d:sagsp}, line 611). Corollary \texttt{c:agsp} (lines
626--638) asserts that some $m=O(\varepsilon^{-1}n\log(q(n)/c_\varepsilon))$
and $\ell=n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$ give $\|K-A\|\le1/q(n)$, with every letter
occurring at most $\kappa\log n$ times in every word, $\kappa=O(1/\varepsilon)$,
with probability at least $1-1/n^3$. The proof (lines 640--644) applies the
matrix Bernstein inequality with $R=C^m+1$ and $\sigma^2\le(C^m+1)^2$, and then
uses
\begin{align}
    C^{2m}=n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}.
    \label{eq:printed}
\end{align}
Theorem~1 of the source (\texttt{thm:main}, lines 213--216) states the running time
$n^{c(d,\varepsilon)}\,\mathrm{poly}(n/\eta)$ with
$c(d,\varepsilon)=2^{O(\log^3d/\varepsilon)}$, independent of $\varepsilon_0$.

\section{The published correction}

The published article~\cite{Landau2015Published}, Methods, states the
complexity exponent as
\begin{align}
    c(d,\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon)
    =2^{O(\log^3d/\varepsilon)}+O(\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon).
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_published_exponent}
\end{align}
The Methods statement of Proposition~1, author PDF page~6, retains the
ground energy in the sampling complexity. The normalization estimate below
makes this dependence explicit. The abstract advertises the uniform
polynomial case with constant ground energy.
The published Supplementary Information, PDF page~6, confirms the same
correction in the proof of Proposition~1: it writes
$C^{2m}=n^{O(\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon)}$ and consequently bounds the
failure probability using a denominator
$n^{O(\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon)}q(n)^2$.  Thus the dependence is present
both in the published Methods statement and in its detailed proof.
The published theorem treats $\varepsilon_0$ as a complexity parameter;
it does not impose an unprinted bounded-energy hypothesis. Thus the omission
of $\varepsilon_0$ in the preprint was corrected in publication. The example below concerns that omitted dependence, and does
not contradict the published theorem.

\section{The point at issue}

Since $1+x\le e^x$,
\begin{align*}
    C=1+\frac{\varepsilon_0}{n-\varepsilon_0}
    \le\exp\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_0}{n-\varepsilon_0}\Bigr),
    \qquad
    C^m\le\exp\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_0m}{n-\varepsilon_0}\Bigr).
\end{align*}
If $\varepsilon_0\le cn$ for a fixed $c<1$, then at powers
$m=O((n/\varepsilon)\log n)$ this gives
$C^{2m}=n^{O(\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon)}$. Even in this range the estimate
is not uniform in the ground energy. The hypotheses $0\le H_i\le1$
allow $\varepsilon_0$ of order $n$; the following family shows that
(\ref{eq:printed}) then fails. The energy-dependent choice of power in
the corrected statement below also covers energies closer to $n$.

\section{A family with extensive ground energy}

Take qubits, $n\ge2$, and let $N_i=|1\rangle\langle1|$ on qubit $i$. Put
\begin{align*}
    H_i=\frac12+\frac14(N_i+N_{i+1}),\qquad 1\le i\le n-1.
\end{align*}
Each $H_i$ acts on the qubits $i$ and $i+1$ and has eigenvalues
$\tfrac12,\tfrac34,1$, so $0\le H_i\le1$. The terms commute, and
\begin{align*}
    H=\frac{n-1}2+\frac14(N_1+N_n)+\frac12\sum_{1<i<n}N_i
\end{align*}
is diagonal in the computational basis. Its unique ground state is
$|0\cdots0\rangle$, with $\varepsilon_0=(n-1)/2$, and its first excited states
flip one end qubit, so $\varepsilon=1/4$ for every $n$. Then
\begin{align*}
    C=\frac{2n}{n+1}\ge\frac43,
    \qquad
    \frac{1-\varepsilon_1/n}{1-\varepsilon_0/n}
    =\frac{n-\varepsilon_1}{n-\varepsilon_0}=1-\frac1{2n+2}.
\end{align*}
The choice of $m$ must make the bound of equation \texttt{e:cutdown} (line 603)
at most $c_\varepsilon/(2q(n))<1$. Since $-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)$, this forces
$m\ge(2n+1)\log(2q(n)/c_\varepsilon)$, and then
\begin{align*}
    C^{2m}\ge\Bigl(\frac43\Bigr)^{2(2n+1)\log(2q(n)/c_\varepsilon)}
    =\Bigl(\frac{2q(n)}{c_\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2(2n+1)\log(4/3)},
\end{align*}
whose exponent grows linearly in $n$ at fixed $\varepsilon$. So
(\ref{eq:printed}) is false for this family, and the Bernstein estimate of the
proof, which needs $\ell$ of order $C^{2m}q(n)^2\log(d^n n^3)$, does not give a
polynomial number of words. Rescaling $K$, which the source allows (line 614),
does not help: the estimate controls $\|K-A\|$ relative to $\|A\|=1$, and the
ratio $R/\|A\|$ is unchanged by a common rescaling.

This note does not decide whether some other argument gives a polynomial $\ell$
for this family; it records only that the printed step and the stated
dependence of $\ell$ and of the running time on $\varepsilon$ alone do not
follow from the source's argument.

\section{A commuting chain with zero local minima}

Let $n=2r+1$ with $r\ge1$, and let $N_j=|1\rangle\langle1|_j$ on a chain
of $n$ qubits. For $1\le k\le r$, set
\begin{align}
    H_{2k-1} & =\frac34N_{2k}+\frac18N_{2k-1},     \\
    H_{2k}   & =\frac12(I-N_{2k})+\frac18N_{2k+1}.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_chain}
\end{align}
Each term is supported on its prescribed adjacent pair, has smallest
eigenvalue zero, and satisfies $0\le H_i\le(7/8)I$. All terms commute.
Their sum is
\begin{align}
    H=\frac r2I+\frac14\sum_{k=1}^{r}N_{2k}
    +\frac18(N_1+N_n)+\frac14\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}N_{2k+1}.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_hamiltonian}
\end{align}
Consequently $\Gamma=|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ is the unique ground vector,
$\varepsilon_0=r/2$, and the gap is exactly $1/8$, attained by flipping
either endpoint. This is a nondegenerate example even if zero local
minimum is imposed as an additional convention.

\section{Failure of the claimed normalized operator-norm estimate}

Put $p=r/n$, so $1/3\le p<1/2$. On $\Gamma$, the operators $P_i$ have
eigenvalue $1$ for odd $i$ and for the dummy label, and eigenvalue $1/2$
for even $i$. For a uniform word of length $m$, let $B$ count the even
labels. Then $B$ has the binomial distribution with parameters $m,p$, and
the eigenvalue of $C^mP_I$ on $\Gamma$ is $Z=C^m2^{-B}$, with
$C=(1-p/2)^{-1}$. In particular,
\begin{align}
    \mathbb EZ=\bigl(C(1-p/2)\bigr)^m=1.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_word_law}
\end{align}
Its square-root moment is
\begin{align}
    \mathbb E\sqrt Z & =\alpha_p^m,                        \\
    \alpha_p         & =\frac{1-p+p/\sqrt2}{\sqrt{1-p/2}}.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_fractional_moment}
\end{align}
Writing $t=1-1/\sqrt2$, direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
    \alpha_p^2
    =1-\frac{p(1-p)t^2}{1-p/2}
    \le 1-\frac1{96}=\frac{95}{96}.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_moment_decay}
\end{align}
Indeed $p(1-p)\ge1/6$, $t\ge1/4$, and $0<1-p/2\le1$.

For $\ell\ge1$ sampled words, let
$Y=\ell^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_j$, so $K\Gamma=Y\Gamma$.
The inequality $\sqrt{\sum_jZ_j}\le\sum_j\sqrt{Z_j}$ and Markov's
inequality imply
\begin{align}
    \Pr(Y\ge1/2)
     & \le \sqrt2\,\mathbb E\sqrt Y
    \le\sqrt{2\ell}\,\alpha_p^m
    \le\sqrt{2\ell}\left(\frac{95}{96}\right)^{m/2}.
    \label{eq:lvv_sampling_failure}
\end{align}
Since $A\Gamma=\Gamma$, the event $\|K-A\|\le1/n$ implies
$Y\ge1-1/n\ge1/2$. For every fixed polynomial bound on $\ell$ and every
$m\ge cn$ with $c>0$ fixed, the right side of
(\ref{eq:lvv_sampling_failure}) tends to zero. In particular, the
prescribed power of order $n\log n$ cannot have the claimed success
probability with a polynomial number of uniform samples.
This concerns the stated normalization and additive operator-norm estimate.
It does not say that every normalized output vector of this commuting
example is poorly approximated: a nonzero scalar multiple of the ground
vector has the same normalized direction.

The bare upper bound $m=O(n\log n)$ also permits small powers, including
$m=0$. The contradiction is to the sampled approximate projection at
the powers required by equation~\path{e:cutdown}, not to an
upper-bound statement interpreted without that requirement.

\section{The corrected statement}

With $R=C^m+1$, an accuracy $\eta>0$, and
\begin{align*}
    \ell\ge\frac{2R^2+2R\eta/3}{\eta^2}\log(8d^nn^3),\qquad
    \kappa\ge\frac{2m}n+2\log(4\ell n^4),
\end{align*}
the matrix Bernstein inequality, the scalar Bernstein inequality for the
number of occurrences of a letter, and a union bound over the $\ell$ words and
the $n$ letters give $\|K-A\|<\eta$ and at most $\kappa$ occurrences of every
letter in every word with probability at least $1-1/(2n^3)$. This holds for
every $m$ and every $\varepsilon_0$. At
$m=\lceil((n-\varepsilon_0)/\varepsilon)\log(1/\eta)\rceil$ and
$0<\eta\le1$, the bound $\varepsilon_0\le n-1$ gives
\begin{align*}
    C^m\le n\,\eta^{-\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon}.
\end{align*}
Hence $\ell$ is polynomial in $n$ and $1/\eta$ whenever $\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon$
is bounded, and $\kappa=O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log n)$ for $\eta=1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$
in that case, as in the source. For general $\varepsilon_0$, $\log\ell$ and
$\kappa$ carry the additional term
$O((\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon)\log(1/\eta))$, and the exponent of the running
time of Theorem~1 acquires a term of order
$\varepsilon_0/\varepsilon$.

\section{Formalization}

The sampling operator is \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjector}, with
the explicit $\ell$ and $\kappa$ of the previous section
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorWordCount} and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorLetterBound}. The probability form is
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.one_sub_le_measureReal_sampledProjector}, and the
existence form, with the rank bound of equation \texttt{e:decomp}, is
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_sampledProjector}. The bounds on $C^m$ are
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.powerNormalization_pow_le_exp} and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.powerNormalization_pow_ceil_le}. These statements
carry only the source's hypotheses. The statement $\ell=n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$
for unbounded $\varepsilon_0$, and the running time of Theorem~1,
are not formalized. The published article corrects the energy dependence;
the formalization of its running-time assertion remains open.

The corrected dependence of the sample count is explicit rather than merely
existential.  For inverse-polynomial accuracy $c/L^s$ and a fixed bound
$B\ge\varepsilon_0/\Delta$, put $r=\lceil B\rceil$.  The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampledProjectorWordCount\_polynomial\_explicit\_degree}
uses the degree
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorWordDegree}
\begin{align*}
  2(sr+1)+(2s+1)=2sr+2s+3.
\end{align*}
Thus the formal sample-count exponent displays the published linear dependence
on the ground-energy-to-gap ratio.  This dependence also passes through the
simultaneous cut decomposition.  The declarations
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorLetterSlope},
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorRankDegree}, and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorTotalDegree} set
\begin{align*}
  a_{\rm letter}&=\frac{2s}{\Delta}+2(p_{\rm word}+4), &
  p_{\rm rank}&=\left\lceil2a_{\rm letter}\log q\right\rceil, &
  p_{\rm total}&=p_{\rm word}+p_{\rm rank}.
\end{align*}
Then
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampledProjector\_uniform\_polynomial\_explicit\_degree}
proves both $\ell D\le CL^{p_{\rm total}}$ and
$D\le CL^{p_{\rm total}}$.  The scalar theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledProjectorTotalDegree\_le\_affine} makes the
linear dependence precise:
\begin{align*}
 p_{\rm total}&\le p_{\rm int}+p_{\rm slope}\lceil B\rceil,\\
 p_{\rm slope}&=2s+\lceil8s\log q\rceil,\\
 p_{\rm int}&=2s+3+
 \left\lceil\left(\frac{4s}{\Delta}+8s+28\right)\log q\right\rceil.
\end{align*}
Thus the complete sampled error-reduction factor has a proved exponent affine
in $\lceil B\rceil$.  The remaining open point
is not a loss at the later existence steps: the theorems
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampled\_viable\_successor\_polynomial\_explicit\_energy\_degree}
and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampled\_successive\_viable\_sets\_polynomial\_explicit\_energy\_degree}
give size and bond degrees $p_{\rm total}+p_0$ and
$p_{\rm total}+r_0$, where the offsets are independent of $B$.  Finally,
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampled\_groundState\_openMPS\_polynomial\_explicit\_energy\_degree}
produces the normalized final MPS with degree $2p_{\rm total}+d_0$, again
with $d_0$ independent of $B$.  The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.sampledGroundStateDegree\_le\_affine} bounds this by
$(2p_{\rm int}+d_0)+2p_{\rm slope}\lceil B\rceil$.  What remains open is the cost of an
implemented algorithm carrying out the successive optimization steps, not the
energy dependence of the mathematical construction.

One part of that finite work is now counted explicitly.  The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_sampled\_successive\_viable\_sets\_polynomial\_total\_cardinality}
chooses the successive families so that, with
$p=p_{\rm total}+p_0$,
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{i=0}^{L}|S_i|\le CL^{p+1}.
\end{align*}
Thus the total number of retained candidate vectors over the entire sweep is
polynomial with the same affine ground-energy dependence, up to one additional
power of $L$.  The number of boundary-contraction convex programs is also
finite and polynomial: at one cut,
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_boundaryNets\_polynomial\_total\_cardinality}
chooses all actual-bond nets with
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{m=0}^{D}|N_m|\le(D+1)(2A+1)^pL^p,
  \qquad p=2(qD)^2.
\end{align*}
The sweep theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_boundaryNets\_sweep\_polynomial\_total\_cardinality}
chooses these nets at every positive cut and proves
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{m=0}^{D}|N_{i,m}|
  \le(D+1)(2A+1)^pL^{p+1}.
\end{align*}
The net used by the size-trimming construction may moreover be chosen with
Hermitian centers, without changing either the covering radius or this
cardinality bound. Indeed, replacing a center $X$ by $(X+X^*)/2$ cannot
increase its trace-norm distance from a Hermitian boundary density. Thus the
boundary residual belongs to the real space of Hermitian matrices. The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.compressedBoundary\_rectangularTraceNorm\_le\_iff\_exists\_posSemidef\_sub}
now gives the corresponding semidefinite representation explicitly: there are
$P,N\ge0$ such that the residual is $P-N$ and
$\operatorname{tr}P+\operatorname{tr}N\le\eta$. The predicate
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsCompressedBoundarySDPFeasibleAtEnergy}
also includes positivity and normalization of the compressed density and its
linear energy inequality. Its feasibility is proved equivalent to the original
compressed density problem at every energy threshold. The finite-precision
interface also controls an inexact boundary-residual equation: trace-norm
error $\delta$ enlarges the physical boundary tolerance from $\eta$ to
$\eta+\delta$, without a dimension factor. Approximate positivity is also
repaired explicitly: for a Hermitian trace-one candidate with least eigenvalue
at least $-\epsilon$, shifting and normalizing gives an exact density within
$2n\epsilon$ in trace norm. Combined with the inexact boundary equation, the
resulting density has boundary tolerance $\eta+\delta+2n\epsilon$ and energy
threshold enlarged by $2n\epsilon\lVert K\rVert$. The exact-normalization
assumption has also been removed: trace defect $\kappa$ gives an exact density
within $\kappa+2n\epsilon$, boundary tolerance
$\eta+\delta+\kappa+2n\epsilon$, and energy slack
$\lVert K\rVert(\kappa+2n\epsilon)$. A complete polynomial-time solver
analysis may also return auxiliary boundary matrices whose least eigenvalues
are only bounded below by $-\epsilon_b$. Shifting both auxiliary matrices by
$\epsilon_b I$ preserves their difference exactly and adds
$2r\epsilon_b$ to the boundary trace budget. Thus all three positive-cone
constraints now admit explicit spectral repair.  The rational exhaustive
search described below produces certified $\delta$, $\kappa$, $\epsilon$, and
$\epsilon_b$ and carries explicit schoolbook arithmetic accounting.  What
remains is the polynomial bit-complexity estimate asserted by the source.
The residual norm itself can now be certified from ordinary matrix entries.
The theorems
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.rectangularTraceNorm\_le\_card\_sq\_mul\_of\_entry\_norm\_le}
and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsAllSpectralApproxCompressedBoundarySDPFeasibleAtEnergy.of\_entrywise\_residual}
show that an order-$r$ residual whose entries have modulus at most $\zeta$ has
trace norm at most $r^2\zeta$.  Hence certified entrywise arithmetic with
$r^2\zeta\le\delta$ directly discharges the residual clause of the unified
solver specification.
Spectral certification is now reduced to entries as well.  The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.neg\_le\_minEigenvalue\_of\_gershgorin}
proves that a Hermitian matrix whose Gershgorin row intervals lie to the right
of $-\epsilon$ has least eigenvalue at least $-\epsilon$.  Its three-variable
wrapper
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsAllSpectralApproxCompressedBoundarySDPFeasibleAtEnergy.of\_gershgorin}
combines these row tests for the density and two auxiliary matrices with the
entrywise residual test.  Gershgorin is only a sufficient shortcut and imposes
no condition on the matrices accepted by the general solver interface.  The
complete alternative is the equivalence
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.Matrix.IsHermitian.neg\_le\_minEigenvalue\_iff\_exists\_gram\_factor}:
$-\epsilon\le\lambda_{\min}(A)$ if and only if
$A+\epsilon I=RR^*$ for some complex matrix $R$.  The wrapper
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsAllSpectralApproxCompressedBoundarySDPFeasibleAtEnergy.of\_gram\_factors}
accepts such factors for all three cone variables and combines them with the
entrywise residual test.  Thus the nonscalar conditions have a complete
finite algebraic certificate; trace and energy remain scalar comparisons.
The factor identities may also be approximate.  The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.neg\_le\_minEigenvalue\_of\_approx\_gram\_factor}
shows that entrywise factorization error $\zeta$ in order $r$ costs at most
$(r+1)\zeta$ in the spectral lower bound.  Its unified wrapper
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsAllSpectralApproxCompressedBoundarySDPFeasibleAtEnergy.of\_approx\_gram\_factors}
absorbs these errors for all three cone variables into $\epsilon$ and
$\epsilon_b$.  Exact Gram factors are recovered at $\zeta=0$, so this stable
certificate does not narrow the feasible cone.  Constructing and encoding
suitable matrices and factors with polynomial bit complexity remains open.
The elementary input-size implication is now explicit.  The theorems

most $2(M^3+2M^2+2)(p+1)$.  Consequently the remaining bit-complexity task is
sharply separated into proving polynomial bounds for the structured orders and

\section{Overall scaling and an unknown energy}

The scalar $C^m$ is not itself an obstacle to using the left-factor
construction without an energy input. The published Methods discussion before Proposition~1,
author PDF page~6, explicitly observes that an overall scalar is immaterial after
normalizing the output vectors. The same point occurs in
\path{algo53.tex}, line~614. One may use the unscaled sum
$\ell^{-1}\sum_jP_{I_j}$; multiplication of all factors by one nonzero
scalar preserves their spans and Schmidt ranks.

What remains energy-dependent in the present formal statement is the
selection of its word length, relative error and sample budget.
A known upper bound on the energy, or a proved sequence of trial bounds
with a valid acceptance and termination argument, would address a different
question from computing the immaterial scalar. Such a bound cannot be
inferred merely by subtracting the global ground energy while retaining
positive original local contractions.

Both source versions use the power of the full original nearest-neighbour
Hamiltonian. Its left and right block sums occur in the size-trimming
optimization; they do not replace the original letters in this sampler.
Subtracting their two separate minima gives a residual ground energy in
$[0,1]$, but the shifted block operators are nonlocal and can have norm of
order the chain length. Their internal-cut ranks are not controlled by the
original-letter estimate. Substituting them would require new norm and
spatial-rank arguments.

\section{Uniform successive families}

Fix the local dimension $q\ge2$ and a positive lower bound $\Delta$ for
the spectral gap. The Gaussian energy-mesh construction, following
Huang~\cite[Section~5.4]{Huang2015GroundState}, supplies a
sequence of viable families whose size and individual bond dimensions
are bounded by one polynomial in the chain length. The coefficients and
degree are chosen before the length and interaction, and the families
are chosen before any ground energy or ground vector. The theorem is
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_uniform_positive_chain_successive_approximation}.
Its proof performs extension, size trimming, independent bond trimming
and Gaussian error reduction at each cut. The final approximation lies
in the span of the family produced at the last cut.

Arbitrary accuracy follows by adjoining $2R^2$ sites with product-state
penalties, for a prescribed integer $R\ge1$. The extended chain has
length $N=L+2R^2$ and gap at least $g=\min\{\Delta,1\}$. A normalized
column of the stage-$L$ witness, with its phase chosen, gives an
original-chain matrix product state $\psi$. There are natural numbers
$P,d$, depending only on $q,\Delta$, such that
\begin{align}
    \|\psi-\Gamma\|^2 & \le R^{-2},        &
    D(\psi)           & \le P(L+2R^2+1)^d.
    \label{eq:lvv_gaussian_arbitrary_accuracy}
\end{align}
The same polynomial bounds the cardinalities and individual bond
dimensions at every stage of the extended construction. This is
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_uniform_positive_chain_successive_accuracy}.
The extension by product states is a consequence of the construction,
not a step claimed to occur in the cited papers.

These results use the corrected trimming radius and the constant-cut
bond described in \path{lvv13_constant_bond.tex}. They require no bound
on the ground energy. They do not repair the false normalized estimate
for the original uniform sampler, establish the sharper printed
dependence on the inverse gap, or assert a computational running time.
The source-level construction and complexity comparisons remain
tracked by issues \#9822 and \#9823 under \#9832.
