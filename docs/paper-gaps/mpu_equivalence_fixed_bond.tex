\section{Source definition}

Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal V$ be matrix product unitary tensors with
physical dimensions $d_a$ and $d_b$. Let $p_a$ and $p_b$ be positive coprime
ancilla dimensions. Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete define strict
equivalence through a continuous path of MPU tensors. They define equivalence
by first attaching identity ancillas and then applying a common blocking length
\cite[lines~706--724]{Cirac2017MPU}. The enlarged physical dimensions satisfy
\begin{align}
    p_a d_a &= p_b d_b.
    \label{eq:mpu_equivalence_fixed_bond_physical_dimension_balance}
\end{align}
The cited definition does not provide a stabilization or identification for
unequal raw virtual bond dimensions.

\section{Formal scope}

The declarations \leanid{MPOTensor.StrictlyEquivalent} and
\leanid{MPOTensor.Equivalent} formalize strict equivalence and equivalence,
respectively. A continuous path must lie in one ambient topological space.
Both definitions therefore fix one virtual bond dimension $D$ along the path
and at its endpoints. They compare different physical dimensions only through
the explicit equality in
Eq.~\ref{eq:mpu_equivalence_fixed_bond_physical_dimension_balance} and the
induced reindexing of physical coordinates.

For the weaker equivalence relation, the definitions attach identity ancillas
before blocking, as prescribed in Ref.~\cite{Cirac2017MPU}. Extending this
relation to unequal raw virtual bond dimensions would require an additional
mathematical stabilization convention that the cited passage does not supply.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{Verdict: scope restriction.} The formal definitions are faithful to
the source within one fixed ambient virtual bond dimension. They do not assert
or require a relation between tensors with unequal raw virtual bond
dimensions. Any such relation requires a separately named stabilization
convention and should not be incorporated silently into the source
definitions.
