\section{Source range}

After the successive singular value decompositions that write a state as
$\ket{\tilde\psi}=V'_{[n]}\cdots V'_{[1]}\ket{\varphi_I}$,
Ref.~\cite[lines~200--202 of the source]{Schoen2005Sequential} states:
``Simple rank considerations show that $V'_{[n-k]}$ has dimension
$2\min[D,2^k]\times\min[D,2^{k+1}]$.'' Ref.~\cite[Section~5.1,
lines~1574--1577]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix} adds that this proof ``also provides
a \emph{recipe} for the generation of any given state (with minimal
resources).''

\section{What is formalized}

For local dimension $d$, the formalization proves the upper bounds: the
isometric open-boundary representation produced by the decompositions has
bond dimension at most $\min(D,d^k)$ at the bond after $k$ sites counted
from the end where the decompositions start, and, after running the
decompositions once from each end, at most $\min(D_k,d^k,d^{N-k})$, where
$D_k\le D$ is the bond dimension of the given representation at that cut.
Read as a statement that isometries of the printed size suffice, the first
quoted sentence is formalized with the source's hypotheses.

\section{Restriction}

Two stronger readings are not formalized. First, the first quoted sentence
can be read as asserting that the dimension is exactly
$2\min[D,2^k]\times\min[D,2^{k+1}]$. Second, the phrase ``with minimal
resources'' asserts that each bond dimension of the produced representation
equals the Schmidt rank of the state across the corresponding cut, the least
bond dimension of any open-boundary representation there. Neither equality
is proved.

\section{Elimination plan}

Prove that, when the decompositions keep only the nonzero singular values,
the bond dimension at each cut equals the rank of the coefficient matrix
across that cut, and that this rank bounds every open-boundary bond
dimension at the cut from below.
