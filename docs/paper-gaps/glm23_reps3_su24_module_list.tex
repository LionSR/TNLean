\section{The source claims}
Garre-Rubio, Lootens and Moln\'ar list the phases of matrix product states
symmetric under matrix product operators representing $su(2)_4$ and
$\mathrm{Rep}(S_3)$~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym}. For $su(2)_4$, with labels
$0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2$, they state that there are only two phases, the one
with five blocks acted on by the fusion rules and the module
$\mathcal M_{TY}$ with four blocks $x,y,z,s$, and they print the action of
every label on the blocks of $\mathcal M_{TY}$.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/2203.12563/REsubmission.tex}, lines 1890--1931.} For
$\mathrm{Rep}(S_3)$, with labels $1,\pi,\psi$, they state that every phase
is characterized by a subgroup of $S_3$, and print the actions on the blocks
for $H=\mathbb Z_1,\mathbb Z_2,\mathbb Z_3,S_3$. They identify the
$\mathbb Z_1$, $\mathbb Z_2$ and $\mathbb Z_3$ phases with restrictions of
the $su(2)_4$ phases along the embedding $1,\pi,\psi\mapsto0,1,2$: the block
$s$ of $\mathcal M_{TY}$, the blocks $\tfrac12,\tfrac32$ of the first phase,
and the blocks $x,y,z$ of $\mathcal M_{TY}$.\footnote{Source traceability:
    lines 1933--1989; the restrictions at lines 1927, 1940, 1957 and 1972.}
Write $N_{ab}^c$ for the multiplicity of $c$ in $a\times b$. The
multiplicities $M_{a,x}^y$ of each phase form a nonnegative integer
representation of the fusion ring,
\begin{align}
    \sum_c N_{ab}^c M_{c,x}^y & =\sum_z M_{b,x}^z M_{a,z}^y ,
    \label{eq:glm23_list_nimrep}
\end{align}
with the unit acting as the identity.

\section{Completeness of the lists}
Formalized: every listed action satisfies (\ref{eq:glm23_list_nimrep}), with the $\mathbb Z_3$ action
corrected as recorded in the note on the $\mathbb Z_3$ table, the restrictions
stated by the source hold, and on a single block the list is complete. A representation on one
block with the unit acting trivially is a ring homomorphism to $\mathbb Z$
that is nonnegative on the labels; for $\mathrm{Rep}(S_3)$ the only one is
$(1,2,1)$, the $\mathbb Z_1$ phase, and $su(2)_4$ has none, since it would force
$m_2=1$, $m_1=2$ and $m_{1/2}^2=3$.\footnote{Results:
    \leanid{MPOTensor.isFusionCharacter_repS3Fusion_iff} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.not_isFusionCharacter_su24Fusion}.}

Completeness beyond one block is not a consequence of
(\ref{eq:glm23_list_nimrep}) with trivial unit action alone. The source takes
it from the classification of module categories, which it cites. For
$\mathrm{Rep}(S_3)$ on two blocks,
\begin{align}
    M_\psi & =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, &
    M_\pi  & =\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\2&1\end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:glm23_list_counterexample}
\end{align}
satisfies (\ref{eq:glm23_list_nimrep}), but the representation
(\ref{eq:glm23_list_counterexample}) is neither the $\mathbb Z_2$
phase nor a sum of two copies of the $\mathbb Z_1$ phase. It violates the
duality $M_{a^*}=M_a^{\mathsf T}$ of a module category, as $\pi$ is
self-dual.\footnote{Results:
    \leanid{MPOTensor.isNIMRep_repS3NonDual} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.not_isTransposeDual_repS3NonDual}.}

\section{Completeness among dual representations}
Formalized: with the duality added, the lists are complete on any finite
number of blocks. Every label of either ring is self-dual, so the duality
says that every $M_a$ is symmetric. In a representation of
$\mathrm{Rep}(S_3)$ satisfying (\ref{eq:glm23_list_nimrep}) with trivial unit
action and symmetric matrices, every block lies in a direct summand
isomorphic to the $\mathbb Z_1$, $\mathbb Z_2$, $\mathbb Z_3$ or $S_3$
phase; for $su(2)_4$, every block lies in a direct summand isomorphic to the
five-block phase or to $\mathcal M_{TY}$. An indecomposable representation
is one of the listed phases.\footnote{Results:
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_isNIMRepSummand_repS3},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_equiv_of_isIndecomposable_repS3},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_isNIMRepSummand_su24} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_equiv_of_isIndecomposable_su24}.}

The argument is local. The matrix $M_\psi$ (or $M_2$) squares to the
identity and is a symmetric matrix of natural numbers, so it is the
permutation matrix of an involution $\sigma$ of the blocks. With
$T=M_\pi$ (or $M_1$), the relations $T=M_\psi T$ and
$T^2=1+T+M_\psi$ give, on the diagonal,
$\sum_z T_{xz}^2=1+T_{xx}+[\sigma x=x]$. This bounds $T_{xx}\le2$ and
leaves a short list of local configurations around each block, each of
which closes into one of the listed summands. For $su(2)_4$ the relations
$\tfrac12\times\tfrac12=0+1$ and $\tfrac12\times2=\tfrac32$ then fix the
action of $\tfrac12$ around each such configuration.

The remaining restriction is in the input: the multiplicities of a module
category are taken with their duality as hypotheses, and the module
categories themselves (their associativity data) are not classified. The
lists are identified at the level of the multiplicities, which is the level
at which the source prints them.
