\section{The source proposition}

Proposition~9.2 of the two-dimensional area-law manuscript of September~24,
2026~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw} (\path{08-scanner.tex}, lines~349--397) states
that, under the scan scales and the entropy input
$S_\Omega(X)\le C_en^{1+e}$, $S_\Omega(Q_j)\le C_e(nL^e+n)$, for every
sufficiently large replica count $k$ some charge round and some common
parameter $p_k\in[\epsilon/2,\epsilon]$ give the defect-energy bound
\[
    E_{\mathrm{def},k}\le Cn^\ell D^2W^2(\log n)^C
    \bigl(\delta_n^{1/8}+\epsilon+n^{-100}\bigr)+Cn^{-1000}+o_k(1),
\]
with $\delta_n=C_e\epsilon^{-1}(n^{e-\mu}+a^{1/4}(\log n)^C)$, and with the
comparator budgets $B_{\mathrm{sh}}\le C_e(nL^e+n)$,
$B_{\mathrm{exc}}\le CnD$ and $\mathcal B\le CnD(\log n)^C$.

Its proof (lines~399--533) uses Lemma~9.1 (the scan histories),
Proposition~7.4 (transport), Proposition~8.1 (comparators), Lemma~2.1
(continuity) and Lemma~2.3 (tails) for the concrete scan.

\section{The restriction}

The formal statement takes the conclusions of those five results, for one
scan at scale $n$, as the fields of one data package, and proves the step of
the source from these conclusions to Proposition~9.2: the integrated entropy
balance over the charge rounds, the averaging selection of a round and of a
parameter with $\mathcal Q(p_k)/(KnD)\le\delta_n+\rho_k$, the H\"older
accounting of the defect energy at the selected point, and the three
comparator budgets.\footnote{The packaged statement is
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Scan.scanner_estimate}.} Each field transcribes
the relevant input conclusion and cites its source passage, except that the
charge-defect continuity requirement is weakened to the almost-everywhere
strong measurability sufficient for the two integral arguments. In particular,
the transported-measure mass equality is required only for interior
interpolation parameters. The constants depend only on the fixed exponents
and on the constants of the inputs, as in the source.

Two harmless differences in the bookkeeping are recorded. The continuity
input bounds the change of the shell entropies by
$C\sqrt{n^{-500}}(1+Cn^2\log q)$, which is $O(n^{-248})$ but not at most
$n^{-248}$ itself; the formal budget lemmas carry the error $n^{-247}$. The
marginal parameter is bounded with logarithmic power $12$, coming from the
crossing count $(\log n)^4$ and the squared dimension term $(\log n)^8$; the
source writes an unspecified power $C$.

\section{Parameter domain of the transported measures}

The transported states of Proposition~7.4 are density matrices for
$0<p<1$ (\path{06-transport.tex}, lines~393--400), so the combined
Fourier--coherent measures have mass $1/2$ there. At $p=1$ an old terminal
leaf has weight $(1-p)w_h=0$, and at $p=0$ a new terminal leaf has weight
$pw_hq_{c\mid h}=0$. The totalized normalized derivative map contains the
factor $\pi_j^{-1}$ and is zero when $\pi_j=0$. Its transported state and
combined measure are then zero. Requiring mass $1/2$ for every real $p$
would therefore exclude the literal transported measures.

The mass hypotheses now have the interior domain $0<p<1$. The measures
remain defined at every parameter; no replacement probability measure is
assigned to an inactive leaf. The charge-defect bound and the old- and
new-leaf energy estimates use the mass hypothesis only in the interior.
The integral over
$[0,1]$ uses the interior bounds and almost-everywhere strong measurability
of the charge defect, since the two boundary points have Lebesgue measure zero. The logarithmic
norm remains continuous on $[0,1]$, and its matching boundary values,
initial and final comparisons, and integrated entropy conclusions are
unchanged. This domain correction does not construct the transported
measures or remove the remaining scanner-input restriction.

\section{Parameter regularity from actual transported expectations}

The present source candidate derives parameter integrability of the exact
old-state Fourier--coherent scalar integral from the accepted transported
expectation theorem. For a continuous real coherent symbol $F$, let $A_F$
be its coherent average and $Q_F=(A_F+A_F^\dagger)/2$. Positivity of the
actual state gives Hermiticity, hence
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_F)
    =\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A_F)
    =\mathcal H_\rho(F).
\end{align}
For a nonzero supplied prevector the accepted parameter-measurability result
and bound $|J_h(F)|\le\|Q_F\|/2$ give integrability on $(0,1)$ and $[0,1]$.
For a zero prevector the actual filtered vector and trace-adjoint state are
zero. This argument includes zero copies and does not assume symmetry.
Finite sums with the original fixed good-history predicate and weights give
the regularity of the actual charge defect.\footnote{The source declarations are
    \leanid{TensorPower.ReplicaTransport.TransportData.intervalIntegrable_oldFourierCoherentIntegral},
    \leanid{TensorPower.ReplicaTransport.TransportData.intervalIntegrable_sum_oldFourierCoherentIntegral},
    and \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Scan.CollarScan.intervalIntegrable_actualChargeEntropyDefect}.}

The scanner package now asks only for almost-everywhere strong measurability
of its scalar charge defect. Its existing nonnegativity and upper bound derive
interval integrability. The derivative telescope retains closed-interval
continuity of the logarithmic norm, differentiation at every interior point,
and the pointwise lower bound; its integral comparison requires only an
integrable lower bound. No absolute continuity assumption is added.
For exact selection, finite averaging chooses a round whose integral is at
most the threshold times interval length. If the original pointwise defect
were strictly above that threshold everywhere, the integral of their
integrable difference would be strictly positive, a contradiction. Thus
selection preserves the exact non-strict inequality, the same round and
parameter, all constants, and the existing remainder.

This derives integrability rather than the manuscript's asserted parameter
continuity. The present combined source changes are not yet compiled. The
finite-sum theorem is connected below to literal fill and charge rounds by
their exact scalar identities. The scalar expectation argument uses the
accepted dependency. The literal-measure construction and its specializations
now use the accepted canonical measure theorem at the updated dependency
revision. This dependency integration does not validate the uncompiled
combined source.
The full scanner package, common physical prevector and comparators, and
lattice ground-state construction remain separate obligations.

\section{Literal measure, scalar identities and parameter regularity}

A private, uncompiled consumer of the accepted canonical measure theorem now
sets the one-copy domain to its actual unit sphere and takes the old and new
measures from the very same terminal transport states, supplied replica vector,
and root path. No classical history or conditional-choice weight is inserted
inside a measure. For $a\ge0$, the source identifies integrals of continuous
ambient symbols restricted to the sphere with the existing Fourier--coherent
integrals. Fixed-parameter finiteness needs no analytic certificate or sign
assumption on $a$. For $a\ge0$ and a supplied nonzero symmetric vector,
the interior mass is $1/2$; this includes zero copies with a nonzero scalar
vector. Inactive old and new leaves at $p=1$ and $p=0$ retain zero measure
without any vector or sign assumption. For $a\ge0$ and a nonzero symmetric
vector, the complementary active leaves have mass $1/2$. No endpoint measure
is replaced by an arbitrary probability.

defect with the existing entropy gain and actual good-history defect for every

to $p\in[0,1]$: outside that interval the transport tree clamps $p$, whereas

\section{Elimination}

The restriction disappears once the data package is constructed from the
scan geometry of Lemma~9.1 and the transported states of Proposition~7.4,
with Propositions~8.1 and Lemmas~2.1 and~2.3 supplying the remaining fields.
Those inputs are separate items of the same formalization program.
