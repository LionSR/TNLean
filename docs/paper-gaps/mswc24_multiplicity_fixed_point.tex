\section{What the source states}
Let $A^i=\bigoplus_{j=1}^b\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{j,1},\dots,\mu_{j,m_j})
    \otimes A_j^i$ with normal $A_j$ forming a basis of normal tensors
\cite[eq.~(S2)]{Malz2024Preparation}, block $q$ sites, and write the polar
decomposition of the blocked map as $B=VP$. The source asserts that $P$
inherits the block structure of $A$,
\begin{align}
    P & =\bigoplus_{j=1}^b\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{j,1}^q,\dots,\mu_{j,m_j}^q)
    \otimes P_j
    \label{eq:mswc24_block_form}
\end{align}
with normal $P_j$ \cite[eq.~(S5)]{Malz2024Preparation}, replaces each $P_j$ by
its fixed point, and obtains the approximating state
\begin{align}
    \ket{\widetilde\phi_N}
     & \propto V^{\otimes M}\sum_{j=1}^b\beta_j\ket{\Omega_j},
    \qquad \beta_j=\sum_{k=1}^{m_j}\mu_{j,k}^N,\qquad
    \ket{\Omega_j}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^M\ket{\omega_j}_{R_kL_{k+1}},
    \label{eq:mswc24_approx_state}
\end{align}
with $N=qM$ \cite[eq.~(S7)]{Malz2024Preparation}. Part~(ii) of the
approximation-error lemma of the Supplemental Material then bounds
$\varepsilon=1-\lvert\braket{\widetilde\phi_N}{\phi_N}\rvert$ by
$O\bigl((N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}\bigr)$ for $q=o(N)$, with
$\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ the largest correlation length of the blocks.

\section{The counterexample}
Take $d=2$ and two normal blocks of bond dimension one, $A_1=(1,0)$ with
$m_1=2$ and $\mu_1=(1,1)$, and $A_2=(0,1)$ with $m_2=1$ and $\mu_2=1$. Then
$A^0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$, $A^1=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1)$, $D=3$, and
\begin{align}
    \ket{\phi_N}\propto2\ket{0\cdots0}+\ket{1\cdots1},\qquad
    \beta=(2,1).
    \label{eq:mswc24_counterexample_target}
\end{align}
For every $q$ the only nonzero blocked matrices are
$B^{0\cdots0}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$ and $B^{1\cdots1}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1)$.
As vectors of $\C^D\otimes\C^D$ they are $e_{00}+e_{11}$ and $e_{22}$, so
$P$ has rank two with range spanned by $(e_{00}+e_{11})/\sqrt2$ and $e_{22}$, and
$V$ maps these to $\ket{0\cdots0}$ and $\ket{1\cdots1}$. On the copy index of the
first block, $P$ is the rank-one matrix
$\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
the square root of the restriction
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ of $B^\dagger B$,
rather than
$\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{1,1}^q,\mu_{1,2}^q)=\mathbf 1$ times a positive part, so
(\ref{eq:mswc24_block_form}) fails.

The blocks are one-dimensional, so every block has the trivial fixed point,
$\omega_1$ is $e_{cc}$ for a copy $c\in\{0,1\}$ and $\omega_2=e_{22}$. Then
$Ve_{cc}=\ket{0\cdots0}/\sqrt2$ and $Ve_{22}=\ket{1\cdots1}$, so
\begin{align}
    V^{\otimes M}\sum_j\beta_j\ket{\Omega_j}
    =2\cdot2^{-M/2}\ket{0\cdots0}^{\otimes M}+\ket{1\cdots1}^{\otimes M},
    \label{eq:mswc24_counterexample_approx}
\end{align}
whose overlap with the normalized target (\ref{eq:mswc24_counterexample_target}) is
$(2^{2-M/2}+1)/\bigl(\sqrt5\,(2^{2-M}+1)^{1/2}\bigr)$. It equals $0.988$, $0.949$,
$0.800$ and $0.555$ at $M=1,2,4,8$, tends to $1/\sqrt5$, and does not depend on
$q$. Taking for instance $q\approx\sqrt N$, so that $q=o(N)$ and $M\to\infty$, the
error tends to $1-1/\sqrt5$, while the printed bound tends to zero.

Placing the pair of the first block on both copies does not help. With
$\omega_1=(e_{00}+e_{11})/\sqrt2$, the two legs at one site come from different
pairs, $V$ annihilates the configurations whose two legs lie in different copies,
and only the two copy-uniform configurations survive, each with amplitude
$2^{-M}$, so the first term is suppressed by $2^{1-M}$ relative to the
target (\ref{eq:mswc24_counterexample_target}), instead of the factor
$2^{-M/2}$ of (\ref{eq:mswc24_counterexample_approx}).

No placement of the pair helps. The source does not say where $\omega_1$ sits
among the copies of the first block, so let $\omega_1=\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}}W_{xy}e_{xy}$
be any unit vector on those copies, with $W$ the $2\times2$ matrix of its
coefficients and $\lVert W\rVert_F=1$. The map $V$ sends $e_{00}$ and $e_{11}$ to
$\ket{0\cdots0}/\sqrt2$ and annihilates $e_{01}$ and $e_{10}$, because $V=V\Pi$ and
$\Pi$ is supported on the diagonal pairs. At each site the two legs come from
neighbouring pairs, so
\begin{align*}
    V^{\otimes M}\ket{\Omega_1}
        =2^{-M/2}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^M}\prod_{k=1}^MW_{x_kx_{k+1}}\ket{0\cdots0}
    =2^{-M/2}\operatorname{Tr}(W^M)\ket{0\cdots0}.
\end{align*}
The eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ of $W$ satisfy
$\lvert\lambda_1\rvert^2+\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^2\leq\lVert W\rVert_F^2=1$
by Schur's inequality, so $T=\operatorname{Tr}(W^M)$ satisfies
$\lvert T\rvert\leq1$ for $M\geq2$. The overlap of the approximating state with the
normalized target is then
$\bigl\lvert4\cdot2^{-M/2}\,\overline T+1\bigr\rvert/
    \bigl(\sqrt5\,(4\cdot2^{-M}\lvert T\rvert^2+1)^{1/2}\bigr)$, which tends to
$1/\sqrt5$ for every choice of the pairs. The one-copy placement is $W=e_{cc}$ with
$T=1$, and the spread pair above is $W=\mathbf 1/\sqrt2$ with $T=2^{1-M/2}$. The
failure is therefore not in the choice of pair: the fixed point of the positive part
of the first block is not a product of nearest-neighbour pairs carrying the weight
$\beta_1$.

\section{What is formalized}
The fixed-point pairs of the blocks of a canonical form, each placed on one copy
of its block, the state $\sum_j\alpha^{(N)}_j\ket{\Omega_j}$ and its GHZ form are
formalized.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.SectorDecomposition.embeddedFixedPointPair} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.SectorDecomposition.canonicalFixedPointState} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/NonNormalCanonicalForm}.}
The counterexample is formalized for the source's construction with the pairs
placed on one copy: for every $q\geq1$ and $M\geq1$ the overlap of
(\ref{eq:mswc24_counterexample_approx}) with the normalized target is
$(4\cdot2^{-M/2}+1)/\bigl(\sqrt5\,(4\cdot2^{-M}+1)^{1/2}\bigr)$, which tends to
$1/\sqrt5$. The transfer maps of the two one-dimensional blocks have no eigenvalue
other than one. The subleading eigenvalue of the source's definition of
$\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ therefore does not exist; the formal statement reads
$\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ as the correlation length at any $\lambda_2$ with
$0<\lvert\lambda_2\rvert<1$ bounding the subleading eigenvalues, which here is
every such $\lambda_2$, so that $e^{-\gamma/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}<1$; along $q=M$,
$N=M^2$, which satisfies $q=o(N)$, the error exceeds
$C(N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}\exp\bigl(C(N/q)e^{-\gamma
            q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}\bigr)$ for every constant $C$.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.nonNormalApproxOverlap_repeatedBlockTensor},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.tendsto_norm_nonNormalApproxOverlap_repeatedBlockTensor},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.not_approximationError_le_repeatedBlockTensor} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/RepeatedBlockCounterexample}.}
The positive part and the support projector of the blocked tensor are computed
from the uniqueness of the positive square root of $B^\dagger B$. The computation
above for an arbitrary placement of the pair of the first block is not formalized.

The hypotheses of part~(ii) of the lemma are formalized for this tensor. After
ordering its bond coordinates it is the direct sum (S2) of the copies of a basis
of normal tensors: both blocks are normal, left-canonical with the diagonal
positive-definite fixed point $1$ (canonical form~II), have self-overlap one, give
linearly independent states for every $N\geq1$, are not related by a gauge
transformation and a phase, and give states that become orthogonal as
$N\to\infty$; every weight equals one. One theorem collects these
facts, with canonical form~II stated for the diagonal
positive-definite fixed point $1$ of each block, the same fixed point from which
the approximating state in its conclusion is built; it also collects the identification of the pairs (each on the first copy of its block) and of
the weights $\beta_j$ with those of the canonical form, and the failure of the
printed bound.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.isBNTCanonicalForm_and_not_approximationError_le_repeatedBlock},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.isBNTCanonicalForm_repeatedBlockSector},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.reindex_toTensor_repeatedBlockSector} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.embeddedFixedPointPair_repeatedBlockSector} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/RepeatedBlockCounterexample}.}

The block form (\ref{eq:mswc24_block_form}) also fails when every multiplicity is
one, as soon as the $q$-site states of distinct blocks are not orthogonal
\cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap}. Restricting to multiplicity one
therefore does not give a version of part~(ii) of the lemma; restricting also to
blocks whose $q$-site states are orthogonal does.

\section{Elimination}
The printed claim stays false: with the coefficients $\beta_j$ of
(\ref{eq:mswc24_approx_state}) the error does not tend to zero for the tensor of the
counterexample. The failure lies in the coefficients, not in the pairs. A correct statement
for repeated blocks may replace $\sum_j\beta_j\ket{\Omega_j}$ by the fixed point of the actual
positive part, whose restriction to the copies of block $j$ has rank one in the copy index:
on the copies of block $j$ the positive part is close to $c_j\,w_jw_j^\dagger\otimes P_j$ with
$c_j=(\sum_k\lvert\mu_{j,k}\rvert^{2q})^{1/2}$ and a unit vector $w_j$ built from the weights
$\mu_{j,k}^q$, and the corrected approximating state is
$V^{\otimes M}\sum_j\beta_jL_j^{\otimes M}\ket{\Omega_j}$, with the source's weights
$\beta_j$ and an on-site isometry $L_j$ that places the pairs of block $j$ on all of its
copies at once \cite{gap:mswc24_repeated_block_corrected_state}. When the $q$-site states of
distinct blocks overlap, the rate must also include the correlation lengths of the mixed
transfer maps \cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap}.

\section{The source's state with corrected coefficients}
The source's construction itself, with the pairs of block $j$ on one copy $k_j$, converges
once its coefficients are changed. Let $K_{j,k}$ place the bond pairs of copy $k$ of block
$j$. The blocked map is $B=\sum_jc_jB_jL_j^\dagger$ with
$L_j=\sum_k(\overline{\mu_{j,k}^q}/c_j)K_{j,k}$, so
$BK_{j,k}=\mu_{j,k}^qB_j=(\mu_{j,k}^q/c_j)BL_j$, and since the partial isometry $V$
vanishes on the kernel of $B$,
\begin{align*}
    VK_{j,k}=\frac{\mu_{j,k}^q}{c_j}\,VL_j .
\end{align*}
Hence $V^{\otimes M}$ sends the pairs on copy $k_j$ to $(\mu_{j,k_j}^q/c_j)^M$ times the
pairs placed by $L_j$, and the state (\ref{eq:mswc24_approx_state}) with the coefficients
\begin{align*}
    \beta'_j=\beta_j\Bigl(\frac{c_j}{\mu_{j,k_j}^q}\Bigr)^M
\end{align*}
in place of $\beta_j$ is a positive multiple of the corrected state, for weights of any
phase and with no orthogonality of the blocks. It therefore has the same error. For the
counterexample above the coefficients are $(2\cdot2^{M/2},1)$, and the state
(\ref{eq:mswc24_counterexample_approx}) becomes
$2\ket{0\cdots0}^{\otimes M}+\ket{1\cdots1}^{\otimes M}$, the target. For a block of
multiplicity one, $\beta'_j=\lvert\mu_{j,k_j}\rvert^N$.

Let the blocks be normal in the gauge $\sum_i(A_j^i)^\dagger A_j^i=\mathbf 1$ with positive
definite fixed points $\sigma_j$ of trace one, from which the pairs are built, let
$\lambda_2$ bound the moduli of the eigenvalues other than one of every transfer matrix of a
block and of all eigenvalues of the mixed transfer matrices of distinct blocks, let
$\xi=-1/\ln\lvert\lambda_2\rvert$, and let $0<\gamma<1$. There are $C,C'>0$, independent
of the weights, such that for all nonzero complex weights, every choice of the copies $k_j$,
every $q\geq1$ and every $M\geq1$ with $\beta\neq0$, the source's state with the
coefficients $\beta'_j$ satisfies
\begin{align*}
    \varepsilon\leq Cy\,e^{Cy},\qquad\varepsilon\leq C'y,\qquad y=Me^{-\gamma q/\xi}.
\end{align*}
This is part~(ii) of the lemma for the source's construction with the coefficients
$\beta'_j$, with $\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ replaced by
$\max(\xi_{\mathrm{diag}},\xi_{\mathrm{off\text{-}diag}})$
\cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap}, and with no factor depending on the weights
\cite{gap:mswc24_repeated_overlap_small_weight_sum}. When the $q$-site states of distinct
blocks are orthogonal at the length $q$, the same bounds hold with $\lambda_2$ bounding only
the eigenvalues of the transfer matrices of the blocks: orthogonality at one length makes the
mixed transfer matrices nilpotent.
