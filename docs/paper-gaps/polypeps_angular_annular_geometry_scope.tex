\section{The source proposition}

Proposition~3.4 of the polynomial-PEPS manuscript~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}
concerns the original normalized ground state $\Omega$ on the open square
$\Lambda_L$. The source is \path{02-information.tex}, lines 635--785, at
revision \nolinkurl{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}.
Fix the model parameters, $a>0$, $A_0\ge1$, $m_0\in\mathbb N$, and $p>0$.
The proposition chooses $D$ and $L_0$ before the Hamiltonian, the marked
points, and the physical regions. For $L\ge L_0$, let $\mathcal V$ contain
at most $m_0$ marked points and let $X,E\subseteq\Lambda_L$ be disjoint.
Suppose $D\log L\le n\le A_0L$, $\operatorname{diam}_\infty X\le A_0n$, and, for every $x\in X$,
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,E)
     & \ge a\max\{D\log L,\min(n,d_{\mathcal V}(x))\}
    ,
    \notag
\end{align}
where $d_{\varnothing}=+\infty$. Then $I_\Omega(X:E)\le L^{-p}$.

The proof chooses $a'<\min(a/100,1/100)$ and $0<d<a'/4$.
It processes dyadic scales $s_j=2^{-j}n$ and combines the conditional
information estimate of Lemma~3.2 with the physical reset of Lemma~3.3.
All distances and enlargements use the ambient $\ell_\infty$ metric
before the regions are intersected with the physical lattice.

\section{Established geometric and information estimates}

The dyadic layers, their cumulative separation, the containment of the
previous scale's selected group, and the physical core buffers are proved.
The first and sole layers are included. When the marked set is empty, the
capped distance is $n$, in agreement with the source convention.
Writing $R=\max\{A_0,2\}$, the number of newly selected grid cells at any
scale is at most
\begin{align}
    (m_0+1)\left(2\left\lceil\frac{R+3a'/2}{d}\right\rceil_{+}+3\right)^2.
    \notag
\end{align}
This estimate is uniform in the grid origin, scale, marked points,
physical domain, and shapes of the layers. It requires no bound on the
number of cells in the previous group.

The physical reset in Lemma~3.3 is proved using only the original
full-system spectral gap. For fixed model parameters and $p>0$, it gives
constants $C_p>0$ and $L_0$. If $I_\Omega(R:C)\le b$, with $b\ge0$ and
$R,C$ disjoint, then cores $R_0\subseteq R$, $C_0\subseteq C$ satisfy
$I_\Omega(R_0:C_0)\le L^{-p}$ when each core is at distance greater than
$C_p(1+b+\log L)$ from the corresponding physical complement.
The uniform numerical comparison with an available buffer $a's/3$ is
also proved.

There is also a finite entropy identity for adding cells. If the
conditional information contributed by cell $G_i$ is at most $c_i$ for
every possible group of preceding cells, then
\begin{align}
    I_\Omega\left(B\cup\bigcup_{i\in S}G_i:C\right)
     & \le I_\Omega(B:C)+\sum_{i\in S}c_i.
    \notag
\end{align}
It follows by induction from the entropy chain identity and remains valid
for overlapping regions and unnormalized vectors.

\section{The conditional cell estimate}

The intermediate cell estimate assumes the sublinear collar bound of the
area-law manuscript, Proposition~9.6~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.
For a fixed positive clearance ratio $c$, it gives constants $C_1>0$ and
$K\ge0$ such that
\begin{align}
    I_\Omega(G:E\mid B) & \le C_1(1+\log s)^K
    \notag
\end{align}
for every cell of side $s\ge1$. Here $G$ is the physical sample of that
cell, $B$ is the physical sample of an arbitrary union of other cells,
$E\subseteq(G\cup B)^c$, and the distance from the full closed ambient
cell to $E$ is at least $cs$. The constants precede the cell, grid origin,
conditioning cells, finite domain, Hamiltonian, and ground vector.
The proof uses the two ordered families of tiles, restricts their
partition to the physical sites without changing the order, and bounds
the residual entropy by its cardinality times $\log q$.

The collar bound is an explicit hypothesis of this intermediate result.
Consequently, the conditional estimates are stated separately from
Lemma~3.2 and Proposition~3.4 under their original model and spectral-gap
hypotheses. The removal of this premise is described below.

\section{Completion of the source statement}

The conditional cell estimate has also been transferred to the original
square-grid coordinates. Relabeling sites and translating the grid origin
by $-(1,1)$ preserve the same ground vector, full-system gap, physical
regions, and closed-cell separation.

The conditional deduction uses a uniform reset at a single scale.
Fix the geometric ratios $a',d$, a cell-count bound $N$, and $p>0$.
The cell estimate and the original-gap reset determine constants
$C_1,K,C_p$ before the system size, state, regions, and scale.
The scalar estimate then chooses $D\ge1$ and $L_0\ge e$ so that, for
all $L\ge L_0$ and $s\ge D\log L$,
\begin{align}
    C_p\bigl(1+L^{-p}+NC_1(1+\log(ds))^K+\log L\bigr)
     & < a's/3.
    \notag
\end{align}
The ambient clearance $(a/2-3a'-d)s$ is positive. It gives disjointness
of the selected region and the enlarged exterior, while both cores have
the required buffer. The information of the old group and every new-cell
estimate concern the same original vector and the same enlarged exterior.
This yields the next core estimate with error $L^{-p}$, including zero
new cells and empty physical regions.

In the finite annular induction, the first old group is empty, and the
previous selected group and enlarged exterior lie in the preceding cores.
Thus the error is restored to $L^{-p}$ at every scale. The exact count
above is the only count of new cells; no factor depending on the number
of scales is introduced. At the last scale, the cumulative set equals
$X$, and monotonicity removes both paddings. The empty marked set and
one-layer case are included.

The resulting conditional theorem fixes $q\ge1$, $J\ge0$, $\Delta>0$
and the collar hypothesis, and retains all the geometric and uniformity
quantifiers of Proposition~3.4. The original unit ground vector and its
full-system gap are unchanged throughout the deduction. It is a separate
conditional theorem.

The physical sublinear collar theorem now derives the collar bound from
the original Hamiltonian and full-system gap assumptions for every
$q\ge1$, $J\ge0$, $\Delta>0$, real clearance $D_0>2R+10$ and
$C_{\rm tpl}\ge24$. Taking $R=1$, $D_0=13$ and $C_{\rm tpl}=24$ therefore
supplies the exact premise of both conditional estimates. These constants
are fixed before every scale, grid, Hamiltonian, ground vector and region.
Thus the deduction introduces neither an area-law hypothesis nor an
independent information bound.

The resulting theorems establish Lemma~3.2 and Proposition~3.4 under
exactly their original model and spectral-gap hypotheses. Their formal
statements are
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_cell_conditionalMutualInformation_le_square}
and \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.angularInformationBound}.
The state, conditioning cells, exterior region and marked points remain
arbitrary as in the source. The cases $q=1$, $J=0$ and empty physical
regions are included.

The cell estimate is tracked by
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/9340}{issue 9340};
the complete angular estimate is tracked by
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/8766}{issue 8766}.
This resolves the collar restriction in the two information estimates.
The finite-domain area law and the construction of the dyadic preparation
protocol remain separate results.
