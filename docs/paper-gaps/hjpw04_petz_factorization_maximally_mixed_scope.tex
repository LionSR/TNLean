\section{Source statement}

For the partial trace
$T=\operatorname{id}_A\otimes\operatorname{tr}_C$ and the product reference
\begin{align}
  \sigma_{ABC}=\rho_A\otimes\rho_{BC},
\end{align}
Hayden--Jozsa--Petz--Winter derive the factorization
\begin{align}
  \widehat T=\operatorname{id}_A\otimes\widehat R
  \label{eq:hjpw-general-factorization}
\end{align}
in equation~(10), where $\widehat R$ is the Petz recovery map associated with
$\rho_{BC}$ and $\operatorname{tr}_C$. Their first-factor state $\rho_A$ is
general; it is not assumed to be maximally mixed.

\section{Formalized specialization}

The current formalization takes
\begin{align}
  \rho_A=d_A^{-1}\mathbf 1_A
\end{align}
and proves the raw support-Petz identity first on elementary tensors,
\begin{align}
  \mathcal R_{d_A^{-1}\mathbf 1_A\otimes\rho_{BC}}(A_0\otimes X_B)
  =A_0\otimes\mathcal R_{\rho_{BC}}(X_B),
\end{align}
then on every bipartite matrix by an operator-Schmidt decomposition.
The corresponding formal declarations are
\begin{center}
  \small
  \leanid{Matrix.partialTraceRightPetzMap_maximallyMixedTensor_kronecker}\\
  \leanid{Matrix.partialTraceRightPetzMap_maximallyMixedTensor}
\end{center}
in \doclink{TNLean/Channel/PetzRecovery}.

The maximally mixed hypothesis makes the first-factor support full and reduces
the first-factor square-root and support-inverse-square-root terms to scalar
multiples of the identity. Those scalars cancel in the Petz sandwich. This
establishes a correct specialization of~\eqref{eq:hjpw-general-factorization},
not the unrestricted source statement.

\section{Open boundary}

The unrestricted factorization remains future work. It requires treating an
arbitrary first-factor state $\rho_A$, including the distinction between the
raw formula on its support and a channel extension on the complementary
subspace when $\rho_A$ is singular. The resulting theorem should recover
HJPW equation~(10) without replacing $\rho_A$ by
$d_A^{-1}\mathbf 1_A$.

Neither the elementary-tensor identity nor its linear extension proves the
Koashi--Imoto/Hayashi direct-sum decomposition of equality states. That
structural theorem requires the separate invariant-state analysis following
the recovery factorization in HJPW.

This scope restriction is tracked by
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/4229}{TNLean issue \#4229}.

\section{Classification}

\emph{Open scope restriction.}  The formal theorem is mathematically correct
for the maximally mixed first factor, while HJPW equation~(10) allows a general
first-factor state.
