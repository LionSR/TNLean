\section{The source conclusion}
Nachtergaele's gap theorem \cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral} includes an
infinite-volume conclusion. Its hypotheses include a positive finite-range
interaction, equality of its zero-energy state face with the prescribed
GVBS face, and equality of the open-chain kernel with the prescribed
local support space at every sufficiently large length. The interaction
range need not exceed a separately supplied injectivity length.
For each pure GVBS ground state $\eta$ and local observable $X$ with $\eta(X)=0$, the uniform finite-volume gap
$\gamma>0$ also satisfies
\begin{align}
    \lim_{M\to-\infty,\,N\to+\infty}
        \eta\bigl(X^*[H_{[M,N]},X]\bigr)
        &\geq\gamma\eta(X^*X).
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_source}
\end{align}
The local source is \path{References/cond-mat_9410110/main.tex},
lines 933--947. Its final proof, at lines 2649--2675, uses convergence
of finite-volume pure ground-state expectations to those of one pure
infinite-volume sector.

\section{The finite-volume estimate}
Let $H\geq0$ act on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let
$K=\ker H$, and suppose $H\geq\gamma P_{K^\perp}$. For every vector
$v$, orthogonal decomposition gives
\begin{align}
    \langle Hv,v\rangle
        &\geq\gamma\bigl(\|v\|^2-\|P_Kv\|^2\bigr).
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_subtraction}
\end{align}
If $\psi\in K$, then
$\langle\psi,X^*[H,X]\psi\rangle=\langle HX\psi,X\psi\rangle$.
Consequently
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi,X^*[H,X]\psi\rangle
        &\geq\gamma\bigl(\|X\psi\|^2-\|P_KX\psi\|^2\bigr).
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_commutator}
\end{align}
These assertions are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/Martingale/GroundExpectationGap.lean}.

The projection in (\ref{eq:infinite_gap_commutator}) is onto the entire
ground space. For a normalized ground vector $\psi$, the condition
$\langle\psi,X\psi\rangle=0$ removes the component along $\psi$;
it does not remove components along other vectors in $K$. Thus scalar
centering alone does not justify replacing the projection term by zero
in a degenerate finite volume.

\section{The proved limiting implication}
Consider Hilbert spaces and operators indexed by expanding volumes $n$.
Suppose their Hamiltonians have the same gap $\gamma$ for all sufficiently
large $n$, and let $\psi_n\in\ker H_n$. If
\begin{align}
    \|X_n\psi_n\|^2&\longrightarrow a,
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_norm_limit}\\
    \|P_{\ker H_n}X_n\psi_n\|^2&\longrightarrow0,
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_projection_limit}\\
    \langle\psi_n,X_n^*[H_n,X_n]\psi_n\rangle&\longrightarrow e,
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_energy_limit}
\end{align}
then (\ref{eq:infinite_gap_commutator}) implies $e\geq\gamma a$.
The formal limiting assertion permits the Hilbert space to vary with $n$.
Its projection-decay hypothesis is explicit; it is not counted as a
formalization of (\ref{eq:infinite_gap_source}).

When $\ker H_n=\mathbb C\psi_n$ and $\|\psi_n\|=1$ for all sufficiently
large $n$, one has
\begin{align}
    \|P_{\ker H_n}X_n\psi_n\|^2
        &=|\langle\psi_n,X_n\psi_n\rangle|^2.
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_rank_one_projection}
\end{align}
Thus convergence of the scalar mean to zero suffices in this case.
The corresponding finite-volume estimate and limiting implication are
also proved in the cited module. Finite-volume uniqueness is an explicit
hypothesis; it is not inferred from purity of the limiting state.

\section{Faithful generating data and finite supports}

Let $A$ be a tensor of positive bond dimension and let $\rho>0$. For a
$k$-site matrix $X$, put
\[
 \eta_{A,\rho}(X)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{tr}\rho}
   \sum_{\sigma,\tau}X_{\sigma\tau}
       \operatorname{tr}(A^\tau\rho(A^\sigma)^*).
\]
No stationarity or normalization is required for the following equivalence:
\[
 \eta_{A,\rho}(X^*X)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G_A(k)\subseteq\ker X.
\]
Indeed, writing $C_b=\sum_\sigma X_{b\sigma}A^\sigma$, one has
\[
 (\operatorname{tr}\rho)\eta_{A,\rho}(X^*X)
   =\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\rho\sum_b C_b^*C_b\right).
\]
Faithfulness of $\rho$ makes this trace zero precisely when every $C_b$ is
zero. The identity
$(X\Gamma_k(Y))_b=\operatorname{tr}(C_bY)$ and the nondegeneracy of the
trace pairing finish the argument. This is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalFaithfulSupport.lean}.
The argument applies also at $k=0$.

If $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=I$, the functional is positive and normalized.
It therefore has a positive representing matrix $\sigma_k$ with trace one:
\[
 \eta_{A,\rho}(X)=\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_kX),
 \qquad \operatorname{ran}\sigma_k=G_A(k).
\]
The range equality follows by comparing the vanishing conditions for
$\eta_{A,\rho}(X^*X)$ and
$\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_kX^*X)$, then testing the orthogonal-complement
projections of the two subspaces. The result is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalDensitySupport.lean}.
Stationarity is unnecessary for these finite densities. Without stationarity
the conclusion does not assert consistency across intervals.

Faithfulness of $\rho$ can be removed for normalized stationary data.
Let $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=I$, let $\rho\geq0$ have nonzero trace, and let
$\sum_iA_i\rho A_i^*=\rho$. The support projection $P$ of $\rho$ satisfies
$(I-P)A_iP=0$. Choose an isometry $V$ with $VV^*=P$ and put
$B_i=V^*A_iV$ and $\sigma=V^*\rho V$. Then $A_iV=VB_i$, $\sigma>0$, and
$\eta_{A,\rho}=\eta_{B,\sigma}$ on every interval. Moreover
$G_B(k)$ is the image of the corner $PM_DP$ under the boundary map of $A$.
Hence $\eta_{A,\rho}(X^*X)=0$ exactly when $X$ annihilates this corner
image, and when $A$ is left canonical the density $\sigma_k$ has this
corner image as its range. These statements are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/StationaryLocalSupport.lean}.

The remaining step is the passage from an arbitrary GVBS boundary limit
(1.1) to normalized stationary data. Nachtergaele takes it from
Fannes--Nachtergaele--Werner \cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral}, local source
lines 1411--1414, and the faithful minimal data used at lines 1724--1738
also rely on the minimality condition of lines 1457--1467. That passage is
not formalized here; the support results therefore start from normalized
stationary generating data. Removing this restriction requires formalizing
the cited normalization and minimality results from a general presentation.
Likewise, results stated for a primitive tensor with canonical data, for a
normalized periodic tensor, or for a finite periodic family take that tensor
presentation as given. Deriving such a presentation from a general GVBS
boundary-limit presentation is part of the same unformalized passage, and the
same elimination plan applies.

\section{The primitive-sector quasi-local inequality}
For one normalized primitive tensor with a positive-definite invariant
matrix, the tensor-specific projection decay is now proved. The exact
boundary compression of an interior observable is the reshuffling of
$E_A^\ell E_X E_A^r$. When both free intervals tend to infinity, this
converges to $\eta_A(X)$ times the limiting boundary Gram operator.
These identities and limits are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableInsertion.lean}.
The required general compression inequality is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/GroundSpaceCompressionNorm.lean}:
for an injective boundary map $T$, with $K=(T^*T)^{-1}$ and range
projection $P$, it gives
\begin{align}
    \|PXP-cP\|&\leq\|K\|\,\|T^*XT-cT^*T\|.
    \label{eq:infinite_gap_compression_bound}
\end{align}
An eventual uniform bound on $K$ transfers vanishing of the pullback defect
to vanishing of the full compression defect, even when the Hilbert spaces
vary. In particular, for $\eta_A(X)=0$, the complete ground-space projection
of $X\psi_n$ tends to zero uniformly over unit boundary ground vectors.
This consequence and local expectation convergence are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/BoundaryObservableCompression.lean}.
The finite expectations are positive, normalized, complex linear, and
consistent under adjoining identity sites. Their operator norm is one;
these state properties are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableExpectation.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableState.lean}.

The fixed local commutator identity is also proved. For an interaction $h$
of range $R\geq1$ and an observable $X$ on $k\geq1$ sites, put
$t=R-1$, $w=2t+k$, and $B=I_t\otimes X\otimes I_t$. If both free
intervals have at least $t$ sites, then $X_n^*[H_n(h),X_n]$ is the
identity-tensor inclusion of $B^*[H_w(h),B]$. This locality identity is in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/BulkObservableCommutator.lean}.
Combining it with the primitive expectation and full projection limits gives
\begin{align}
    \gamma\operatorname{Re}\eta_A(X^*X)
        &\leq\operatorname{Re}\eta_A\bigl(B^*[H_w(h),B]\bigr).
    \label{eq:primitive_local_commutator_gap}
\end{align}
For every positive interaction $h$ with kernel $G_R(A)$, a positive
$\gamma$ independent of $X$ is derived at every $R\geq D^4+1$, where
$D>0$ is the bond dimension. Comparison with the canonical interaction
preserves the open-chain kernel and gives a uniform gap. There is no
supplied energy-limit assumption and no finite-volume uniqueness assumption.
The canonical and positive-interaction statements are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PrimitiveLocalCommutatorGap.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PrimitiveLocalParentInteractionGap.lean}.

The consistent local expectations now have a continuous positive extension
$\omega_A$ to the quasi-local algebra, with $\omega_A(I)=\|\omega_A\|=1$.
Finite-region compatibility, interval evaluation, and translation invariance
are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableRegionExpectation.lean},
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableQuasiLocalState.lean}, and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalObservableTranslationInvariance.lean}.
If $j_{a,k}$ includes a $k$-site matrix observable at position $a$, then
$\omega_A(j_{a,k}(X))=\eta_A(X)$. Consequently
(\ref{eq:primitive_local_commutator_gap}) is also the quasi-local inequality
$\gamma\operatorname{Re}\omega_A(j_{a,k}(X)^*j_{a,k}(X))
\leq\operatorname{Re}\omega_A(j_{a-t,w}(B^*[H_w(h),B]))$.
This formulation is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/QuasiLocalPrimitiveGap.lean}.

\section{The multiblock projection limit and sector purity}
For several sectors, the ground-space projection also contains the other
sectors. Their local means need not vanish when $\eta_A(X)=0$ in the
chosen sector. These cross-sector components are now removed without
asserting scalarity on the entire multiblock ground space. A ground vector
in sector $\alpha$ lies in its prefix support. A distant observable commutes
with the prefix projections, so
\begin{align}
    \|P_\beta(N)X_{\ell,r}P_\alpha(N)\|
        &\leq\|P_\beta(\ell)P_\alpha(\ell)\|\,\|X\|
          \longrightarrow0 \qquad (\alpha\neq\beta).
    \label{eq:multiblock_prefix_cross_compression}
\end{align}
This estimate is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/BoundaryCrossObservableCompression.lean}.
The diagonal compression tends to zero by centering. The sum of the sector
projections approaches the projection onto their joint span. Thus all
column compressions vanish in norm, and so does the full ground projection
of $X\psi_n$ for bounded vectors in the chosen sector. The finite-sector
assembly and tensor consequence are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/GroundSpaceSectorCompressionDecay.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/BlockBoundaryObservableCompression.lean}.

For a weighted direct sum of normalized primitive blocks with nonzero
weights, simultaneous injectivity at $S>0$ gives the exact joint kernel

The exact local support of the completed state is also proved in

\section{Support from the eventual kernel condition}
Let $h\geq0$ have positive range $R$, and suppose that
$\ker H_N(h)=G_N$ for all sufficiently large $N$.
A normalized positive state $\varphi$ satisfying
$\varphi(\tau_a(h))=0$ for every $a\in\mathbb Z$ then satisfies
$\varphi(P_{G_N})=1$ on every sufficiently large interval.
Indeed, positivity and finite dimensionality give
$H_N(h)\geq\kappa_N(1-P_{G_N})$ for some $\kappa_N>0$.
Applying $\varphi$ shows that $\varphi(1-P_{G_N})=0$.
No uniform lower bound on $\kappa_N$, translation invariance of
$\varphi$, or parent-kernel identity at the original range is required.
This implication is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/QuasiLocalGroundStateSupport.lean}.

For finitely many inequivalent primitive sectors with faithful invariant
matrices, eventual joint support also determines the entire state:
\begin{align}
  \varphi=\sum_{\alpha}t_\alpha\eta_\alpha,\qquad
  t_\alpha\geq0,\qquad\sum_{\alpha}t_\alpha=1.
  \label{eq:multiblock_supported_state_decomposition}
\end{align}
The coefficients are derived from expectations of the sector projections
on expanding centered intervals. Asymptotic orthogonality makes their total
mass tend to one; compactness supplies limiting coefficients. Compression
of each local observable identifies its expectation, and continuity extends
the identity to the completed quasi-local algebra. Translation invariance
of $\varphi$ is not assumed. This implication is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/MultiblockQuasiLocalFace.lean}.

Conversely, eventual annihilation of the joint MPS space by the
open-chain sums gives zero local expectation in every constructed
sector state. Each summand has the same expectation in such a state,
and a volume of length at least $R$ contains at least one summand.
Positivity is unnecessary for this converse implication, proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/LocalParentExpectationFromOpenKernel.lean}.

The zero-energy face therefore equals the convex hull in
(\ref{eq:multiblock_supported_state_decomposition}). Since each primitive
sector state is pure among all quasi-local states, and a pure state in a
finite convex decomposition equals a constituent, the pure zero-energy
states are exactly the constructed sectors. Both equivalences are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/MultiblockQuasiLocalGroundStates.lean}.
This classification concerns the given finite primitive sector family;
more general periodic GVBS presentations are not thereby classified.

\section{One constant for finite chains and all pure ground states}
The original interaction range need not satisfy $R\geq S+1$ when the
source's eventual finite-volume kernel identity is assumed. Choose a
sufficiently large canonical range $W\geq R$ with uniform gap $\delta>0$
and with both open-chain kernels equal to the joint MPS space. Positivity
and finite dimensionality give
\begin{align}
  \kappa P_{G_W^\perp}\leq H_W(h)
  \qquad\text{for some }\kappa>0.
\end{align}
Sum this comparison over complete $W$-site windows in an $N$-site chain.
Every original range-$R$ term occurs at most $W-R+1$ times, so
\begin{align}
  \kappa H_{N,W}^{\mathrm{can}}
    \leq (W-R+1)H_N(h).
\end{align}
The coincident kernels transfer the canonical gap to the lower bound
$\kappa\delta/(W-R+1)$. A minimum with the positive gaps of the finitely
many shorter volumes gives a common constant $\gamma>0$ for every length.
The comparison and its consequence are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/Martingale/OpenInteractionRangeComparison.lean}
and \path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/Martingale/EventualKernelOpenGap.lean}.

Full joint projection decay and finite-range locality then give
(\ref{eq:infinite_gap_source}) in every constructed sector with this same

state with such a sector, so the bound holds in every pure state of the
zero-energy face. This paired finite- and infinite-volume conclusion is
proved in \path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/QuasiLocalGroundStateGap.lean}.
Simultaneous word span of an original, unnormalized family suffices:

\section{Blocking and periodic tensor representations}
The support and purity assertions also hold on the original chain when a
primitive presentation is obtained only after blocking. Let $L>0$ and suppose
\begin{align}
  G_{A^{[L]}}(N)=G_{\bigoplus_j\mu_j B_j}(N)
  \qquad(N\geq0),\qquad \mu_j\ne0,
  \label{eq:exact_blocked_presentation}
\end{align}
where the $B_j$ are inequivalent primitive tensors with faithful invariant
matrices. The blocking isomorphism induces a transport $T_L$ identifying
the entire state spaces and their pure states.

For any normalized positive state, support on every original interval is
equivalent to support on every interval aligned with the block partition.
Every interval lies in an aligned interval. Restriction of the larger MPS
space orders the corresponding projections, and exact transport gives
\begin{align}
  \varphi(P_{G_A(I)})=1\quad\text{for every nonempty }I
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  T_L^{-1}\varphi(P_{G_{A^{[L]}}(J)})=1
    \quad\text{for every nonempty blocked }J.
\end{align}

\section{The original-chain commutator gap for periodic tensors}
The infinite-volume inequality is now proved directly on the original chain
for a periodic tensor $A$. Its hypotheses are positive physical dimension,
$R>0$, $h\geq0$, and the eventual original kernel identity
$\ker H_h(N)=G_A(N)$. The primitive sectors, faithful matrices, and their
inequivalence are derived from periodicity. There is one $\delta>0$ such
that every pure state $\varphi\in\mathcal F_h$ and every local $X$ with
$\varphi(X)=0$ satisfy
\begin{align}
 \lim_{u,v\to\infty}\varphi\bigl(X^*[H_{[a-u,a+k+v)},X]\bigr)
   \geq\delta\,\varphi(X^*X).
\end{align}
The limit is literal, with complex order as in the source, and both margins
may diverge along any filter. Translation invariance of a competing state is
not assumed. This is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicQuasiLocalGroundStateGap.lean}.

The same conclusion holds for the joint tensor
$T=\bigoplus_j\mu_jA_j$ of a finite family of normalized periodic tensors
with nonzero weights. Blocking by the product of their periods gives an
exact primitive presentation at every length, with faithful invariant
matrices and inequivalence derived internally. The constant is chosen before
all states, observables, filters, and margins. This extension is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicBlockFamilyQuasiLocalGap.lean}.
An empty family has an empty zero-energy state face; no ground-state existence
is asserted in that case.

The comparison uses positivity before taking a limit. If a positive
functional $f$ satisfies $f(H)=0$ for $H\geq0$, Cauchy--Schwarz implies
$f(YH)=0$ for every $Y$. Thus
\begin{align}
 f\bigl(X^*[H,X]\bigr)=f(X^*HX).
\end{align}
If $0\leq C\leq MH$, then $f(C)=0$ and
\begin{align}
 f\bigl(X^*[C,X]\bigr)\leq M f\bigl(X^*[H,X]\bigr).
\end{align}
Apply this to the blocked Hamiltonian comparison, where $M=KL+1-R$.
Finite-range locality identifies each commutator energy with one fixed
patch once both margins contain the interaction range. These identities
transport a blocked gap $\gamma$ to the original constant $\gamma/M$.

interval and then written in blocked coordinates. The stabilized commutator

interval representation. Hence the estimate applies also to observables

nonnegativity follow from the positive-functional identity above; no energy
convergence is supplied as a hypothesis. The generic transport is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/BlockedQuasiLocalGap.lean}.

\section{Construction on the original periodic chain}
For a normalized tensor of period $m$, the original interaction and its kernel
identity are now derived rather than assumed. Let $(P_u)_{u\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z}$
be the Hermitian orthogonal cyclic projections, with
$\sum_uP_u=I$ and $P_uA_i=A_iP_{u+1}$. Define
\begin{align}
 W_n&=\{X:P_uX=XP_{u+n}\text{ for every }u\},\\
 V_n&=\{X:P_{u+n}X=XP_u\text{ for every }u\}.
\end{align}
Every word of length $n$ belongs to $W_n$. Simultaneous word spanning of the
inequivalent primitive tensors obtained by period-blocking yields a length
$q m$ whose words span $W_{qm}$. All sector dimensions, invariant matrices,
and inequivalence assertions are derived from the periodic tensor.

The spanning equality propagates to every later original length. If
$X\in W_{qm+r}$ and $A_w$ is a word of length $r$, then
$XA_w^*\in W_{qm}$. Left-canonical normalization gives
\begin{align}
 X=\sum_{|w|=r}(XA_w^*)A_w,
 \qquad \sum_{|w|=r}A_w^*A_w=I.
\end{align}
Thus the words of length $qm+r$ span $W_{qm+r}$.
Every local MPS vector has a boundary representative in $V_n$: replace an
arbitrary boundary $X$ by $\sum_uP_{u+n}XP_u$ without changing its trace
pairing with the length-$n$ words. Moreover, $X\in V_n$ implies
$X^*\in W_n$. When $W_n$ is spanned, vanishing of the boundary vector therefore
implies $\operatorname{tr}(X^*X)=0$, hence $X=0$. The boundary map is
injective on the relevant cyclic space, without injectivity on the full
matrix algebra.

Comparing the two one-site restrictions and using this restricted
injectivity gives, for every sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{align}
 G_A(n+2)=\bigl(G_A(n+1)\otimes\mathbb C^d\bigr)
             \cap\bigl(\mathbb C^d\otimes G_A(n+1)\bigr).
\end{align}
Choose $R$ above this threshold and let $h$ be the orthogonal projection onto
$G_A(R)^\perp$. Positivity of the open sum makes its kernel the simultaneous
kernel of all local terms. Iterating the intersection then gives
\begin{align}
 h\geq0,\qquad \ker H_h(N)=G_A(N)\quad(N\geq R).
\end{align}
This construction includes every original length. It is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicOriginalIntersection.lean}.

Section~9 now applies to this derived interaction. Consequently periodicity
alone gives one positive $\delta$, the exact open kernels above, the
zero-energy face classification of Section~8, and the literal
infinite-volume inequality for every pure zero-energy state and every
centered local observable. The constant precedes all states and exhaustions.
The existence and gap composition is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicParentInteractionGap.lean}.
The finite-volume gap at every original length is established in
Section~11. Identification of a general GVBS presentation with these tensor
support spaces remains separate.

\section{The finite-chain gap at every original length}

Let $A$ be the normalized periodic tensor of Section~10, with period $m$.
The canonical interaction there admits a constant $\delta>0$ such that
\begin{align}
  \delta\|v\|\leq\|H_h(N)v\|
  \qquad\bigl(N\geq0,\quad v\perp\ker H_h(N)\bigr).
\end{align}
For $N\geq R$, its kernel is $G_A(N)$, and positivity gives the equivalent
spectral lower bound
\begin{align}
  \langle v,H_h(N)v\rangle\geq\delta\|v\|^2
  \qquad\bigl(N\geq R,\quad v\perp G_A(N)\bigr).
\end{align}
In particular, the lower bound is independent of the residue of $N$ modulo
$m$. This is a positive lower bound; it does not identify the
explicit numerical constant in the finite-gap proposition of
\cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral}, lines 2593--2614 of the local source.

Write $N=(K+M)m+r$, where $0\leq r<m$. Cover the interval by its initial
$(K+M)m$ sites and its terminal $Mm+r$ sites. Their common interval has
$Mm$ sites. In the blocked cyclic-sector coordinates, denote the right,
left, and full boundary maps of sector $j$ by $T_j,L_j,C_j$. Their joint
ranges are precisely the transported original ground spaces of these
three intervals.

For a single sector, the supported Gram inverses give the exact identity
\begin{align}
  P_{\operatorname{ran}T_j}P_{\operatorname{ran}L_j}
     -P_{\operatorname{ran}C_j}
   =T_jH_{T_j}\mathcal D_jH_{L_j}L_j^*.
\end{align}
The finite mixed Gram displacement $\mathcal D_j$ tends to zero as
$M\to\infty$. For all sufficiently large $M$, the two boundary maps and two supported
inverses have bounds uniform in $K$ and $r$. Consequently the same-sector
projection defect tends to zero. Products involving two distinct sectors
also tend to zero, by the primitive prefix-angle estimate, itself derived from mixed-transfer decay. Finally, each joint
range projection is approximated by the sum of the individual sector
projections. Combining these three assertions gives
\begin{align}
  \|P_{G_A(N)}-P_{\mathrm{left}}P_{\mathrm{right}}\|
     \longrightarrow0
  \qquad(M\to\infty)
\end{align}
along every choice of $K$ and $r$. A surjective configuration isometry
preserves this norm; taking the adjoint puts the two projections in the
displayed order.
The estimates and their physical transport are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/ResidualWindowJointProjectionDecay.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/ResidualCoverProjectionDefect.lean}.

The convergence is uniform in the exterior lengths. Indeed, if there were
arbitrarily large $M$ for which some pair $(K,r)$ had defect greater than
$1/2$, choosing such a pair at each of those $M$ would contradict the
convergence along every choice. Fix $M$ sufficiently large that the defect
is at most $1/2$ for all exterior lengths and that $Mm\geq R$.

The left interval has the aligned gap of Section~8. The finitely many
terminal volumes $Mm+r$, $0\leq r<m$, have a positive minimum gap above
their actual kernels. The exact original-chain intersection identifies
the intersection of the two covered kernels with $G_A(N)$. Every local
term occurs in the two covered Hamiltonians at most twice, and hence
\begin{align}
  H_{\mathrm{left}}+H_{\mathrm{right}}\leq2H_h(N).
\end{align}
The two-projection gap estimate now gives one positive lower bound for
all $N\geq Mm$. The finitely many shorter volumes each have a positive
lower bound on the orthogonal complement of their actual kernel. Taking
one further finite minimum gives the displayed estimate at every $N$.
The construction involves neither a divisibility
condition on $N$ nor additional sector, kernel, or decay hypotheses.
The common interaction range, exact kernels for $N\geq R$, and the
all-length gap are proved together in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicOpenParentGap.lean}.
The identification of the support spaces of a general GVBS presentation
with these tensor boundary spaces remains separate.

The finite- and infinite-volume conclusions hold for one and the same
canonical interaction. More precisely, periodicity chooses a positive range
$R$, a positive matrix $h$, and constants $\delta,\gamma>0$ such that the
finite-chain estimate above holds with $\delta$, while every pure zero-energy
state satisfies the literal infinite-volume commutator inequality of
Section~9 with $\gamma$. The two constants need not agree. All four choices
precede the state, local observable, and interval exhaustion. This combined
statement is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicParentGap.lean}.

\section{Stationary faithful generators and their parent gaps}

If, in addition, $E_A(\rho)=\rho$, every invariant projection is reducing.
The finite-dimensional reducing decomposition gives a nonempty family of
irreducible corners $B_j$ and isometries $V_j$ satisfying
\[
 V_j^*V_j=I,\qquad \sum_jV_jV_j^*=I,\qquad
 A_iV_j=V_jB_j^i,\qquad V_j^*A_i=B_j^iV_j^*.
\]
Each corner remains trace preserving, since
$\sum_i(B_j^i)^*B_j^i=V_j^*(\sum_iA_i^*A_i)V_j=I$.
Irreducibility and this normalization give a finite period for each corner.
The isometric resolution identifies the support spaces at every length:
\[
 G_A(N)=\bigvee_jG_{B_j}(N)=G_{\bigoplus_jB_j}(N)
 \qquad(N\geq0).
\]
The full presentation, including both intertwining directions, is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulPeriodicGroundSpace.lean}.
No minimality or purity assumption is used, and repeated corners are allowed.

For stationary data, the compatible finite expectations define a quasi-local
state $\omega_{A,\rho}$. The same density $\sigma_k$ represents every translated
interval restriction of this state, and its range remains $G_A(k)$. This
literal state-support statement is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulLocalStateSupport.lean}.

The periodic presentation also gives an infinite-volume gap for these
supplied generators without assuming that $A$ is irreducible or periodic.
Let $h\geq0$ have positive range and suppose that
$\ker H_h(N)=G_A(N)$ for every sufficiently large $N$. There is a single
$\gamma>0$ such that, for every pure zero-energy state $\varphi$, every
centered local observable $X$, and every exhaustion with both boundary
margins diverging,
\[
 \lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb Z}
   \varphi\bigl(X^*[H_{h,\Lambda},X]\bigr)
 \geq \gamma\varphi(X^*X).
\]
The periodic-family gap theorem applies to the same interaction after the
all-length support identity. The constant precedes the state, observable,
and exhaustion. This conditional exact-kernel consequence is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulQuasiLocalGap.lean}.

The interaction and kernel identity can now also be constructed from the
faithful stationary generators. Their periodic presentation and the family
intersection theorem give $R_0>0$ such that
\[
 \ker H^{\mathrm{open}}_{R,N}(A)=G_A(N)
 \qquad(N\geq R\geq R_0).
\]
Choose the canonical projection of range $R_0$. It is positive, has these
exact open kernels, and annihilates the generated state on every translate.
The preceding commutator theorem applies to this same interaction. Thus
neither an interaction nor an eventual kernel identity is supplied in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulParentInteractionGap.lean}.

The original-length family gap also gives a uniform finite-volume bound
for every sufficiently large canonical range. For each such range $R$ there
is $\delta_R>0$ such that, at every $N\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 \delta_R\|v\|\leq\|H^{\mathrm{open}}_{R,N}(A)v\|
 \qquad\text{for }v\perp\ker H^{\mathrm{open}}_{R,N}(A).
\end{align}
The exact boundary-kernel assertion still concerns $N\geq R$.
Thus the constructed interaction has both finite and infinite gaps, and
contains the generated stationary state in its zero-energy face. This is
proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulParentFiniteGap.lean}.

A given positive interaction $h$ of range $R>0$ needs only the eventual
identity $\ker H_h(N)=G_A(N)$. Choose a long window $W$ beyond both this
threshold and the canonical threshold. Finite-dimensional positivity gives
$H_h(W)\geq\kappa P_{G_A(W)^\perp}$ for some $\kappa>0$.
The sum of the translated windows counts each interaction term at most
$W-R+1$ times. Comparison with the range-$W$ canonical Hamiltonian therefore
transfers its gap to the given interaction. A positive minimum over the
remaining finitely many volumes extends the bound above their actual
kernels. The same given $h$ has the literal infinite-volume commutator gap.
No assumption on $\ker h$ at the interaction range is added. These results
are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/Martingale/CanonicalGapToInteractionGap.lean}
and
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/FaithfulGivenInteractionGap.lean}.
They give Theorem~1.2 in the supplied faithful stationary-generator setting.
For a positive finite-volume Hamiltonian, the norm gap is equivalent to
the lower bound on its nonzero spectrum; it therefore gives
$\langle v,H_h(N)v\rangle\geq\delta\|v\|^2$ above the kernel.
Taking the positive minimum of the finite and infinite constants gives one
common lower bound for both conclusions, as in the source statement.

Faithfulness can be obtained by restricting supplied stationary generating
data to their support. Suppose only that $\rho\geq0$ has nonzero trace and
$E_A(\rho)=\rho$. Its support projection $P$ is invariant:
$(I-P)A_iP=0$. Choose an isometry $V$ with $VV^*=P$, and put
\[
 B_i=V^*A_iV,\qquad \sigma=V^*\rho V.
\]
Then $\sigma>0$, $\sum_iB_i^*B_i=I$, $E_B(\sigma)=\sigma$, and
$A_iV=VB_i$. Moreover,
\[
 \rho=V\sigma V^*,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\sigma=\operatorname{tr}\rho,\qquad
 \eta_{A,\rho}(X)=\eta_{B,\sigma}(X)
\]
at every finite length, including zero. These assertions are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/StationarySupportCompression.lean}.
The compressed bond dimension is positive because its stationary trace is
nonzero. This restriction preserves the specified state; it need not preserve
the original tensor's full boundary spaces. Invariance of $P$ alone does not
give $V^*A_i=B_iV^*$, and unused original directions can enlarge $G_A(k)$.
For every $k\geq0$, the original quasi-local state nevertheless has one
positive density of trace one on every translated $k$-site interval, whose
range is exactly $G_B(k)$. This follows from the preserved finite expectations
and the faithful support theorem for $B,\sigma$, and is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/StationaryLocalStateSupport.lean}.

Equality of the finite insertion expectations also identifies the actual
quasi-local states:
\[
 \omega_{A,\rho}=\omega_{B,\sigma}.
\]
Every finite-region observable can be placed in a containing interval, where
the two functionals agree. Continuity and density of the local observables
then give equality on the quasi-local algebra. This is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/QuasiLocalExpectationEquality.lean}.
Thus the support restriction preserves the entire specified state, rather
than merely its finite density ranges.

The compressed faithful generators therefore construct a positive parent
interaction for the original stationary state. Its sufficiently long open
kernels are $G_B(N)$, the original state belongs to its zero-energy face,
and the same interaction has one positive infinite-volume commutator gap
in every pure state in that face. This construction assumes only normalized
generators and a nonzero positive stationary matrix; bond and physical
dimensions are derived. It is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/StationaryParentInteractionGap.lean}.
The original stationary state need not be pure.
The same construction now also supplies $\delta>0$ with
\begin{align}
 \delta\|v\|\leq\|H_h(N)v\|
 \qquad\text{for every }N\geq0\text{ and }v\perp\ker H_h(N).
\end{align}
The constants for the finite and infinite gaps belong to this same
interaction. The all-length result is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/StationaryParentFiniteGap.lean}.

For a supplied physical-first isometry
$V:\mathbb C^D\to\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^D$, put
$A_i(a,b)=V((i,a),b)$. Direct matrix multiplication gives
\begin{align}
 \sum_iA_i^*A_i=V^*V,
 \qquad
 V^*(X\otimes B)V=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}A_i^*BA_j.
\end{align}
Trace duality identifies this one-site Heisenberg insertion with the
corresponding Schr\"odinger insertion. These identities are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/SuppliedIsometryInsertion.lean}.
The multi-site order is now explicit as well. Write
$F_X(B)=V^*(X\otimes B)V$. For every finite list and every matrix $\rho$,
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\rho\,(F_{X_1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{X_n})(I)\bigr)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(E_{X_n\otimes\cdots\otimes X_1}(\rho)\bigr).
\end{align}
Here the physical observable transfer on the right uses forward matrix
words. The identity includes the empty list and needs no positivity,
normalization, or stationarity. Its induction uses one-site trace duality
and the product rule for inserted transfers, and is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/SourceOrderedInsertion.lean}. Thus the
source-written product has precisely the reversed physical order in the
present convention.

These results identify finite supports and periodic sectors once stationary
generating data are supplied. They do not construct those data from an
arbitrary GVBS presentation.
The source discussion of minimum auxiliary dimension is not the assertion
that the observable-generated algebra is the whole matrix algebra. The preceding multi-site identity records the required physical reversal
for a supplied isometric representation.


\section{Original-length periodic overlaps and joint intersections}

For a normalized irreducible periodic tensor $A$, there are constants
$C>0$ and $n_0$ such that
\[
 x\in G_A(n),\ n\geq n_0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists u:\ \Gamma_n^A(u)=x,\ \|u\|\leq C\|x\|.
\]
This bound holds at every original length. Primitive cyclic sectors give
supported residual Gram inverses uniformly in the residue length. If $S_j$
is one residual boundary map and $H_j$ its supported Gram inverse, then
$S_jH_jS_j^*=P_{\operatorname{ran}S_j}$. The corresponding original boundary
is obtained by right multiplication by $V_j^*$. Small pairwise sector overlaps
give
\[
 \frac12\sum_j\|x_j\|^2\leq\Bigl\|\sum_jx_j\Bigr\|^2.
\]
This lower bound controls each component by $2\|x\|$, so summing the
individual bounded preimages gives the assertion. Quotient and remainder
then include every sufficiently large original length.

For inequivalent periodic tensors $A,B$, the original mixed transfer operator
has spectral radius strictly below one. Hence
$\|\Gamma_n^A{}^*\Gamma_n^B\|\to0$. Combining this decay with the bounded
preimages gives, for each $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 |\langle x,y\rangle|\leq\varepsilon\|x\|\|y\|
 \qquad(x\in G_A(n),\ y\in G_B(n)).
\]
The two tensors may have different periods and bond dimensions. The argument
uses the original mixed transfer operator and therefore supplies the estimate
at every sufficiently large original length. These results are proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicGroundSpaceIndependence.lean}.

For a finite family, sufficiently small pairwise overlaps imply independence
by the lower Gram estimate. On the common middle interval, independence makes
the two sector decompositions unique. The individual one-step intersections
can consequently be combined:
\[
 \left(\sum_jG_j(n+1)\otimes\mathbb C^d\right)
 \cap\left(\mathbb C^d\otimes\sum_jG_j(n+1)\right)
 =\sum_jG_j(n+2).
\]
This geometric implication is proved without normalization or virtual
injectivity in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/JointGroundSpaceIntersection.lean}.
Iteration gives the exact canonical open kernels once the individual
intersections and middle independence hold eventually; the latter two facts
remain explicit in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicFamilyInteractionCriterion.lean}.

An arbitrary finite periodic family need not be inequivalent. Select one
representative of each gauge and phase class first. Gauge invariance of
boundary spaces and nonzero block weights preserve the original joint space
at every length. The selected spaces are eventually independent, so the
intersection criterion constructs a positive canonical interaction with
exact original kernels for every volume at least its range. The
periodic-family commutator theorem then supplies a common positive
infinite-volume gap for that constructed interaction. No sector separation,
independence, interaction, or kernel identity is supplied. The result is
proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicBlockFamilyParentInteractionGap.lean}.
It concerns an explicitly supplied finite periodic generating family; the
identification with an arbitrary source GVBS boundary-limit presentation
remains separate.


The common blocking can also be resolved into literal primitive cyclic
sectors. Their isometric inclusions satisfy $\sum_sV_sV_s^*=I$; the residual
word Gram has the inverse-cyclic tail projection in each sector. The
supported inverse estimates and vanishing mixed overlaps then give the
physical residual-cover limit at every original residue. Consequently, for
some $R_0>0$, every $R\geq R_0$ has exact kernels at $N\geq R$ and a
uniform norm gap above the actual kernel at every $N\geq0$. The same
constructed canonical interaction has the literal infinite-volume gap.
No primitive-resolution or residual-limit hypothesis is supplied in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicBlockFamilyOpenParentGap.lean}.
The empty family is included by its direct all-length Hamiltonian formula.


For the original weighted periodic family, a prescribed positive interaction
of positive range with eventual exact joint kernels has the same two gap
conclusions. The finite-volume bound uses its actual kernel also at short
lengths; the infinite-volume bound concerns the identical supplied
interaction. No interaction-range lower threshold or local-kernel equality
is required. This given-interaction form is proved in
\path{TNLean/MPS/ParentHamiltonian/PeriodicBlockFamilyInteractionGap.lean}.
