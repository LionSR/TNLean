\section{The assertion}

This note concerns the reverse implication in Theorem~4.1 of de las Cuevas,
Cirac, Schuch, and P\'erez-Garc\'ia
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}.\footnote{For traceability, this
note corresponds to
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/1046}{issue \#1046}. Related
formal work is recorded in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/670}{issue \#670},
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/821}{issue \#821},
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/622}{issue \#622}, and
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/664}{issue \#664}.} The relevant
source passages are
\path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:717--731} and
\path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:812--818}.

Let $B=(B^1,\ldots,B^d)$ be a matrix-product-state tensor and let $p\geq 1$.
Its transfer channel is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B(X)
    &=\sum_{i=1}^d B^iX(B^i)^\dagger.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_transfer_channel}
\end{align}
The source calls a trace-preserving completely positive map $\mathcal E$
$p$-divisible when there is a trace-preserving completely positive map
$\mathcal E'$ such that
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E
    &=(\mathcal E')^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_channel_divisibility}
\end{align}
This condition concerns channels and imposes no bound on the number of Kraus
operators required to represent the root $\mathcal E'$.

The source calls $B$ $p$-refinable when there are a tensor
$A=(A^1,\ldots,A^d)$ on the same physical alphabet and an isometry
\begin{align}
    W
    &:\mathbb C^d\longrightarrow(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes p}
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_isometry}
\end{align}
such that
\begin{align}
    \ket{V_{pN}(A)}
    &=W^{\otimes N}\ket{V_N(B)}
    \qquad\text{for every }N.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_identity}
\end{align}
Thus the tensor root has exactly the original $d$ physical labels. For $B$ in
irreducible form~II, Theorem~4.1 of
Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible} states the unconditional equivalence
\begin{align}
    B\text{ is }p\text{-refinable}
    \quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \mathcal E_B\text{ is }p\text{-divisible}.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_source_equivalence}
\end{align}

\section{The missing Kraus-rank step}

Assume that $\mathcal E_B$ is $p$-divisible. By
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_channel_divisibility}), there is a
trace-preserving completely positive map $\mathcal E'$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B
    &=(\mathcal E')^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}
\end{align}
The reverse proof then asserts the existence of a tensor
$A=(A^1,\ldots,A^d)$ for which
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B
    &=\mathcal E_A^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_tensor_root}
\end{align}
It proceeds by applying the isometric freedom of Kraus representations to two
representations of the same channel.

The passage from
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}) to
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_tensor_root}) is not automatic. To
realize $\mathcal E'$ as the transfer channel of a $d$-letter tensor, the root
must have a Kraus representation with at most $d$ terms. Equivalently, its
minimal Kraus rank, which equals its Choi rank, must satisfy
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E')
    &\leq d.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}
\end{align}
If the minimal Kraus rank is $r>d$, then $\mathcal E'$ can be represented by
an $r$-letter tensor but not by a $d$-letter tensor. The resulting isometry
would have codomain $(\mathbb C^r)^{\otimes p}$ rather than the required
codomain $(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes p}$.

This obstruction is mathematical rather than notational. A completely
positive map can require more than a prescribed number of Kraus operators.
The channel identity
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}) does not exclude the
possibility that every Kraus representation of $\mathcal E'$ has more than
$d$ terms. The reverse implication therefore needs either a proof that the
irreducible-form hypotheses force a root satisfying
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}) or an alternative
argument that avoids a $d$-letter tensor root.

\section{The formal statement}

The formal definition follows the channel-level notion of $p$-divisibility
from Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}: it asserts the existence of a
trace-preserving completely positive root channel and imposes no Kraus-rank
bound.\footnote{The definition is
\leanid{MPSTensor.IsPDivisibleChannel} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/Theorem41Defs.lean}.}

The proved reverse step takes a selected bounded-rank root explicitly. The
theorem \leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_channel_root_hasKrausRankLE} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/Theorem41Reverse.lean}
assumes $\mathcal E_B=\mathcal F^p$ and a Kraus representation of
$\mathcal F$ with at most $d$ operators. Padding that representation with
zeros gives a $d$-letter tensor $A$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B&=\mathcal E_{A^{[p]}}.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}
\end{align}
The blocked tensor $A^{[p]}$ and the original tensor $B$ provide two finite
Kraus representations of the same completely positive map, and isometric
freedom of Kraus representations gives the isometry
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_isometry}) needed for
refinement. This step is
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_transferMap_eq_blockTensor}. Neither
irreducible form nor a global reconstruction predicate is needed for it.

The conditional reverse theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.thm_4_1_p_refinement_reverse} assumes the inverse
canonicalization statement \leanid{MPSTensor.PRefinementInverseCanonicalization}:
if $B$ is in irreducible form~II and $\mathcal E_B$ is $p$-divisible, then a
$d$-letter tensor $A$ satisfying
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}) exists. Its
proof is the preceding isometric-freedom step. The stronger hypothesis
\leanid{MPSTensor.PRefinementInverseRootKrausRankBound}, which bounds the Kraus
rank of every trace-preserving root of $\mathcal E_B$, implies inverse
canonicalization by
\leanid{MPSTensor.pRefinementInverseCanonicalization_of_rootKrausRankBound}.

The remaining source assertion is that unbounded channel divisibility yields
a root of Kraus rank at most $d$. Selection remains unproved. Identifying
Kraus rank with Choi rank does not by itself establish the required bound.

\section{Why the bound cannot quantify over every root}

The sufficient hypothesis that every channel root has Kraus rank at most $d$
is strictly stronger than existential selection. The following
finite-dimensional calculation shows that it fails for some targets. The calculation below uses sector dimensions $(1,3,3,1,1)$. A smaller
variant with dimensions $(1,2,2,1,1)$ gives the same obstruction.

Let $\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}\mathcal H_i$, with
sector dimensions $(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)=(1,3,3,1,1)$. Write $X_{ii}$ for the
diagonal compression of $X$ to $\mathcal H_i$. Define a channel by
\begin{align}
  \mathcal E(X)|_{\mathcal H_{i+1}}
    &=\operatorname{tr}(X_{ii})\frac{I_{\mathcal H_{i+1}}}{n_{i+1}},
  \label{eq:dccsp17_cyclic_root_channel}
\end{align}
with all off-diagonal output blocks zero. A Kraus family consists of the
matrix units from $\mathcal H_i$ to $\mathcal H_{i+1}$, each divided by
$\sqrt{n_{i+1}}$. These Kraus matrices are linearly independent, so
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E)
    &=\sum_i n_i n_{i+1}=17, &
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E^2)
    &=\sum_i n_i n_{i+2}=13.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_cyclic_root_ranks}
\end{align}
Both maps preserve trace and are irreducible: a nonzero invariant subspace
contains one full sector after applying the matrix-unit Kraus operators,
and then contains every sector by cyclicity. On the span of the normalized
sector identities, $\mathcal E$ acts by the five-cycle; it annihilates the
complement consisting of off-diagonal blocks and traceless diagonal blocks.
Thus its peripheral spectrum is precisely the fifth roots of unity. The
same is true of $\mathcal E^2$, since multiplication by two permutes the
five-cycle. A minimal Kraus tensor for $\mathcal E^2$ is therefore a
13-letter, trace-preserving irreducible tensor of period five, and can be
regarded as a one-block irreducible form with weight one. Its square root
$\mathcal E$ violates the universal bound.

This example does \emph{not} refute bounded-root selection. Identify
$\mathcal H_1$ and $\mathcal H_2$ unitarily, and replace just the completely
depolarizing branch from $\mathcal H_1$ to $\mathcal H_2$ by that unitary
channel. Call the resulting channel $\mathcal F$. Its preceding branch
prepares $I_{\mathcal H_1}/3$, and its following branch takes the trace into
the one-dimensional space $\mathcal H_3$. Consequently
$\mathcal F^2=\mathcal E^2$, while its Kraus rank is
$17-9+1=9$. A proof of the source converse must be free to select this
alternative root rather than bound an arbitrarily supplied root.

The alternative-root construction persists when the two three-dimensional
reset states are different. Write their positive eigenvalue distributions as
$(a_i)_{i=1}^3$ and $(b_j)_{j=1}^3$. There is a nonnegative coupling
$(t_{ji})$ with column sums $a_i$, row sums $b_j$, and at most five nonzero
entries: successively match a remaining row mass and column mass, exhausting
at least one at each step. If $(e_i)$ and $(f_j)$ are the respective
eigenbases, the Kraus operators
\begin{align}
  K_{ji}&=\sqrt{t_{ji}/a_i}\,|f_j\rangle\langle e_i|
  \label{eq:dccsp17_reset_state_coupling}
\end{align}
define a trace-preserving channel carrying the first state to the second.
Replacing the middle reset branch by this channel leaves the square
unchanged and gives a root of rank at most $17-9+5=13$.

Adding a self-loop of positive weight at the last one-dimensional sector,
with the remaining positive weight on its original outgoing edge, makes
both the root and its square primitive. Their ranks are respectively
$18$ and $16$: the two-step sector pairs are
$(0,2),(1,3),(2,4),(3,0),(3,4),(4,0),(4,1),(4,4)$.
Every sufficiently long path connects every pair of sectors, and the reset
states are positive definite, which proves primitivity. The same branch
replacement leaves the square unchanged and produces a root of rank at
most $14$. Thus neither primitivity nor unequal reset states turns these
rank-decrease examples into counterexamples to existential selection.
These calculations have not been formalized in Lean.

The seven-dimensional variant with sector dimensions $(1,2,2,1,1)$ is
formalized by
\leanid{MPSTensor.supplied_root_rank_counterexample} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementSuppliedRootObstruction.lean}.
Its root has Choi rank $10$, while its square has Choi rank $9$.
The theorem records that the nine-letter target is periodic and has a
one-block irreducible form, that the supplied root is a channel, and that
this root has no Kraus representation with at most nine operators.
The rank calculation uses linear independence of the scaled matrix units;
irreducibility follows by propagating an invariant subspace through the
five sectors. Replacing the middle two-dimensional reset by a unitary
again preserves the square and gives a root of rank $10-4+1=7$.
This last alternative-root calculation is not part of the formal theorem.
Thus the formal obstruction concerns compression of a supplied root,
while the general existential selection problem remains open.

\section{A sufficient condition on the Kraus span}

Let $S$ be the linear span of a Kraus family of a channel root $\mathcal F$.
Write $S^q$ for the span of products of $q$ elements of $S$.
The Kraus ranks of $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal F^p$ are respectively
$\dim S$ and $\dim S^p$. If $S^{p-1}$ contains an invertible matrix $K$,
then multiplication by $K$ is an injective linear map from $S$ into $S^p$.
Consequently
\[
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal F)
  \leq \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal F^p)
  \leq d.
\]
In particular, an invertible element of $S$ suffices, by taking its
$(p-1)$st power. This proves bounded-root selection whenever some root has
that property. It does not settle the general case: the unequal sector
dimensions of the cyclic example above force every element of its original
Kraus span to be singular. The rank identity and multiplication argument are formalized in
\path{TNLean/Algebra/KrausSpanRank.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RootWordSpan.lean}. The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_root_isUnit_mem_wordSpan_pred}
in \path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/RefinementFromInvertibleWordSpan.lean}
proves refinement from such a tensor root, allowing its initial Kraus
alphabet to have any finite size. This is a sufficient condition and does
not assert that an arbitrary divisible channel has such a root.

For a single irreducible target block, every channel root is irreducible.
Indeed, a subspace invariant under the root's Kraus operators is invariant
under all their $p$-fold products. If the root has period $m$, the arbitrary
blocking theorem then gives $\gcd(m,p)=1$, since its $p$th power remains
irreducible. These two necessary conditions are formalized in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementRootIrreducibility.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementRootPeriod.lean}. The declarations
\leanid{MPSTensor.isIrreducibleFamily_of_transferMap_eq_blockTensor} and
\leanid{MPSTensor.IsPeriodic.coprime_of_isIrreducibleFamily_blockTensor}
allow different Kraus alphabet sizes for the target and root; neither
assumes the required rank bound. Thus an irreducible counterexample to bounded-root selection
would require singular Kraus spans for every possible root, despite this
coprimality constraint. No such counterexample or general selection argument
has been established here.

\section{Invertible targets admit the required rank bound}

The following reduction is formalized in
\path{TNLean/Algebra/InjectivePositiveMapRank.lean}.
Here invertibility means invertibility as a linear endomorphism of
$M_D(\C)$, not invertibility of the Choi matrix. If
$\mathcal E=\mathcal F^p$ is invertible and $p>0$, then every completely
positive root $\mathcal F$ has Choi rank at most that of $\mathcal E$.
Neither irreducibility nor trace preservation is needed for this assertion.

First, let $\Phi$ be an injective positive complex-linear map between
matrix algebras, and let $A\ge0$. Write $P$ and $Q$ for the support
projections of $A$ and $\Phi(A)$, respectively. Every positive matrix $X$
supported on $P$ satisfies $X\le cA$ for some $c\ge0$, because $A$ is
positive definite on its support. Hence
$0\le\Phi(X)\le c\Phi(A)$, and $\Phi(X)$ is supported on $Q$.
Every matrix in the corner determined by $P$ is a complex linear
combination of positive matrices in that corner. Thus $\Phi$ restricts
to an injective complex-linear map between the two corners. Comparing
their dimensions gives
\begin{align}
  (\operatorname{rank} A)^2
  &\le (\operatorname{rank}\Phi(A))^2,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{rank} A\le\operatorname{rank}\Phi(A).
  \label{eq:dccsp17_injective_positive_rank}
\end{align}

If $\mathcal F$ is an invertible completely positive map, its amplification
$\mathcal F\otimes\mathrm{id}$ is both injective and positive. Apply
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_injective_positive_rank}) to the Choi matrix of a
completely positive map $\mathcal G$. The Choi identity for composition
then yields
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{rank} C_{\mathcal G}
  &\le \operatorname{rank} C_{\mathcal F\circ\mathcal G}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_injective_composition_choi_rank}
\end{align}
Since invertibility of $\mathcal F^p$ implies invertibility of
$\mathcal F$, iteration proves the asserted bound. In particular, if
$\mathcal E$ has a Kraus representation with $d$ operators, every channel
root of an invertible $\mathcal E$ has a representation with at most $d$
operators. No assumption on eigenvalue multiplicities is required.
The declarations
\leanid{Channel.choiRank_le_comp_of_injective} and
\leanid{Channel.choiRank_le_pow_of_injective} prove the composition and
power inequalities. The resulting refinement theorem is
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_isPDivisibleChannel_of_injective} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/RefinementFromInjectiveTransfer.lean}.
It requires no canonical-form assumption.

Consequently any obstruction to bounded-root selection must have a
singular target channel. For this remaining case, a first-order argument
alone is insufficient. If the root has Kraus span $S$ of dimension $r$
and the target has Kraus span $S^p$ of dimension $s$, the differential
of the power map is
\begin{align}
  H&\longmapsto
     \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathcal F^k H\mathcal F^{p-1-k}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_power_differential}
\end{align}
It maps the $r^2$-dimensional real space of Hermitian Choi perturbations
supported on the vectorization of $S$ into the corresponding
$s^2$-dimensional space for $S^p$. When $r>s$ it therefore has a nonzero
kernel. For an irreducible target, every direction in this kernel preserves
trace to first order, as shown below. Extending such a direction to an
exact family of roots and reaching a smaller Choi support remain unproved.

\section{Trace preservation at an irreducible target}

The following reductions are mathematical arguments; they are not yet
asserted as Lean theorems. Let $\mathcal E$ be an irreducible CPTP map on
$M_D(\mathbb C)$, where $D>0$, and let $p>0$. Its adjoint fixed space is
$\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal E^*)=\mathbb C I$.

First, every completely positive root $\mathcal F^p=\mathcal E$ is
trace preserving. Indeed, $\mathcal F$ commutes with $\mathcal E$, so
$\mathcal F^*(I)$ is fixed by $\mathcal E^*$. Thus
$\mathcal F^*(I)=cI$ for some $c\ge0$. Taking the $p$th power gives
$c^p=1$, hence $c=1$.

Second, suppose that a Hermiticity-preserving perturbation $H$ belongs to
the kernel of the differential in
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_power_differential}). Put $T=\mathcal F^*$ and
$h=H^*(I)$. Since $T(I)=I$, the adjoint differential equation evaluated
at $I$ gives
\begin{align}
  \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}T^k h&=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_trace}
\end{align}
Multiplying by $T-I$ yields $(\mathcal E^*-I)h=0$. Consequently $h=cI$,
and (\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_trace}) gives $pcI=0$. Hence $h=0$:
every such perturbation preserves trace to first order. This argument
requires neither diagonalizability nor a nonzero spectrum.

In particular, if the root and target have Choi ranks $r>s$, the dimension
count above supplies a nonzero Hermitian Choi perturbation in the kernel
of the differential. It automatically preserves trace, and the perturbed
map is CPTP for sufficiently small real parameters. This is only a
first-order statement about the root equation. For $p=2$ one has
\begin{align}
  (\mathcal F+tH)^2&=\mathcal E+t^2H^2
  \qquad\text{if }\mathcal F H+H\mathcal F=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_second_order}
\end{align}
The differential equation does not itself eliminate the quadratic term.
An exact deformation therefore requires a further argument; rank
minimization and compactness alone do not supply one. These reductions
apply to a single irreducible target. A target with several irreducible
blocks can have a larger adjoint fixed space, so the same proof does not
establish trace preservation in that setting.

\section{The zero eigenspace cannot simply be discarded}

A root commutes with its power and therefore preserves the decomposition
into the generalized zero eigenspace and the sum of the nonzero generalized
eigenspaces. This is a decomposition of the vector space of matrices; it
need not be a decomposition into matrix algebras or positive subspaces.
Consequently changing the root on one summand need not preserve complete
positivity.

There are two concrete difficulties with deleting the zero-eigenspace
action. First, the seven-dimensional cyclic-reset example above already
annihilates the zero eigenspace of its square. Its rank-seven alternative
root instead adds a square-zero action: traceless matrices in the first
two-dimensional sector are carried unitarily to the second, and the next
reset annihilates them. Thus decreasing the Kraus rank can require adding
nilpotent action, rather than removing it.

Second, deletion can destroy even positivity. Consider the matrices
\begin{align}
 P&=\frac1{60}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 21&1&21&17\\15&29&3&13\\18&22&6&14\\6&8&30&16
 \end{pmatrix}, &
 Q&=\frac1{40}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 12&-4&20&12\\9&17&5&9\\10&10&10&10\\9&17&5&9
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_matrices}
\end{align}
Both matrices have all column sums equal to one. The entries of $P$
are strictly positive, whereas $Q_{12}=-1/10$.
Put $u=(2,1,2,-5)^{\mathsf T}$, $v=(3,7,-9,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, and
$N=uv^{\mathsf T}/120$. Direct multiplication gives
\begin{align}
 P&=Q+N, & N^2&=QN=NQ=0, & Q(Q-I)(5Q-I)&=0.
 \label{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_identities}
\end{align}
In particular $P^2=Q^2$. The square-free polynomial in
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_identities}) shows that $Q$ is
semisimple. Its eigenvalues are $1,1/5,0,0$, while $P$ has the same
nonzero part and a nonzero nilpotent part on the zero eigenspace.

For a real matrix $R$, define a linear map on $M_4(\C)$ by
$\mathcal F_R(X)=\operatorname{diag}(R\operatorname{diag}(X))$, where the
inner diagonal denotes the vector of diagonal entries. Since every entry
of $P$ is positive and its columns sum to one, $\mathcal F_P$ is a CPTP
map. It is primitive: it sends every nonzero positive matrix to a positive
definite diagonal matrix. Its square $\mathcal E=\mathcal F_P^2$ has a
semisimple zero eigenspace. Removing the action of $\mathcal F_P$ on that
eigenspace gives exactly $\mathcal F_Q$, which still satisfies
$\mathcal F_Q^2=\mathcal E$. Nevertheless $\mathcal F_Q$ is not positive,
as its value on the second diagonal matrix unit has first diagonal entry
$-1/10$.

These exact rational calculations have not been formalized in Lean.
They disprove the proposed deletion procedure, not bounded-root selection:
$P$ and $P^2$ have strictly positive entries, so both channels have Choi
rank $16$ and the original root already satisfies the required rank bound.
The singular-target problem must therefore control complete positivity
while choosing the zero-eigenspace action; algebraic spectral truncation
alone does not do so.

\section{Conclusion}

The formal reverse theorem has an additional hypothesis relative to
Theorem~4.1 of Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}. The source claims that
channel-level $p$-divisibility of $\mathcal E_B$ suffices to construct a
$p$-refinement of $B$, but its proof uses a tensor root whose existence
requires the Kraus-rank bound
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}). This bound is
absent from the source definition of $p$-divisibility and is not proved in the
cited converse paragraph.

For invertible target channels, the preceding rank argument supplies the
required bound for every root. For singular target channels it remains open
whether, under the irreducible-form hypotheses, a channel root can always
be chosen with Kraus rank at most $d$. The formal development
therefore proves the reverse implication from a selected bounded-rank
root, and under the inverse canonicalization hypothesis
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}). Until a paper-level
argument establishes the required rank bound, the reverse implication must
remain conditional on this additional mathematical input.
