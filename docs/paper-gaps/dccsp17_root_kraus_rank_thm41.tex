\section{The assertion}

This note concerns the reverse implication in Theorem~4.1 of de las Cuevas,
Cirac, Schuch, and P\'erez-Garc\'ia
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}.\footnote{For traceability, this
note corresponds to
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/1046}{issue \#1046}. Related
formal work is recorded in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/670}{issue \#670},
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/821}{issue \#821},
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/622}{issue \#622}, and
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/664}{issue \#664}.} The relevant
source passages are
\path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:717--731} and
\path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:812--818}.

Let $B=(B^1,\ldots,B^d)$ be a matrix-product-state tensor and let $p\geq 1$.
Its transfer channel is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B(X)
    &=\sum_{i=1}^d B^iX(B^i)^\dagger.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_transfer_channel}
\end{align}
The source calls a trace-preserving completely positive map $\mathcal E$
$p$-divisible when there is a trace-preserving completely positive map
$\mathcal E'$ such that
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E
    &=(\mathcal E')^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_channel_divisibility}
\end{align}
This condition concerns channels and imposes no bound on the number of Kraus
operators required to represent the root $\mathcal E'$.

The source calls $B$ $p$-refinable when there are a tensor
$A=(A^1,\ldots,A^d)$ on the same physical alphabet and an isometry
\begin{align}
    W
    &:\mathbb C^d\longrightarrow(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes p}
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_isometry}
\end{align}
such that
\begin{align}
    \ket{V_{pN}(A)}
    &=W^{\otimes N}\ket{V_N(B)}
    \qquad\text{for every }N.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_identity}
\end{align}
Thus the tensor root has exactly the original $d$ physical labels. For $B$ in
irreducible form~II, Theorem~4.1 of
Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible} states the unconditional equivalence
\begin{align}
    B\text{ is }p\text{-refinable}
    \quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \mathcal E_B\text{ is }p\text{-divisible}.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_source_equivalence}
\end{align}

\section{The missing Kraus-rank step}

Assume that $\mathcal E_B$ is $p$-divisible. By
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_channel_divisibility}), there is a
trace-preserving completely positive map $\mathcal E'$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B
    &=(\mathcal E')^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}
\end{align}
The reverse proof then asserts the existence of a tensor
$A=(A^1,\ldots,A^d)$ for which
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B
    &=\mathcal E_A^p.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_tensor_root}
\end{align}
It proceeds by applying the isometric freedom of Kraus representations to two
representations of the same channel.

The passage from
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}) to
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_tensor_root}) is not automatic. To
realize $\mathcal E'$ as the transfer channel of a $d$-letter tensor, the root
must have a Kraus representation with at most $d$ terms. Equivalently, its
minimal Kraus rank, which equals its Choi rank, must satisfy
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E')
    &\leq d.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}
\end{align}
If the minimal Kraus rank is $r>d$, then $\mathcal E'$ can be represented by
an $r$-letter tensor but not by a $d$-letter tensor. The resulting isometry
would have codomain $(\mathbb C^r)^{\otimes p}$ rather than the required
codomain $(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes p}$.

This obstruction is mathematical rather than notational. A completely
positive map can require more than a prescribed number of Kraus operators.
The channel identity
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_root_channel}) does not exclude the
possibility that every Kraus representation of $\mathcal E'$ has more than
$d$ terms. The reverse implication therefore needs either a proof that the
irreducible-form hypotheses force a root satisfying
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}) or an alternative
argument that avoids a $d$-letter tensor root.

\section{The formal statement}

The formal definition follows the channel-level notion of $p$-divisibility
from Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}: it asserts the existence of a
trace-preserving completely positive root channel and imposes no Kraus-rank
bound.\footnote{The definition is
\leanid{MPSTensor.IsPDivisibleChannel} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/Theorem41Defs.lean}.}

The proved reverse step takes a selected bounded-rank root explicitly. The
theorem \leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_channel_root_hasKrausRankLE} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/Theorem41Reverse.lean}
assumes $\mathcal E_B=\mathcal F^p$ and a Kraus representation of
$\mathcal F$ with at most $d$ operators. Padding that representation with
zeros gives a $d$-letter tensor $A$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_B&=\mathcal E_{A^{[p]}}.
    \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}
\end{align}
The blocked tensor $A^{[p]}$ and the original tensor $B$ provide two finite
Kraus representations of the same completely positive map, and isometric
freedom of Kraus representations gives the isometry
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_refinement_isometry}) needed for
refinement. This step is
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_transferMap_eq_blockTensor}. Neither
irreducible form nor a global reconstruction predicate is needed for it.

The conditional reverse theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.thm_4_1_p_refinement_reverse} assumes the inverse
canonicalization statement \leanid{MPSTensor.PRefinementInverseCanonicalization}:
if $B$ is in irreducible form~II and $\mathcal E_B$ is $p$-divisible, then a
$d$-letter tensor $A$ satisfying
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}) exists. Its
proof is the preceding isometric-freedom step. The stronger hypothesis
\leanid{MPSTensor.PRefinementInverseRootKrausRankBound}, which bounds the Kraus
rank of every trace-preserving root of $\mathcal E_B$, implies inverse
canonicalization by
\leanid{MPSTensor.pRefinementInverseCanonicalization_of_rootKrausRankBound}.

The remaining source assertion is that unbounded channel divisibility yields
a root of Kraus rank at most $d$. Selection remains unproved. Identifying
Kraus rank with Choi rank does not by itself establish the required bound.

\section{Why the bound cannot quantify over every root}

The sufficient hypothesis that every channel root has Kraus rank at most $d$
is strictly stronger than existential selection. The following
finite-dimensional calculation shows that it fails for some targets. The calculation below uses sector dimensions $(1,3,3,1,1)$. A smaller
variant with dimensions $(1,2,2,1,1)$ gives the same obstruction.

Let $\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}\mathcal H_i$, with
sector dimensions $(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)=(1,3,3,1,1)$. Write $X_{ii}$ for the
diagonal compression of $X$ to $\mathcal H_i$. Define a channel by
\begin{align}
  \mathcal E(X)|_{\mathcal H_{i+1}}
    &=\operatorname{tr}(X_{ii})\frac{I_{\mathcal H_{i+1}}}{n_{i+1}},
  \label{eq:dccsp17_cyclic_root_channel}
\end{align}
with all off-diagonal output blocks zero. A Kraus family consists of the
matrix units from $\mathcal H_i$ to $\mathcal H_{i+1}$, each divided by
$\sqrt{n_{i+1}}$. These Kraus matrices are linearly independent, so
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E)
    &=\sum_i n_i n_{i+1}=17, &
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal E^2)
    &=\sum_i n_i n_{i+2}=13.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_cyclic_root_ranks}
\end{align}
Both maps preserve trace and are irreducible: a nonzero invariant subspace
contains one full sector after applying the matrix-unit Kraus operators,
and then contains every sector by cyclicity. On the span of the normalized
sector identities, $\mathcal E$ acts by the five-cycle; it annihilates the
complement consisting of off-diagonal blocks and traceless diagonal blocks.
Thus its peripheral spectrum is precisely the fifth roots of unity. The
same is true of $\mathcal E^2$, since multiplication by two permutes the
five-cycle. A minimal Kraus tensor for $\mathcal E^2$ is therefore a
13-letter, trace-preserving irreducible tensor of period five, and can be
regarded as a one-block irreducible form with weight one. Its square root
$\mathcal E$ violates the universal bound.

This example does \emph{not} refute bounded-root selection. Identify
$\mathcal H_1$ and $\mathcal H_2$ unitarily, and replace just the completely
depolarizing branch from $\mathcal H_1$ to $\mathcal H_2$ by that unitary
channel. Call the resulting channel $\mathcal F$. Its preceding branch
prepares $I_{\mathcal H_1}/3$, and its following branch takes the trace into
the one-dimensional space $\mathcal H_3$. Consequently
$\mathcal F^2=\mathcal E^2$, while its Kraus rank is
$17-9+1=9$. A proof of the source converse must be free to select this
alternative root rather than bound an arbitrarily supplied root.

The alternative-root construction persists when the two three-dimensional
reset states are different. Write their positive eigenvalue distributions as
$(a_i)_{i=1}^3$ and $(b_j)_{j=1}^3$. There is a nonnegative coupling
$(t_{ji})$ with column sums $a_i$, row sums $b_j$, and at most five nonzero
entries: successively match a remaining row mass and column mass, exhausting
at least one at each step. If $(e_i)$ and $(f_j)$ are the respective
eigenbases, the Kraus operators
\begin{align}
  K_{ji}&=\sqrt{t_{ji}/a_i}\,|f_j\rangle\langle e_i|
  \label{eq:dccsp17_reset_state_coupling}
\end{align}
define a trace-preserving channel carrying the first state to the second.
Replacing the middle reset branch by this channel leaves the square
unchanged and gives a root of rank at most $17-9+5=13$.

Adding a self-loop of positive weight at the last one-dimensional sector,
with the remaining positive weight on its original outgoing edge, makes
both the root and its square primitive. Their ranks are respectively
$18$ and $16$: the two-step sector pairs are
$(0,2),(1,3),(2,4),(3,0),(3,4),(4,0),(4,1),(4,4)$.
Every sufficiently long path connects every pair of sectors, and the reset
states are positive definite, which proves primitivity. The same branch
replacement leaves the square unchanged and produces a root of rank at
most $14$. Thus neither primitivity nor unequal reset states turns these
rank-decrease examples into counterexamples to existential selection.
These calculations have not been formalized in Lean.

The seven-dimensional variant with sector dimensions $(1,2,2,1,1)$ is
formalized by
\leanid{MPSTensor.supplied_root_rank_counterexample} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementSuppliedRootObstruction.lean}.
Its root has Choi rank $10$, while its square has Choi rank $9$.
The theorem records that the nine-letter target is periodic and has a
one-block irreducible form, that the supplied root is a channel, and that
this root has no Kraus representation with at most nine operators.
The rank calculation uses linear independence of the scaled matrix units;
irreducibility follows by propagating an invariant subspace through the
five sectors. Replacing the middle two-dimensional reset by a unitary
again preserves the square and gives a root of rank $10-4+1=7$.
This last alternative-root calculation is not part of the formal theorem.
Thus the formal obstruction concerns compression of a supplied root,
while the general existential selection problem remains open.

\section{A sufficient condition on the Kraus span}

Let $S$ be the linear span of a Kraus family of a channel root $\mathcal F$.
Write $S^q$ for the span of products of $q$ elements of $S$.
The Kraus ranks of $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal F^p$ are respectively
$\dim S$ and $\dim S^p$. If $S^{p-1}$ contains an invertible matrix $K$,
then multiplication by $K$ is an injective linear map from $S$ into $S^p$.
Consequently
\[
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal F)
  \leq \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Kraus}}(\mathcal F^p)
  \leq d.
\]
In particular, an invertible element of $S$ suffices, by taking its
$(p-1)$st power. This proves bounded-root selection whenever some root has
that property. It does not settle the general case: the unequal sector
dimensions of the cyclic example above force every element of its original
Kraus span to be singular. The rank identity and multiplication argument are formalized in
\path{TNLean/Algebra/KrausSpanRank.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RootWordSpan.lean}. The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_root_isUnit_mem_wordSpan_pred}
in \path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/RefinementFromInvertibleWordSpan.lean}
proves refinement from such a tensor root, allowing its initial Kraus
alphabet to have any finite size. This is a sufficient condition and does
not assert that an arbitrary divisible channel has such a root.

For a single irreducible target block, every channel root is irreducible.
Indeed, a subspace invariant under the root's Kraus operators is invariant
under all their $p$-fold products. If the root has period $m$, the arbitrary
blocking theorem then gives $\gcd(m,p)=1$, since its $p$th power remains
irreducible. These two necessary conditions are formalized in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementRootIrreducibility.lean} and
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/RefinementRootPeriod.lean}. The declarations
\leanid{MPSTensor.isIrreducibleFamily_of_transferMap_eq_blockTensor} and
\leanid{MPSTensor.IsPeriodic.coprime_of_isIrreducibleFamily_blockTensor}
allow different Kraus alphabet sizes for the target and root; neither
assumes the required rank bound. Thus an irreducible counterexample to bounded-root selection
would require singular Kraus spans for every possible root, despite this
coprimality constraint. No such counterexample or general selection argument
has been established here.

\section{Invertible targets admit the required rank bound}

The following reduction is formalized in
\path{TNLean/Algebra/InjectivePositiveMapRank.lean}.
Here invertibility means invertibility as a linear endomorphism of
$M_D(\C)$, not invertibility of the Choi matrix. If
$\mathcal E=\mathcal F^p$ is invertible and $p>0$, then every completely
positive root $\mathcal F$ has Choi rank at most that of $\mathcal E$.
Neither irreducibility nor trace preservation is needed for this assertion.

First, let $\Phi$ be an injective positive complex-linear map between
matrix algebras, and let $A\ge0$. Write $P$ and $Q$ for the support
projections of $A$ and $\Phi(A)$, respectively. Every positive matrix $X$
supported on $P$ satisfies $X\le cA$ for some $c\ge0$, because $A$ is
positive definite on its support. Hence
$0\le\Phi(X)\le c\Phi(A)$, and $\Phi(X)$ is supported on $Q$.
Every matrix in the corner determined by $P$ is a complex linear
combination of positive matrices in that corner. Thus $\Phi$ restricts
to an injective complex-linear map between the two corners. Comparing
their dimensions gives
\begin{align}
  (\operatorname{rank} A)^2
  &\le (\operatorname{rank}\Phi(A))^2,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{rank} A\le\operatorname{rank}\Phi(A).
  \label{eq:dccsp17_injective_positive_rank}
\end{align}

If $\mathcal F$ is an invertible completely positive map, its amplification
$\mathcal F\otimes\mathrm{id}$ is both injective and positive. Apply
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_injective_positive_rank}) to the Choi matrix of a
completely positive map $\mathcal G$. The Choi identity for composition
then yields
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{rank} C_{\mathcal G}
  &\le \operatorname{rank} C_{\mathcal F\circ\mathcal G}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_injective_composition_choi_rank}
\end{align}
Since invertibility of $\mathcal F^p$ implies invertibility of
$\mathcal F$, iteration proves the asserted bound. In particular, if
$\mathcal E$ has a Kraus representation with $d$ operators, every channel
root of an invertible $\mathcal E$ has a representation with at most $d$
operators. No assumption on eigenvalue multiplicities is required.
The declarations
\leanid{Channel.choiRank_le_comp_of_injective} and
\leanid{Channel.choiRank_le_pow_of_injective} prove the composition and
power inequalities. The resulting refinement theorem is
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_isPDivisibleChannel_of_injective} in
\path{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/RefinementFromInjectiveTransfer.lean}.
It requires no canonical-form assumption.

Consequently any obstruction to bounded-root selection must have a
singular target channel. For this remaining case, a first-order argument
alone is insufficient. If the root has Kraus span $S$ of dimension $r$
and the target has Kraus span $S^p$ of dimension $s$, the differential
of the power map is
\begin{align}
  H&\longmapsto
     \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathcal F^k H\mathcal F^{p-1-k}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_power_differential}
\end{align}
It maps the $r^2$-dimensional real space of Hermitian Choi perturbations
supported on the vectorization of $S$ into the corresponding
$s^2$-dimensional space for $S^p$. When $r>s$ it therefore has a nonzero
kernel. For an irreducible target, every direction in this kernel preserves
trace to first order, as shown below. Extending such a direction to an
exact family of roots and reaching a smaller Choi support remain unproved.

\section{Trace preservation at an irreducible target}

The following reductions are mathematical arguments; they are not yet
asserted as Lean theorems. Let $\mathcal E$ be an irreducible CPTP map on
$M_D(\mathbb C)$, where $D>0$, and let $p>0$. Its adjoint fixed space is
$\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal E^*)=\mathbb C I$.

First, every completely positive root $\mathcal F^p=\mathcal E$ is
trace preserving. Indeed, $\mathcal F$ commutes with $\mathcal E$, so
$\mathcal F^*(I)$ is fixed by $\mathcal E^*$. Thus
$\mathcal F^*(I)=cI$ for some $c\ge0$. Taking the $p$th power gives
$c^p=1$, hence $c=1$.

Second, suppose that a Hermiticity-preserving perturbation $H$ belongs to
the kernel of the differential in
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_power_differential}). Put $T=\mathcal F^*$ and
$h=H^*(I)$. Since $T(I)=I$, the adjoint differential equation evaluated
at $I$ gives
\begin{align}
  \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}T^k h&=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_trace}
\end{align}
Multiplying by $T-I$ yields $(\mathcal E^*-I)h=0$. Consequently $h=cI$,
and (\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_trace}) gives $pcI=0$. Hence $h=0$:
every such perturbation preserves trace to first order. This argument
requires neither diagonalizability nor a nonzero spectrum.

In particular, if the root and target have Choi ranks $r>s$, the dimension
count above supplies a nonzero Hermitian Choi perturbation in the kernel
of the differential. It automatically preserves trace, and the perturbed
map is CPTP for sufficiently small real parameters. This is only a
first-order statement about the root equation. For $p=2$ one has
\begin{align}
  (\mathcal F+tH)^2&=\mathcal E+t^2H^2
  \qquad\text{if }\mathcal F H+H\mathcal F=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_tangent_second_order}
\end{align}
The differential equation does not itself eliminate the quadratic term.
An exact deformation therefore requires a further argument; rank
minimization and compactness alone do not supply one. These reductions
apply to a single irreducible target. A target with several irreducible
blocks can have a larger adjoint fixed space, so the same proof does not
establish trace preservation in that setting.

\section{The zero eigenspace cannot simply be discarded}

A root commutes with its power and therefore preserves the decomposition
into the generalized zero eigenspace and the sum of the nonzero generalized
eigenspaces. This is a decomposition of the vector space of matrices; it
need not be a decomposition into matrix algebras or positive subspaces.
Consequently changing the root on one summand need not preserve complete
positivity.

There are two concrete difficulties with deleting the zero-eigenspace
action. First, the seven-dimensional cyclic-reset example above already
annihilates the zero eigenspace of its square. Its rank-seven alternative
root instead adds a square-zero action: traceless matrices in the first
two-dimensional sector are carried unitarily to the second, and the next
reset annihilates them. Thus decreasing the Kraus rank can require adding
nilpotent action, rather than removing it.

Second, deletion can destroy even positivity. Consider the matrices
\begin{align}
 P&=\frac1{60}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 21&1&21&17\\15&29&3&13\\18&22&6&14\\6&8&30&16
 \end{pmatrix}, &
 Q&=\frac1{40}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 12&-4&20&12\\9&17&5&9\\10&10&10&10\\9&17&5&9
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_matrices}
\end{align}
Both matrices have all column sums equal to one. The entries of $P$
are strictly positive, whereas $Q_{12}=-1/10$.
Put $u=(2,1,2,-5)^{\mathsf T}$, $v=(3,7,-9,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, and
$N=uv^{\mathsf T}/120$. Direct multiplication gives
\begin{align}
 P&=Q+N, & N^2&=QN=NQ=0, & Q(Q-I)(5Q-I)&=0.
 \label{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_identities}
\end{align}
In particular $P^2=Q^2$. The square-free polynomial in
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_nilpotent_deletion_identities}) shows that $Q$ is
semisimple. Its eigenvalues are $1,1/5,0,0$, while $P$ has the same
nonzero part and a nonzero nilpotent part on the zero eigenspace.

For a real matrix $R$, define a linear map on $M_4(\C)$ by
$\mathcal F_R(X)=\operatorname{diag}(R\operatorname{diag}(X))$, where the
inner diagonal denotes the vector of diagonal entries. Since every entry
of $P$ is positive and its columns sum to one, $\mathcal F_P$ is a CPTP
map. It is primitive: it sends every nonzero positive matrix to a positive
definite diagonal matrix. Its square $\mathcal E=\mathcal F_P^2$ has a
semisimple zero eigenspace. Removing the action of $\mathcal F_P$ on that
eigenspace gives exactly $\mathcal F_Q$, which still satisfies
$\mathcal F_Q^2=\mathcal E$. Nevertheless $\mathcal F_Q$ is not positive,
as its value on the second diagonal matrix unit has first diagonal entry
$-1/10$.

These exact rational calculations have not been formalized in Lean.
They disprove the proposed deletion procedure, not bounded-root selection:
$P$ and $P^2$ have strictly positive entries, so both channels have Choi
rank $16$ and the original root already satisfies the required rank bound.
The singular-target problem must therefore control complete positivity
while choosing the zero-eigenspace action; algebraic spectral truncation
alone does not do so.

\section{A five-sector perturbation argument, not yet formalized}

For the paper calculations in the following subsections, $C_{\mathcal F}$
denotes the unnormalized Choi matrix $\sum_i|A_i\rangle\!\langle A_i|$.
Its rank agrees with the normalized Choi rank used by the Lean declarations.


The following paper argument goes beyond the diagonal cyclic-reset example.
It has not yet been formalized in Lean. Let
$V=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}V_i$, with sector dimensions
$(1,2,2,1,1)$, and put
\begin{align}
  S=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Hom}(V_i,V_{i+1})\subset M_7(\mathbb C).
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_five_sector_kraus_span}
\end{align}
Suppose a supplied channel $\mathcal F$ has Kraus span exactly $S$.
Its Choi rank is $\dim S=10$. There is a channel $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$
of Choi rank at most nine satisfying
$\widetilde{\mathcal F}^{\,p}=\mathcal F^p$ for every integer $p\ge2$.
In particular, it is a bounded square root of $\mathcal F^2$, whose Choi
rank is nine. No unitality or absence of coherences is required.

Here is the construction. Define the off-diagonal matrix corners
$C_i=\operatorname{Hom}(V_{i+1},V_i)$, of dimensions $(2,4,2,1,1)$.
The cyclic Kraus support implies
$\mathcal F(C_i)\subset C_{i+1}$; write $A_i:C_i\to C_{i+1}$ for the
restrictions. Since $\dim C_1=4>2=\dim C_2$, choose a nonzero
$u\in\ker A_1$. Since $\dim C_4=1<2=\dim C_0$, choose a nonzero complex
linear functional $\varphi$ on $C_0$ annihilating $\operatorname{ran}A_4$.
The rank-one map $H_0:C_0\to C_1$, $H_0(x)=\varphi(x)u$, is nonzero and
satisfies $A_1H_0=H_0A_4=0$.

Extend $H_0$ to a Hermiticity-preserving map $\mathcal H$ on $M_7$ by
setting $\mathcal H(x^*)=H_0(x)^*$ for $x\in C_0$, and setting it to
zero on every other matrix corner. Its outputs are off-diagonal, so it is trace
annihilating. The preceding two identities and the cyclic corner support
give
\begin{align}
  \mathcal F\mathcal H=\mathcal H\mathcal F=\mathcal H^2=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_perturbation_annihilation}
\end{align}
It follows by expanding products that
$(\mathcal F+a\mathcal H)^p=\mathcal F^p$ for every real $a$ and $p\ge2$.
This is an exact affine family, rather than only a first-order variation.
The exact power identity is separately formalized by
\leanid{add_pow_eq_of_annihilating_square_zero} in
\texttt{TNLean/Algebra/AnnihilatingPowerPerturbation.lean}; the channel
support conditions that produce the direction are still paper arguments.

The Choi matrix of $\mathcal H$ is supported on the vectorization of $S$:
its only nonzero Choi blocks join the first two summands of $S$, together
with their conjugate-transposed blocks. Since $\mathcal F$ has Kraus span
exactly $S$, its Choi matrix is positive definite on this ten-dimensional
support. Thus $\mathcal F+a\mathcal H$ is completely positive for all
sufficiently small real $a$. Trace preservation holds for every $a$.
The set of admissible $a\ge0$ is closed and bounded: its positive Choi
matrices have constant trace seven, while $\mathcal H\ne0$. At its maximum,
the Choi matrix cannot remain positive definite on $S$, since otherwise
positivity would persist for a slightly larger parameter. Its rank is
therefore at most nine, proving the assertion.

Finally, multiplication of the full rectangular summands gives
$S^p=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Hom}(V_i,V_{i+p})$ for every $p\ge1$.
Their dimensions are respectively $11,10,9,9,10$ for residues
$p=0,1,2,3,4$ modulo five. The Kraus-span/Choi-rank identity therefore
shows that this same selected channel has Choi rank no greater than
$\mathcal F^p$ for every $p\ge2$.

This proves a restricted paper-level selection result for supplied roots
with the stated full cyclic Kraus span. It does not classify arbitrary
singular roots, and it does not assert the same conclusion when the Kraus
span is a proper subspace of $S$. The positivity-at-the-endpoint argument
and the cyclic-corner construction remain to be formalized.

\subsection{The full cyclic family with dimensions $(1,m,n,1,1)$}

There is a stronger paper argument, also not yet formalized, for every
$m,n\ge1$. Assume the Kraus span of $\mathcal F$ is the full cyclic space
$S=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Hom}(V_i,V_{i+1})$ for sector dimensions
$(1,m,n,1,1)$. Its Choi rank is $mn+m+n+2$, while the Choi rank of its
square is $d_2=2m+2n+1$. One can select a channel of Choi rank at most
$d_2$ with all powers of order at least two unchanged. For $m=n=2$,
the argument below in fact gives the stronger bound eight.

Partition the positive definite Choi matrix on $S$ by separating the
middle summand $\operatorname{Hom}(V_1,V_2)$ from all other summands:
\begin{align}
  C_{\mathcal F}=\begin{pmatrix}X&C\\ C^*&Z\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  Y=Z-C^*X^{-1}C\ge0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_schur_complement_partition}
\end{align}
The size of $X$ is $a=m+n+2$, and $X$ is positive definite. Leave $X$ and
$C$ fixed and vary only the residual positive matrix $Y$ on the middle
summand. Such a change alters only the map from $M_m$ in sector one to
$M_n$ in sector two. Let $\rho\in M_m$ be the density matrix prepared by
the preceding map from the one-dimensional sector zero.

Impose two linear constraints on the residual completely positive map:
its output trace functional and its value at $\rho$ remain fixed. These
are at most $m^2+n^2$ real scalar constraints. The feasible positive
matrices form a nonempty compact convex set: the trace functional
constraint fixes their trace. At an extreme point of this set the rank
$k$ satisfies $k^2\le m^2+n^2$. Indeed, Hermitian matrices supported on a
rank-$k$ support have real dimension $k^2$. If that dimension exceeded the
number of constraints, a nonzero Hermitian direction would preserve the
constraints and allow positive perturbations of both signs, contradicting
extremality. Since $m,n>0$, this gives $k\le m+n-1$.

Replace $Y$ by such an extreme point. The new whole Choi matrix is
positive, and the Schur-complement congruence shows that its rank is
$a+k\le2m+2n+1=d_2$. Let $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ be its map and put
$\mathcal H=\widetilde{\mathcal F}-\mathcal F$. The trace constraint makes
$\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ trace preserving and gives
$\mathcal F\mathcal H=0$, because the next sector map is the trace to a
one-dimensional sector. The state constraint gives
$\mathcal H\mathcal F=0$, because the preceding map prepares $\rho$.
The changed input and output sectors are distinct, so $\mathcal H^2=0$.
Thus all powers of order at least two are exactly unchanged.

The full rectangular word spans have dimensions $m^2+n^2+3$,
$mn+m+n+2$, and $2m+2n+1$ for residues zero, plus or minus one, and
plus or minus two modulo five, respectively. Each is at least $d_2$:
the differences are $(m-1)^2+(n-1)^2$, $(m-1)(n-1)$, and zero.
Consequently the selected root has Choi rank at most that of every power
of order at least two. This is a selection result for the stated full
cyclic Kraus spaces, including arbitrary coherences and nonunital targets;
it does not prove selection for general singular channels or proper
subspaces of these cyclic spaces.

\subsection{Cyclic roots with one nontrivial sector-to-sector map}

The selection argument extends further at the paper level; this extension
is not yet formalized either. Suppose a channel $\mathcal F$ is supported
on cyclic sectors $(1,m,n,1,\ldots,1)$, with $\ell\ge4$ sectors and
$m,n\ge1$. Its Kraus span need not fill the rectangular summands.
Assume its map from sector zero to sector one prepares a positive
definite density matrix $\rho\in M_m$. Then there is a channel
$\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ such that
\begin{align}
  \widetilde{\mathcal F}^{\,p}=\mathcal F^p\quad(p\ge2),\qquad
  \operatorname{rank}C_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}}
  \le\operatorname{rank}C_{\mathcal F^2}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_selected_cyclic_square_root}
\end{align}
This bounds a selected square root by the actual target rank. It does not
bound the selected root by every higher power's rank. The preparation is
faithful, in particular, when $\mathcal F$ is irreducible: a faithful
stationary state restricts on sector one to a positive scalar multiple
of $\rho$.

Choose a minimal noise Hilbert space $K$ for a Kraus representation of
$\mathcal F$. Denote the coefficient vectors of its rectangular blocks by
\begin{align}
  (u_i)_{i=1}^m,\quad (a_{ji})_{j=1,i=1}^{n,m},\quad
  (v_j)_{j=1}^n,\quad w_1,\ldots,w_k,\qquad k=\ell-3\ge1.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_cyclic_noise_coefficients}
\end{align}
The $u_i$ are linearly independent because their Gram matrix is $\rho$,
the $v_j$ are orthonormal because the next map is the trace, and each
$w_j$ is a unit vector. Put
\begin{align}
  U=\operatorname{span}\{u_i\},\quad V=\operatorname{span}\{v_j\},\quad
  K_0=U+V+\sum_{j=1}^k\mathbb Cw_j,\quad r_0=\dim K_0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_outside_noise_space}
\end{align}
Project every $a_{ji}$ onto $K_0$ and its orthogonal complement. The
residual vectors define a completely positive map $R:M_m\to M_n$.
All coefficient vectors outside this middle map are unchanged, and the
rank of the whole root is $r_0+\operatorname{rank}C_R$.

Let $A=R(\rho)$ and let $B\in M_m$ represent the output trace functional
of $R$, so that $\operatorname{tr}R(X)=\operatorname{tr}(BX)$.
Write $a=\operatorname{rank}A$ and $b=\operatorname{rank}B$.
If $R\ne0$, then $a,b\ge1$: faithfulness of $\rho$ is used here.
Construct a completely positive $R'$ with these same two constraints and
at most $a+b-1$ rank-one Kraus operators as follows. Set
$D=\rho^{1/2}B\rho^{1/2}$, whose rank is $b$ and whose trace equals
$\operatorname{tr}A$. Write spectral decompositions
\begin{align}
  A=\sum_{i=1}^a\alpha_i|e_i\rangle\!\langle e_i|,\qquad
  D=\sum_{j=1}^b\beta_j|f_j\rangle\!\langle f_j|,
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_residual_spectral_decompositions}
\end{align}
with positive coefficients. The two lists have the same total mass.
A nonnegative coupling $(c_{ij})$ with these row and column sums can be
chosen with at most $a+b-1$ nonzero entries: successively assign the smaller
remaining row or column mass to its cell and remove the exhausted row or
column. Each step exhausts at least one, and the last step exhausts both.
For each nonzero entry use the Kraus matrix
\begin{align}
  L_{ij}=\sqrt{c_{ij}}\,|e_i\rangle\!\langle f_j|\rho^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_residual_coupling_kraus}
\end{align}
Then $\sum_{ij}L_{ij}\rho L_{ij}^*=A$ and
$\sum_{ij}L_{ij}^*L_{ij}=B$. Thus the resulting map $R'$ has the same
prepared-state output and trace functional as $R$, with Choi rank
$s\le a+b-1$. This construction avoids an extremality argument.

The finite coupling ingredient is separately formalized by
\leanid{SparseCoupling.exists_positive_mass_coupling} and its probability
specialization \leanid{SparseCoupling.exists_probability_coupling} in
\texttt{TNLean/Algebra/SparsePositiveCoupling.lean}. These results supply
at most $a+b-1$ nonnegative weighted pairs with the prescribed marginals.
The spectral Kraus construction is separately proved by
\leanid{Matrix.exists_kraus_coupling_of_trace_eq} and its faithful-input
version \leanid{Matrix.exists_kraus_state_effect_coupling} in
\texttt{TNLean/Algebra/KrausMarginalCoupling.lean}. They construct the
replacement map with the asserted rank bound. The whole-channel cyclic
selection argument in this subsection is still not a Lean theorem.

The comparison with the target rank uses the two-step coefficient
vectors, with tensor factors ordered by the first and second step.
The blocks outside the middle map form the path space
\begin{align}
  W=(V\otimes\mathbb Cw_1)
      +\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb C(w_j\otimes w_{j+1})
      +(\mathbb Cw_k\otimes U),\qquad q=\dim W.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_outside_path_word_space}
\end{align}
The elementary path-space inequality is $r_0\le q+1$; equality forces
$V,\mathbb Cw_1,\ldots,\mathbb Cw_k,U$ to form a direct sum.
To verify it, start with $V\otimes w_1$, of dimension $n$.
Adjoining the next vertex subspace $E$ adds an edge $w_j\otimes E$.
The old edge space has its right factors in the old vertex span, so the
edge dimension increases by at least the increase of the vertex-span
dimension. Thus a deficit of one cannot reappear once it disappears.
While the deficit is one, the existing vertex spaces are independent,
and the last vertex is absent from the old left-factor span. The new
edge then meets the old edge space trivially, so the deficit persists
only if the new vertex space is independent of the preceding ones.
This proves both assertions by induction along the path.

Project the other two blocks of two-step coefficient vectors onto
$K_0^\perp\otimes K_0$ and $K_0\otimes K_0^\perp$.
Their Gram matrices have ranks $b$ and $a$, respectively. These spaces
are orthogonal to each other and to $W$. Since the full word span
contains $W$, projection onto their sum proves
\begin{align}
  d=\operatorname{rank}C_{\mathcal F^2}\ge q+a+b.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_target_word_rank_bound}
\end{align}
For $R\ne0$, replace its residual coefficient vectors by those of $R'$
in a fresh noise space orthogonal to $K_0$. The new whole root has rank
\begin{align}
  r_0+s\le(q+1)+(a+b-1)\le d.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_selected_cyclic_rank_bound}
\end{align}
The difference changes only the middle sector map, has zero output trace,
and kills $\rho$. It is annihilated by $\mathcal F$ on both sides and
has square zero. Trace preservation and every power of order at least
two are therefore unchanged.

The isolated-corner lift is separately formalized by
\leanid{Matrix.exists_annihilating_corner_perturbation} in
\texttt{TNLean/Algebra/CyclicCornerPerturbation.lean}. It takes supplied
orthogonal corner isometries, the exact incoming preparation and outgoing
trace identities, and a nonzero Hermitian-preserving rectangular map
with zero trace functional that kills the prepared matrix. It constructs
the full matrix-algebra perturbation, proves it nonzero and trace zero,
and proves both annihilation identities, its square-zero identity and
preservation of every power from the square onward.

The same module also constructs the rectangular direction when both
corner dimensions are at least two and the prepared matrix is Hermitian.
The theorem
\leanid{Matrix.exists_nonzero_trace_zero_map_killing_hermitian} constructs
a nonzero Hermitian-preserving map of zero output trace that kills the
prepared matrix; no positivity, normalization or faithfulness hypothesis
on that matrix is required. The declaration
\leanid{Matrix.exists_annihilating_corner_perturbation_of_two_le} combines
this construction with the corner lift. The branch identities remain
explicit assumptions. Support inside the original Choi face, and hence
complete positivity of a rank-reducing perturbation, are not proved by
these corner results.

If $R=0$ and $r_0\le q$, the original root already has rank at most $d$.
In the exceptional case $r_0=q+1$, the path-space equality implies
$V\cap(U+\sum_j\mathbb Cw_j)=0$.
The coefficient vectors of the two-step trace map from sector one to
sector three lie in $K_0\otimes V$, whereas $W$ lies in
$K_0\otimes(U+\sum_j\mathbb Cw_j)$. They cannot all vanish because
that map is trace preserving. Thus they add at least one dimension to
$W$, giving $d\ge q+1=r_0$. This completes the argument.

This result still assumes the specified cyclic support and faithful
preparation. The general singular nonunital case, including other cyclic
sector patterns, primitive channels without this support, general
exponents, and multi-block form II, remains open. None of the arguments
in this subsection is currently a Lean theorem.

\subsection{Adding a scalar loop: primitive nonunital examples}

The preceding paper proof extends beyond the cyclic support hypothesis.
Keep the sectors $(1,m,n,1,\ldots,1)$ and their forward edges, but permit
one additional Kraus block from sector zero to itself. Assume its noise
coefficient vector $h$ is nonzero, and that preparation into sector one
is faithful. Its positive input $\rho$ is now subnormalized rather than
necessarily a density matrix. No argument below requires trace one.
The same selected-square-root conclusion holds, still not yet formalized.

The residual middle-map replacement is unchanged. A perturbation with
zero trace functional and zero value at $\rho$ is annihilated by the
root on both sides and has square zero. The target-rank comparison also
has the same two residual summands of ranks $a,b$. Only the outside word
space changes: in addition to the path space $W$ it contains
\begin{align}
  h\otimes h,\qquad h\otimes U,\qquad w_k\otimes h.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_scalar_loop_words}
\end{align}
These correspond to distinct two-step matrix corners, so they occur as
individual word coefficient vectors. Let $W'$ denote their span together
with $W$, let $K'_0=K_0+\mathbb Ch$, and put $q'=\dim W'$ and
$r'_0=\dim K'_0$. Then $q'\ge r'_0$.

To verify this inequality, choose a complex linear functional $f$ on
$K'_0$ nonzero on all the nonzero coefficient generators
$u_i,v_j,w_1,\ldots,w_k,h$. Such a functional exists because a finite
union of proper hyperplanes cannot fill the dual of a finite-dimensional
complex vector space. Define
\begin{align}
  \delta_f(x\otimes y)=f(y)x-f(x)y.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_delta_f_def}
\end{align}
The graph on these generators whose edges supply the tensor spaces in
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_scalar_loop_words}) and $W$ is connected. After dividing each generator $x$ by $f(x)$,
the image $\delta_f(W')$ under
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_delta_f_def}) contains all differences
along this graph. Those
differences span $\ker f$, of dimension $r'_0-1$. On the other hand,
$h\otimes h$ is a nonzero element of $W'\cap\ker\delta_f$.
Rank-nullity therefore gives $q'\ge r'_0$.

If the residual map $R$ is nonzero, its sparse replacement has rank at
most $a+b-1$, while the target rank is at least $q'+a+b$. Thus the new
whole root has rank at most $r'_0+a+b-1$, strictly smaller than the target
rank. If $R=0$, the original root has rank $r'_0\le q'$ and already
satisfies the desired bound. Every power of order at least two is preserved
in both cases.

When the Kraus span fills all the specified rectangular edge spaces and
the scalar loop, the root and its square are primitive. Indeed, the
cycle connects every sector to sector zero, and powers of the scalar
loop allow a path through zero to be padded to any sufficiently large
length. The corresponding homogeneous word spans therefore contain
every matrix unit for all sufficiently large lengths. This extension
covers primitive nonunital targets with arbitrary allowed coherences;
it is not a proof for unrestricted primitive channels. The connected
word-space comparison is separately formalized by
\leanid{Matrix.finrank_span_le_wordSpace_add_one} and
\leanid{Matrix.finrank_span_le_wordSpace_of_diagonal} in
\texttt{TNLean/Algebra/ConnectedWordSpan.lean}. Their hypotheses supply a
connected graph, a linear functional nonzero on every coefficient, and
the specified oriented outer products in the word space. They do not
construct that data from a channel. Their finite-graph specializations
\leanid{Matrix.finrank_span_le_wordSpace_add_one_of_nonzero} and
\leanid{Matrix.finrank_span_le_wordSpace_of_diagonal_of_nonzero} derive the
functional over an infinite field from nonzero coefficients.
The whole-channel assertions in this
subsection remain paper arguments, not Lean theorems.

\subsection{Three consecutive two-dimensional sectors}

A further paper argument treats a different sector pattern. Let
$\ell\ge5$ and suppose the Kraus span of a channel $\mathcal F$ is the
full cyclic space on sectors $(1,2,2,2,1,\ldots,1)$.
Then there is a channel $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ such that
\begin{align}
  \widetilde{\mathcal F}^{\,p}=\mathcal F^p\quad(p\ge2),\qquad
  \operatorname{rank}C_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}}
  \le\ell+7=\operatorname{rank}C_{\mathcal F^2}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_three_sector_selected_root}
\end{align}
The original root has rank $\ell+8$. These ranks follow by counting
the rectangular summands of the one-step and two-step word spaces.
For odd $\ell$, the square is irreducible of period $\ell$.
Arbitrary coherences among the specified Kraus blocks are allowed.
This selection assertion is not yet a Lean theorem.
When $\ell=5$, the word-space dimensions for positive exponents congruent
to $0,1,2,3,4$ modulo five are $14,13,12,12,13$, respectively.
Thus the same selected root is bounded by the Choi rank of every power
of order at least two in this five-sector case.

Write $\rho_1$ for the prepared density matrix on sector one,
$P:M_2\to M_2$ and $Q:M_2\to M_2$ for the next two sector maps,
and $\rho_2=P(\rho_1)$. All are trace preserving where appropriate;
the full Kraus-span assumption makes $\rho_1$ positive definite.
There are two cases.

First suppose $Q$ is not a rank-one linear map. Some traceless Hermitian
$u$ then satisfies $Q(u)\ne0$. Choose a nonzero Hermitian $\phi$ such
that $\operatorname{tr}(\phi\rho_2)=\operatorname{tr}(\phi u)=0$:
these are at most two real linear constraints in the four-dimensional
Hermitian space. The Hermitian-preserving complex linear map
$H(X)=u\operatorname{tr}(\phi X)$ satisfies
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{tr}H(X)=0,\quad H(\rho_2)=0,\quad H^2=0,\quad QH\ne0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_three_sector_direction}
\end{align}
For a real parameter $t$, change the two diagonal sector maps to
\begin{align}
  P_t=(I+tH)P,\qquad Q_t=Q(I-tH),
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_three_sector_gauge}
\end{align}
and leave all other diagonal and off-diagonal corner maps unchanged.
The whole perturbation is $\mathcal F_t=\mathcal F+tK$, with $K$ nonzero.
It is Hermitian preserving and has zero output trace. Direct corner
composition gives
\begin{align}
  \mathcal F K+K\mathcal F=0,\qquad K^2=0,\qquad \mathcal F^2K=0.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_three_sector_annihilation}
\end{align}
At the first adjacent pair, cancellation is $QHP-QHP=0$.
At the incoming preparation, it is $H(\rho_2)=0$; at the outgoing
trace, it is $\operatorname{tr}H=0$. Two further steps kill the two
possible outputs of $K$ by the trace map. Thus
$\mathcal F_t^p=\mathcal F^p$ for every $p\ge2$.

The Choi perturbation is supported on the full cyclic Kraus space, where
$C_{\mathcal F}$ is positive definite. Hence $\mathcal F_t$ is completely
positive for all sufficiently small real $t$. The completely positive
parameters form a closed bounded interval: the Choi trace is constant
and the perturbation is nonzero. At an endpoint the Choi rank is at most
$\ell+7$. Trace preservation and all the required powers are unchanged.

Now suppose $Q$ has linear rank one. Trace preservation makes it a
replacement channel, so $Q(X)=\sigma\operatorname{tr}X$ for a density
matrix $\sigma$. Partition the root Choi matrix into the four-dimensional
Kraus block of $P$ and the outside blocks, of total dimension $\ell+4$.
Its Schur residual is a completely positive map $R:M_2\to M_2$ with
positive definite Choi matrix. Replace $R$ by the sparse state--effect
coupling with the same value at $\rho_1$ and the same trace functional.
It uses at most $2+2-1=3$ Kraus operators. The new whole root therefore
has rank at most $\ell+4+3=\ell+7$. Its difference from $\mathcal F$
is annihilated on the left by the replacement channel $Q$, on the right
by preparation of $\rho_1$, and has square zero. All powers of order at
least two are preserved in this case as well.

The power integration used in the first case is separately proved by
\leanid{add_pow_eq_of_sq_eq_of_sq_mul_eq_zero} and
\leanid{add_pow_eq_of_anticommuting_square_zero}. They are algebraic
identities, not proofs of the corner construction, its completely
positive boundary argument, or the whole selection assertion.
This treats full Kraus spaces with three consecutive two-dimensional
sectors. Proper subspaces of that support, other sector dimensions, and
the unrestricted singular nonunital problem remain open.

The same paper argument also treats full cyclic spaces on
$(1,m,2,r,1,\ldots,1)$ with $m,r\ge2$ and $\ell\ge5$.
The root and square ranks are
$R=3(m+r)+\ell-4$ and $d=mr+m+r+\ell-1$, so
$R-d=1-(m-2)(r-2)\le1$. If $R\le d$, the original root suffices.
Otherwise, the non-replacement case uses the same map $H$ on the
two-dimensional middle sector and lowers the rank at a boundary point.
In the replacement case, the Schur residual of $P:M_m\to M_2$ has
rank $2m$ and is replaced by at most $m+1$ Kraus operators, decreasing
the total rank by at least $m-1\ge1$. The same power identities apply.
This extension, too, remains a paper argument with the stated full-span
and sector-dimension assumptions.

\section{Explicit bounded roots from a commuting projected semigroup}

A further class of singular nonunital channels admits bounded roots in
arbitrary dimension. Assume the target has the supplied representation
\begin{align}
  \mathcal E=e^{t\mathcal L}\circ\mathcal P,\qquad t\ge0,
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_projected_semigroup_representation}
\end{align}
where $e^{u\mathcal L}$ is a channel for every $u\ge0$, and $\mathcal P$ is
an idempotent channel commuting with $\mathcal L$. These are explicit extra
hypotheses relative to general channel divisibility. No representation
result for arbitrary divisible channels is claimed.

For every positive integer $p$, set
$\mathcal F=e^{(t/p)\mathcal L}\circ\mathcal P$. Commutation, the semigroup
law, and $\mathcal P^p=\mathcal P$ show that $\mathcal F$ is a channel and
$\mathcal F^p=\mathcal E$. Put
$\mathcal R=e^{(t-t/p)\mathcal L}$. Since $t-t/p\ge0$, this is completely
positive; as a linear exponential, it is injective, with linear inverse
$e^{-(t-t/p)\mathcal L}$. The inverse is used only for injectivity.
The identity $\mathcal E=\mathcal R\circ\mathcal F$ and the injective
composition inequality give
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{rank}C_{\mathcal F}\le\operatorname{rank}C_{\mathcal E}.
  \label{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_projected_semigroup_root_rank}
\end{align}
Consequently a tensor with this transfer-map representation is refinable
for every positive exponent on its original physical alphabet. Neither
unitality nor irreducibility is needed, and the projection may make the
target singular. The representation is not deduced from the hypotheses of
the general converse.

The results
\leanid{Channel.choiRank_comp_expSemigroup_mono},
\leanid{Channel.exists_root_choiRank_le_of_projected_semigroup}, and
\leanid{MPSTensor.isPRefinable_of_projected_semigroup_transfer} in
\texttt{TNLean/MPS/Periodic/Symmetry/RefinementFromProjectedSemigroup.lean}
prove the rank comparison, bounded-root construction, and refinement for
this class. The first result applies more generally to any fixed completely
positive $\mathcal P$ and any two times $0\le s\le t$.

\section{Conclusion}

The formal reverse theorem has an additional hypothesis relative to
Theorem~4.1 of Ref.~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}. The source claims that
channel-level $p$-divisibility of $\mathcal E_B$ suffices to construct a
$p$-refinement of $B$, but its proof uses a tensor root whose existence
requires the Kraus-rank bound
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_required_rank_bound}). This bound is
absent from the source definition of $p$-divisibility and is not proved in the
cited converse paragraph.

For invertible target channels, the preceding rank argument supplies the
required bound for every root. The supplied commuting projection-semigroup representation also gives
bounded roots for the singular targets just described. For general singular
target channels it remains open whether, under the irreducible-form
hypotheses, a channel root can always
be chosen with Kraus rank at most $d$. The formal development
therefore proves the reverse implication from a selected bounded-rank
root, and under the inverse canonicalization hypothesis
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_root_kraus_rank_thm41_inverse_canonicalization}). Until a paper-level
argument establishes the required rank bound, the reverse implication must
remain conditional on this additional mathematical input.
