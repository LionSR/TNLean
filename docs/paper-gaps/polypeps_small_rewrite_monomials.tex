\section{The source lemma}

Lemma~6.3 of the September~24, 2026 polynomial-PEPS
manuscript~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS} (\path{05-frames.tex},
lines~188--219) relates two one-sheet encoded frames $\mathcal F_{\rm old}$ and
$\mathcal F_{\rm new}$ on an $L\times L$ lattice that agree outside a bounded
number of affected holes ($r_{\rm old}$ old holes and $r_{\rm new}$ new holes)
and are covered by $m$ square patches with cylinder projectors $P_1,\dots,P_m$
of accuracy $\varepsilon>0$. The lemma assumes four geometric and ownership
conditions: (i) the patch outer squares and the affected holes are disjoint
from the outer footprints of all untouched holes; (ii) the patch inner squares
cover every site whose raw owner changes and the entire outer footprint of
every affected hole; (iii) in each patch outer square, the sites outside the
old inner holes have at most two old raw owners, and the sites outside the new
inner holes have at most two new raw owners; and (iv) all raw owners in the
patch outer squares and all affected tag owners belong to a fixed bounded list
of parties. With $K_{\rm old},K_{\rm new}$ denoting the frame encodings and
$\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm old}},\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm new}}$ the encoded
reference vectors, Lemma~6.3 asserts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the transition operator
  $M=K_{\rm new}P_m\cdots P_1K_{\rm old}^{\dagger}$ is a contraction satisfying
  $\|M\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm old}}-\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm new}}\|
   \le(m+r_{\rm old})\varepsilon$;
\item for every fixed exponent $a>0$, $M$ admits a contractive approximation
  $M_a$ with $\|M_a-M\|\le L^{-a}$ that expands into a polynomial number of
  monomials allowed by Theorem~5.2 using only the specified parties.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What is proved}

Both parts of Lemma~6.3 are proved in Lean under the paper's hypotheses.

First, part~1 is proved for arbitrary tag orderings using only condition~(i):
because the encodings of holes with disjoint outer footprints commute,
reordering the tag registers to place the untouched holes first in both frames
is a basis permutation that intertwines the frame encodings and preserves the
operator norm and reference-vector error.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.norm_rewrite_le_one_and_refVec_sub_le},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.norm_rewrite_refVec_sub_le}, and, for
arbitrary tag orderings,
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_norm_le_one_and_refVec_sub_le_of_perm}.}

Second, the proof of part~2 (\path{05-frames.tex}, lines~254--341) is
formalized through the branch expansion, residual wire network, Schmidt
truncation, and register-level monomial decomposition. Expanding the hole
encoders $K_{\rm new}$, the patch projectors $P_m\cdots P_1$, and the decoders
$K_{\rm old}^\dagger$ into their rank-one cylinder terms expresses $M$ as a sum
over $N$ branches $\beta=(a,s,b)$. In each branch, the sheet operator
$B_aC_sA_b^\dagger$ is an ordered product of rank-one operators
$\ket{k}\bra{b}_S\otimes\mathbb{1}$ coming from the new-hole encodings
$\ket0\bra{u'_r}$, the patch projectors $\ket{w_i}\bra{w_i}$, and the old-hole
decodings $\ket{u_t}\bra0$. This product equals the contraction of a directed
wire network whose vertices are the individual bras and kets of the factors and
whose wires connect each ket output at a site to the next bra acting on that
site.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.SiteChain.chainOp_apply}.}
Because no wire connects a vertex to itself and every vertex vector is
normalized, the tree-network Schmidt truncation (Lemma~6.2) applies with one
open-leg group per vertex, bounding the operator norm of the residual error by
its Hilbert--Schmidt norm independently of the local site dimensions.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.SiteChain.norm_approxOp_le} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.SiteChain.exists_chainOp_truncation}.}
By conditions (i)--(iv), only the $2m$ patch bras and kets carry open legs, and
each open-leg group is owned by at most two old or two new parties from the
specified list. Truncating each internal wire to rank $k$ approximates each
branch within $2m/\sqrt k$ by at most $k^{2m}$ rank-one product terms
$T_{\beta,i}$ with coefficients $|c_{\beta,i}|\le 1$, so for any $\delta>0$ the
rescaled operator
$M_\delta=(1+\tfrac12\delta)^{-1}\sum_\beta\ket a\bra b\otimes
\bigl(\sum_ic_{\beta,i}T_{\beta,i}\bigr)\otimes\mathbb{1}$ is a contraction
satisfying $\|M_\delta-M\|\le\delta$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_branch_truncation} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_contractive_approx}.}

To express each term $\ket a\bra b\otimes T_{\beta,i}\otimes\mathbb{1}$ as an
allowed monomial on the frame registers (lines~306--316), the formalization
uses the site-and-tag register layout (lines~74--75 and~84--85), with one raw
register per site and one register per tag held by its owner.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.layoutRegs},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.layoutIso}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.layoutIso_layoutVec}.}
Up to a scalar of modulus at most one, each branch term is realized by the
composition of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item on the open input legs of each patch bra, a normalized pair effect (or
  single-party contraction) across the at most two old owners, followed by
  preparing $\ket0$ at those owners;
\item at every site of a selected patch or hole square, contracting with $\bra0$
  at the old owner and preparing $\ket0$ at the new owner (lines~258--268);
\item on the open output legs of each patch ket, contracting with $\bra0$ at
  the at most two new owners and preparing the normalized pair source (or
  single-party state) of the term;
\item contracting with $\bra b$ on the affected old tags and preparing $\ket a$
  on the affected new tags at their tag owners.
\end{enumerate}
All other sites lie outside the selected squares and retain their owners by
condition~(ii). Each monomial uses only the specified parties and at most $2m$
normalized pair sources and $2m$ normalized pair effects.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.exists_rankOneBlock_chain},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.SiteChain.termOp_eq_smul_prod},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_termChain}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_allowed_monomial_approx}.}

Finally, with $k=\lceil(4Nm/\delta)^2\rceil+1$, the expansion has at most
$Nk^{2m}$ monomials; if each affected hole and patch has at most $D$ cylinder
terms, then $N\le D^E$ with $E=r_{\rm new}+m+r_{\rm old}$, giving at most
$D^E k'^{2m}$ monomials with
$k'=\lceil(4D^Em/\delta)^2\rceil+1$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.card_branch_le} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_allowed_monomial_approx_of_card_le}.}
In the paper's setting, each hole and patch projector is a sum of $\sum_j d_j\le
CL^c$ cylinder projectors (\path{05-frames.tex}, lines~34--46 and
lines~254--256, giving $N\le L^{C_1}$ and $Nk^{g_0}$ at lines~331--332 and
341--342), while $m,r_{\rm old},r_{\rm new}\le b$ and the party list of
condition~(iv) has at most $b$ parties (lines~189--192 and line~204). Setting
$\delta=L^{-a}$ for $L\ge 1$ bounds the total monomial count by
\begin{align}
  (C+1)^{3b}\bigl(16b^2(C+1)^{6b}+1\bigr)^{2b}\,
  L^{3bc+4b(3bc+\lceil a\rceil)},
  \label{eq:polypeps_small_rewrite_monomials_count}
\end{align}
which is polynomial in $L$ with degree and coefficient depending only on
$C,c,b,a$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_allowed_monomial_approx_polynomial}.}
When $\|\Omega\|\le 1$, $\varepsilon=L^{-60}$ (lines~21--25), and $a=30$, the
count is bounded by~\eqref{eq:polypeps_small_rewrite_monomials_count} with
$\lceil a\rceil=30$ and the reference-vector error satisfies
$\|M_{30}\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm old}}-\Omega_{\mathcal F_{\rm new}}\|\le
L^{-30}+2bL^{-60}\le(2b+1)L^{-20}$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.SmallPatchRewrite.exists_allowed_monomial_approx_refVec}.}

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

The earlier scope restriction---which stopped at the operator-level product
expansion before constructing the register-level monomials and the polynomial
count---is resolved. Both
assertions of Lemma~6.3 are proved under the paper's hypotheses and the
standing cylinder-count bound $\sum_j d_j\le CL^c$ (which comes from
Proposition~4.1). Nothing in Lemma~6.3 remains open.
