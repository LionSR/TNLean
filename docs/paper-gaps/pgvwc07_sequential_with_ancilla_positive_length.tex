\section{Source range}

The theorem ``Sequential generation with ancilla'' of
Ref.~\cite[Section~5, lines~1569--1573]{PerezGarcia2007Matrix} states that
``the three sets of multipartite states which can be generated by the above
sequential schemes with ancilla are all equal to the set of states with OBC
MPS representation with maximal bond dimension $D$.'' The theorem carries no
lower bound on the number $N$ of sites or on the ancilla dimension $D$.

\section{The degenerate cases}

For $N=0$ the only coefficient of an open-boundary representation, whose
first and last bond dimensions are one, is the empty product $1$. The
open-boundary states on the empty chain are therefore the vector $1$ only,
while the probabilistic scheme with $D\ge1$ generates every scalar
$\langle\varphi_F|\varphi_I\rangle$ and the deterministic and transition
schemes generate every scalar of modulus one. For $D=0$ the ancilla space
is zero, every generated vector is zero, and no vector has an open-boundary
representation with bond dimension at most $0$. Neither case is one the
authors intend: the section concerns spin chains of $N\ge1$ sites and an
ancilla that carries a state.

\section{Convention}

The equalities of sets are formalized for chains of $N\ge1$ sites, and the
equality for the probabilistic scheme also for ancillas of dimension $D\ge1$.
The deterministic and transition schemes need no lower bound on $D$, since
their normalization conditions are unsatisfiable when $D=0$. On the empty
chain the two directions of the probabilistic and deterministic equalities
are formalized up to a nonzero scalar, which is the exact form of the
statement there.
