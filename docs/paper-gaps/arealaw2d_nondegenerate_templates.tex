\section{The source definition}

Definition~9.3 of the September~24, 2026 two-dimensional area-law
manuscript~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw} (\path{08-scanner.tex}, lines~546--564)
defines a template with scale parameters $(n,s_0)$ and template constant
$C_{\rm tpl}$ as a nonempty finite set $T\subset\mathbb Z^2$ of the form
\[
  T=\bigcup_{a=1}^{N_0}(P_a\cap\mathbb Z^2),
  \qquad n\ge C_{\rm tpl}N_0(s_0+1),
  \qquad \operatorname{diam}_\infty T\le n,
\]
with $N_0\ge1$, where each piece $P_a\subset\mathbb R^2$ is a closed convex
rectangle or triangle of $\ell_\infty$-diameter at most $s_0$ whose sides have
slope $0,\infty,1$, or $-1$.

\section{The formal convention}

In the formalization, a triangle has three noncollinear vertices and a
rectangle has two orthogonal adjacent sides of positive
length.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Geometry.TemplatePolygon}.}
Line segments and single points are excluded as degenerate polygons. The
nonemptiness of $T$ and the inequality $N_0\ge1$ are already explicit in the
paper. Because nondegeneracy is built into the definition of a template
polygon, downstream theorems need no separate nondegeneracy hypothesis.

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

As the remark after Definition~9.3 notes (lines~566--569), the paper's
estimates use the pieces $P_a$ only through their count $N_0$ and their
diameters. Excluding degenerate polygons matches the intended meaning of
``rectangle'' and ``triangle'' without affecting any theorem in the paper.
Nothing remains open.
