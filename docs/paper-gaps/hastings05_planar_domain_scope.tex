\section{The source statement}

Section~IV of Hastings' \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians
locally}~\cite{Hastings2006Solving}
(arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3, \path{proj3new.tex}, lines~1017--1079)
works on an arbitrary finite metric lattice $(V,d)$. The term $H_i$ is
supported within distance $R$ of its centre $i$, and at most $S$ sites lie
within distance $R$ of any site. Its matrix product form is
\begin{align}
  \rho=\sum_{\alpha}\Bigl(\prod_{i\in V}F_i(\alpha)\Bigr)
          \bigotimes_{j\in V}\rho_j(\alpha_j),
  \label{eq:hastings05_product_metric_form}
\end{align}
where $F_i$ depends only on indices at distance at most $l$ from $i$.
The source asserts that every Trotter product has this form with range
$R$ and at most $nS$ index components at a site after $n$ Trotter steps
(lines~1069--1075). For the cluster-truncated operator it also states
index range exponential in $l^d$ (lines~1273--1275), and exponential in
$(\beta/\beta_0)l^d$ for its extension to every temperature
(lines~1318--1320).

\section{Arbitrary finite metric lattices}

The product form is now proved on every finite set of sites with prescribed
coefficient neighborhoods $N_i$, with no planar embedding or connectivity
assumption. A finite metric lattice is the instance
$N_i=\{j:d(i,j)\le l\}$. Coefficient locality is preserved by products:
pair the two index values at every site, multiply the single-site operators,
and multiply the coefficients. This also proves closure under powers.

For an operator supported on $X\subseteq N_{i_0}$, expand in single-site
matrix units and attach the matrix-element coefficient to $i_0$. The
coefficient reads only the labels on $X$ and at $i_0$, and a diagonal
indicator at every site outside $X$ reads only that site's own label.
Consequently the radius stays $R$ even when the centre lies outside $X$;
the support may also be empty. Exponentiation preserves support, so the
same argument gives every finite Trotter product on an arbitrary finite
metric lattice.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.hasProductForm_of_mem_supportedOperators_radius},
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.hasProductForm_trotterProduct_of_dist_le}, in
\doclink{TNLean/Lattice/ProductForm}.}

The truncated operator also has the product form on an arbitrary nonempty
finite metric lattice. The index at each site records its cluster of terms,
if any, and a matrix-unit label. Every retained cluster has support of
diameter at most $l$, so all indices read by its coefficient at $i$ lie in
$N_i$. Terms acting on no site contribute a scalar coefficient at any
chosen site. The truncation here is exactly the diameter truncation used
in the analytic error bound.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.hasProductForm_truncatedExp_of_dist_le}
and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.hasProductForm_truncatedExp_pow_of_even_of_dist_le},
in \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/ClusterExpansion/TruncatedAllTemperatures}.}
The nonempty-site convention is that of the source, which has $N\ge1$.
The previous planar predicate
\texttt{\detokenize{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.IsLargeWalk}} is replaced by
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.IsLargeSupport}, defined for any
coefficient neighborhoods; the metric specialization is equivalent to
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.IsLargeDiam}. The former assertion
\texttt{\detokenize{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.IsValidCluster.exists_walk}} is
correspondingly replaced by
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.IsValidCluster.related}, which
states locality for two sites of a valid cluster without imposing a walk metric.

Thus the former restriction to planar domains is removed. Both the Trotter
index count and the local index range of the cluster-truncated operator are
proved below. The dimensional bound makes explicit the volume growth of
the lattice; that growth is not implied by the axioms of a metric.

\section{The Trotter index count}

The index type may now depend on the site, as in the source's indices
$\alpha_i$. For an operator supported on $X$, the construction assigns a
matrix-unit label with $q^2$ values at sites in $X$ and a one-element index
set at every other site. The coefficient at the centre reads only the labels
on $X$; labels outside $X$ are unnecessary. If $a_j$ factors of one Trotter
step act on site $j$, their product therefore has exactly $(q^2)^{a_j}$
index values there. With a repetition-free list of centres and supports
within their radius-$R$ balls, symmetry of the metric and the ball count
imply $a_j\le S$. After $n$ Trotter steps the index range is consequently
at most $(q^2)^{nS}$.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ProductForm.ofSupported},
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ProductForm.card_index_ofSupported}, and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_productForm_trotterProduct_card_index_of_dist_le},
in \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/TrotterIndexCount}.}
This proves the source's count at lines~1069--1075. Empty term supports
introduce no components, and at $n=0$ every index set has one value.

\section{Local cluster index counts}

At a site $i$, an index that records an invalid cluster, or a cluster
not containing $i$ in its supports, makes the coefficient at $i$ vanish.
Such values can therefore be removed from that site's index set without
changing the operator or the coefficient neighborhoods. Every term $k$
of a retained cluster containing $i$ acts on some site $j$, and hence
\[
 d(i,c_k)\le d(i,j)+d(j,c_k)\le l+R.
\]
This argument does not require the centre to lie in the support. Put
$M_i=\#\{k:d(i,c_k)\le l+R\}$. There are at most $2^{M_i}$ sets of
these terms, one additional value for no cluster, and $q^2$ matrix-unit
labels. The actual product form consequently satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal A_i|\le q^2(1+2^{M_i}).
\]
Empty-support terms contribute a separate scalar factor whose site
index sets have one value, regardless of their number or centres.
Thus distant scalar terms do not enlarge the local cluster labels.
The $m$-th power has index range at most
$[q^2(1+2^{M_i})]^m$. For Hermitian terms and even $m$, it is positive
semidefinite.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.exists_productForm_truncatedExp_card_index}
and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.exists_productForm_truncatedExp_pow_card_index},
in \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/ClusterExpansion/LocalIndexCount}.}

\section{The dimensional interpretation}

The source's exponential bound in $l^d$ presupposes polynomial growth
of the number of local interaction labels. If
\[
 M_i\le C(\lceil l+R\rceil+1)^D,
\]
the proved bounds are
\[
 q^2\bigl(1+2^{C(\lceil l+R\rceil+1)^D}\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl[q^2\bigl(1+2^{C(\lceil l+R\rceil+1)^D}\bigr)\bigr]^m.
\]
For fixed local dimension and interaction range, these are exponential
in $l^D$ and $ml^D$, respectively, as in lines~1273--1275 and
1318--1320. Both implications from the stated volume bound are proved.

The volume bound itself is proved for distinct centres in any finite
subset of $\mathbb Z^D$ with the coordinate maximum metric. A ball of
radius $r$ is contained in a coordinate box with side length
$2\lceil r\rceil+1$, so its cardinality is at most
\[
 (2\lceil r\rceil+1)^D\le2^D(\lceil r\rceil+1)^D.
\]
The conclusion also transfers through an injective coordinate map that
does not increase distances into this integer lattice. Thus it applies
to intrinsic walk metrics whenever coordinate maximum distance is at
most the physical distance.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.card_centres_integerLattice_ball_le},
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.card_centres_integerLattice_ball_le_polynomial}, and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.card_centres_ball_le_polynomial_of_dist_le}, and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.card_centres_ball_le_polynomial_of_isometry}, in
\doclink{TNLean/Lattice/IntegerLatticeBallCount}.}
Thus one can take $C=2^D$ in this metric setting. With several terms at a
centre, the general theorem counts their labels and requires the
corresponding volume bound including this multiplicity.

An arbitrary metric lattice need not have polynomial volume growth.
Its source-faithful local statement is the bound in terms of $M_i$;
the dimensional interpretation requires the specified geometry. This
clarification replaces the former planar scope restriction and completes
the geometry and index-count obligations of
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/9854}{issue 9854}.
