\section{The source setting}

Section~IV of Hastings' \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians
    locally}~\cite{Hastings2006Solving} (arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3,
\texttt{proj3new.tex}, lines 1017--1079) works on an arbitrary lattice with a
metric $d$, interaction terms $H_i$ supported within distance $R$ of a site $i$,
and at most $S$ sites within distance $R$ of any site. The matrix product
form displayed at source lines 1024--1039 asks for coefficient functions $F_i$ that depend only on the
indices $\alpha_j$ with $d(i,j)\le l_{\mathrm{proj}}$. The source claims that
every Trotter product $\rho_n=[\prod_i e^{-\beta H_i/n}]^n$ has this form
exactly, with range $R$, and that after grouping the indices of all factors at
a site each site carries at most $nS$ indices (lines 1069--1075).

\section{Finite Trotter products: geometry and index count resolved}

The product form is now defined on arbitrary finite sites with physical
spaces $\mathbb C^{d_j}$ and prescribed coefficient neighbourhoods $U_i$.
Its finite auxiliary index set is common to all sites. Coefficient locality
means dependence only on the indices in $U_i$. The original planar
walk-local definition is a specialization, not a second notion.

If $i\in U_i$ and $X_i\subseteq U_i$, a supported factor admits a
single-site matrix-unit expansion. Products preserve the order of the
operators at each site. The counted construction assigns a pair of physical
labels only to sites touched by the factor. In $n$ sweeps, each containing
every centre once, site $j$ therefore carries
$n\#\{i:j\in X_i\}$ such pairs. Put
$D=\max(1,\max_j d_j)$ and
$S=\max_j\#\{i:d(j,i)\le R\}$, taking an empty maximum to be zero.
For $X_i\subseteq\{j:d(i,j)\le R\}$, symmetry of the metric bounds the
number of pairs by $nS$. Padding yields a common auxiliary index set of
cardinality $(D^2)^{nS}$ for the actual finite Trotter product.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.Locality.ProductForm},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.Locality.hasProductForm_trotterProduct_of_metric}, and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.Locality.exists_productForm_metric_trotter_card_bound}.}

This proves the finite-site assertion at source lines 1053--1077 without a
planar embedding or constant physical dimension. Empty supports, zero local
dimensions and the empty site set are included. At zero steps the product
is the identity. For an arbitrary list of centres, each factor is counted
with its multiplicity; the bound $nS$ applies to one occurrence per centre
in a sweep. The older construction assigned labels at every site for every
factor, giving $nm$ components for $m$ factors per sweep. The new
construction removes those inactive components. No convergence estimate
between the Trotter product and the Gibbs operator follows from this
finite-product identity alone.

\section{The truncated operator}
Lines 1256--1279 of the source state that the truncated operator
$\rho(\beta,l_{\mathrm{proj}})$ of the high-temperature expansion is exactly
in the matrix product form of range $l_{\mathrm{proj}}$. On a finite planar
domain with walk distance this is
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.ClusterExpansion.hasProductForm_truncatedExp}, for
terms supported on arbitrary sets of sites (empty supports included), with a
cluster counted as large when two of its sites are joined by no walk of length
at most $l_{\mathrm{proj}}$. The domain is assumed nonempty, as the
source's lattice of $N$ sites is. On the empty domain the product form can
only represent the identity: there is exactly one index function, and both the
product of coefficients and the Kronecker product are empty. Every operator
there is a scalar, and the truncated operator of a single term $H=1$ is
$e^{-\beta}\ne1$ for $\beta\ne0$, so it has no product form there, while
on a nonempty domain the scalar factor of the terms acting on no site is put
into the coefficient of one site. The source's count of index values at a site
(lines 1273--1275) is not established for this construction either.

\section{Remaining restrictions and elimination plan}

Only the truncated-operator result retains the planar geometry restriction
and an unproved quantitative bound on the local index range. Its current
index records a set of interaction terms and a pair of physical labels;
the finite Trotter count does not bound the number of such cluster labels.
To remove these restrictions, express the cluster construction on arbitrary
finite metric sites and replace the recorded global term set by local data
whose number can be bounded as in source lines 1273--1275. Preserve the
nonempty-site hypothesis needed to represent scalar factors from terms
acting on no site. These tasks remain part of
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/9854}{issue 9854}.
