\section{The source setting}

Section~IV of Hastings' \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians
    locally}~\cite{Hastings2006Solving} (arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3,
\texttt{proj3new.tex}, lines 1017--1079) works on an arbitrary lattice with a
metric $d$, interaction terms $H_i$ supported within distance $R$ of a site $i$,
and at most $S$ sites within distance $R$ of any site. The matrix product
form displayed at source lines 1024--1039 asks for coefficient functions $F_i$ that depend only on the
indices $\alpha_j$ with $d(i,j)\le l_{\mathrm{proj}}$. The source claims that
every Trotter product $\rho_n=[\prod_i e^{-\beta H_i/n}]^n$ has this form
exactly, with range $R$, and that after grouping the indices of all factors at
a site each site carries at most $nS$ indices (lines 1069--1075).

\section{The formalized setting}

The sites form a finite domain $\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z^2$, with locality
measured by walks in its nearest-neighbour graph. For a specified centre
$i\in\Lambda$, every operator supported on a set $X$ whose sites are joined
to $i$ by walks of length at most $R$ has a product form of range $R$.
The support may be empty and need not contain its centre. The expansion
attaches the matrix-element coefficient to $i$ and retains the diagonal
identity factor at every site off $X$, including $i$ when $i\notin X$.
Thus the radius condition in the source is now preserved without increasing
$R$ to $2R$. The earlier diameter-bounded theorem follows by choosing any
site of a nonempty support as its centre.

For a family of operators $H_i$ with these centred supports, every finite
ordered Trotter product has a product form of the same range $R$, by closure
under exponentials, products and powers. The centre-indexed statement does
not require a Hamiltonian with diameter-bounded supports.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.hasProductForm_of_mem_supportedOperators_radius},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.hasProductForm_trotterProduct_of_radius}, and the
    corollary \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.hasProductForm_of_mem_supportedOperators},
    in \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/ProductForm}.}
The remaining geometric restriction is the use of finite planar domains
with walk locality in place of arbitrary metric site sets.

\paragraph{Elimination plan.} Define the product form on an arbitrary finite
metric site set, with coefficient locality measured by its given metric.
Repeat the supported-operator expansion with a specified centre and support
inside its radius-$R$ ball, then prove product closure and the centre-indexed
Trotter theorem in this setting. These proofs require no planar embedding;
the present walk-local statements remain specializations. The source's
index-component count requires a separate construction that assigns
components only to sites touched by a factor and uses the bound $S$ on each
radius-$R$ ball. These extensions are part of
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/9854}{issue 9854}.

\section{What is not claimed}

The formalization proves the existence of the product form with range $R$ for
every centre-indexed Trotter product whose term supports lie within radius
$R$ of their centres. It does not establish the source's count of the indices
at a site. The source groups at each site one index component for every
occurrence of a factor acting near that site, at most $nS$ components. Here
each factor $e^{-\beta H_i/n}$ is expanded in single-site matrix units at every
site. On local dimension $q$, a product of $m$ factors therefore carries $m$
components at each site, one for every factor wherever it acts, each with
$q^2$ values, and its $n$-th power, the Trotter product $\rho_n$, carries
$nm$ such components. Any later use of the
product form that needs the index count must establish it separately.
