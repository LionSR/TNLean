\section{The source statement}

Fix an increasing sequence of finite volumes
$\Lambda_0=\emptyset\subset\Lambda_1\subset\Lambda_2\subset\cdots$ and local
Hamiltonians $H_\Lambda$ defined for every volume $\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_m$
with $m\leq n$. Nachtergaele's spectral-gap criterion assumes three
conditions for one and the same finite length $l$, at
lines~1030--1094 of the local source for
Ref.~\cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral}.

Condition~C1 assumes, for a window parameter $q$, that there is a constant
$d_q$ with
\begin{align}
    0
        &\leq\sum_{n=q}^{N}H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}
         \leq d_qH_{\Lambda_N}.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c1}
\end{align}
Condition~C2 assumes that $\mathcal G_\Lambda=\ker H_\Lambda$ is a non-trivial
subspace, writes $G_\Lambda$ for the orthogonal projection onto
$\mathcal G_\Lambda\otimes\mathcal H_{\Lambda_N\setminus\Lambda}$, and assumes
a local gap $\gamma_q>0$ with
\begin{align}
    H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}
        &\geq\gamma_q\left(\mathbf 1-G_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}\right)
        &&\text{for all }n\geq n_q.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c2}
\end{align}
With the conventions $G_{\Lambda_0}=\mathbf 1$ and $G_{\Lambda_{N+1}}=0$, the
martingale differences
\begin{align}
    E_n
        &=G_{\Lambda_n}-G_{\Lambda_{n+1}}
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_martingale_difference}
\end{align}
are mutually orthogonal projections summing to $\mathbf 1$. Condition~C3
assumes $\epsilon_l<1/\sqrt{l+1}$ with
\begin{align}
    \left\lVert G_{\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}}E_n\right\rVert
        &\leq\epsilon_l
        &&\text{for all }n\geq n_l.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c3}
\end{align}
Theorem~2.1(i), at lines~1119--1130, concludes that for any $N$ and any $\psi$
with $G_{\Lambda_N}\psi=0$,
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi\mid H_{\Lambda_N}\psi\rangle
        &\geq\frac{\gamma_{l+1}}{d_{l+1}}
             \left(1-\epsilon_l\sqrt{l+1}\right)^2
             \lVert\psi\rVert^2.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}
\end{align}

\section{The point at issue}

The printed proof, at lines~1195--1259, resolves $\psi$ over the whole range
\begin{align}
    \psi
        &=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}E_n\psi,
    &
    \lVert\psi\rVert^2
        &=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\lVert E_n\psi\rVert^2,
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_resolution}
\end{align}
derives for each index $n$ and each pair of positive weights $c_1,c_2$ the
estimate
\begin{align}
    \left(2-c_1-\frac{\epsilon_l^2}{c_2}\right)\lVert E_n\psi\rVert^2
    -c_2\sum_{m=n-l}^{n}\lVert E_m\psi\rVert^2
        &\leq
          \frac{1}{c_1\gamma_{l+1}}
          \left\langle\psi\mid
            H_{\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}}\psi\right\rangle,
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_per_index}
\end{align}
and then sums it over the same full range.

Three separate hypotheses enter
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_per_index}) and its
summation, and each carries its own lower endpoint. The first term on the
right is estimated by C2 for the volume
$\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}$, that is by
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c2}) at window $q=l+1$ and
index $n+1$, which needs $n+1\geq n_{l+1}$. The coefficient
$\epsilon_l^2/c_2$ comes from C3 at index $n$, which needs $n\geq n_l$. The
summation applies C1 at $q=l+1$; reindexing
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c1}) by $k=n+1$ turns its
left-hand sum into
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=l+1}^{N}H_{\Lambda_k\setminus\Lambda_{k-l-1}}
        &=\sum_{n=l}^{N-1}H_{\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}},
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_c1_reindex}
\end{align}
so C1 budgets exactly the local terms with $n\geq l$. The summed estimate is
therefore available on
\begin{align}
    n_0
        &=\max\{l,\;n_l,\;n_{l+1}-1\}
        \leq n<N,
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold}
\end{align}
and not below. What it yields there is
\begin{align}
    \kappa\left(1-t\right)^2P
        &\leq\langle\psi\mid H_{\Lambda_N}\psi\rangle
              +\kappa\left(1-t\right)tS,
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold_estimate}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
    \kappa
        &=\frac{\gamma_{l+1}}{d_{l+1}},
    &
    t
        &=\epsilon_l\sqrt{l+1}\in[0,1),
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_constants}\\
    P
        &=\sum_{n=n_0}^{N-1}\lVert E_n\psi\rVert^2,
    &
    S
        &=\sum_{m=\max\{0,n_0-l\}}^{n_0-1}\lVert E_m\psi\rVert^2.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_masses}
\end{align}
Passing from
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold_estimate}) to
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}) requires
$P=\lVert\psi\rVert^2$ and $S=0$. The stated conventions
$\Lambda_0=\emptyset$, $G_{\Lambda_0}=\mathbf 1$, and
$G_{\Lambda_{N+1}}=0$ do not make the early differences $E_n$ vanish, and the
source states no padding convention for the early local Hamiltonians. The
question is whether C1, C2, and C3 nevertheless force the missing mass to
vanish.

\section{The printed statement fails}

They do not. The following model satisfies C1, C2, and C3 with their printed
lower endpoints, for every $l$ and every $N$, and violates
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}).

Let the sites be $x=1,2,\ldots$ with $\mathcal H_x=\mathbb C^2$ and basis
$\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle$, let $\Lambda_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and fix
$0<\delta<1$. Take one-site interactions
\begin{align}
    h_1
        &=\delta\,\lvert1\rangle\langle1\rvert_1,
    &
    h_x
        &=\lvert1\rangle\langle1\rvert_x
    \quad(x\geq2),
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_interaction}
\end{align}
and set $H_\Lambda=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h_x$ for every volume $\Lambda$, so that
every $H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_m}$ is defined. Writing
$P_x=\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert_x$, the kernel of $H_\Lambda$ is spanned by
$\lvert0\rangle^{\otimes\Lambda}$ and
\begin{align}
    G_\Lambda
        &=\prod_{x\in\Lambda}P_x,
    &
    E_n
        &=\left(\prod_{x\leq n}P_x\right)\left(\mathbf 1-P_{n+1}\right)
    \quad(n<N).
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_projections}
\end{align}
These ground spaces are non-trivial, the projections $G_\Lambda$ commute,
and $G_{\Lambda_0}=\mathbf 1$, so the martingale structure of the source is in
place.

Condition~C1 holds with $d_q=q$ for every $q$ and every $N$: each $h_x$ lies
in $H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}$ for at most $q$ values of $n$ in
$\{q,\ldots,N\}$, and all terms are non-negative, so
\begin{align}
    0
        &\leq\sum_{n=q}^{N}H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}
         \leq qH_{\Lambda_N}.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_c1}
\end{align}
Condition~C2 holds with $\gamma_q=1$ and $n_q=q+1$: for $n\geq q+1$ the
volume $\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}=\{n-q+1,\ldots,n\}$ excludes site $1$,
so its Hamiltonian is a sum of commuting complementary projections and
\begin{align}
    H_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}
        &=\sum_{x=n-q+1}^{n}\left(\mathbf 1-P_x\right)
         \geq\mathbf 1-\prod_{x=n-q+1}^{n}P_x
         =\mathbf 1-G_{\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-q}}.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_c2}
\end{align}
The middle inequality is checked in the joint eigenbasis of the commuting
$P_x$: both sides vanish where every $P_x$ acts as the identity, and the left
side is at least $1$ elsewhere. Condition~C3 holds with $\epsilon_l=0$ for
every $l$ and every $n\geq0$, since $P_{n+1}$ occurs in
$G_{\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}}$ and
\begin{align}
    G_{\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_{n-l}}E_n
        &=\left(\prod_{x\leq n}P_x\right)
          P_{n+1}\left(\mathbf 1-P_{n+1}\right)
         =0.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_c3}
\end{align}
For $n<l$ the volume is read with $\Lambda_{n-l}=\Lambda_0=\emptyset$ and the
same computation applies. Thus C1, C2, and C3 hold for one and the same $l$,
with
\begin{align}
    d_{l+1}
        &=l+1,
    &
    \gamma_{l+1}
        &=1,
    &
    \epsilon_l
        &=0,
    &
    n_l
        &=l+1.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_constants}
\end{align}
The constants are the ones the source itself would supply: they are uniform in
$N$, and $\epsilon_l=0$ is admitted by C3, whose only requirement is
$\epsilon_l<1/\sqrt{l+1}$.

Now take
\begin{align}
    \psi
        &=\lvert1\rangle_1\otimes\lvert0\rangle^{\otimes(N-1)}.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_vector}
\end{align}
Then $P_1\psi=0$, hence $G_{\Lambda_N}\psi=0$, and $\psi$ is a unit vector
with $H_{\Lambda_N}\psi=\delta\psi$. The printed conclusion
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}) would give
\begin{align}
    \delta
        &=\langle\psi\mid H_{\Lambda_N}\psi\rangle
         \geq\frac{\gamma_{l+1}}{d_{l+1}}\left(1-0\right)^2
         =\frac{1}{l+1},
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_contradiction}
\end{align}
which is false whenever $\delta<1/(l+1)$. The choice of $\delta$ is
independent of $N$, so the failure is not an artefact of small volumes: it
persists for every $N\geq1$ with the same $l$, the same constants, and the
same threshold $n_l$.

The mechanism is exactly the one located in the previous section. Here
$\psi=E_0\psi$, so the entire martingale mass sits at the index $n=0$, which
is below the threshold
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold}) for every $l$.
Condition~C2 fails at that index --- the weakened bond $h_1$ has local gap
$\delta$, not $\gamma_{l+1}=1$ --- and this is precisely what its lower
endpoint $n_{l+1}=l+2$ permits. The printed proof estimates the index
regardless.

A formal instance of this model is verified in the Lean development. Every
operator above is diagonal in the computational basis, so the span of the
single-excitation vectors
$\lvert0^m10^{N-1-m}\rangle$, $0\leq m<N$, is invariant under all of them, and
the model restricts to that span with $E_m$ the projection onto the $m$-th
coordinate. The restriction of
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_interaction}) at
$l=0$ and $\delta=1/2$ refutes the printed statement in every dimension
$N\geq2$.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{FrustrationFree.UnrestrictedNachtergaeleEstimate} transcribes
    (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}) with C1,
    C2, and C3 imposed on the range
    (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold}), and
    \leanid{FrustrationFree.not_unrestrictedNachtergaeleEstimate} refutes it.}

\section{The minimal repair}

Along the printed route the missing hypothesis is determined uniquely.
Suppose (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion}) is to
be deduced from
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold_estimate}).
Subtracting the two inequalities, the deduction is available exactly when
\begin{align}
    \kappa\left(1-t\right)^2
    \left(\lVert\psi\rVert^2-P\right)
    +\kappa\left(1-t\right)tS
        &\leq0.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_deficit}
\end{align}
Here $\kappa>0$ and $1-t>0$, and by
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_resolution}) the deficit
$\lVert\psi\rVert^2-P$ equals $\sum_{n<n_0}\lVert E_n\psi\rVert^2$, so both
summands in
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_deficit}) are
non-negative. Both must therefore vanish, and the vanishing of the first
already implies the vanishing of the second, since $S$ is a subsum of it.
The minimal added hypothesis is thus
\begin{align}
    E_n\psi
        &=0
        &&\text{for all }0\leq n<n_0,
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair}
\end{align}
which, because $\sum_{n<n_0}E_n=\mathbf 1-G_{\Lambda_{n_0}}$ and the $E_n$ are
mutually orthogonal projections, is equivalent to
\begin{align}
    G_{\Lambda_{n_0}}\psi
        &=\psi.
    \label{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair_projection}
\end{align}
Under (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair}) the
conclusion (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_conclusion})
holds with the printed coefficient and with C1, C2, and C3 assumed only on
the source range
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold}).\footnote{Formal
    declaration:
    \leanid{FrustrationFree.NestedGroundProjections.energy_lower_bound_of_nachtergaele_c1_c3_of_martingaleDifference_below_eq_zero};
    the equivalence with
    (\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair_projection}) is
    \leanid{FrustrationFree.NestedGroundProjections.martingaleDifference_apply_eq_zero_of_lt}.}
The earlier full-range formulation
\leanid{FrustrationFree.NestedGroundProjections.energy_lower_bound_of_nachtergaele_c1_c3_full_range},
which assumes C1, C2, and C3 on every index $0\leq n<N$, is the case
$n_0=0$, where
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair}) is vacuous.

The repair is a hypothesis on the pair $(\psi,n_0)$, not a strengthening of
C1, C2, or C3, and it is what applications supply. In the nonwrapping open
matrix-product-state parent Hamiltonian the early martingale differences are
identically zero, so
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair}) holds for every
$\psi$; that specialization is recorded in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/cpgsv21_martingale_overlap.tex}.

\section{Conclusion}

Theorem~2.1(i) of Ref.~\cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral} is false as printed.
Its conditions C1, C2, and C3 carry lower endpoints; its proof estimates every
martingale difference; and no convention stated in the source removes the
difference between the two ranges. The one-site chain
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_model_interaction}) with a
weakened leftmost interaction satisfies all three conditions with constants
uniform in $N$ and violates the printed bound by the factor $\delta(l+1)$.

The correct statement adds
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_repair}), the vanishing of
the martingale differences below the threshold
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_threshold}). That
hypothesis is not merely sufficient: by
(\ref{eq:nachtergaele96_theorem_2_1_lower_endpoint_deficit}) it is forced by
the estimate the printed proof actually establishes. The intended
applications, in which the initial volumes carry no ground-space defect,
satisfy it.
