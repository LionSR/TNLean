\section{The source statement}

The main theorem of~\cite{Hastings2007AreaLaw} and its Lemma~1 use the
lengths
\begin{align}
    \xi=\max(2v/\Delta E,\xi_C),\qquad\xi'=6\xi,
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_source}
\end{align}
where $\Delta E$ is the gap and $v$, $\xi_C$ are a velocity and a length
scale of a Lieb--Robinson bound.\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex},
    lines~139--142.} That bound is printed as
\begin{align}
    \|[A(t),B]\|\le c\,|X|\,\|A\|\,\|B\|\,e^{-\xi_C\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)}
    \quad\text{for }|t|\le l/v,
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}
\end{align}
for operators $A$, $B$ supported on sets $X$, $Y$; the source says only that
such $v$ and $\xi_C$ exist, with $v$ of order $J$ and $\xi_C$ of order
one.\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex}, lines~96--112.}

\section{The reading}

As printed, (\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}) multiplies the
distance by $\xi_C$ and bounds the time by $l/v$, a length not defined at
that point. The reading consistent with the name ``length scale'' and with
the use of $2v/\Delta E$ as a competing length is that the commutator decays
as $e^{-\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)/\xi_C}$ for $|t|\le\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)/v$.
Any pair $(v,\xi_C)$ for which this holds may be used.

\section{The estimate used on the chain}

For range-$R$ Hamiltonians with terms of norm at most $J$, the chain
commutator bound for $A$ supported on $X$ and $B$ supported on a single
site $y$ is
\begin{align}
    \|[A(t),B]\|\le C|X|\,\|A\|\,\|B\|\,e^{v_0|t|-\operatorname{dist}(X,y)/2},
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_chain}
\end{align}
with $C=2+4|b_0|e^{2R}$, $v_0=2(R+1)|b_0|e^{2R}+1$ and $b_0=2^{2R+1}J$.%
\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.norm_heisenberg_commutator_le_chainDistance}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/LiebRobinson}.}
Its support factor is linear in $|X|$, as in
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}). The factorization uses it
through a partial-trace localization onto the $k$-neighbourhood of $X$,
whose error carries $|X|^2$. There $X$ is the support of a commutator
$[H,W]$ with at most $6R$ sites, so the square changes only a constant
depending on $R$, not the length or the rate. The estimate
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_chain}) still differs from
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}) in two ways:
\begin{itemize}
    \item its prefactor $C$ grows with $J$, whereas the source's $c$ is a
          numerical constant;
    \item $B$ acts on a single site rather than on an arbitrary set $Y$.
\end{itemize}
For these reasons $(v_0,2)$, or any pair derived from it, is not shown to
be a Lieb--Robinson pair in the sense of
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}).

The factorization uses the length
\begin{align}
    \xi=\max(16v_0/\Delta E,8)=8\max(2v_0/\Delta E,1).
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_value}
\end{align}
It has the algebraic form of
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_source}), but its justification here
is the chain estimate and the explicit error bounds of the
construction.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.lemmaOneLength}
    and \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_projectorFactorization} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/Hastings/ProjectorFactorization}.}
Because of the constant term of $v_0$, the length
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_value}) is not homogeneous in
$(J,\Delta E)$. Lemma~1 concerns only the ground state, so it may be
applied to $H/\Delta E$, whose gap is $1$ and whose terms have norm at most
$J/\Delta E$. With $v_0=1+c_RJ$ and $c_R=2(R+1)2^{2R+1}e^{2R}$, this gives
Lemma~1 with the length
\begin{align}
    \hat\xi=16\bigl(1+c_RJ/\Delta E\bigr)\le2\max(2v/\Delta E,16),
    \qquad v=8c_RJ,
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_scaled}
\end{align}
which depends on $J$ and $\Delta E$ only through $J/\Delta E$, with $v$
proportional to $J$, as in the source.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.lemmaOneLength_div}
    and \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_projectorFactorization_div} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/Hastings/ProjectorFactorizationScaling}.}

For either length, the construction gives a contraction $O_B$ without a
positivity assertion and error $C_1e^{-l/\xi'}$ for every $\xi'>6\xi$.
The endpoint $\xi'=6\xi$ is not established by this argument; no
counterexample to the endpoint factorization is asserted. The remaining
rate question is recorded in \path{hastings07_gaussian_average_exponent.tex}.
Replacing $O_B$ by $O_B^\dagger O_B$ gives a positive semidefinite contraction
with error of order $e^{-l/(2\xi')}$, as in the source's note added and
\path{hastings07_lemma1_positivity_rate.tex}.

\section{The missing polynomial bound}

Lemma~1 requires $C_1(\xi)$ to be bounded by a polynomial in $\xi$.
\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex}, lines~258--262.}
The theorem presently proves only the existence of a constant depending
on $R$, $J$, $\Delta E$ and $\xi'$, uniform in the chain length, local
dimension, ground state, cut and window length. In the unscaled statement
the explicit coefficients are not homogeneous in the energy scale: they
contain terms such as $2/\Delta E$ and $J^2/\Delta E$, so no bound in $\xi$
alone is possible there. In the statement for $H/\Delta E$ every coefficient
is a polynomial in $J/\Delta E$, and $J/\Delta E\le\hat\xi/(16c_R)$; the
absorption of $\sqrt l$ costs $1/\sqrt{1/(6\hat\xi)-1/\xi'}$, which is
$O(\sqrt{\hat\xi})$ for $\xi'=\kappa\hat\xi$ with a fixed $\kappa>6$. A
polynomial bound on $C_1$ in $\hat\xi$, with coefficients depending on $R$
and $\kappa$, therefore follows once the constant is made explicit. This
has not yet been done.

\section{Remaining work}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Make $C_1$ explicit and bound it by a polynomial in $\hat\xi$ at
          $\xi'=\kappa\hat\xi$, as outlined above. For the source's
          nearest-neighbour terms ($R=1$) the coefficients are then numerical.
    \item Establish a Lieb--Robinson estimate in the form
          (\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}): a numerical
          prefactor, the factor $|X|$, and $B$ on an arbitrary set. Only then
          is $(v,\xi_C)$ identified as a source pair.
    \item The endpoint $\xi'=6\xi$ remains open.
\end{itemize}
These tasks remain part of \ghissue{9811}. The separate blueprint entry for
the printed Lemma~1 remains unmarked as proved. The established
factorization with the stated restrictions does not complete the source's
full quantitative result.
