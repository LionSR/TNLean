\section{The source statement}

The main theorem of~\cite{Hastings2007AreaLaw} and its Lemma~1 use the
lengths
\begin{align}
    \xi=\max(2v/\Delta E,\xi_C),\qquad\xi'=6\xi,
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_source}
\end{align}
where $\Delta E$ is the gap and $v$, $\xi_C$ are a velocity and a length
scale of a Lieb--Robinson bound.\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex},
    lines~139--142.} That bound is printed as
\begin{align}
    \|[A(t),B]\|\le c\,|X|\,\|A\|\,\|B\|\,e^{-\xi_C\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)}
    \quad\text{for }|t|\le l/v,
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}
\end{align}
for operators $A$, $B$ supported on sets $X$, $Y$; the source says only that
such $v$ and $\xi_C$ exist, with $v$ of order $J$ and $\xi_C$ of order
one.\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex}, lines~96--112.}

\section{The reading}

As printed, (\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_lrb}) multiplies the
distance by $\xi_C$ and bounds the time by $l/v$, a length not defined at
that point. The reading consistent with the name ``length scale'' and with
the use of $2v/\Delta E$ as a competing length is that the commutator decays
as $e^{-\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)/\xi_C}$ for $|t|\le\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)/v$.
Any pair $(v,\xi_C)$ for which this holds may be used.

\section{The estimate used on the chain}

The chain localization estimate used for range-$R$ Hamiltonians with terms
of norm at most $J$ has the factor $a|X|^2e^{v_0|t|-k/2}$ at distance $k$,
with $a$ and $v_0$ depending on $R$ and $J$. For $|t|\le k/(8v_0)$,
\begin{align}
    a|X|^2e^{v_0|t|-k/2}
    \le a|X|^2e^{-3k/8}\le a|X|^2e^{-k/8}.
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_pair}
\end{align}
This comparison concerns the exponential factor. It retains the quadratic
support factor and the dependence of $a$ on the interaction parameters;
it does not establish the source's bound with a linear factor $|X|$ and a
numerical prefactor for arbitrary supports. Absorbing polynomial factors
for the particular windows used in the factorization is not such a bound.
Thus this argument does not identify $(8v_0,8)$ as a Lieb--Robinson pair
satisfying the source's full estimate.

The factorization uses the length
\begin{align}
    \xi=\max(16v_0/\Delta E,8)=8\max(2v_0/\Delta E,1).
    \label{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_value}
\end{align}
It has the algebraic form of
(\ref{eq:hastings07_lemma1_decay_length_source}), but its justification here
is the chain localization estimate and the explicit error bounds of the
construction.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.lemmaOneLength}
    and \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists_projectorFactorization} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/Hastings/ProjectorFactorization}.}
For this $\xi$, the construction gives a contraction $O_B$ without a
positivity assertion and error $C_1e^{-l/\xi'}$ for every $\xi'>6\xi$.
The endpoint $\xi'=6\xi$ is not established by this argument; no
counterexample to the endpoint factorization is asserted. The remaining
rate question is recorded in \path{hastings07_gaussian_average_exponent.tex}.
Replacing $O_B$ by $O_B^\dagger O_B$ gives a positive semidefinite contraction
with error of order $e^{-l/(2\xi')}$, as in the source's note added and
\path{hastings07_lemma1_positivity_rate.tex}.

\section{The missing polynomial bound}

Lemma~1 requires $C_1(\xi)$ to be bounded by a polynomial in $\xi$.
\footnote{\path{References/0705.2024/law16.tex}, lines~258--262.}
The theorem presently proves only the existence of a constant depending
on $R$, $J$, $\Delta E$ and $\xi'$, uniform in the chain length, local
dimension, ground state, cut and window length. No polynomial bound in
$\xi$ alone has been established. In particular, the explicit coefficients
retain separate factors involving $J^2/\Delta E$ and $1/\Delta E$;
uniformity in the chain length does not remove that dependence.

\section{Remaining work}

To recover the source's quantitative statement, establish a
Lieb--Robinson estimate with its stated support dependence and controlled
prefactor, and justify the corresponding velocity and length scale.
Then bound the factorization constant by a polynomial in that length,
with the source's parameter dependence. This requires accounting for
energy rescaling, the localization and cutoff constants, and the
short-length estimate. A bound allowing $\xi'>6\xi$ must also state its
dependence on the positive difference of reciprocal lengths
$1/(6\xi)-1/\xi'$; it does not settle the endpoint.
These tasks, together with the endpoint rate, remain part of
\ghissue{9811}. The separate blueprint entry for the printed Lemma~1
remains unmarked as proved. The established factorization with the stated
restrictions does not complete the source's full quantitative result.
