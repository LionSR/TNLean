\section{Source construction}

Section~II.F of arXiv:1010.3732 connects two symmetric injective matrix product
states after replacing them by their isometric forms. Let their bond dimensions
be $D_0$ and $D_1$. The construction embeds the two bond spaces as consecutive
direct summands of an ambient space whose dimension is
\begin{align}
    D_{\mathrm{amb}}
        &=
        D_0+D_1.
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_ambient_dimension}
\end{align}
It then interpolates between maximally entangled vectors supported on the two
summands.

The source display in
\path{Papers/1010.3732/paper_v3.tex}, lines~900--903, reads
\begin{align}
    \ket{\omega_{\mathrm{src}}(\gamma)}
        &=
        (1-\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^{D_0}\ket{i,i}
        +
        \gamma\sum_{i=D_0+1}^{D_1}\ket{i,i}.
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_source_interpolation}
\end{align}
The source immediately states that this vector has bond dimension
$D_0+D_1$.

\section{Correction}

The second summand must occupy the $D_1$ coordinates immediately following
the first $D_0$ coordinates. Its index set is
\begin{align}
    I_1
        &=
        \{D_0+1,\ldots,D_0+D_1\},
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_second_index_set}
\end{align}
rather than $\{D_0+1,\ldots,D_1\}$. The corrected interpolation is
\begin{align}
    \ket{\omega(\gamma)}
        &=
        (1-\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^{D_0}\ket{i,i}
        +
        \gamma\sum_{i=D_0+1}^{D_0+D_1}\ket{i,i}.
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_corrected_interpolation}
\end{align}

At the first endpoint,
\begin{align}
    \ket{\omega(0)}
        &=
        \sum_{i=1}^{D_0}\ket{i,i},
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_first_endpoint}
\end{align}
which is the maximally entangled vector on the first bond summand. At the
second endpoint,
\begin{align}
    \ket{\omega(1)}
        &=
        \sum_{i=D_0+1}^{D_0+D_1}\ket{i,i},
    \label{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_second_endpoint}
\end{align}
which is the maximally entangled vector on the second bond summand. The upper
limit $D_0+D_1$ agrees with the ambient dimension in
(\ref{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_ambient_dimension}). By contrast,
the source range can be empty when $D_0\geq D_1$.

\section{Blueprint and formal status}

The corrected formula
(\ref{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_corrected_interpolation}) occurs in
the proof of the blueprint theorem \path{thm:spt_classification} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch12_symmetry_spt.tex}. An inline
\path{Local fix} comment there points to this note.

The separation argument at source lines~929--953 is explicitly restricted to
smooth MPS paths. The blueprint theorem therefore concerns symmetric MPS paths
and their parent Hamiltonians. It does not claim invariance under arbitrary
gapped Hamiltonian paths.

The fixed-dimension virtual argument is now proved for continuous invertible
projective matrices, without requiring them to be unitary: the determinant
admits a continuous root on the parameter interval, and the resulting
determinant-one factor system takes values in a finite set of roots of unity.
For a prescribed continuous family of one-site injective tensors of that
fixed positive dimension, with symmetry preserving the periodic vectors
exactly, continuous invertible virtual matrices can now be derived on the
whole interval. The inverse Gram matrix gives a continuous right inverse
of the coefficient map. A matrix-unit column selects a local conjugating
matrix; a finite subdivision and scalar uniqueness join these choices.
The subdivision is chosen separately for each group element, so no
finiteness assumption on the group is needed. The resulting virtual
projective representations have factor systems continuous in the interval
parameter for each fixed pair of group elements, and cohomologous endpoint
factors. Arbitrary endpoint choices have the same classes by
scalar gauge uniqueness. Continuity here is in the interval parameter,
separately for each group element; no joint continuity in a topological
group variable is asserted.

The scalar character of the physical ground line need not be imposed as
part of the phase condition. At each finite chain length, the continuous
nonzero MPS vector gives a continuous invariant line. Its eigenvalue under
each fixed physical symmetry operator is continuous and belongs to that
operator's finite spectrum, hence is constant along the interval. These
eigenvalues form a unitary character. Positive-length ray equality transfers
these eigenvalues to each minimal one-site injective representative,
even when its dimension changes. The characters at lengths two and three
determine one common on-site character: if the chain eigenvalues are $c_N(g)$, then
$\chi(g)=c_3(g)/c_2(g)$ and $c_N(g)=\chi(g)^N$ for every $N\geq2$.
Injectivity extends the representative's rephased vector equality to every
chain length; ray equality transfers its one-site symmetry back to the
ambient tensor. Thus the common character can be derived without ambient
injectivity or a locally constant minimal dimension.
This argument does not add a one-site-ring hypothesis or a further
chain-length threshold.

The passage from an exact MPS ground-state path to such a continuous
fixed-dimension injective tensor family remains to be proved, as does the
change-of-support argument of Appendix~C,
\path{app:coh-class-cont-dimchange}, in
\path{Papers/1010.3732/paper_v3.tex}, lines~2653--2717. In particular, the
larger ambient bond space used by the direct-sum interpolation must not be
assumed injective at parameters where the minimal bond dimension decreases.
The remaining separation theorem must include such parameters while retaining
the exact MPS ground-state hypothesis; the Hamiltonian-only converse is a
different unresolved statement.

Continuity of an ambient tensor alone does not give continuity of its
minimal canonical support. For example, let $X,Y,Z$ be the Pauli matrices
and consider the four-letter tensor
\[
 A_0(t)=\mathbf{1}_2,\qquad
 A_1(t)=tX,\qquad A_2(t)=tY,\qquad A_3(t)=tZ.
\]
The on-site action of $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ is the fixed diagonal
sign action induced by conjugation with $X$ and $Z$. For every $t>0$ the
tensor is one-site injective; its virtual generators anticommute, so its
projective class is nontrivial. At $t=0$ the periodic vectors are nonzero
multiples of the product vector $\ket{0}^{\otimes N}$, whose minimal bond
dimension is one and whose virtual class is trivial. This example concerns
raw tensor continuity, not a uniformly gapped path with a unique exact MPS
ground state. Indeed, the canonical two-site local ground spaces tend to
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\ket{00},\;
   \ket{0a}+\ket{a0}:a=1,2,3\};
\]
the limiting two-site ring therefore has additional ground vectors.
Thus the independent relation's uniform-gap and exact-ground-state
conditions cannot be discarded when deriving continuous canonical data.
The derivation of those data remains an outstanding theorem, rather than
an additional hypothesis in the phase relation.

Appendix~C starts with differentiable standard-form tensors and asserts an
extension to continuous tensors by approximation (lines~2660--2664).
It contains no additional spectral-gap estimate; the finite-chain gap is
used earlier to obtain ground-state continuity (lines~953--993).
Consequently the required canonical-support construction must justify the
passage from that continuity to the standard-form support used in the
appendix. The displayed inverses of the off-diagonal and complementary
blocks in lines~2692--2706 also require a separate formulation when the
blocks are rectangular or singular. The source passage alone does not
provide that general support-compression lemma.

For the middle, fixed-point part of that construction,

The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.isSameExactMPSGappedPhase_of_isInjective_cohomologous}

The proved forward construction is distinct from the unresolved separation
argument of Appendix~C. The latter must derive compatible continuous
balanced canonical data and surviving invariant single-bond subspaces from
an independent physical exact MPS ground-state path. The local
dimension-direction assertion can already be justified without canonical
coordinates. The coefficient matrix of the periodic two-site vector has
rank $D^2$ for a one-site injective representative of dimension $D$.
Its continuity and rank lower semicontinuity prove
\leanid{MPSTensor.ExactMPSGroundPath.eventually_minimalBondDimension_ge}:
near a parameter with representative dimension $D_0$, every representative
has dimension at least $D_0$. This concerns a physical two-boundary Schmidt
space; it does not identify an invariant subspace of the single virtual
bond or its projective factor.

Local continuous frames
for supplied continuous fixed-rank orthogonal projections, compression of
supplied continuous unitary projective actions, and continuous spectrally
separated corners are proved auxiliary results. They do not establish the
required implication from the physical gap. Continuity of virtual actions
in these results is in the path parameter separately for each group element;
continuity of the factors is separately for each fixed pair. No joint
continuity in a topological group variable is asserted.

The restriction argument is now also proved for supplied continuous
ambient tensors $B(t)$ and continuous positive trace-one bond densities
$\sigma(t)$. Pointwise support frames identify the minimal tensors as
$A^i(t)=K(t)^\dagger B^i(t)K(t)$. A separated spectral corner retains
the full support at the base parameter and admits a continuous isometric
frame nearby. Restricting the actual unitary virtual actions to that
frame preserves their factors exactly. This proves local cohomology
invariance when the compressed density commutes with those actions and
the minimal tensor is injective at the base. Neither the minimal
dimensions nor the chosen support frames or virtual matrices need be
continuous. No projective representation on the complementary bond space
is assumed.

For a continuous unital tensor family, a one-dimensional adjoint fixed
space at the base parameter gives a local continuous positive trace-one
stationary density, unique among all trace-one fixed matrices. The proof
augments $I-\mathcal E^*$ by the trace normalization to obtain an
invertible linear map, then uses continuity of its inverse. Positivity
follows by identification with a stationary state. Uniqueness descends
through support compression and forces the actual virtual actions to
commute with the compressed density. Thus the declaration
\leanid{MPSTensor.eventually_cohomologousTo_of_continuous_unital_supported_family}
proves local constancy of the virtual class with injectivity only at the
base parameter. The pointwise normalized stationary density is not
assumed continuous. These results remove several auxiliary continuity
and invariance assumptions once the continuous unital bond family is
available. They do not construct that
family from the independent physical exact MPS path and its uniform gap;
this remains the analytic question required for the general converse.

The declaration
\leanid{MPSTensor.exists_local_continuous_canonicalNormalization_of_isInjective}
now supplies local continuous Perron data, an explicit unital normalization,
and a unique normalized adjoint stationary density for any supplied continuous
tensor family in a fixed positive bond dimension, with injectivity only at the
base point. No stationary-space hypothesis is added. This completes the
normalization once a continuous realization is available; choosing that
realization through a change of minimal bond dimension remains separate.

Finite-ring reconstruction is now proved for already injective realizations.
The declaration
\leanid{MPSTensor.samePositiveMpvRay_of_mpv_two_three_eq_smul}
shows that nonzero proportionality at ring lengths two and three determines
the whole positive-length ray family. Two-site traces identify the physical
coefficient quotient, and three-site traces recover multiplication on it;
Skolem--Noether then gives the scalar and bond conjugation. The trace form
is complex bilinear, so its inverse is not a positive spectral-support
construction. Continuous canonical coordinates through a change of quotient
dimension still require a separate theorem.

At constant minimal dimension,
\leanid{Matrix.exists_local_continuous_traceQuotientUnit}
constructs local continuous quotient multiplication and its unit from
$(C^\dagger C)^{-1}C^\dagger R$, where $C=GF$ is a fixed section of the
two-site trace form and $R$ contains contracted three-site traces. Minimal
injective tensors and their scalar normalizations are required only
pointwise. The trace form is complex bilinear; only $C^\dagger C$ is positive
Hermitian. The local continuous realization is now supplied by
\leanid{MPSTensor.exists_local_continuous_unitalTensor_of_samePositiveMpvRay}:
it constructs an injective unital tensor for the same full periodic-ray family,
and a continuous unique normalized adjoint stationary density, without a
continuous choice of the pointwise minimal representatives. Changes of minimal
bond dimension remain open.

The required realization need not be obtainable by gauging the supplied
ambient tensor. For example, let $a$ be a normalized one-site vector and
put $B^i=a_iI_2$. At every positive length $N$, the periodic MPS vector is
$2a^{\otimes N}$, so its ray agrees with the bond-one product tensor.
The adjoint transfer map of $B$ is the identity on $M_2(\C)$ and hence has
a four-dimensional fixed space. Every invertible bond conjugation leaves
these scalar letters unchanged. This redundant realization can accompany
a constant, uniformly gapped product-state parent Hamiltonian. Thus the
physical gap cannot force a simple adjoint fixed space for the particular
ambient realization recorded in the path. The checked declarations
\leanid{MPSTensor.repeatedProductTensor_fixedSpace_finrank} and
\leanid{MPSTensor.repeatedProductTensor_samePositiveMpvRay} record the
stationary-space dimension and periodic-ray comparison for the product
vector $a=\ket{0}$. The missing theorem must choose
a new continuous unital realization of the same ground-state rays, rather
than assert that every supplied realization admits such a gauge.

\section{Verdict}

Replacing the upper limit $D_1$ in
(\ref{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_source_interpolation}) by
$D_0+D_1$, as in
(\ref{eq:spc11_spt_interpolation_upper_range_corrected_interpolation}), is a
local source correction. It restores the intended direct-sum coordinates and
is the version used in the blueprint. Restricting the classification statement
to MPS and parent-Hamiltonian paths is a scope clarification that matches the
source proof.
