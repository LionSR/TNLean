\section{The source argument}
Hastings, \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians locally}~\cite{Hastings2006Solving},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0508554v3}{arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3},
source \path{proj3new.tex}, lines 1603--1642, considers a translationally
invariant system on a $d$-dimensional lattice with $n$ ground states, and the
filtered local terms $M_i$ with their compressions $P M_i P$ to the ground
space. Choosing the ground states as eigenvectors of the translations, with
momenta $\vec k_a$, the matrix elements satisfy
$(M_{\vec x,y})_{ab}=(M_{\vec x+\vec x',y})_{ab}
\exp[i\vec x'\cdot(\vec k_a-\vec k_b)]$ (lines 1613--1618).

The source then assumes the ``simplest case'' (lines 1620--1625): every
component of every momentum is a rational multiple $2\pi p/q$ whose
denominator $q$ does not depend on the system size. From this it derives a
supercell, whose size in direction $m$ is the least common multiple of the
denominators $q^m_a$, such that ``all of the $n$ ground states are unchanged by
translation by a supercell'' (lines 1626--1630). For a translation $\vec x'$
by whole supercells, $[M_{\vec x_1,y_1},M_{\vec x_2,y_2}]$ and
$[M_{\vec x_1,y_1},M_{\vec x_2+\vec x',y_2}]$ then agree in the low-energy
sector, and choosing $\vec x'$ to place the second site at a distance of order
the linear size of the system makes the commutator exponentially small in that
size (lines 1634--1641).

\section{The restriction}
The formalization does not assume rational momenta. It assumes directly a
subgroup $S$ of the symmetry group whose site permutations $P_g$ leave every
ground vector unchanged, $P_g v=v$ for $g\in S$ and $v$ in the ground space,
which is the conclusion of lines 1626--1630. From this it proves, for
covariant terms $H_{g\cdot k}=P_gH_kP_g^\dagger$ and an invariant Hamiltonian,
\begin{align}
    PM_{g\cdot j}P&=PM_jP,
    \label{eq:hastings05_translation_supercell_compression}\\
    [PM_iP,PM_jP]&=[PM_iP,PM_{g\cdot j}P]\qquad(g\in S),
    \label{eq:hastings05_translation_supercell_commutator}
\end{align}
so that any bound on the low-energy commutator at the pair $(i,g\cdot j)$
applies at $(i,j)$. For an abelian group the subgroup is then constructed from
the momenta, as in the first step of the elimination plan below. The geometric
statement that some $g\in S$ moves $j$ to a distance of order the linear size
from $i$, which is where the source uses the rational momenta, is formalized
as well, as described below.

\section{Elimination plan}
For an abelian group $G$, such as the translations of a finite torus, the
operators $P_g$ commute with $H$ and with one another, so they restrict to
commuting unitaries on the ground space. A joint eigenbasis gives characters
$\chi_a$ of $G$, one for each ground state, which are the momenta of the
source. The subgroup $S=\bigcap_a\ker\chi_a$ then fixes every ground vector,
with no further hypothesis. This first step is formalized: the momentum
sectors span the ground space, and the intersection of the kernels of the
characters with a nonzero sector is exactly the subgroup fixing the ground
space.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.iSup_momentumSector},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.mem_supercell_iff} and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sitePermMatrix_mulVec_eq_of_mem_supercell} in
    \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/TranslationMomentum}.}
The size-independent denominators of lines 1620--1625 are what make the
cosets of this $S$ boundedly spaced, which gives the geometric statement
above. This second step is formalized for a lattice with $n$ cells along
one direction, through the cell coordinate $\pi$ along that direction and the
unit translation $e$ in it, for a graph whose edges join cells at distance at
most one: if $\chi(g)^q=1$ for every character with a nonzero sector, then
every $q$-th power lies in $S$, and some translation $(e^t)^q$ carries any site
to graph distance more than $\lfloor n/2\rfloor-q$ from any
other.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.pow_mem_supercell},
    \leanid{SimpleGraph.ringNorm_sub_le_edist},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_mem_supercell_lt_edist_add} and its ring case
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_mem_supercell_lt_edist_add_ring} in
    \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/TranslationSupercellSpacing}.}
What remains is to state the composite bound, that the low-energy commutator
is exponentially small in $n$, as one theorem.

\section{A slip}
At line 1638 the source writes the distance ``between $(\vec x_1,y_2)$ and
$(\vec x_2+\vec x',y_2)$''; the first site is $(\vec x_1,y_1)$.

\section{The case not covered}
Lines 1643--1652 mention a case the source calls ``very artificial'', in
which the denominators depend on the system size and the supercell is as
large as the system. The source states that the commutators are still
exponentially small there, but does not give the proof. This case is not
covered.

\section{The formalization}
The step is formalized for a group acting on the sites of a finite system, a
subgroup fixing every ground vector, covariant terms and an invariant
Hermitian Hamiltonian; with the setting of eq.~\texttt{blecm} (local terms on a
finite graph and a gap above $E_0$) it also gives the decay at separation
$l$.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.groundCompression_sitePermMatrix_conj},
