\section{The source lemma}

Section~7 of the September~24, 2026 polynomial-PEPS manuscript\footnote{%
\url{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Polynomial-PEPS-approximation-of-gapped-square-grid-ground-states-September-24-2026/build/main.tex},
file \texttt{06-geometry.tex}, lines 254--319.}
repaints an aligned square $S=(nr,ns)+[0,n]^2$ of side length $n>0$ in the
dyadic hierarchy from an initial party label $A$ to a final party label $B$.
Along each edge of $S$, the construction uses the normalized normal coordinate
$x=d/w(s)$, where $d$ is the signed inward normal distance to the edge, $s\in[0,n]$
is the tangential coordinate along the edge, and $w(s)=10^{-3}\min(s,n-s)$ is
the local band width. The repainting schedule performs one central birth inside
$S$, seven elementary births and deaths per edge, and one two-sheet lens
exchange per edge.

Lemma~7.2 states that there is a universal constant $a_0>0$ such that for every
birth or death in this schedule and every point $y$ in the closure of the region
whose party label changes in that step,
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{dist}\bigl(y,\overline{\{f\ne P_\circ\}}\bigr)
        &\ge a_0\min\{n,d_{\mathcal V}(y)\},
    \label{eq:openai26_dyadic_geometry_guide_properties_clearance}
\end{align}
where $f$ is the surrounding guide before the step, $P_\circ$ is the surrounding
party label, and $d_{\mathcal V}(y)$ is the $\ell_\infty$-distance from $y$ to
the finite set $\mathcal V$ of marked points of the step. For a lens exchange,
$y$ ranges over the closure of the points that do not carry the common exterior
label $C$ on both sheets before the exchange, and the obstructing set is
replaced by the boundary $\partial Y$ of the lens $Y=\{-7<x<-7/2\}$, so that
$\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial Y)\ge a_0\min\{n,d_{\mathcal V}(y)\}$.

\section{What is proved}

An initial formalization proved the clearance bound
(\ref{eq:openai26_dyadic_geometry_guide_properties_clearance}) with
$a_0=1/5000$ on $S=[0,n]^2$ by taking as hypotheses the local guide properties
that the paper's proof derives at lines 227--241:
\begin{itemize}
    \item for an edge birth or death, the guides before, after, and surrounding
    the step carry the prescribed normal words on the edge band $\{-8<x<2\}$,
    and the guides before and after agree outside that band;
    \item for the central birth, the surrounding guide equals $A$ on the open
    square $(0,n)^2$;
    \item for the lens exchange, the main guide equals $C$ on the exterior band
    $\{-8<x<0\}$, while the auxiliary guide carries the auxiliary word on
    $\{-8<x<2\}$ and equals $C$ outside it.
\end{itemize}
The pairwise disjointness of the bands $\{-8<x<2\}$ along distinct edges of $S$
was also proved.\footnote{Initial clearance and band-disjointness declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.BandOperation.clearance},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.centralBirth_clearance},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.lensExchange_clearance}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.disjoint_edgeBand}.}

The full repainting schedule of guides is now constructed in Lean, and
Lemma~7.2 is proved for the schedule on every translated dyadic square
$S=(nr,ns)+[0,n]^2$ with $a_0=1/5000$ under the sole hypothesis $n>0$. Starting
from any plane labelling that equals $A$ on the open square and equals a
constant label $C_e$ on each open exterior neighboring square across edge $e$,
the schedule defines the guide after the central birth, the guide after
completing any subset of edges, and the main and auxiliary guides during each
edge sequence.\footnote{Guide schedule declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.completedGuide},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.mainGuide}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.auxGuide}.}
Because the four edge bands are disjoint, on any edge $e$ not yet completed the
completed guide carries the starting word $C_e\mid A\mid B$ with transitions at
$x=0,1$, the lens exchange produces the main word, consecutive steps on each
sheet match, and after all four edges are completed the open square carries $B$
while the exterior of the closed square retains its initial labels. Translation
invariance of the $\ell_\infty$-distance transports the clearance bounds with
$a_0=1/5000$ to every dyadic square $(nr,ns)+[0,n]^2$.\footnote{Schedule
verification and translated block-repainting declarations:

To connect the plane labellings to the paper's chamber-sampling convention
(lines 62--80), the formalization establishes three further properties:

\paragraph{1. Boundary curves and lattice sites.}
Twice during a repainting---when initializing an edge $e$ and when passing from
the final guide of one square to the starting block guide of the next---the
schedule replaces a plane labelling by one that agrees with it outside the
dividing curves $x=0$, $x=1$, or $\partial S$. Because every lattice site is a
cell center $(i+\tfrac12,j+\tfrac12)$, its normal distance $d$ to any integer
grid line is a half-odd integer, so $1000d$ is a nonzero integer while
$1000w(s)=\min(s,n-s)$ is a half-odd integer. Consequently, no cell center lies
on $\partial S$ or on the curves $x=0$ or $x=1$, and both replacements leave
the party assignment of every lattice site unchanged.\footnote{Boundary-curve
and cell-center declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.completedGuide_eq_mainGuide_start},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_final},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsCellCenter.mem_openSquare_or_notMem_closedSquare},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsCellCenter.notMem_edgeInterface},

\paragraph{2. Displaced readings.}
In the paper, a guide is specified by its open polygonal chambers and evaluated
on chamber boundaries after a small generic displacement $v\in\mathbb R^2$. Say
that $f'$ is the displaced reading of a plane labelling $f$ along $v$ if
$f'(p)=f(p+\varepsilon v)$ for every $p\in\mathbb R^2$ and all sufficiently
small $\varepsilon>0$. Because the closures of $\{f_1'\ne f_2'\}$ and
$\{f'\ne c\}$ are contained in the closures of $\{f_1\ne f_2\}$ and $\{f\ne c\}$,
every clearance inequality of Lemma~7.2 transfers from the schedule's plane
labellings to their displaced readings along any vector $v$. Moreover, because
the band boundaries and squares are defined by piecewise affine inequalities,
every schedule guide is eventually constant along every ray $p+\varepsilon v$,
so its displaced reading exists, is unique, and preserves the disjointness of
the outer holes of temporary guides.\footnote{Displaced-reading declarations:

\paragraph{3. Chamber sampling and uniform footprint ratios.}
Every guide in the schedule is locally constant outside a locally finite union
of lines with normal vectors $(\pm t,1000)$ or $(1000,\pm t)$ for
$t\in T=\{-8,-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. A displacement $v$ is generic if it is
not parallel to any of these finitely many line directions (for instance,
$v=(1,1)$). For every generic $v$, the ray $p+\varepsilon v$ immediately enters
an open chamber (the interior of a constant-label region, so labels confined to
boundaries are never sampled, as line 77 requires), and the chamber-sampled
guide coincides with the displaced reading and satisfies Lemma~7.2 with
$a_0=1/5000$.\footnote{Chamber-sampling declarations:

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

The initial restriction to unconstructed guides satisfying assumed local band
properties is resolved. Lemma~7.2 and the chamber-sampling properties hold for
the constructed dyadic repainting schedule with $a_0=1/5000$ under $n>0$ alone,
and nothing in Lemma~7.2 remains open.
