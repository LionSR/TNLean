\section{The source statement}

Section~7 of the September 24, 2026 polynomial PEPS manuscript\footnote{%
\url{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Polynomial-PEPS-approximation-of-gapped-square-grid-ground-states-September-24-2026/build/main.tex},
file \texttt{06-geometry.tex}, lines 254--319.}
repaints an aligned square $S=(nr,ns)+[0,n]^2$ of the dyadic hierarchy from its
old label $A$ to its final label $B$. Along each edge it uses the normal ratio
$x=d/w(s)$ with $w(s)=10^{-3}\min(s,n-s)$, and it runs a central birth, seven
elementary births and deaths per edge, and one lens exchange per edge. Lemma~7.2
asserts that there is one constant $a_0>0$ such that, for every birth or death
among these operations and for every point $y$ of the closure of its changed
region,
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{dist}\bigl(y,\overline{\{f\ne P_\circ\}}\bigr)
        &\ge a_0\min\{n,d_{\mathcal V}(y)\},
    \label{eq:openai26_dyadic_geometry_guide_properties_clearance}
\end{align}
where $f$ is the surrounding guide of the schedule, $P_\circ$ the surrounding
label, and $d_{\mathcal V}$ the distance from the marked points. For a lens
exchange the point $y$ ranges instead over the closure of the positions that are
not common $C$ on the two guides before the exchange, and the obstructing set is
the boundary of the lens $Y=\{-7<x<-7/2\}$:
$\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial Y)\ge a_0\min\{n,d_{\mathcal V}(y)\}$.

\section{The earlier formal statement}

Initially the schedule of guides was not constructed. The
estimate~(\ref{eq:openai26_dyadic_geometry_guide_properties_clearance}) was
proved with $a_0=1/5000$ for every guide having the properties that the source's
proof uses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item for an edge birth or death, the guides before, after and surrounding
    the operation carry the source's normal words on the band $\{-8<x<2\}$, and
    the guides before and after agree off that band;
    \item for the central birth, the surrounding guide equals $A$ on the open
    square;
    \item for the lens exchange, the main guide equals $C$ on the exterior band
    $\{-8<x<0\}$, and the auxiliary guide carries the auxiliary word on
    $\{-8<x<2\}$ and equals $C$ off it.
\end{itemize}
The square is placed at the origin. These are the
declarations \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.BandOperation.clearance},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.centralBirth_clearance} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.lensExchange_clearance}.

The source derives the listed properties at lines 227--241: the bands
$\{-8<x<2\}$ along distinct edges are disjoint, so the operations along one edge
do not meet those along another, and the exterior part of a band stays in one
neighboring square, which carries a constant label. The disjointness is proved
as \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.disjoint_edgeBand}. The formal statements
therefore cover the source's operations once the guides are built, and no
hypothesis absent from the source's argument is used. A translate of $S$ only
translates the coordinates, and sup distances are translation invariant.

\section{Resolution}

The schedule of guides is now constructed, and Lemma~7.2 is proved for it on
every square of the hierarchy, with $a_0=1/5000$.

The starting guide of a repainting of $S=[0,n]^2$ is any labelling of the plane
that is $A$ on the open square and carries one label $C_e$ on the open exterior
neighboring square across each edge~$e$. The guide after the central birth is
$B$ on the central region. The guide after the edges of a list $E$ have been
completed replaces the band $\{-8<x<2\}$ of each completed edge by the word
$C_e\mid B$ at $0$. During the construction along an edge~$e$, the main and
auxiliary guides are the completed guide and the uniform guide $C_e$ with the
band $\{-8<x<2\}$ of $e$ replaced by the current normal word. These are the
declarations \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.completedGuide},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.mainGuide} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.auxGuide}.

The derivation of the guide properties at lines 227--241 is proved: by the
band disjointness, on the band of an edge not yet completed the completed guide
carries $C_e\mid A\mid B$ at $0,1$
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.completedGuide_eq_edgeStartWord});
the lens exchange produces the main word
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.lensExchange_mainGuide});
consecutive elementary operations on one sheet match; and after the four edges
the open square carries $B$ while every point outside the closed square keeps
its starting label. The estimates are transported to the translate
$(nr,ns)+[0,n]^2$ of the square, whose starting guide is uniform on $n$-blocks,
and stated in the source's form with closures:
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_central},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_auxStep},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_mainStep} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_exchange}. Their only
hypothesis is $n>0$; the block labels and the final label are arbitrary.

One convention remains, and it follows the source's own. A guide of the source
is specified by its open polygonal chambers, and its labels are read at the
lattice sites, the cell centers $(i+\tfrac12,j+\tfrac12)$, after one small
generic displacement (lines 62--80). A formal guide is a labelling of the whole
plane, and the estimates are proved for the labellings that the schedule
specifies at each operation; within one edge construction consecutive labellings
match exactly. Twice the schedule passes to a labelling that agrees with the one
reached only off finitely many curves. At the start of the construction along an
edge~$e$, the completed guide is replaced by the main guide with the starting
word $C_e\mid A\mid B$, which agrees with it off the curves $x=0$ and $x=1$ of
the band of $e$
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.RepaintingBaseline.completedGuide_eq_mainGuide_start}).
At the end of a repainting, the final guide is replaced by the block guide of
the next repainting, which agrees with it off the boundary of the repainted
square (\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_final}).

These replacements matter for the formal changed regions: the set where two
labellings differ is sensitive to their values on a curve, and if the labelling
reached at the start of an edge were carried through the operations unchanged,
the curve $x=1$ would enter the changed region of a main death and the estimate
would fail there. The estimates are therefore not claimed for every labelling
that agrees with the schedule's off these curves. The replacements do not matter
for the source: the labellings represent the same partition into open chambers,
and no lattice site lies on the curves. A cell center is never on the boundary
of an aligned square of integer side, and never on the curves $x=0$ or $x=1$ of
the band of one of its edges: there $d$ is a half-odd integer, so $d\ne0$ and
$1000d$ is an integer, while $1000\,w(s)=\min(s,n-s)$ is a half-odd integer
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsCellCenter.mem_openSquare_or_notMem_closedSquare},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsCellCenter.notMem_edgeInterface}). Hence the
labels at every lattice site are unchanged by both replacements
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_start_site},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_final_site}). This is the
source's sampling convention, not a hypothesis of the formal statements.

\section{Labels on the interface curves}

On an interface curve $x=t$ of a band the formal words take the label for
$x\ge t$. The source reads a guide on a boundary after one small generic
displacement $v$ into an incident chamber. The normal coordinate $d$ points into
the square, and the curves have slopes at most $8/1000$ in the edge coordinates.
So a displacement whose two components are not small relative to each other
crosses to the side $x>t$ along two edges of a square and to the side $x<t$ along
the other two, and the formal labellings and the source's sampled guides differ on
the interface curves of two edges.

The estimates do not depend on this difference. Say that a labelling $f'$ reads
$f$ after the displacement $v$ if $f'(p)=f(p+\varepsilon v)$ for every point $p$ and
all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. If $f_1'$, $f_2'$ read $f_1$, $f_2$ after the
same $v$ and $f_1=f_2$ near $p$, then $f_1'(p)=f_2'(p)$. Hence the set where
$f_1'\ne f_2'$ lies in the closure of the set where $f_1\ne f_2$, the set where
$f'\ne c$ lies in the closure of the set where $f\ne c$, and the same holds for the
positions that are not common $C$. Every estimate of Lemma~7.2 is stated for these
closures, so each passes from the formal labellings to every reading of them, for
every displacement $v$
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsDisplacedReading.closure_ne_subset},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_central_of_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_auxStep_of_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_mainStep_of_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_exchange_of_reading}).

Readings of the formal labellings exist and are unique, for every displacement.
Along a ray $p+\varepsilon v$ the coordinates, the band width and the normal
coordinates agree for small $\varepsilon>0$ with affine functions of $\varepsilon$,
whose signs are then eventually constant; the bands, the central region, the sup
squares and the blocks are finite combinations of strict inequalities and floors of
such functions, so every formal labelling, and every one homogenized on finitely many
open squares, is eventually constant along every ray
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.isRayConstant_blockSchedule},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsRayConstant.exists_isDisplacedReading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsDisplacedReading.unique}). The region of a label
in a reading lies in the closure of its region in the labelling, so a true vertex of a
reading is a true vertex of the labelling, and the outer holes of the readings of the
temporary guides are disjoint
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.homogenize_reading_outerHoles_disjoint}).

\section{The sampled guides are the readings}

The source's sampled guides are identified with these readings. The open chamber of
a label of a labelling $f$ is the interior of the region of $f$ with that label, and
the guide sampled from the chambers of $f$ after $v$ takes at $p$ the label of the
chamber containing $p+\varepsilon v$ for all small $\varepsilon>0$
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsChamberSampling}). A sampled guide is a reading.

Every guide of the schedule, the block guide, every guide of the repainting of a
block, and each of these homogenized on finitely many open squares, is constant near
every point off a set that lies, near every point, in finitely many lines whose normals
are $(\pm t,1000)$ or $(1000,\pm t)$ with $t$ in
$T=\{-8,-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. Indeed the interface curve $x=t$ of a band is
$1000\,d=t\min(s,n-s)$, a union of two segments; the edges, the grid lines and the
sides of the treated squares are lines with $t=0$; the grid lines form a locally finite
family; and a strict inequality between continuous functions, a bound on $d/w(s)$ off
$w(s)=0$, and the integer part of a coordinate are constant off the sets where the two
sides agree or the coordinate is a grid value
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.HasLineWalls},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.hasLineWalls_schedule}). So the open chambers of a
formal guide are the open sets on which it is constant, off a locally finite family of
segments. A displacement $v$ is generic if it is parallel to none of these lines, that
is, if it avoids finitely many lines through the origin; $(1,1)$ is generic, and so is
every nonzero multiple of a generic vector. A line not parallel to $v$ meets the ray
$p+\varepsilon v$ in at most one point, so for generic $v$ the ray leaves the walls at
once and lies, for all small $\varepsilon>0$, in one open chamber
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.IsLocallyInLines.eventually_notMem}). Hence for
every generic $v$ the sampled guide of every guide of the schedule exists, is unique,
and equals its reading
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.isChamberSampling_schedule}), and the estimates of
Lemma~7.2 hold for the sampled guides with $a_0=1/5000$
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_central_of_chamberSampling},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_auxStep_of_chamberSampling},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_mainStep_of_chamberSampling},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.blockRepainting_exchange_of_chamberSampling}).
The estimates need no hypothesis beyond $n>0$; the genericity of the displacement,
which the source assumes, is used only for the existence of the sampled guides.

The uniform footprint ratios of the point treatment and of the block guides also hold
for the readings, with holes at the true vertices of the readings. A reading takes only
values of the labelling, and a reading of a relabelled similar image of a template is
the same image of a reading of the template; the templates are eventually constant
along rays, so their finitely many readings serve as templates. The ratio $\nu$ then
depends on $v$, which the source fixes once all the guides are specified, before the
patch ratio is chosen
(\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_pointTreatment_footprintRatio_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_pointTreatment_footprintRatio_iUnion_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_blockGuide_footprintRatio_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_directRepainting_footprintRatio_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_resizing_footprintRatio_reading},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_resizing_footprintRatio_mixed_reading}).
Nothing of Lemma~7.2 or of its sampling convention remains open.
