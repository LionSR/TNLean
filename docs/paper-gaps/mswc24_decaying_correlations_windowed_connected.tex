\section{What the source states}
Let $A$ be an injective tensor with transfer map $\mathcal E_A$, in the gauge
$\sum_i A^{i\dagger}A^i=\mathbb 1$, $\mathcal E_A(\rho)=\rho$, $\rho>0$,
$\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1$. Let $\lambda_2$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal E_A$
of largest modulus other than $1$, with correlation length
$\xi=-1/\log\lvert\lambda_2\rvert>0$, and let $\ket{\phi_N}$ be the normalized
translation-invariant matrix product state on $N$ sites. Lemma~2 of the
Supplemental Material of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation} asserts observables
$\mathcal O,\mathcal O'$ of norm one such that for every integer $s>1$ and all
sufficiently large $N$
\begin{align}
    \bra{\phi_N}\mathcal O_1\ket{\phi_N}
     & =\bra{\phi_N}\mathcal O'_s\ket{\phi_N}=0,
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_one_point} \\
    \bra{\phi_N}\mathcal O_1\mathcal O'_s\ket{\phi_N}
     & \ge c\,e^{-(s-1)/\xi},
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_printed_bound}
\end{align}
with $c>0$ independent of $s$ and $N$.\footnote{Source traceability: label
    \path{lm:2} of the arXiv LaTeX source, section ``Proof of Theorem~1'' of
    the Supplemental Material; the two displays are labelled \path{auxform1}
    and \path{auxform2} there. No copy of this source is kept in
    \path{Papers/}.}

\section{What holds and what fails}
\paragraph{The source's observables.}
Let $\ell_2\neq0$ be a linear functional with
$\ell_2\circ\mathcal E_A=\lambda_2\ell_2$. The proof of Lemma~2 expands the
transfer powers in ordinary eigenvectors, and therefore assumes that
$\mathcal E_A$ is diagonalizable. Under that assumption it chooses, by
injectivity, observables whose one-site maps are the rank-one maps
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_{\mathcal O}(Z)
     & =\ell_2(Z)\,\rho,
     &
    \mathcal E_{\mathcal O'}(Z)
     & =\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\,R_2,
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_source_choice}
\end{align}
with $R_2$ an eigenvector for $\lambda_2$ normalized so that $\ell_2(R_2)=1$,
rescaled to norm one. If $\lambda_2$ has a nontrivial Jordan block, every
left eigenvector of that block vanishes on every right eigenvector of the
same block. The repair recorded below keeps
$\ell_2$ a left eigenvector and takes $R_2$ in the generalized eigenspace with
$\ell_2(R_2)=1$. Since $\ell_2$ is a left eigenvector for $\lambda_2$
and $\rho$ an eigenvector for $1\neq\lambda_2$, $\ell_2(\rho)=0$; since
trace preservation makes $\operatorname{Tr}$ a left eigenvector for $1$, it
vanishes on the generalized eigenspace of $\lambda_2$, so
$\operatorname{Tr}R_2=0$. Both one-point functions are therefore $0$ at every
$N\ge s$, the chain lengths on which the site $s$ exists, so
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_one_point}) is exact and algebraic, not
asymptotic. Because $\ell_2\circ\mathcal E_A^{\,s-2}=\lambda_2^{\,s-2}\ell_2$, the
two-point function is exactly
\begin{align}
    \bra{\phi_N}\mathcal O_1\mathcal O'_s\ket{\phi_N}
     & =\frac{\lambda_2^{\,s-2}}{\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_A^{\,N}},
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_exact}
\end{align}
up to the positive constant of the rescaling, and
$\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_A^{\,N}\le D^2$. Hence the modulus form
\begin{align}
    \bigl\lvert\bra{\phi_N}\mathcal O_1\mathcal O'_s\ket{\phi_N}\bigr\rvert
     & \ge c\,e^{-(s-1)/\xi}
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_modulus}
\end{align}
holds for every $s\ge2$ and every $N\ge s$.

\paragraph{The printed real inequality.}
When $\lambda_2$ is not real, the pair
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_source_choice}) cannot be Hermitian:
a Hermitian observable has a map with
$\mathcal E_{\mathcal O}(Z^\dagger)=\mathcal E_{\mathcal O}(Z)^\dagger$, whereas
$R_2^\dagger$ is a generalized eigenvector for $\bar\lambda_2\neq\lambda_2$, so
$\ell_2(R_2^\dagger)=0\neq\overline{\ell_2(R_2)}$. The correlator
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_exact}) is then complex, and the printed
inequality (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_printed_bound}) is false:
writing $\lambda_2=\lvert\lambda_2\rvert e^{i\theta}$, the real part is a
multiple of $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^{s-2}\cos((s-2)\theta)$, which is negative
for infinitely many $s$ since $\theta$ is not a multiple of $\pi$; it vanishes
at some $s$ only for particular $\theta$, such as $\theta=\pi/2$, and never
for $\theta=\pi/3$.

The printed inequality also fails when $\lambda_2$ is real and negative. Then
$\mathcal E_A$ preserves Hermitian matrices, so $R_2$ and $\ell_2$ may be
taken Hermitian and the source's rank-one maps are realized by Hermitian
observables, but the correlator (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_exact})
has the sign of $\lambda_2^{\,s-2}$ and is negative at every odd $s$. For
example, the qubit tensor with Kraus matrices $\sqrt{q_0}\,I$,
$\sqrt{q_1}\,\sigma_x$, $\sqrt{q_2}\,\sigma_y$, $\sqrt{q_3}\,\sigma_z$,
where $q_0=1/16$ and $q_1=q_2=q_3=5/16$, is injective, and every nontrivial
eigenvalue of its transfer map equals $-1/4$. Non-Hermiticity of the source's
pair is therefore forced only for nonreal $\lambda_2$, while the printed
inequality fails for every nonreal and every negative $\lambda_2$.

\paragraph{The Hermitian reading.}
Reading $\mathcal O,\mathcal O'$ as Hermitian does not rescue the printed
form. Put $P(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\,\rho$ and $t=s-L-1$, where the
observables act on $L$ sites ($L=1$ in the source). For Hermitian observables
the limit of the connected correlator as $N\to\infty$ is
\begin{align}
    G_\infty(\mathcal O,\mathcal O';s)
     & =\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl(\mathcal E_{\mathcal O}
    \bigl((\mathcal E_A-P)^{t}\bigl(\mathcal E_{\mathcal O'}(\rho)\bigr)\bigr)\Bigr),
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_limit}
\end{align}
which is real. When $\lambda_2$ and $\bar\lambda_2$ are the only eigenvalues
of modulus $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert$ and both are semisimple, its leading part
is
\begin{align}
    \lvert\lambda_2\rvert^{t}\bigl(a\,e^{it\theta}+\bar a\,e^{-it\theta}\bigr)
     & =2\lvert a\rvert\,\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^{t}\cos(t\theta+\varphi),
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_cosine}
\end{align}
with $a=\lvert a\rvert e^{i\varphi}$, and the remainder is smaller by a factor
tending to zero in $t$. When $\theta/\pi$ is irrational the cosine comes
arbitrarily close to zero for infinitely many $t$, so for every Hermitian pair
and every $c>0$ the correlator falls below $c\,e^{-(s-1)/\xi}$ for infinitely
many $s$. An explicit injective example has $d=4$, $D=2$, and a gate
angle $\psi$,
\begin{align}
    A^0
     & =\tfrac12\one,
     &
    A^1
     & =\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\psi}),
     &
    A^2
     & =\tfrac12\sigma_x,
     &
    A^3
     & =\tfrac12\sigma_y,
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_example}
\end{align}
with $\sum_iA^{i\dagger}A^i=\one$ and transfer spectrum
$\{1,(1+e^{i\psi})/4,(1+e^{-i\psi})/4,0\}$. The phase $\theta=\arg(\lambda_2)$
of (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_cosine}) is $\psi/2$. At
$\psi=\pi/2$ one has $\theta=\pi/4$, so $t\theta$ is an odd multiple of
$\pi/2$ at $t=2,6,10,\dots$, and the
Hermitian parts of the source's observables
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_source_choice}) have one-point functions
$0$ and a correlator that vanishes exactly at $s=4,8,12,\dots$ for every $N\ge s$
and is negative at $s=5,6,7$.

\paragraph{The rest of the proof.}
The source's Theorem~1 is true, and its proof goes through with the modulus
form (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_modulus}) for the source's
pair (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_source_choice}): the lower bound
on the trace distance comes from the duality
$\lvert\operatorname{Tr}((\rho_0-\sigma_1)\mathcal Q)\rvert
    \le\lVert\rho_0-\sigma_1\rVert_1\lVert\mathcal Q\rVert$, which needs no
Hermiticity, and the minimization that follows uses only moduli. Four further
points need repair, none of which changes the conclusion.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Lemma~2 is stated for injective $A$ but applied in Theorem~1 to a
          normal $A$ on unblocked sites. For normal $A$ the map
          $\mathcal Q\mapsto\mathcal E_{\mathcal Q}$ is onto only for
          observables on $L$ sites, where products of $L$ matrices span the
          matrix algebra.
    \item The proof expands
          $\mathcal E_A^{\,s-1}=\sum_i\lambda_i^{s-1}\ket{R_i}\bra{L_i}$ and
          so assumes $\mathcal E_A$ diagonalizable. If $\lambda_2$ has a
          nontrivial Jordan block, a left eigenvector and a right eigenvector
          of that block pair to zero.
          The repair takes $\ell_2$ a left eigenvector and $R_2$ in the
          generalized eigenspace with $\ell_2(R_2)=1$; then
          $\ell_2(\mathcal E_A^{\,t}R_2)=\lambda_2^{\,t}$ and still
          $\operatorname{Tr}R_2=0$.
    \item The trace-distance bound (display \path{auxbound}) omits a factor
          $\tfrac12$: the duality above gives
          $d(\rho_0,\sigma_1)\ge\tfrac12\lvert\widetilde a_{\mathcal Q}
              -b_{\mathcal Q}\rvert$.
    \item The choice $s=2T+1$ places the observables at distance exactly
          $2T$, whereas the source's factorization statement covers distances
          larger than $2T$; the choice is valid only for a circuit with a
          sharp light cone. The formal statements require distance larger
          than $2T$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The corrected statement}
Let $A$ be normal, with products of $L\ge1$ matrices spanning the matrix
algebra, in the same gauge, and put
\begin{align}
    G_N(X,Y;s)
     & =\bra{\phi_N}X_1Y_s\ket{\phi_N}
    -\bra{\phi_N}X_1\ket{\phi_N}\bra{\phi_N}Y_s\ket{\phi_N}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_connected}
\end{align}
There are Hermitian $\mathcal O,\mathcal O'$ of norm one, $c>0$,
$K\in\{1,2\}$ with $K=1$ when $\lambda_2$ is real, and $s_0\ge L+2$ such that
every $s\ge s_0$ has some $s'\in\{s,\dots,s+K-1\}$ with
\begin{align}
    \bigl\lvert G_N(\mathcal O,\mathcal O';s')\bigr\rvert
     & \ge c\,e^{-(s'-1)/\xi}
     &
     & \text{for all } N\ge3s'.
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_corrected}
\end{align}
The window of $K$ consecutive separations removes the zeros of
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_cosine}): the Vandermonde matrix of the
distinct unimodular numbers $e^{\pm i\theta}$ is invertible, so of any two
consecutive values of $a\,e^{it\theta}+\bar a\,e^{-it\theta}$ one has modulus
at least a fixed $c_0>0$. The statement uses Hermitian observables because
the proof of Theorem~1 below controls variances of Hermitian operators; for
such observables the window is necessary, by the example
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_example}). The connected correlator
makes the bound independent of the one-point functions; it does not correct
the source, whose choice
(\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_source_choice}) makes them vanish
exactly. For injective $A$ and $L=1$ the hypotheses are those of the source.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_decayingCorrelations} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/DecayingCorrelationBound}.}

\section{Verdict}
The printed Lemma~2 is false as printed: the qubit tensor above is injective
and nondegenerate, satisfies the source's hypotheses, and violates the printed
inequality at every odd $s$. More generally the inequality fails as a real
inequality when $\lambda_2$ is not real or is negative, and fails for Hermitian
observables in general. The repair is nevertheless local: the modulus form (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_modulus}) holds
for the source's own observables, and the source's Theorem~1 is true. The
formal statement (\ref{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_corrected}) is the
Hermitian variant; its proof is the source's spectral argument together with
the Vandermonde bound, and it suffices for Theorem~1: that proof needs one
separation $s'$ of order $T_N+L$ for each $N$, and moving $s'$ by at most
$K-1\le1$ sites preserves $s'=o(\log N)$ and $N\ge3s'$.

The proof of Theorem~1 in the blueprint replaces the source's product-fidelity
step by the concentration of averages of translated observables. Tracking its
constants, a state prepared in depth $T$ with overlap at least $\eta$ with
$\phi_N$ requires
\begin{align}
    T
     & \ge\frac\xi4\bigl(\ln N-\ln\ln N\bigr)-\xi\ln\frac1\eta-C_A,
    \label{eq:mswc24_decaying_correlations_depth_constant}
\end{align}
with $C_A$ depending only on $A$. The same constant $\xi/4$ is implicit in the
source's final inequality, which compares $N/\ln N$ with a multiple of
$e^{4T/\xi}$; only its explicit statement is new.
