\section{The source argument}

Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete build their multi-block proof of
the Fundamental Theorem on a canonical form
\cite[Section~II]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. Let
$A=(A^i)_{i=1}^d$ be a tensor with physical dimension $d$. In the source
canonical form, a change of basis on the bond space gives
\begin{align}
    A^i
        &= \bigoplus_{k=1}^r \mu_k A_k^i,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_canonical_form}
\end{align}
where each block tensor $A_k$ is normal and each scalar $\mu_k$ is nonzero.
This is Equation~\path{eq:II_CF1} of
Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. Immediately after that equation, the source
imposes the convention
\begin{quote}
    ``we can always choose $|\mu_k|\leq 1$ and at least one of them equals one,
    something which we will assume from now on.''
    \cite[paragraph after \texttt{eq:II\_CF1}]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}
\end{quote}
This normalization is part of the definition used throughout the paper; it is
not a later consequence. Under the strict modulus ordering of the
basis-of-normal-tensors arrangement, the dominant block therefore satisfies
$|\mu_1|=1$, while every sub-dominant block satisfies $|\mu_k|<1$.

The same paper proves the proportional Fundamental Theorem and its equal-MPV
corollary \cite[Section~II]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. Consider tensors $A$ and $B$
written in basis-of-normal-tensors form. Let
$(A_j)_{j=1}^g$ and $(B_j)_{j=1}^g$ be their representative families, and let
$\mu_{j,q_a}$ and $\nu_{j,q_b}$ be the corresponding coefficients, where
$q_a=1,\ldots,r_{a,j}$ and $q_b=1,\ldots,r_{b,j}$. The proportional
Fundamental Theorem identifies the representatives by
\begin{align}
    B_j
        &= e^{i\phi_j}Y_j A_j Y_j^{-1},
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_representative_gauge}
\end{align}
for phases $\phi_j$ and invertible matrices $Y_j$. The trace-pair argument in
the proof of the equal-MPV corollary then gives the scalar identity
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{r_{a,j}}\mu_{j,q}^N
        &= \sum_{q=1}^{r_{b,j}}
            (\nu_{j,q}e^{i\phi_j})^N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_power_sum}
\end{align}
This is the unlabeled identity used in the source proof
\cite[Section~II]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The elementary power-sum lemma cited
there implies
\begin{align}
    r_{a,j}
        &= r_{b,j}
        =: r_j,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_equal_multiplicities}\\
    \{\mu_{j,q}:1\leq q\leq r_j\}
        &= \{\nu_{j,q}e^{i\phi_j}:1\leq q\leq r_j\},
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_weight_multiset}
\end{align}
where the second equality is an equality of multisets. The matching bond
dimensions and the global gauge formula follow.

The source extracts the coefficient data from the power sums in
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_power_sum}.
It uses a finite family of values of $N$ as input to a separation lemma for
sums of $N$th powers. It does not invoke convergence of any quantity indexed
by $N$.

\section{The formal canonical form}

The formal counterpart of the canonical form used by the
basis-of-normal-tensors route is
\leanid{IsNormalCanonicalFormBNT}.\footnote{%
    \path{TNLean/MPS/BNT/Construction.lean}, lines 181--189.}
Its mathematical data consist of a weight family
$\mu:\mathrm{Fin}\,r\to\C$ with strict modulus ordering, nonvanishing
$\mu_k\neq 0$ for every $k$, and the blockwise normality inherited from the
underlying canonical form. In the version examined here, the modulus of the
dominant weight was unconstrained.

The source definition additionally requires
\begin{align}
    |\mu_1|
        &= 1,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_missing_dominant_normalization}
\end{align}
where the source uses index $1$ and the formal development uses index $0$.
The blueprint chapter on basis-of-normal-tensors form records the strict
modulus ordering in its separated canonical-form definitions. The
canonical-form chapter separately records a phase--modulus decomposition
\begin{align}
    \mu_k
        &= \eta_k|\mu_k|,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_phase_modulus_decomposition}
\end{align}
with $\eta_k$ on the unit circle. Neither location originally imposed
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_missing_dominant_normalization}.\footnote{%
    The basis-of-normal-tensors definitions are
    \texttt{def:cf\_bnt} and \texttt{def:normal\_cf\_bnt} in
    \path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch10_bnt.tex}, lines 70--100 and 119--141,
    respectively. The phase--modulus decomposition is recorded in
    \path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch09_canonical.tex}, lines 125--139.}

This omission affects downstream arguments. The source normalization places
the dominant block on the unit circle, as required by the spectral and
irreducibility arguments, and places every sub-dominant block strictly inside
the unit disk. The latter property is central to the comparison with the
discarded formal limit hypothesis.

\section{The discarded formal proportional comparison}

The discarded formal route to the proportional Fundamental Theorem replaced
the source power-sum identity in
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_power_sum}
with coefficient sequences indexed by chain length. For every block index
$j$, it introduced a sequence $N\mapsto a_{N,j}\in\C$ and a designated limit
$a_j^\infty\in\C$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    a_{N,j}
        &\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} a_j^\infty,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_formal_coefficient_limit}\\
    a_j^\infty
        &\neq 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_formal_nonzero_limit}
\end{align}
It imposed analogous data $b_{N,k}\to b_k^\infty\neq 0$ on the $B$-side. It
also introduced proportionality scalars $c_N$ with a nonzero limit $c$:
\begin{align}
    c_N
        &\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} c,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_scalar_limit}\\
    c
        &\neq 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_nonzero_scalar_limit}
\end{align}
This route identified the scalars by comparing limits rather than by applying
the source power-sum lemma.

Consider the natural specialization in which the $j$th source sector contains
one selected representative, equivalently one copy in the coefficient
multiplicity notation, so that $r_j=1$. If $\mu_j^A$ denotes the corresponding
source weight, the formal coefficient array was chosen as
\begin{align}
    a_{N,j}
        &= (\mu_j^A)^N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_natural_coefficient_array}
\end{align}
The associated blueprint passage is the remark
\texttt{rem:cf\_coeff\_arrays} following
\texttt{lem:ft\_proportional} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch11_fundamental_theorem_proof.tex},
lines 172--202. Under
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_natural_coefficient_array},
the requirements in
Eqs.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_formal_coefficient_limit}
and
\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_formal_nonzero_limit}
become conditions on the powers $(\mu_j^A)^N$.

\section{The design history of the limit hypothesis}

The convergence-to-nonzero-limit hypothesis does not appear in
Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. It also does not appear in the formalization's
initial proof plan. A memo committed on 2026-02-22 describes the intended proof
of the proportional Fundamental Theorem and its equal-MPV corollary from that
source.\footnote{%
    \path{docs/audits/2026-02-22_thm4_4_proof_plan_ref1606.txt}, committed in
    \texttt{64c018ab} earlier the same day as the introducing implementation
    \texttt{dd54b79e}.}
The memo proposes a span-equality bridge and a finite-length scalar power-sum
identity for matching the $\mu$-coefficients.

Using its notation, the memo records the scalar identity
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^{x_a}\lambda_{a,k}^N
        &= \sum_{k=1}^{x_b}\lambda_{b,k}^N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_memo_scalar_power_sum}
\end{align}
It proposes formalizing
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_memo_scalar_power_sum}
as a fixed-length theorem requiring only finitely many values of $N$. Its
planned basis-of-normal-tensors application is
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{r_{a,j}}\mu_{j,q}^N
        &= \sum_{q=1}^{r_{b,j}}
            (\nu_{j,q}e^{i\phi_j})^N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_memo_bnt_power_sum}
\end{align}
The proposed scalar theorem would then give
Eqs.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_equal_multiplicities}
and
\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_weight_multiset}.
The memo contains neither a convergence statement nor a nonzero-limit
hypothesis.

The memo also identifies an apparent circularity in the span-equality route.
Let $V^{(N)}(A_j)$ denote the length-$N$ periodic matrix-product vector
generated by $A_j$, and define $V^{(N)}(B_k)$ analogously. Deducing span
equality from proportionality appears to require linear independence at a
witness length, while the surrounding basis-of-normal-tensors permutation step
uses span equality to obtain the required linear independence. The memo
proposes replacing global span equality with eventual span equality:
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{span}\{V^{(N)}(A_j)\}_j
        &= \operatorname{span}\{V^{(N)}(B_k)\}_k
        \qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_eventual_span_equality}
\end{align}
The surrounding argument already uses only this eventual form, so the proposed
weakening resolves the circularity at the type level.

The implementation committed later that day followed neither proposal. It did
not formalize the fixed-length scalar identity planned for the
$\mu$-coefficient comparison, and it did not adopt
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_eventual_span_equality}
as the bridge. Instead, it added global convergence and nonzero-limit
hypotheses to the basis-of-normal-tensors bridge theorems and later bundled
them into one formal data structure. These fields encode neither the source
power-sum identity nor the memo's eventual span equality.

The project record gives no contemporaneous mathematical reason for this third
route. A commit on 2026-05-09 describes the resulting structure as
``MPV-level Newton--Girard convergence data, not a \S II 283--302
Wedderburn-style similarity.'' This statement describes the final structure
but does not explain the departure from the memo.\footnote{%
    The introducing commit is \texttt{dd54b79e}, ``Add ScalarPowerSumIdentity,
    BNTConstruction, BNTPermutationThm44.'' Despite the name, the
    \leanid{ScalarPowerSumIdentity} module added by that commit does not
    implement the fixed-length scalar identity planned from the elementary
    power-sum lemma in Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The structure refactor
    that bundled the limit fields is \texttt{9e096a14} (2026-03-22), and the
    later Newton--Girard description appears in the message of
    \texttt{8ab1a5db} (2026-05-09).}

The design history therefore shows that the limit hypothesis follows neither
the cited source nor the formalization's recorded proof plan.

\section{The mathematical incompatibility}

This section concerns only the strict-weight,
one-representative-per-block specialization
\begin{align}
    a_{N,j}
        &= (\mu_j^A)^N,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_specialized_a_array}\\
    b_{N,k}
        &= (\mu_k^B)^N.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_specialized_b_array}
\end{align}
It does not concern the more general sector coefficient arrays
\begin{align}
    c_{S,j}^{(N)}
        &= \sum_q \mu_{j,q}^N,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_sector_coefficient_array}
\end{align}
which retain repeated copies within a basis-of-normal-tensors sector.

The source normalization gives
\begin{align}
    |\mu_1^A|
        &= 1,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_dominant_modulus}\\
    |\mu_j^A|
        &< 1
        \qquad (j\geq 2).
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_source_subdominant_modulus}
\end{align}
For every sub-dominant block,
\begin{align}
    |(\mu_j^A)^N|
        &= |\mu_j^A|^N,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_power_modulus}\\
    |\mu_j^A|^N
        &\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_subdominant_decay}
\end{align}
Consequently, the only possible limit of $a_{N,j}$ is
\begin{align}
    a_j^\infty
        &= 0
        \qquad (j\geq 2),
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_subdominant_zero_limit}
\end{align}
which contradicts
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_formal_nonzero_limit}.

For the dominant block, the modulus of $(\mu_1^A)^N$ remains one. The sequence
converges only when $\mu_1^A=1$. A nontrivial root of unity produces a periodic,
nonconvergent sequence, while a generic phase also has no limit.

The mismatch is structural. Under the source normalization, a multi-block
family with $r>1$ cannot instantiate the discarded data using
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_specialized_a_array},
because every sub-dominant block violates the nonzero-limit requirement.
Without the source normalization, the formal limit hypothesis may be
satisfiable, but the resulting object no longer carries the canonical-form
data required by Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}.

The source proof avoids this obstruction because it never takes the
$N\to\infty$ limit of $(\mu_j^A)^N$. It recovers the multiset of weights from a
finite family of polynomial identities in $N$. In the strict,
one-representative specialization, once basis-of-normal-tensors linear
independence provides a witness length $N_0$, one comparison at $N_0$ determines
the coefficients up to the expected phase or root-of-unity ambiguity. In the
general case with multiplicities, the source separation lemma and the
2026-02-22 memo use the finite range
$N=1,\ldots,\max(r_{a,j},r_{b,j})$.

\section{Cross-check with the blueprint}

The blueprint entries that formerly paralleled the convergence and
nonzero-limit interface have been demoted or removed from the source-labeled
theorem route. They must not be identified as formalizations of the
proportional Fundamental Theorem or its equal-MPV corollary in
Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The active blueprint instead records the
source comparison as an open block-matching step whose proof must use the
basis-of-normal-tensors power-sum argument rather than external coefficient
limits.

The blueprint and the former formal definition agree on one point: both record
strict modulus ordering but originally omitted
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_missing_dominant_normalization}.

\section{What a corrected formalization would say}

A source-faithful formalization requires two changes.

First, the canonical-form data must include
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_missing_dominant_normalization}.
Strict modulus ordering then gives
\begin{align}
    |\mu_k|
        &\leq 1
        \qquad (1\leq k\leq r),
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_all_weight_bound}
\end{align}
with strict inequality for every sub-dominant block.\footnote{%
    The formal field carrying the strict ordering is
    \leanid{mu_strict_anti} in
    \leanid{IsNormalCanonicalFormBNT}; see
    \path{TNLean/MPS/BNT/Construction.lean}, lines 184--185.}
The dominant normalization belongs in
\leanid{IsNormalCanonicalFormBNT} and in the corresponding blueprint
definition. It transcribes the source convention rather than adding a new
assumption.

Second, the proportional comparison must use finite power-sum data. In the
general coefficient-multiplicity setting, its blockwise hypothesis should be
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{r_{a,j}}\mu_{j,q}^N
        &= \sum_{q=1}^{r_{b,j}}
            (\nu_{j,q}e^{i\phi_j})^N,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_corrected_finite_power_sum}\\
    1
        &\leq N
        \leq \max(r_{a,j},r_{b,j}).
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_finite_power_sum_range}
\end{align}
An equivalent formulation may use the finite set of witness lengths produced
after the span and linear-independence inputs become available. The source
separation lemma then yields
Eqs.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_equal_multiplicities}
and
\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_weight_multiset}.
This is the route proposed by the 2026-02-22 memo for the general
multi-multiplicity case; it is not a single-witness argument in that
generality.

In the strict-weight, one-representative specialization, the finite comparison
reduces to one witness length $N_0$. Basis-of-normal-tensors linear independence
at that length is supplied by the direct-sum span theorem on a basis of normal
tensors. If $c$ is the proportionality scalar, the blockwise relation is
\begin{align}
    (\mu_j^A)^{N_0}
        &= c^{N_0}(\mu_j^B)^{N_0},
        \qquad j=1,\ldots,g.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_single_witness_weights}
\end{align}
This identity determines $\mu_j^A$ from $\mu_j^B$ and $c$ up to an $N_0$th
root of unity. That ambiguity is absorbed by the phase $\phi_j$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_representative_gauge}.
No convergence statement or nonzero limit for $(\mu_j^A)^N$ is required.

The corrected route is therefore finite and source-faithful. The
multi-multiplicity case uses the finite power-sum identities from the proof of
the equal-MPV corollary
\cite[Section~II]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}; the single-witness argument is only its
strict-weight specialization. Once the direct-sum span theorem supplies
linear independence, this route also avoids the circularity that the memo
identified in the span-equality bridge.

\section{Conclusion}

The canonical-form definition and the discarded proportional MPV comparison
differed from Ref.~\cite{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv} in two coupled respects. The
canonical-form definition omitted the convention $|\mu_1|=1$. The proportional
comparison replaced the source's finite power-sum identities with a convergence
hypothesis on $(\mu_j^A)^N$. Under the source normalization, that convergence
hypothesis fails on every sub-dominant block because its only possible limit is
zero.

The proof-plan memo of 2026-02-22 already proposed a source-faithful finite
power-sum route. The implementation instead introduced a
convergence-to-nonzero-limit hypothesis that appears in neither the source nor
the memo.

\paragraph{Status: supplier-side normalization.}

The supplier now realizes the weight normalization used at line~246. The
declaration
\leanid{MPSTensor.exists_isBNTCanonicalForm_afterBlocking_pos_normalized}
rescales the prepared weights by their maximal modulus
\begin{align}
    m
        &= \max_k |\mu_k|,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_supplier_maximal_modulus}
\end{align}
using \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_weight_normalization}. The rescaled weights
satisfy
\begin{align}
    |\mu_k|
        &\leq 1
        \qquad\text{for every }k,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_supplier_weight_bound}\\
    |\mu_{k_0}|
        &= 1
        \qquad\text{for some }k_0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_supplier_unit_witness}
\end{align}
The construction records the rescaling cost as the per-site scalar $m^N$ in
the positive-length matrix-product identity. This positive-length identity is
the canonical source formulation; it contains no length-zero coefficient.

The normalized supplier assumes exactly that the positive-length
matrix-product family is nonzero. Thus there exist $N\geq 1$ and a word $w$ of
length $N$ such that
\begin{align}
    \tr(A^w)
        &\neq 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_nonzero_positive_length_trace}
\end{align}
This condition persists under every blocking. For each $p\geq 1$, some
$q\geq 1$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    \tr((A^w)^{pq})
        &\neq 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_nonzero_blocked_trace}
\end{align}
Otherwise, the Newton--Girard trace identities would imply that $(A^w)^p$,
and hence $A^w$, is nilpotent, contrary to
Eq.~\ref{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_nonzero_positive_length_trace}.
Repeating $w$ to length $pqN$ therefore gives a nonzero coefficient after
blocking by $p$.

This hypothesis excludes precisely the zero positive-length family, for which
the unit-modulus weight required at line~246 is impossible. The subsequent
proposition says ``for any tensor'', but the standing convention at line~246
already presupposes a nonempty weight family. The nonzero family is therefore
the source's intended scope, and the supplier hypothesis makes that convention
explicit. The zero family is a degenerate reading and receives no separate
clause.

\paragraph{Status: structural realignment.}

The structural realignment is complete. The canonical-form definition now
includes the source convention $|\mu_1|=1$ as a field of
\leanid{IsNormalCanonicalFormBNT}, and every constructor propagates it. The
convergence-to-nonzero-limit fields and the later per-$N$ coefficient package
have been removed from the formal development. Until a source-faithful residual
comparison theorem is available, the surviving boundary retains the
block-permutation and gauge-phase comparison as an explicit input.

The earlier one-shot nonzero-overlap theorems and their matching wrappers have
also been deleted. They claimed nondecay for every block from arbitrary bounded
coefficient arrays, but their hypotheses did not support that conclusion: a
non-dominant coefficient may vanish identically unless the source residual
peeling argument or the power-sum sector structure has already been encoded.
The remaining \Lean{} interfaces therefore retain the proportional matching
conclusion as an explicit input until the residual comparison theorem is
proved.

The remaining task is the proof. The paper-faithful route first applies the
block-selection step of the proportional Fundamental Theorem
\cite[line~1182]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The equal-MPV corollary then uses the
finite power-sum comparison and global gauge assembly
\cite[lines~1184--1192]{Cirac2016MPDO_arXiv}. The proof will consume the
per-$N$ hypotheses and the missing residual comparison data. For the currently
dominant surviving sector, the lower-bound argument uses
\begin{align}
    \lVert b_{N,1}\rVert
        &= 1,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_dominant_coefficient_norm}\\
    \lVert b_{N,k}\rVert
        &\leq 1,
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_bounded_coefficient_norm}\\
    c_N
        &\neq 0.
    \label{eq:cpsv16_cf_normalization_and_proportional_comparison_nonzero_per_length_scalar}
\end{align}
The weight comparison then uses one witness length in the strict-weight
specialization or finitely many witness lengths in the multi-multiplicity case.
