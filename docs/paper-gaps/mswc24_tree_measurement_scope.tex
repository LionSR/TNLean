\section{What the source states}
The paragraph ``Tree-RG circuit with measurements''
of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation} observes that the isometries of the tree circuit,
eq.~(16), ``act on a constant number of sites which, although spatially
separated, can be teleported at neighboring registers with a constant
overhead'', by ``creating nearest-neighbor entangled pairs, then performing
simultaneous measurements, and correcting (without postselection) based on the
measurement outcomes''. Hence ``every isometry in eq.~(16) takes constant time
using measurement'', the tree circuit has $O(\log\log(N/\varepsilon))$ layers,
and, with the fixed-point state prepared in constant depth, a short-range
correlated matrix product state is prepared with measurements in depth
$O(\log\log(N/\varepsilon))$. The source fixes no model of circuits with
measurements.

\section{What is formalized}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The model.} A measurement round is a local circuit, a
          measurement of some sites in the computational basis, and a product of
          single-site unitaries chosen from the outcomes; a preparation is a
          sequence of rounds, the depth being the total number of layers of
          their circuits. Every round is a protocol of
          $\mathrm{QCcc}_\ell$ of~\cite{Piroli2021LOCC} of no larger depth;
          that paper performs a single round and remarks that ``one could also
          define a more general scheme with multiple rounds of LOCC''. A
          sequence of rounds is a composition of such transformations. The
          number of rounds is not bounded: the tree with $k$ levels uses $2k$
          rounds, whereas in the class $\mathrm{QCcc}^{(k)}_\ell$ of the
          paragraph ``Phases of matter'' of~\cite{Piroli2021LOCC} the number
          $k$ of composed transformations does not depend on the system
          size. Several rounds are needed because a
          teleported register must be corrected before the next isometry acts on
          it.
    \item \emph{Teleportation.} For qudits of any dimension $d$, any number of
          registers is moved along chains of nearest-neighbour hops of any
          length, in one round of depth $2$; for every outcome the round acts
          as $d^{-H}$ times the permutation of sites, $H$ the number of hops.
    \item \emph{One layer.} Two-site gates on pairwise disjoint stretches
          $a,\dots,a+2L+1$, with the sites strictly inside each stretch in
          $\ket0$, are applied in two rounds of total depth $5$, whatever $L$.
          A gate joins the sites $a$ and $a+2L+1$, at odd separation along the
          ring; gates at even separation, or on overlapping stretches, are not
          covered, and the tree needs neither.
    \item \emph{The tree.} A binary tree of two-site gates with $k$ levels, on the
          complete blocks of $2^k$ sites and on the complete smaller blocks
          after them, is applied with measurements in depth $5k$, on the vectors
          with $\ket0$ strictly inside the complete blocks of every level.
    \item \emph{Registers of several sites.} A register is $s$ consecutive
          sites. A unitary on two registers $a,\dots,a+s-1$ and
          $a+2L+s,\dots,a+2L+2s-1$ is applied by teleporting the first register
          site by site, the site $a+s-1$ first, in $s$ rounds of depth $2$,
          applying the unitary to $2s$ consecutive sites by a local circuit of
          depth $K$, and teleporting back; a layer of such unitaries on disjoint
          stretches takes depth $4s+K+2$, whatever $L$. Trees of such unitaries
          on the $M$ blocks of $q=s2^{k+1}$ sites take depth $(k+1)(4s+K+2)$.
    \item \emph{The approximating state.} If the blocked tensor of $A$ over $s$
          sites is injective, a register carries $\mathbb{C}^{D^2}$, every layer
          of the tree factorization of the blocked tensor over $s$ sites is an
          isometry, and the tree of unitaries extending these isometries
          implements the isometry $V$ of the polar decomposition of $A$ blocked
          over $q$ sites. After the pairs of the fixed point, prepared by a local
          circuit, the protocol outputs the approximating state of eq.~(10) with
          blocks of $q$ sites, in depth at most $C(k+1)$ with $C$ depending on $d$
          and $s$.
    \item \emph{The depth.} For a normal tensor, $s$ is a length at which the
          blocked tensor in the gauge of eq.~(5) is injective, and with
          Lemma~1'(i) at slope $a=\xi$ the error is at most $\varepsilon$ once
          $s2^{k+1}\ge a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b$. If moreover
          $s2^{k+1}\le2(a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b)$, then
          $k\le\log_2(a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b)$ and the depth is
          $O(\log\log(N/\varepsilon))$.
\end{enumerate}
The blocking over $s$ sites makes the two-site injectivity assumed for the
multiscale network of~\cite{gap:mswc24_tree_mera_scope} unnecessary here: the
tree is that of the blocked tensor, whose sites are the registers.

\section{The restriction formerly adopted: the chain length}
The block length $q=s2^{k+1}$ divided the chain length $N$, so that the $N/q$
blocks of eq.~(10) all had $q$ sites and every tree was complete. The depth
bound held for the chain lengths $N$ divisible by $s2^{k+1}$ for some $k$ with
$a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b\le s2^{k+1}\le2(a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b)$, for instance
$N=Ms2^K$ with $K$ large, and not for every chain length; since the block length
$s2^{k+1}$ is even, no odd $N$ was covered. The source states the depth for every
$N$.

\section{Elimination plan}
The depth bound for every chain length of the circuit without measurements
uses unequal blocks: the last block has length $q'$ with $q\le q'<2q$
\cite{gap:mswc24_depth_upper_bound_divisible_length}. A complete binary tree of
registers covers $s2^{k+1}$ sites, so the last block needs a tree whose leaves
have unequal lengths; each node is still a unitary on a bounded number of sites,
so the depth stays $O(k)$.

\section{Resolution}
The restriction on the chain length is removed by trees on leaves of unequal
widths.\footnote{Formal declarations:

    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/UnequalTreePreparation}, and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedWithMeasurementRoundsInDepth_le_log_log_of_mpvState_ne_zero}
    and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedWithMeasurementRoundsInDepth_le_log_log}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/LogLogDepthEveryLength}.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Halves of unequal lengths.} For polar decompositions
          $\mathcal B_{n_i}=V_{n_i}P_{n_i}$ of $A$ blocked over $n_1$ and over
          $n_2$ sites, $V_{n_1+n_2}=(V_{n_1}\otimes V_{n_2})W$, where $W$ is the
          partial isometry of the polar decomposition of the tensor
          $(a,b)\mapsto P_{n_1}^aP_{n_2}^b$ of two neighbouring sites. No
          injectivity is needed: the initial projector of
          $V_{n_1}\otimes V_{n_2}$ fixes that tensor, so
          $V_{n_1}\otimes V_{n_2}$ composes with its partial isometry. The tree
          below uses injectivity only to make $W$ and the leaf maps isometries.
    \item \emph{The tree of a block.} A block of $\ell\ge2^{h+2}s$ sites is cut
          into $2^{h+1}$ leaves of even widths, as equal as possible, at least
          $2s$ each; when $\ell$ is odd the last leaf has one more site. The node
          $p$ of depth $j\le h$ covers $2^{h+1-j}$ consecutive leaves, its
          registers are its first $s$ sites and the last $s$ sites of its last
          leaf, not counting the extra site, and its unitary extends the isometry
          $W$ joining its two halves. The leaves carry unitaries extending
          $V_{\ell'}$ on their $\ell'$ sites. The widths are even, so the two
          registers of every node are an even number of sites apart, as the
          register gates require. The tree implements $V_\ell$ on the input of
          the block.
    \item \emph{The legs of the pairs.} The register length is $s=L+2$, with $L$
          the injectivity length of the tensor in the gauge of eq.~(5), so that
          $D\le d^{s-1}$ and a leg of a pair is written on the first $s-1$ sites
          of a register, the last site in $\ket0$. For odd $\ell$ the right leg
          of the pair of a block then ends on the extra site, which carries
          $\ket0$, and the input of the block lies on the two registers of the
          root.
    \item \emph{The depth.} The depths $0,\dots,h$ of the trees of all the blocks
          are applied one after the other with measurements, and the leaves by
          one local circuit; for blocks of lengths between $2^{h+2}s$ and
          $2^{h+1}c$ the depth is at most $C(h+1)$, with $C$ depending on $d$, $s$
          and $c$.
    \item \emph{Every chain length.} With $q=\lceil a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b\rceil$
          and $q\le N$, the chain is cut into $M-1$ blocks of $q$ sites and one of
          $q'=q+(N\bmod q)<2q$ sites, Lemma~1'(i) for unequal blocks bounds the
          error by $\varepsilon$, and $2^{h+2}s\le q<2^{h+3}s$ gives the depth
          $C(h+1)\le c\log(\log(N/\varepsilon)+1)$. A chain with $N<q$ is one
          block, and $\ket{\phi_N}$ itself is the state of that block with the
          pair $\omega\propto P\ket{\mathbb 1}$; below $4s$ sites it is prepared
          in bounded depth.
\end{enumerate}
The main theorem holds for every $N\ge2$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1$ with
$\ket{\phi_N(A)}\neq0$, the presupposition recorded in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/mswc24_depth_upper_bound_nonzero_state.tex}, and for every
$N\ge N_0$.
The bound $c\log(\log(N/\varepsilon)+1)$ is $O(\log\log(N/\varepsilon))$; the
shift by $1$ keeps it positive for $N/\varepsilon\ge2$.

\section{Supported polar trees}
For a tensor injective on a common set $S$ of virtual pairs after a fixed
length, choose a coordinate inclusion $J\colon\mathbb C^\chi\to\mathbb C^{D^2}$
onto $S$. The restricted factors $\widehat V_n=V_nJ$ are isometries and obey
$\widehat V_{l+r}=(\widehat V_l\otimes\widehat V_r)\widehat W_{l,r}$, where
$\widehat W_{l,r}=(J^\dagger\otimes J^\dagger)W_{l,r}J$ satisfies
$\widehat W_{l,r}^\dagger\widehat W_{l,r}=I$.
The bounded-leaf tree and its measurement implementation now apply with
arbitrary virtual dimension $\chi$. They preserve all supported
superpositions simultaneously. For each fixed measurement history the
branch scalar is independent of the input on the zero-scratch subspace;
the final equality specifies every physical site.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isometryTree_matrix_eq_supportedCfgPolarIso} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/SupportedPolarTree}, and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_rounds_blockLayerOp_supportedPolarTree} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/SupportedTreePreparation}.}
The supported trees now compose with the constant-depth sparse GHZ seed
and one simultaneous pair-window unitary. The bond encodings end in a zero
site, and an equality of full physical configurations aligns them with the
even tree-root cutoff also for odd block lengths.

\section{Canonical non-normal resolution}
The all-length bound now holds for the supplied canonical repeated-block
class with normal inequivalent left-canonical blocks and positive definite
trace-one transfer fixed points.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_supported_block_preparation_constants} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/CoherentBlockTreePreparation},
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_log_log_preparation_of_supported_block_approximation} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/SupportedLogLogPreparation}, and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedWithMeasurementRoundsInDepth_repeatedBlockSum_le_log_log_of_inequivalent}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OverlappingLogLogPreparation}.}
The same constant precedes $N$ and $\varepsilon$. It covers every $N\ge2$
with nonzero periodic vector and $0<\varepsilon\le1$, finite-block overlap,
complex copy phases, repetitions, and vanishing individual sector sums.
The approximation uses the unweighted direct sum, and its common rate
bounds the mixed maps as well as the individual maps, as in the inherited
corrections of~\cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap}.

For $q=\lceil a\log(N/\varepsilon)+b\rceil\le N$, use the unequal
blocks and the amplitude-uniform exponential error estimate. If $N<q$
and $N\ge4s$, use a whole-ring supported tree of height $O(\log N)$.
The exact one-block pair is required to have support only at $M=1$;
it need not have that support at other ring sizes. Only the finitely many
lengths $N<4s$ use bounded exact synthesis. Thus no accuracy-dependent
linear-depth fallback or free $N$-site gate is hidden in the bound.

Nearest-neighbour unitary layers are counted. Measurement, classical
communication and on-site correction are free, and the number of rounds
may grow with the tree height. Every nonzero-probability history gives the
target up to a scalar, with no postselection or discarded environment.
The original-to-canonical transport for arbitrary tensors and their
periodicity data is not asserted here.

\section{The remaining restriction}
A two-site gate between distant sites joins sites at odd separation, on pairwise
disjoint stretches; gates at even separation, or on overlapping stretches, are
not covered, whereas the paragraph of the source speaks of teleporting spatially
separated sites in general. Neither the tree of two-site gates nor the trees of
registers need such gates. This geometric limitation does not restrict the
canonical non-normal construction just described. The earlier
logarithmic-depth construction and its corrections are recorded
in~\cite{gap:mswc24_measurement_preparation_scope}.
