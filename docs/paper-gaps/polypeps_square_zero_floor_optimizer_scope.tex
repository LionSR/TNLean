\section{The source statement}

Let $L,q,m\ge0$ be integers, let $V_L=\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^2$, and use the
physical Hilbert space $\ell^2(V_L\to\{0,\ldots,q-1\};\mathbb C)$.
The original Hamiltonian is $H=\sum_v h_v+\sum_e h_e$, where $h_v$ is
supported at the site $v$ and $h_e$ at the two endpoints of an edge $e$.
Their Euclidean operator norms are at most the real interaction bound $J$,
and the total $H$ is Hermitian. Let $\Omega$ be a unit ground vector with
energy $E_0\in\mathbb R$ and full-system projector-gap parameter
$\Delta\in\mathbb R$, so $H\Omega=E_0\Omega$ and
$H-E_0I\ge\Delta(I-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)$.

For $q>0$ and under its unique-ground-vector hypothesis, the companion source
of~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS} selects one family of positive semidefinite
regional filters on supplied nested regions
$X_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{m-1}\subseteq V_L$.
Each edge crosses at most one region and $0<a_j\le1$. For a region $X$,
let $b(X)$ count its crossing edges oriented right or up, each physical edge
occurring once. The filters satisfy
$\sum_r\lambda_r(F_j)^{2/a_j}=1$, and their local continuous linear maps
act in ascending order, so the output is
$\psi(F,\Omega)=\widehat F_{m-1}\cdots\widehat F_0\Omega$.
The selected family maximizes its output norm against every admissible family,
has nonzero output, and its normalization $\phi=\psi/\|\psi\|$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 |\operatorname{Re}\langle\phi,H\phi\rangle-E_0|
 &\le q^2J\sum_j b(X_j)a_j^2,
 \label{eq:polypeps_square_zero_floor_energy_bound}\\
 \Delta(1-|\langle\Omega,\phi\rangle|^2)
 &\le q^2J\sum_j b(X_j)a_j^2.
 \label{eq:polypeps_square_zero_floor_fidelity_bound}
\end{align}
The Hamiltonian, ground vector and full-system gap are the original ones.
Only the total Hamiltonian must be Hermitian.\footnote{Source: \texttt{openai/math} revision
\texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a},
\texttt{lean/OAI/MathematicalPhysics/PEPSFilters/Basic.lean}, lines 151--179;
\texttt{EnergyOptimizer.lean}, \texttt{exists\_maximizer\_energy}, lines 62--109.}

\section{The adapted original-grid comparison}

TNLean retains its existing square-grid state and regional-configuration
coordinates and adapts the source's positive-filter definitions in its
\texttt{Pinned} namespace. The adaptation preserves positive semidefiniteness,
the real eigenvalue-power normalization, repeated region occurrences,
ascending local action, and inverse-zero normalization. The local lift is
identified with the existing tensor-factor lift entry by entry.

At each positive weight, conjugating by the regional configuration permutation
identifies admissible bundled filters with all floor-zero feasible canonical
matrix families. Both matrix conversions are inverse. The canonical output
is exactly $U\psi(F,\Omega)$ for the existing physical configuration isometry
$U$, and the normalized output is exactly $U\phi(F,\Omega)$ even at zero
output. A global maximum on either side therefore compares with every
competitor on the other side, including singular matrices; it does not
compare only a selected spectral subclass.\footnote{Declarations in
\texttt{PinnedNestedFilterCoordinates.lean}:

These comparisons reuse QICLean's PSD trace-power characterization
of~\cite{QICLean2026FilterFeasibility} and ordered-output transport
of~\cite{QICLean2026FilterOutput}. They require no nesting, positive dimension,
invertibility or nonzero-output assumption. Positive weights are needed only
for the admissibility and maximum comparisons.

Applying the canonical physical theorem once and pulling back its selected
family gives a single adapted maximizing family with nonzero output, unit
normalization and bounds~(\ref{eq:polypeps_square_zero_floor_energy_bound})
and~(\ref{eq:polypeps_square_zero_floor_fidelity_bound}) for the original
$H$, $\Omega$ and $\Delta$. The conversion does not choose another optimizer.
The boundary count is exactly the original once-counted forward-edge count.
No regional gap, factor loss or extra termwise Hermiticity premise is added.

\section{The vacuous negative-bound case}

The canonical physical theorem permits every real interaction bound $J$.
Every admissible support is nonempty. For an unused admissible support, choose
one of its sites $v$. The original site-term bound gives
$0\le\|h_v\|\le J$, which also bounds the zero term assigned to that support.
On the empty square there are no admissible supports, so no sign condition on
$J$ is imposed. The original-grid theorem therefore applies the canonical
physical theorem directly to the original Hamiltonian and transports its
selected family and exact crossing budget without replacing $J$.

The theorem also allows $q=0$ on the empty grid, whose configuration set has
one element. By contrast, a nonempty region with $q=0$ has no configurations
and no trace-normalized filter. The representation statements keep these
cases distinct. No positivity of $\Delta$ is needed for the weighted fidelity
bound; no uniqueness assertion for nonpositive $\Delta$ is made.

\section{What remains outside the result}

This is an attributed adaptation, not an identification with unimported OAI
Lean types. The theorem uses TNLean's existing \texttt{SquareHamiltonian} and
its native full-system gapped-ground-state predicate. It does not import the
source's \texttt{IsGridHamiltonian}, \texttt{UniqueGround} or
\texttt{FullSystemGap} declarations. A theorem literally stated on those
source types would require their import and a checked model conversion under
a compatible toolchain; the source and current TNLean toolchains differ.
No new source dependency is introduced here.

The regions, their nesting and one-crossing property, and the weights remain
inputs. Their geometric construction, the two-family decomposition,
Proposition 4.1, inverse-density minimizers, and adaptive-patch or complete
PEPS compositions are separate work. The former filter-representation,
original-coordinate and negative-$J$ restrictions have been removed for the
adapted interface. This note stays open for the literal model comparison and
geometric obligations; it records no error in the source.
