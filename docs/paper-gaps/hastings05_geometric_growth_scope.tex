\section{The source assumptions}

Hastings, \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians locally},
arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3~\cite{Hastings2006Solving}, assumes a finite metric
space of sites, interaction range $R$, interaction norm bound $J$, and a
uniform bound $S$ for the number of sites within distance $R$ of a site
(\path{proj3new.tex}, lines 169--190). These assumptions do not imply
polynomial growth of larger balls. For example, finite balls in a
bounded-degree binary tree have uniformly bounded radius-one balls but
exponentially many vertices at intermediate distances from their root.
The qubit Hamiltonian $\sum_i |1\rangle\langle1|_i$ on such a tree is a
nonzero local Hamiltonian with a unique ground state and gap one.

\section{The restricted partial-trace estimate}

The partial-trace localization estimate in
\path{TNLean/Lattice/GaussianFilteredTermLocalization.lean} assumes a
polynomial bound $K_g(k+1)^{k_g}$ for the cardinality of every graph sphere
of radius $k$. Its approximation is the normalized partial trace of a
full Gaussian-filtered interaction onto a graph neighbourhood. Both the
growth hypothesis and the construction differ from the source's
truncated local evolution in eq.~(mid), lines 285--307. This estimate
remains a valid specialization, with its growth hypothesis explicit; it
is not the general finite-metric theorem.

\section{The actual truncated filter without polynomial growth}

The weighted comparison in
\path{TNLean/Lattice/GaussianFilteredTruncationWeighted.lean} instead
sums the omitted interactions before replacing their individual spatial
decays by a common bound. Join two sites whenever their metric distance
is at most $R$. This graph has degree at most $S$. For any
$\mu>\log(S+1)$ its exponential distance kernel is summable, since a
radius-$k$ graph ball has at most $(S+1)^k$ vertices. A bound on the
interaction norm at each anchor therefore bounds the entire omitted
tail. Choosing this decay rate before applying the Lieb--Robinson
estimate yields exponential localization without a polynomial sphere
bound.

For $R>0$, there are $c_1,\eta>0$ and $C\ge0$, depending only on
$R,S,J,\Delta$, such that, for every $\ell\ge R$, the actual source
construction with the normalized Gaussian weight is
\begin{align}
 M_i&=\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2\pi q}}
       \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-(t\Delta)^2/(2q)}
          e^{itH_i^{\rm loc}}h_i e^{-itH_i^{\rm loc}}\,dt,
 &q&=c_1\ell\Delta,
 &H_i^{\rm loc}&=\sum_{d(i,j)\le\ell-R}h_j.
 \label{eq:hastings05_growth_filter}
\end{align}
It is Hermitian, supported within distance $\ell$ of $i$, has norm at
most $J$, and satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|h_i\|e^{-q/2}+\|\widetilde h_i-M_i\|
     &\le \|h_i\|C e^{-\eta\ell},
 &\Big\|H-\sum_iM_i\Big\|&\le |\Lambda|JC e^{-\eta\ell}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_growth_error}
\end{align}
Here $\widetilde h_i$ is the filter using the full Hamiltonian.
The constants are chosen before the finite metric space and the
Hamiltonian. These statements are proved by
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_metric_truncated_filter_localization} and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_metric_truncated_filter_sum_bound}.
For $R=0$, distinct-site interactions commute and the full and truncated
filters equal the original interaction exactly; see
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.gaussianFilteredTerm_full_and_sum_eq_of_zero_range}.

\section{Local commutators and the separate volume sum}

The local commutator estimate also needs no polynomial sphere bound.
Let $P_0$ project onto the original ground eigenspace, with gap lower bound
$\Delta>0$. For positive interaction radius $R$, put
\begin{align}
 v&=4(S+1)|SJ|e^4+1,&
 \gamma&=\min\{1/4,\Delta/(8v)\},&
 \lambda&=\gamma/R.
 \label{eq:hastings05_metric_commutator_rate}
\end{align}
For supported observables $W$ and $B$ in radius-$\rho$ balls about $i,j$,
the estimate is
\begin{align}
 \|[P_0WP_0,P_0BP_0]\|
 &\le \bigl(4|B(j,\rho)|+4\bigr)\|W\|\,\|B\|
       e^{2\lambda\rho}e^{-\lambda d(i,j)}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_metric_commutator}
\end{align}
Finite-support propagation at a fixed rate, followed by a second Gaussian
filter under the original Hamiltonian, proves this bound. The filter
parameter is chosen from the separation and the original gap. The actual
locally truncated operators have the required supports and norms at most
$J$, so their compressions satisfy (\ref{eq:hastings05_metric_commutator})
with the product of norms replaced by $J^2$. No gap for a local truncated
Hamiltonian is assumed. These statements are proved by
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_groundCompression_commutator_le_metricBalls}
and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_groundCompression_metric_truncated_commutator_le}.

The displayed rate uses $R>0$; the exact zero-radius filter identities are
separate. The estimate retains the source's local support-cardinality
factor. It does not sum the distance kernel over all sites. Uniform
volume sums therefore still require their stated geometric hypotheses;
the unrestricted basis-sum claim is addressed in
\path{hastings05_tree_basis_counterexample.tex}.

The missing $q$ in the printed Gaussian exponent and the choice of decay
length are addressed in the existing notes
\path{hastings05_filtered_term_approximation.tex} and
\path{hastings05_truncated_dynamics.tex}. No assertion about a basis in
a degenerate ground space follows from this metric-growth argument.
