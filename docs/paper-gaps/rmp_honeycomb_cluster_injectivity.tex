\section{The source claim}
Section~IV of \cite{Cirac2021Matrix} introduces the cluster state as follows:
\begin{quote}
    ``The canonical example for an injective PEPS with global symmetries is
    the cluster state on the honeycomb lattice [\ldots] This state has a very
    simple PEPS representation: [figure] with [vertex tensor]
    $=\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}$, [bond matrix]
    $=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1\\ 1 &
                -1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
        [\ldots] To use the fundamental theorem of PEPS, we first block two sites
    of this PEPS such as to make it uniform [\ldots]''
\end{quote}
\footnote{Source traceability: \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex},
    lines 1445--1452.}
The review defines injectivity tensor by tensor: ``A PEPS tensor $A$ is
called injective if it is injective as a linear map
$A:(\mathbb C^D)^{\otimes r}\to\mathbb C^d$ from the virtual to the physical
system'', and it reserves a separate word for injectivity after blocking: a
tensor ``is called \emph{normal} if it becomes injective after blocking a
sufficiently large rectangular region''.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}, lines 1927--1938.}

\section{The counterexample}
Let $H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}
    \right)$. The vertex tensor $\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}$ of a vertex of degree
three maps the virtual space $(\C^2)^{\otimes3}$, of dimension eight, to
$\C^2$. Its image is two-dimensional, so it is not injective, and composing
with invertible bond matrices on its legs does not change this. More
generally, a vertex tensor of the graph-state PEPS on a vertex of degree
$k\geq2$ is a family of $2^k\geq4$ vectors in $\C^2$, which cannot be
linearly independent.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.not_linearIndependent_graphStatePEPS_component},
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.not_isVertexInjective_honeycombClusterPEPS}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/PEPS/Examples/HoneycombCluster}.}
The two-site blocking that the source performs to make the tensor uniform
does not help either: the blocked tensor of a unit cell has four virtual legs
of dimension two and a physical space of dimension four, so it maps a space of
dimension sixteen to one of dimension four.

The data are not degenerate: all bond dimensions are two and the state is
nonzero, with coefficient $2^{-|E|/2}$ at the all-zero configuration. The
claim ``injective PEPS'' is therefore false as printed, both for the site
tensor and for the uniform two-site tensor to which the source applies the
fundamental theorem.

\section{The corrected claim}
The PEPS becomes injective after blocking over hexagonal plaquettes, so it is
normal in the sense of the review, with plaquettes in place of rectangles. A
plaquette has six sites, each with exactly one bond leaving the plaquette.
With the boundary indices $\beta$ fixed, the blocked tensor sends $\beta$ to
\begin{align}
    \prod_{\text{inner bonds}}CZ\;\bigotimes_{u}M_u\ket{\beta_u},
    \label{eq:rmp_honeycomb_cluster_injectivity_plaquette_block}
\end{align}
up to normalization, where each $M_u$ is the identity or $H$ according to the
side on which the bond matrix of the leaving bond is placed. Both maps are
invertible, so the blocked tensor is injective. The honeycomb vertex set is
partitioned by every third plaquette, on a torus whose dimensions are
compatible with this pattern, and the blocked network is a triangular lattice
of injective tensors. The fundamental theorem for normal tensors applies to
this blocked network.

\section{Elimination plan}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formalize injectivity of the plaquette-blocked tensor and the
          partition of the honeycomb torus into plaquettes.
    \item State the corrected claim, that the honeycomb cluster PEPS is
          injective after plaquette blocking. No injectivity of the
          honeycomb PEPS is asserted until then.
\end{enumerate}
