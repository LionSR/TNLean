\section{The printed step}
In the proof of Theorem~4.1 of~\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}, at
\path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:801--804}, every residue $u$ modulo $m$ is
written as $u=\alpha_u+pk_u$ with $0\le\alpha_u<r=\gcd(m,p)$ and
$0\le k_u<q=m/r$. The phases $c_\alpha$, $0\le\alpha<r$, are given in the
source construction together with the hypothesis $c_\alpha^q=1$
(\cite{DeLasCuevas2017Irreducible}, \path{Papers/1708.00029/main.tex:784},
where they are written $c_{j,\alpha}$ with $c_{j,\alpha}^{m_j/r_j}=1$); the
note does not derive this condition. The local phases $d_u=f_{u+1}/f_u$ with
$f_u=c_{\alpha_u}^{k_u}$ are claimed to satisfy
\begin{align}
    \prod_{k=0}^{p-1}d_{u+k}
     & =\frac{c_{\alpha_{u+p}}^{k_{u+p}}}{c_{\alpha_u}^{k_u}}=c_{\alpha_u}
    \label{eq:dccsp17_orbit_phase_wraparound_product}
\end{align}
``since by definition $k_{u+p}=k_u+1$''. For the bounded representative
$k_u=q-1$ this is false: then $k_{u+p}=0$.

\section{Correction}
Adding $p$ fixes $\alpha_u$ and replaces $k_u$ by $k_u+1$ modulo $q$.
Since $c_{\alpha}^q=1$, the power $c_{\alpha_u}^{k_{u+p}}$ equals
$c_{\alpha_u}^{k_u+1}$ in every case, including $k_u=q-1$, so
(\ref{eq:dccsp17_orbit_phase_wraparound_product}) holds as stated. The
correction concerns only the justification; the identity and the theorem are
unchanged.
