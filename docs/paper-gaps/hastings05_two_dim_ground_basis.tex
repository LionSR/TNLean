\section{The source passage and what is proved}

For a ground space of dimension $n=2$ the source chooses a basis that
diagonalizes $A=PM_jP$, with eigenvalues $r\ne s$ and
$\delta\lambda_j=|r-s|$ (lines~1678--1688). For an orthonormal eigenbasis
$v,w$ and any $B$, $\langle v,[A,B]w\rangle=(r-s)\langle v,Bw\rangle$, so
both off-diagonal elements of $B$ are at most $\lVert[A,B]\rVert/|r-s|$. The
off-diagonal part
$\langle v,Bw\rangle\lvert v\rangle\langle w\rvert
+\langle w,Bv\rangle\lvert w\rangle\langle v\rvert$ maps the two
coordinates of a vector to orthogonal directions, so its norm is at most the
larger element, and
\begin{align}
  o(i)\le\frac{\lVert[PM_iP,PM_jP]\rVert}{\delta\lambda_j}
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_oi}
\end{align}
(lines~1688--1690). Since $PM_iv$ lies in the span of $v,w$ and
$\langle v,PM_iv\rangle=\langle v,M_iv\rangle$,
\begin{align}
  \lVert M_iv-\langle v,M_iv\rangle v\rVert
  \le\lVert(1-P)M_iv\rVert+o(i),
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_low}
\end{align}
which is eq.~\texttt{low} up to $o(i)$ (lines~476--491); eq.~\texttt{facld}
bounds the first term.

\section{Status}

Under a polynomial sphere-growth bound with constants independent of system
size, and for every fixed finite ground-space dimension, an orthonormal basis is
constructed for which the sum of the off-diagonal parts of the source's
\emph{truncated} operators $M_i$ is bounded uniformly in the number of sites and
terms. The recursion selects at most $n-1$ original indices, retains the
compression cross terms, and stops on scalar subspaces of arbitrary dimension.
Neither a supplied basis nor a separating-label hypothesis is assumed, and no
compressed spectral denominator or finite-dimensional conditioning constant
occurs in the final bound.

The Hamiltonian generator parameters and the support parameters of the localized
observables are kept separate. The source's second Gaussian filter is taken
under the original Hamiltonian; no gap for a truncated Hamiltonian is assumed.
The direct observable bound is uniform when the ground-space dimension and
the original local, geometric and localization parameters are fixed. For a
localization radius depending on the system size, a separate proved perturbation
bound uses the original-term basis and the total error of eq.~\texttt{apb};
uniformity then requires a uniform bound on that total error.

This proves the qualitative uniform assertion under the additional growth
hypothesis. Source lines 169--190 assume only finite-range local bounds and a
metric, which do not imply polynomial growth. The unrestricted metric-lattice
assertion is false, as proved mathematically by
\href{https://sirui-lu.com/TNLean/paper-gaps/hastings05_tree_basis_counterexample.pdf}
{a uniformly gapped tree counterexample}. The counterexample family and
its gap proof have not been formalized in Lean. The additional geometric
hypothesis is therefore a correction of the unrestricted source assertion,
not a condition known to follow from the source. Its role is explained in
\href{https://sirui-lu.com/TNLean/paper-gaps/hastings05_geometric_growth_scope.pdf}
{the note on geometric growth}. The finite-dimensional basis construction is
complete, and its physical summation is proved under polynomial sphere growth.
The printed quantitative
dependence $O(J^2/(c_1l\Delta E\,\delta\lambda_j))$ is replaced by the explicit
constants below. The source's spectral-denominator estimate remains valid in
its stated eigenbasis, but the uniform construction eliminates that denominator
by combining spectral-spread and commutator estimates. The cross terms and the
geometric shell factors account for the correction to the printed sketch.

\section{A compression with simple spectrum}

For $n>2$ the source finds the $M_j$ with the largest spread of eigenvalues,
bounds the off-diagonal elements between its eigenvectors, and passes to
further $M_k$ for eigenvectors sharing an eigenvalue of $M_j$
(lines~1695--1705). Because the compressions commute only approximately, this
iteration is not a proof as stated. When one compression $A=PM_jP$ has $n$
eigenvalues on the ground space that are pairwise at least $\delta>0$ apart,
its orthonormal eigenbasis $v_0,\dots,v_{n-1}$ needs no iteration. With indices
modulo $n$, the off-diagonal part $o_B$ of any $B$ is the sum over $k\ne0$ of
the shifts
$S_k=\sum_a\langle v_a,Bv_{a+k}\rangle\lvert v_a\rangle\langle v_{a+k}\rvert$.
By Bessel's inequality each shift has norm at most its largest element, and
each element is at most $\lVert[A,B]\rVert/\delta$, so
\begin{align}
  o(i)\le(n-1)\frac{\lVert[PM_iP,PM_jP]\rVert}{\delta},
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_simple}
\end{align}
which for $n=2$ is~\eqref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_oi}. The bound
\eqref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_low} holds for every basis vector
$v_a$.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_norm_commutator_div},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sum_norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le} and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_apply_sub_inner_smul_le_add_offDiagonalPartFamily}
    in \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyBasisSimpleSpectrum}.}
This is the first level of the iteration of the next section, with $\delta$ a
given lower bound on the spacing.

\section{Repeated eigenvalues: the iteration}

Record the source's iteration by real labels $r_\ell(a)$ of the basis vectors at
levels $\ell=0,1,\dots$: the class of $a$ at level $\ell$ is the set of $c$
with $r_m(c)=r_m(a)$ for every $m<\ell$, and the compression $A_\ell=PM_{j_\ell}P$
of level $\ell$ acts diagonally on each class,
$\langle v_c,A_\ell v_d\rangle=r_\ell(c)\delta_{cd}$. Expanding both products in
the basis gives, for $a$, $b$ in one class $C$ at level $\ell$,
\begin{align}
  (r_\ell(a)-r_\ell(b))\langle v_a,Bv_b\rangle
  =\langle v_a,[A_\ell,B]v_b\rangle
  -\sum_{c\notin C}\bigl(\langle v_a,A_\ell v_c\rangle\langle v_c,Bv_b\rangle
    -\langle v_a,Bv_c\rangle\langle v_c,A_\ell v_b\rangle\bigr).
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_iteration}
\end{align}
The indices $c\notin C$ are separated from $a$ and $b$ at an earlier level, so
the sum is a product of matrix elements already bounded there. Dividing by the
smallest difference $\delta_\ell>0$ of distinct labels of level $\ell$ within a
class, which is positive in finite dimension and is the source's
$\delta\lambda$ at that level, bounds every off-diagonal element at the first
level separating its two vectors. With two levels, $M_j$ and then $M_k$, every
off-diagonal element of $PM_iP$ is at most
\begin{align}
  \max\Bigl(\frac{\lVert[A_0,B]\rVert}{\delta_0},\
  \frac{1}{\delta_1}\Bigl(\lVert[A_1,B]\rVert
    +2n\frac{\lVert[A_0,A_1]\rVert\,\lVert[A_0,B]\rVert}{\delta_0^2}\Bigr)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_two_levels}
\end{align}
and $o(i)$ is at most $n-1$ times this. For any number of levels, let every
commutator $[A_\ell,A_m]$ be at most $K$ and every $[A_\ell,B]$ at most $K_B$.
By induction on the level, the elements of the $A_m$ separated at level $\ell$
are at most $\alpha_\ell$, and those of $B$ at most $\beta_\ell$, where
$\alpha_0=K/\delta_0$, $\beta_0=K_B/\delta_0$ and
\begin{align}
  \alpha_{\ell+1}=\max\Bigl(\alpha_\ell,\frac{K+2n\alpha_\ell^2}{\delta_{\ell+1}}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  \beta_{\ell+1}=\max\Bigl(\beta_\ell,\frac{K_B+2n\alpha_\ell\beta_\ell}{\delta_{\ell+1}}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_levels}
\end{align}
If the given labels separate every pair of distinct basis vectors after $L$
levels, then $o(i)\le(n-1)\beta_{L-1}$. This is an additional condition on the
recorded iteration, not an automatic consequence of the source's stopping
criterion (lines~1707--1708). A terminal subspace may have dimension greater than
one when all remaining compressions are scalar there. Its off-diagonal elements
already vanish, so no separation is needed.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.abs_sub_mul_norm_inner_le},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_inner_le_of_mem_labelClass},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_inner_le_levelBoundB},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_levelBoundB},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_inner_le_of_two_levels} and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_of_two_levels} in
    \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyBasisIteration}.}
The bound $\beta_\ell$ is linear in $K_B$, and with two levels every term decays
in $d(i,j)$ or $d(i,k)$, so the sum over $i$ behind eq.~\texttt{oibu} stays
bounded.

This is a local correction of the sketch. The source states that the further
compressions bound the remaining off-diagonal elements, but the commutator of
the compression of $M_k$ to a class with that of $B$ differs from $[M_k,B]$ by
the cross terms of~\eqref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_iteration}, which
the sketch omits and which are kept here.


\section{A uniform construction when the ground space has dimension two}

The spectral denominator can be removed from the qualitative two-dimensional
assertion without assuming a uniform lower bound on a compressed spectral gap.
Set $w_i=\|h_i\|$ and $D_i=w_i^{-1}A_i$, using $D_i=0$ when $w_i=0$.
The latter convention is consistent because $\|A_i\|\le w_i$.
Choose $j$ maximizing the spectral spread $\rho$ of $D_j$, and diagonalize $D_j$.
If $\rho=0$, every $D_i$ is scalar and all off-diagonal parts vanish.
Otherwise every off-diagonal entry $z_i=|\langle v_a,A_iv_b\rangle|$, $a\ne b$,
satisfies
\begin{align}
 z_i\le w_i\rho,
 \qquad \rho z_i\le \|[D_j,A_i]\|.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_normalized_bounds}
\end{align}
The first estimate follows because a Hermitian operator in dimension two differs
from one of its eigenvalues times the identity by a rank-one operator whose norm
is its spectral spread. The second is the eigenbasis commutator identity.

If $\|[A_j,A_i]\|\le C_*w_jw_i e^{-\gamma d(a_j,a_i)}$, normalization and
(\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_normalized_bounds}) give
\begin{align}
 z_i^2\le w_i\rho z_i\le C_*w_i^2e^{-\gamma d(a_j,a_i)},
 \qquad z_i\le\sqrt{C_*}\,w_i e^{-\gamma d(a_j,a_i)/2}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_half_decay}
\end{align}
At infinite graph distance the right side is interpreted as zero. In dimension
two the norm of the off-diagonal part is at most the largest off-diagonal entry.
Thus, with $C_*=Ce^{\gamma(2R+1)}$ from
(\ref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_blecm_exp}), the norm budget and graph
sphere bound yield an actual orthonormal ground-space basis satisfying
\begin{align}
 \sum_i o(i)\le\sqrt{C_*}\,|b_0|K_g S_{\gamma/2,k}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_uniform_two}
\end{align}
This constant is uniform in the number of sites and terms and contains no
compressed spectral denominator. The construction maximizes the normalized
spread rather than the unnormalized spread used in the source. The source's
explicit $1/\delta\lambda_j$ estimate remains valid in its own chosen basis.
The printed dependence on $c_1,l,\Delta E$ is not identified with the corrected
constant in (\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_uniform_two}).

\section{A uniform spectral split}

A binary spectral refinement removes inverse spectral denominators from its
one-step estimate. Let $D_i$ be finitely many Hermitian operators on an
$m$-dimensional Hilbert space, $m\ge2$, and suppose
\begin{align}
 \|[D_i,D_j]\|\le C f(i,j),\qquad C\ge0,\qquad f(i,j)=f(j,i)\ge0.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_split_commutators}
\end{align}
No bound on $\|D_i\|$ is needed. Either all the $D_i$ are scalar, or choose a
term $D_j$ of largest spectral spread $\rho>0$. Its ordered eigenvalues have
an adjacent gap at least $\rho/(m-1)$. Let $P$ project onto the eigenvectors
above this gap and put $Q=1-P$. Both projections have nonzero range.

Every $D_i$ differs from its least eigenvalue times the identity by norm at
most its spread, which is at most $\rho$. Hence $\|PD_iQ\|\le\rho$.
Across the selected gap the commutator identity gives each matrix coefficient
of $PD_iQ$ a bound $(m-1)C f(i,j)/\rho$. Expanding in rank-one operators
there are at most $m^2$ coefficients, so
\begin{align}
 \rho\|PD_iQ\|\le m^3 C f(i,j),\qquad
 \|PD_iQ\|\le\sqrt{m^3 C}\sqrt{f(i,j)}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_split_cross}
\end{align}
The reverse block has the same norm by Hermitian adjunction. These
projections and estimates are constructed, without an assumed separating
basis or a supplied projection.\footnote{Declarations
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_selfAdjoint_spectral_split} and
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.exists_family_spectral_split}, in
\doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyBasisSpectralSplit}.}

If $0\le f\le1$ and $f(i,j)f(j,k)\le f(i,k)$, the exact identity
\begin{align}
 [PD_iP,PD_kP]
 &=P[D_i,D_k]P-PD_iQD_kP+PD_kQD_iP
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_split_compression}
\end{align}
gives
\begin{align}
 \|[PD_iP,PD_kP]\|
 &\le C(1+2m^3)\sqrt{f(i,k)}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_split_child}
\end{align}
For $f(i,k)=e^{-\gamma d(i,k)}$ the new rate is $\gamma/2$.
The kernel formulation also permits zero kernels between disconnected
components; no finite-distance convention is imposed. Both cross terms in
(\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_split_compression}) are retained.\footnote{Declaration
\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_commutator_compression_le_kernel}, in
\doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyBasisSpectralSplit}.}
This is the uniform inductive step. The complete recursive basis and its
summed bound are separate from this one-step assertion.

\section{Uniform recursion in every finite dimension}

The binary split also gives an actual basis with uniform bounds in every fixed
finite dimension. Let $k_0(i,j)=e^{-\gamma d(a_i,a_j)}$, with value zero at
infinite distance, and define $k_{t+1}=\sqrt{k_t}$. The graph triangle inequality
implies $k_t(i,j)k_t(j,l)\le k_t(i,l)$, including disconnected components;
moreover $0\le k_t\le1$ and $k_t\le k_{t+1}$.
Thus $k_t(i,j)=e^{-\gamma d(a_i,a_j)/2^t}$ at finite distance.

Normalize the original compressions once, setting $D_i=\|h_i\|^{-1}A_i$ and
$D_i=0$ for a zero term. The original commutator estimate becomes
$\|[D_i,D_j]\|\le C_*k_0(i,j)$, where
$C_*=Ce^{\gamma(2R+1)}$ and $C$ is evaluated at $|K_g|$.
For total ground-space dimension $N=n$, put
\begin{align}
 M_N=1+2N^3,\qquad C_t=C_*M_N^t,\qquad
 D_N(C_*)=\sqrt{N^3C_*M_N^N}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_recursive_coeff}
\end{align}
At a node of depth $t$ and dimension $m$, maintain $t+m\le N$ and the
commutator bound $C_tk_t$. If the compressed family is scalar, choose any
orthonormal basis of that subspace and stop; its dimension need not be one.
Otherwise the proved binary spectral split gives a proper subspace $K$ and an
original anchor $j$ such that both cross-block norms are at most
$\sqrt{m^3C_t}\,k_{t+1}(i,j)$. Retaining both compression cross terms gives,
on either child subspace, the commutator coefficient
$C_t+2m^3C_t\le C_{t+1}$ with kernel $k_{t+1}$.

The two child dimensions $p,q$ are positive, satisfy $p+q=m$, and strictly
decrease. Apply induction and combine the two orthonormal bases. Concatenating
the child anchor lists and the new anchor gives at most
$(p-1)+(q-1)+1=m-1$ entries. At every node,
$\sqrt{m^3C_t}\le D_N(C_*)$ and $k_{t+1}\le k_N$.
The constructed orthonormal basis and a list $s$ of at most $n-1$ original
anchors therefore satisfy, for $a\ne b$,
\begin{align}
 |\langle v_a,A_iv_b\rangle|
 \le D_n(C_*)\,\|h_i\|\sum_{j\in s}e^{-\lambda d(a_i,a_j)},
 \qquad \lambda=\gamma/2^n.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_recursive_entries}
\end{align}
The list counts repeated choices at different nodes. Its length bound is
independent of the number of sites and terms.

Using the norm budget and summing graph spheres at rate $\lambda$ gives
\begin{align}
 \sum_i o(i)\le(n-1)^2D_n(C_*)\,|b_0|\,|K_g|S_{\gamma/2^n,k}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_recursive_sum}
\end{align}
One factor $n-1$ bounds an off-diagonal operator by its largest entry, and the
other bounds the number of selected anchors. Every factor on the right is
controlled by the fixed ground-space dimension and the original local and
geometric parameters. No uniform conditioning of a finite-dimensional quotient,
no lower bound on a compressed spectral gap, and no separation hypothesis is
used. For the original terms and their first Gaussian filters, the separate
two-dimensional estimate (\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_uniform_two}) retains the
sharper rate $\gamma/2$.

\section{The actual truncated operators}

The operators in the source's eq.~\texttt{oibu} are not the first filters of
$h_i$ under the original Hamiltonian. They are
\begin{align}
 M_i=\operatorname{Filter}_{r,\Delta E}(H_{s_i},h_i),
 \qquad H_{s_i}=\sum_{j\in s_i}h_j,
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_truncated_terms}
\end{align}
with the truncated Hamiltonians of eq.~\texttt{mid}. Filtering under $H_{s_i}$
preserves Hermiticity and the norm bound $\|M_i\|\le\|h_i\|$, and the defining
localization data give supports $Y_i$ with $X_i\subseteq Y_i$ and with every
term of $H_{s_i}$ supported in $Y_i$.

Let $v_{\rm obs}$ bound $|Y_i|$ and let $R_{\rm obs}$ bound the diameters of
$Y_i$. Write $R,v_R,b_0$ for the original Hamiltonian generator parameters.
Applying the second Gaussian filter to $M_i$ under the original Hamiltonian,
as in source lines 1548--1564, gives
\begin{align}
 \|[PM_jP,PM_iP]\|
 \le C_{\rm obs}\|M_j\|\|M_i\|e^{-\gamma d(a_j,a_i)},
 \qquad \gamma=\min\left(\frac14,\frac{\Delta E}{8v}\right),
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_observable_decay}
\end{align}
with value zero at infinite distance. Here $v=2v_R|b_0|e^{2R}+1$ is still the
\emph{original generator} velocity, while
\begin{align}
 C_{\rm obs}
 =\left[2(2+4|b_0|e^{2R})v_{\rm obs}^2|K_g|C_k+6\right]
 e^{\gamma(2R_{\rm obs}+1)},
 \qquad C_k=\frac{2^k k!e^{1/2}}{1-e^{-1/2}}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_observable_constant}
\end{align}
Thus the support cardinality of the observables affects the prefactor and their
diameter affects the conversion from support separation to anchor distance;
neither replaces the generator parameters in the velocity.

Normalize $PM_iP$ by $\|h_i\|$, using zero for a zero original term. The norm
bound on $M_i$ and (\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_observable_decay}) give the same
unweighted commutator hypothesis as in the recursive construction, with
$C_*=C_{\rm obs}$. The original norm budget still bounds the weights at the
original anchors. Consequently an actual orthonormal ground-space basis satisfies
\begin{align}
 \sum_i o(M_i)\le(n-1)^2D_n(C_{\rm obs})\,|b_0|\,|K_g|S_{\gamma/2^n,k}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_actual_truncated_sum}
\end{align}
This conclusion has no cumulative approximation term $N\varepsilon$ and does
not identify the compression of $M_i$ with that of $h_i$. Uniformity here refers
to fixed localization support parameters, as displayed in
(\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_observable_constant}). If the localization radius is
allowed to grow with the system size, its displayed dependence must also be
tracked; a separate perturbative comparison with the first filters can then use
the total truncation error of eq.~\texttt{apb}.

\section{A localization radius depending on the system size}

The fixed-radius bound (\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_actual_truncated_sum}) is not a
uniform estimate along an arbitrary choice of growing localization regions,
because $C_{\rm obs}$ contains their diameter and cardinality. A different basis
choice avoids this dependence when the total approximation error is controlled.

For an orthonormal family $v$ of length $n$, taking the off-diagonal part is
linear and satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|\operatorname{off}_v(A)\|&\le(n-1)\|A\|,\nonumber\\
 \|\operatorname{off}_v(A)\|
 &\le\|\operatorname{off}_v(B)\|+(n-1)\|A-B\|.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_perturbation}
\end{align}
Ground-space compression is contractive on differences. Choose the basis
constructed from the original compressions $Ph_iP$, and denote its uniform
summed bound by $B_{\rm orig}$, with the original generator support parameters
in every constant. The same basis works for every first filter parameter
$r\ge0$ and every truncation family $s_i$. Put
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E(r,s)=\sum_i\|
 \operatorname{Filter}_{r,\Delta E}(H,h_i)-
 \operatorname{Filter}_{r,\Delta E}(H_{s_i},h_i)\|.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_total_truncation_error}
\end{align}
Since $P\operatorname{Filter}_{r,\Delta E}(H,h_i)P=Ph_iP$, summing
(\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_perturbation}) gives
\begin{align}
 \sum_i o(M_i)\le B_{\rm orig}+(n-1)\mathcal E(r,s).
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_coupled_radius}
\end{align}
No localization radius occurs in $B_{\rm orig}$. Thus a coupled choice
$r=r(N)$ and $s=s(N)$ with a uniform bound $\mathcal E(r(N),s(N))\le\varepsilon$
gives the uniform bound $B_{\rm orig}+(n-1)\varepsilon$.
The required bound on $\mathcal E$ must be proved from the truncation estimate
\texttt{apb}; growth of the radius by itself does not imply it. This comparison
keeps the error explicitly and makes no fixed-radius uniformity claim along
an unverified size-dependent choice.

\section{An auxiliary finite-dimensional conditioning estimate}

Write $A_i=(Ph_iP)|_{\operatorname{ran}P}$ and let $V$ be their real linear
span. Every element of $V$ is Hermitian, so $\dim_{\mathbb R}V\le n^2$.
Choose $m=\dim_{\mathbb R}V$ original terms $A_{j_1},\dots,A_{j_m}$ spanning
$V$. Define
\begin{align}
 T:V&\longrightarrow\prod_{r=1}^m\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{ran}P),
 &T(B)&=([A_{j_r},B])_{r=1}^m,
 &Z&=\ker T.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_commutator_map}
\end{align}
The product has the maximum operator norm and $V$ has the operator norm.
Since the selected operators span $V$, every member of $Z$ commutes with every
member of $V$. In particular the Hermitian operators in $Z$ commute pairwise.
Choose a common orthonormal eigenbasis $v_1,\dots,v_n$ of $Z$. Joint
eigenspaces may have arbitrary multiplicity: no separation of their basis
vectors is assumed or needed.

Equip $V/Z$ with its quotient norm, and set
\begin{align}
 \kappa(T)=\|\overline T^{-1}\|,
 \qquad \overline T:V/Z\longrightarrow\operatorname{ran}T.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_conditioning}
\end{align}
The inverse is a linear map between finite-dimensional normed spaces, so its
norm is finite. The convention gives $\kappa(T)=0$ when $V=Z$.
For $a\ne b$, the functional $S_{ab}(B)=\langle v_a,Bv_b\rangle$ has norm at
most one and vanishes on $Z$. It therefore descends to a contraction on $V/Z$,
and
\begin{align}
 |\langle v_a,Bv_b\rangle|
 &\le \|B+Z\|\le\kappa(T)\max_r\|[A_{j_r},B]\|
 \le\kappa(T)\sum_r\|[A_{j_r},B]\|.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_constructed_entries}
\end{align}
Consequently the constructed basis satisfies
\begin{align}
 \sum_i o(i)\le(n-1)\kappa(T)\sum_{r=1}^m\sum_i\|[A_{j_r},A_i]\|.
 \label{eq:hastings05_basis_constructed_sum}
\end{align}
The compressed filtered terms have the same ground-space matrix elements, so
this also bounds their off-diagonal parts.

The role of $\kappa(T)$ can be compared precisely with the spectral denominators
in the source argument. A separating refinement gives a bound on the
\emph{off-diagonal map} through the commutator map, with coefficient
$\beta_{L-1}(1)$ in (\ref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_levels}).
The inverse norm in (\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_conditioning}) instead bounds the
\emph{distance to the centre}, and hence every contraction vanishing there.
These are different norm estimates; no equality, nor a bound of $\kappa(T)$ by a
single spectral denominator, is asserted. The two-dimensional estimate
(\ref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_oi}) retains its sharper explicit
factor $1/\delta\lambda_j$.

This auxiliary estimate is not the proof of the uniform bound above. The
existence of a finite $\kappa(T)$ for each system does not make it uniform
along a sequence of systems. To obtain a uniform bound from
(\ref{eq:hastings05_basis_constructed_sum}), one must bound this conditioning
uniformly, just as an estimate using the source's spectral denominators must
bound those denominators away from zero. No such additional hypothesis is
inserted into the finite-dimensional construction; its conclusion displays the
actual constant.

\section{The summed bound, eq.~\texttt{oibu}}

The compression $PM_iP$ of a filtered term equals $Ph_iP$ for every $q\ge0$, so
eq.~\texttt{blecm} may be applied to each pair $(i,j)$ with its own time
window. If $X_i$ is disjoint from the sites within graph distance $l$ of $X_j$,
the choice $T=l/(4v)$ and $q=T\Delta E$, with $v=2v_R|b_0|e^{2R}+1$, gives
\begin{align}
  \lVert[Ph_jP,Ph_iP]\rVert\le C\lVert h_j\rVert\,\lVert h_i\rVert\,e^{-\gamma l},
  \qquad \gamma=\min\Bigl(\frac14,\frac{\Delta E}{8v}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_blecm_exp}
\end{align}
with an explicit constant $C$; for anchors at distance $D$ the separation is
at least $D-2R-1$. Grouping the terms by their anchors, the norm budget $b_0$
of each site and the polynomial growth $K_g(r+1)^k$ of the graph spheres give
\begin{align}
  \sum_i\lVert[Ph_jP,Ph_iP]\rVert
  \le Ce^{\gamma(2R+1)}\,|b_0|\,K_g\,
    \frac{k!\,(2/\gamma)^ke^{\gamma/2}}{1-e^{-\gamma/2}}\,\lVert h_j\rVert,
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_oibu}
\end{align}
independently of the number of sites, so that $\sum_io(i)$ is at most $(n-1)/\delta$
times this in an eigenbasis of $PM_jP$ with spacing at least $\delta$.
For the iteration, the bound $\beta_\ell$ of~\eqref{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_levels}
is homogeneous of degree one in $K_B$. Taking for $K_B(i)$ the sum over the
levels $\ell<L$ of $\lVert[A_\ell,Ph_iP]\rVert$, with $A_\ell=Ph_{j_\ell}P$ and
$K=2J^2$ for terms of norm at most $J$, gives
\begin{align}
  \sum_io(i)\le(n-1)\,\beta_{L-1}(1)\sum_{\ell<L}Ce^{\gamma(2R+1)}\,|b_0|\,K_g\,
    \frac{k!\,(2/\gamma)^ke^{\gamma/2}}{1-e^{-\gamma/2}}\,\lVert h_{j_\ell}\rVert,
  \label{eq:hastings05_two_dim_ground_basis_oibu_levels}
\end{align}
with no factor counting the number of sites, provided the displayed spectral
constants and the number of levels are kept fixed.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.Lattice.norm_groundCompression_commutator_le_exp},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sum_norm_groundCompression_commutator_le},
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sum_norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_oibu} and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sum_norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_oibu_levels} in
    \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyCommutatorSum};
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.levelBoundB_eq_mul} and
    \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.sum_norm_offDiagonalPartFamily_le_levelBoundB} in
    \doclink{TNLean/Lattice/LowEnergyBasisIteration}.}
This gives size independence when the spectral denominators and the number of
selected levels are uniformly controlled. Their uniformity is not established by
the conditional iteration theorem. The printed dependence $O(J^2/(c_1l\Delta E\,\delta\lambda_j))$ is not established:
the constant here contains neither $c_1$ nor $l$, and since
$k!\,(2/\gamma)^k/(1-e^{-\gamma/2})$ grows like $\gamma^{-(k+1)}$ with
$\gamma=\Delta E/(8v)$ for small gaps, it scales like $(v/\Delta E)^{k+1}$.
On a chain, where the spheres have bounded size and $k=0$, this is the source's
$1/\Delta E$; on a lattice of dimension $D$, where $k=D-1$, it is
$(v/\Delta E)^D$.
