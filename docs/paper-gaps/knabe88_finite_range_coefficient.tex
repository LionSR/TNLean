\section{Source statements}

For a nearest-neighbor chain, let $m$ be the number of local interaction
terms in each open window, and let $\gamma$ be the gap of the corresponding
open-window Hamiltonian. Knabe's finite-size criterion has threshold
\begin{align}
    \gamma
        &>\frac{1}{m}.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_knabe_threshold}
\end{align}
This threshold originates in Ref.~\cite{Knabe1988Energy}. It is quoted for
matrix-product-state parent Hamiltonians at lines~1483--1489 of the local
source for Ref.~\cite{PerezGarcia2007Matrix} and at lines~2194--2197 of the
local source for Ref.~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}. Those passages state only the
threshold.

The closed-form periodic coefficient in the nearest-neighbor case is
\begin{align}
    \delta
        &=\frac{m\gamma-1}{m-1}.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_nearest_neighbor_coefficient}
\end{align}
The derivation below obtains this expression as the range-two specialization
of a finite-range coefficient.

\section{Finite-range derivation}

Let $N$ and $m$ be positive integers. Let $h_i$, for
$i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, be orthogonal projections, and define the
periodic Hamiltonian $H$, the length-$m$ window operators $A_s$, and the
separation-$q$ pair sums $Q_q$ by
\begin{align}
    H
        &=\sum_i h_i,
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_periodic_hamiltonian}\\
    A_s
        &=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}h_{s+a},
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_window_hamiltonian}\\
    Q_q
        &=\sum_i(h_i h_{i+q}+h_i h_{i-q}).
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_pair_sum}
\end{align}
Assume
\begin{align}
    1
        &\leq R\leq m,
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_range_bounds}\\
    N
        &\geq2m,
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_cycle_size}
\end{align}
and assume that terms at cyclic separation at least $R$ commute. Cyclic
double counting gives
\begin{align}
    \sum_s A_s^2
        &=mH+\sum_{q=1}^{m-1}(m-q)Q_q.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_window_square_sum}
\end{align}

For orthogonal projections, the operator inequality
\begin{align}
    h_i h_j+h_j h_i
        &\leq h_i+h_j
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_projection_pair_bound}
\end{align}
implies
\begin{align}
    Q_q
        &\leq2H.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_pair_sum_upper_bound}
\end{align}
If $q\geq R$, the separated projections commute, so
\begin{align}
    Q_q
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_pair_sum_nonnegative}
\end{align}
Expansion of $H^2$ also gives
\begin{align}
    H+\sum_{q=1}^{m-1}Q_q
        &\leq H^2.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_hamiltonian_square_lower_bound}
\end{align}

Define
\begin{align}
    c
        &=m-R+1.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_baseline_coefficient}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
    m-q
        &=c+(R-1-q).
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_weight_decomposition}
\end{align}
The correction term splits into short-range and separated contributions:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{m-1}(R-1-q)Q_q
        &=\sum_{q=1}^{R-2}(R-1-q)Q_q
          +\sum_{q=R}^{m-1}(R-1-q)Q_q.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_correction_split}
\end{align}
The short-range contribution satisfies
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{R-2}(R-1-q)Q_q
        &\leq
          2H\sum_{q=1}^{R-2}(R-1-q),
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_short_range_bound}
\end{align}
because its weights are positive and
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_pair_sum_upper_bound}) holds
termwise. The separated contribution satisfies
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=R}^{m-1}(R-1-q)Q_q
        &\leq0,
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_separated_bound}
\end{align}
because $R-1-q\leq0$ and $Q_q\geq0$. The nonnegativity applies throughout
the required range since
\begin{align}
    R
        &\leq q\leq m-1\leq N-R.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_separation_range}
\end{align}
The triangular sum is
\begin{align}
    \sum_{q=1}^{R-2}(R-1-q)
        &=\frac{(R-1)(R-2)}{2}.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_triangular_sum}
\end{align}

Substituting
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_weight_decomposition}) into
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_window_square_sum}), then applying
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_hamiltonian_square_lower_bound}),
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_short_range_bound}), and
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_separated_bound}), yields
\begin{align}
    \sum_s A_s^2
        &\leq
          cH^2+(m-c+(R-1)(R-2))H.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_preliminary_upper_bound}
\end{align}
The scalar coefficient simplifies as
\begin{align}
    m-c+(R-1)(R-2)
        &=(R-1)^2.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_scalar_simplification}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{align}
    \sum_s A_s^2
        &\leq cH^2+(R-1)^2H.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_window_square_upper_bound}
\end{align}

Assume that every window satisfies the local gap inequality
\begin{align}
    A_s^2
        &\geq\gamma A_s.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_local_gap_inequality}
\end{align}
Summing over $s$ and using
\begin{align}
    \sum_s A_s
        &=mH
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_window_sum}
\end{align}
gives
\begin{align}
    H^2
        &\geq
          \frac{m\gamma-(R-1)^2}{m-R+1}H.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_finite_range_bound}
\end{align}
The coefficient is positive under the condition
\begin{align}
    (R-1)^2
        &<m\gamma.
    \label{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_positive_coefficient_condition}
\end{align}
For $R=2$,
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_finite_range_bound}) reduces to
the nearest-neighbor coefficient
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_nearest_neighbor_coefficient}).

\section{Open chains by zero padding}

Let $R\geq1$, $m\geq R$, and $N\geq R$. On the Hilbert space of the
physical $N$-site chain, let $h_i$ be the nonwrapping range-$R$ parent
projections at starts $0\leq i\leq N-R$. Set $P=N+2m$. For
$s\in\mathbb Z/P\mathbb Z$, with representative
$\bar s\in\{0,\ldots,P-1\}$, define
\begin{align}
    \widetilde h_s
        &=\begin{cases}
            h_{\bar s},&\bar s+R\leq N,\\
            0,&\bar s+R>N,
          \end{cases}
    \label{eq:knabe_open_padding}\\
    H_N^{\mathrm{open}}
        &=\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z/P\mathbb Z}\widetilde h_s.
    \label{eq:knabe_open_sum}
\end{align}
The padding changes only the indexing set of the interaction terms, not the
physical volume. Zero is an orthogonal projection. For $R\leq e$ and
$e+R\leq P$, the representatives of $s$ and $s+e$ obey either
$\bar s+R\leq\overline{s+e}$ or
$\overline{s+e}+R\leq\bar s$. Thus the two active physical intervals
are disjoint, and the padded terms commute at the required oriented
separations. Also $P\geq2m$, as required by the cyclic inequality.

Define the padded windows
\begin{align}
    A_s&=\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\widetilde h_{s+q}.
    \label{eq:knabe_open_windows}
\end{align}
Write $C=N-R+1$ for the number of active starts and $t=\bar s$.
If $t+m\leq P$, the active starts in the window form
$[a,b)$ with $a=t$ and $b=\min\{t+m,C\}$, provided $t<C$;
otherwise the window is zero. If $t+m>P$, the initial part of the
window lies beyond the physical chain, and the active starts after wrapping
form $[a,b)$ with $a=0$ and $b=\min\{t+m-P,C\}$.
The inactive interval between two successive copies of the active starts
has length $P-C=2m+R-1$, so a window of $m$ starts cannot meet both copies.

Whenever $a<b$, put $W=b-a+R-1$ and $n=b+R-1$.
Then $R\leq W\leq m+R-1$, $W\leq n\leq N$, and $A_s$ is the
range-$R$ open Hamiltonian on $[n-W,n)=[a,b+R-1)$, acting independently
on the remaining sites. In particular, the boundary windows can have
fewer than $m$ active interaction terms.

Suppose that every range-$R$ open parent Hamiltonian on $W$ sites has
norm gap at least $\gamma>0$ on its kernel complement for
$R\leq W\leq m+R-1$. A physical coordinate isometry identifies each
nonzero $A_s$ with such a $W$-site Hamiltonian tensored with the identity.
The same norm gap therefore holds on the kernel complement of $A_s$.
For a positive operator, this is equivalent to $A_s^2\geq\gamma A_s$.
The zero windows satisfy that inequality as well. Applying the cyclic
bound~(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_finite_range_bound}) to the
padded family gives
\begin{align}
    \delta&=\frac{m\gamma-(R-1)^2}{m-R+1},
    \label{eq:knabe_open_delta}\\
    (H_N^{\mathrm{open}})^2
        &\geq\delta H_N^{\mathrm{open}}.
    \label{eq:knabe_open_gap}
\end{align}
If $m\gamma>(R-1)^2$, then $\delta>0$, and
$\delta\|v\|\leq\|H_N^{\mathrm{open}}v\|$ for every
$v\perp\ker H_N^{\mathrm{open}}$ and every $N\geq R$.
Thus finitely many open volumes give one lower bound at all physical
volumes. The hypothesis includes all $R\leq W\leq m+R-1$; the gap
of the single largest window alone is not the hypothesis of this open criterion.

\section{Compact continuous families}

The finite-size criterion also gives an open-chain version of the
finite-window compactness argument in Appendix~A of
Ref.~\cite{Schuch2011Phases}. Suppose a continuous family of block tensors
has one common positive simultaneous injectivity length $S$, and fix
$R\geq S+1$. At each parameter $x_0$, the pointwise open-chain gap theorem
supplies $\gamma_{x_0}>0$, uniformly in the physical volume.
Choose $m\geq R$ with $m\gamma_{x_0}/2>(R-1)^2$.
The ground projections and open Hamiltonians on the finitely many volumes
$R\leq W\leq m+R-1$ are continuous. Their gaps therefore remain at least
$\gamma_{x_0}/2$ in one neighborhood of $x_0$.
The coefficient~(\ref{eq:knabe_open_delta}) gives one positive all-volume gap throughout
that neighborhood. A finite subcover gives a positive common gap on every
compact parameter set. Continuous positive interactions with the same local
kernels inherit this gap through a common positive lower comparison constant.

This argument applies at the sufficient range $R\geq S+1$. It does not
assert a gap for every shorter interaction range. The genuine short-range
counterexample to that unrestricted claim is recorded in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/cpgsv21_short_range_parent_gap.tex}.

\section{Verdict}

The nearest-neighbor threshold
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_knabe_threshold}) is attributed to
Ref.~\cite{Knabe1988Energy}. The arbitrary finite-range coefficient
(\ref{eq:knabe88_finite_range_coefficient_finite_range_bound}) is a TNLean
derivation, formalized by
\leanid{ProjectionGeometry.quadraticForm_sum_projections_of_cyclic_knabe}.
It is not attributed to Knabe, to
Theorem~2.1(i) of Nachtergaele's martingale argument
\cite{Nachtergaele1996Spectral}, or to the two MPS reviews
\cite{PerezGarcia2007Matrix,Cirac2021Matrix}.

The open-chain coefficient~(\ref{eq:knabe_open_delta}) is a further consequence
of that cyclic inequality, obtained by zero padding. It is formalized by
\leanid{MPSTensor.openParentHamiltonianES_gap_of_finite_open_gaps}, with the
padding and clipped-window identities proved separately. This open criterion
requires gaps on finitely many open volumes, including the clipped boundary
volumes. It is not attributed to Knabe's printed periodic theorem.

The coefficient clarification is resolved by these derived cyclic and open
criteria. For compact continuous families at a sufficient interaction range,
the finite-window continuity argument supplies the needed local gaps and
gives a common positive open-chain gap. The source's unrestricted shorter-range
assertion is a separate false-source issue, not an unresolved coefficient
calculation.
