\section{The source lemma}

Fix an $L\times L$ square lattice $\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^2$ and its dyadically
padded grid $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^2$. Lemma~8.2 of the September~24, 2026
polynomial-PEPS manuscript~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}
(\path{07-assembly.tex}, lines~122--127) states that the virtual links of the
party tensor network can be routed along nearest-neighbor paths on the padded
grid $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^2$ with constant edge congestion, and that folding the
padded grid back into $\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^2$ by reflection preserves constant
congestion and leaves the physical output coordinates unchanged.

In the paper, Lemma~8.2 is stated without explicit hypotheses because it refers
to the specific party network produced by the distribution protocol of
Proposition~7.1 (\path{06-geometry.tex}, lines~12--53), whose parties are
placed at the anchor vertices of the dyadic blocks (\path{07-assembly.tex},
lines~103--114). The proof of Lemma~8.2 (lines~128--162) uses four properties
of that network:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every block anchor lies on the padded grid $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^2$
  (lines~113--114);
\item each dyadic block has at most $B$ attached parties (lines~113--114, from
  item~1 of Proposition~7.1);
\item each party is incident to at most $\Delta$ virtual links
  (lines~140--141, from item~2 of Proposition~7.1);
\item for every virtual link, the $\ell_\infty$-distance between the anchors of
  its two endpoint parties is at most $c$ times either endpoint's dyadic scale
  (lines~130--132, from item~3 of Proposition~7.1).
\end{enumerate}
Proposition~7.1 also guarantees that the two endpoint scales of every link are
equal or adjacent, though the congestion bound does not need that property.

\section{What is proved and the extra hypotheses}

Because Proposition~7.1 is not yet formalized, the specific party network of
the protocol is not yet constructed in Lean. Instead, Lemma~8.2 is proved for
an arbitrary party network whose parties are assigned to dyadic blocks and satisfy
properties 1--4 as explicit hypotheses. Under those four hypotheses, the edge
congestion on the padded grid is at most $2(2c+1)B\Delta$, the edge congestion
after folding onto $\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^2$ is at most $8(2c+1)B\Delta$, and if
every virtual link has dimension at most $D$, the folded network forms a
projected entangled pair state (PEPS) on the open $L\times L$ square grid with
bond dimension at most
$\max(D,1)^{8(2c+1)B\Delta}$.\footnote{The padded congestion bounds are
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.DyadicPlacement.load_horizontal_le} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.DyadicPlacement.load_vertical_le}; the
folded congestion bound is
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.foldedRouting_card_traversal_le}; the PEPS
construction is
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.exists_squarePEPS_of_dyadicRouting}.}

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

The routing argument itself is complete, and its congestion constants depend
only on $B$, $\Delta$, and $c$, as in the paper. What remains is to formalize
Proposition~7.1 and the placement of its parties at the dyadic block anchors.
Property~1 will then follow from the anchor placement (\path{07-assembly.tex},
lines~113--114), and properties 2--4 will follow from items 1--3 of
Proposition~7.1 with constants $B$, $\Delta$, and $c$ independent of $L$ and the
Hamiltonian.
