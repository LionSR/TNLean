\section{The source theorem}

Theorem~5.2 of the September~24, 2026 polynomial-PEPS manuscript compresses the
output density operator of a distributed quantum circuit into an operator
tensor network with one node per
party~\cite[Theorem~5.2]{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}.\footnote{The cited source
is the manuscript at
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}{\texttt{openai/math@adc7f1241b42}},
\emph{Polynomial PEPS approximation of gapped square-grid ground states},
the preliminaries and the section on distributed density
compression~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}.}
The printed statement of Theorem~5.2 does not explicitly state that the finite
set of parties is nonempty.

In the degenerate case of an empty party set, the theorem fails as printed. The
preliminaries specify that an empty tensor product has dimension
one~\cite[preliminaries]{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS} (\texttt{01-preliminaries.tex},
lines 5--7). A circuit on zero parties can still apply a scalar contraction
$c\in\mathbb C$ with $|c|\le 1$ to $\mathbb C$. Starting from the empty product
vector $1\in\mathbb C$, the resulting reduced density operator on $\mathbb C$
is $|c|^2$, whereas an operator tensor network with zero nodes and zero links
evaluates to the empty product $1$. For $c=1/2$, the two operators differ by
$|1 - 1/4| = 3/4$ and cannot be made arbitrarily close.

\section{The formal convention}

The formalization builds the nonemptiness of the party set into the definition
of a distributed construction. All other features match the paper: each party
holds a finite-dimensional memory, the initial state is a tensor product of
normalized party vectors, and the circuit retains the private output registers
until the final partial trace~\cite[Theorem~5.2]{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}
(\texttt{04-compression.tex}, lines 17--30 and 137--151). No upper bound is
imposed on the private register dimensions.

In the proof of Theorem~5.2, the tensor-network construction places local
tensors at the parties and absorbs scalar coefficients and averaging weights
into them~\cite[proof of Theorem~5.2]{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}
(lines 565--578). Having at least one party guarantees a node that can absorb
these scalars, including any scalar factor produced by a gate with no
participating parties. The circuit definition itself remains unrestricted and
allows zero-party gates.

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

Because the nonemptiness condition is part of the definition of a distributed
construction, it is not repeated as a separate hypothesis on downstream
theorems. Every application in the paper acts on a square grid of side length
$L\ge 2$, where the party set is nonempty, so the convention does not affect
any downstream result. Nothing remains open.
