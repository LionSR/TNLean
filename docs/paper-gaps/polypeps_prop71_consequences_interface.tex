\section{The source proposition}

Proposition~7.1 of the polynomial PEPS approximation manuscript of
September~24, 2026~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS} (\path{06-geometry.tex},
lines~12--53, at revision \texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a})
asserts that for all sufficiently large grid side lengths $L$, with constants
uniform in the Hamiltonian and in $L$, there exists a protocol that distributes
the ground vector $\Omega$ through the padded dyadic hierarchy. Its four items
describe:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the parties and their product initialization;
\item the division of the chronology into changes, their gates, and the bounded
  lifetime participation of each party;
\item the dyadic scales and grid addresses of the parties acted on by each
  gate;
\item the sequence of intermediate reference vectors, ending at a tensor
  product $\Omega \otimes \Xi$ with an auxiliary vector $\Xi$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The formal statement}

Proposition~7.1 is not yet proved in the formal development. Instead, the
formal statement defines the subset of properties of Proposition~7.1 that the
PEPS assembly of Section~8 uses. A protocol is formalized as a distributed
construction in the sense of Theorem~5.2 of Ref.~\cite{OpenAI2026PolynomialPEPS}
whose chronology is divided into $n$ changes, with each party attached to a
square of the padded dyadic hierarchy and with a reference vector $r_t$ at each
stage $0 \le t \le n$, starting at the initial product vector $r_0$. Let
$C > 0$ be a scale coefficient and let $b, k, r \in \mathbb N$ be fixed degree
and exponent bounds. The retained properties are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item there are at most $C L^2$ parties in total, and at most $b$ parties are
  attached to any square of the padded hierarchy;
\item there are at most $C L^3$ changes, each change contains at most $b$
  nonprivate gates acting on at most $b$ parties each, and each party
  participates in at most $b$ changes over its lifetime;
\item each gate is a sum of at most $C L^k$ monomials with absolute coefficient
  sum at most $C L^k$, at most $r$ pair effects and at most $r$ pair sources per
  monomial;
\item any two parties acted on by a gate have equal or adjacent dyadic scales
  and grid addresses separated by at most $b$ times either endpoint scale;
\item if $W_t$ denotes the contraction of stage $t$, the reference vectors
  $r_t$ and terminal auxiliary vector $\Xi$ satisfy
  \begin{align}
      \lVert W_t r_t - r_{t+1}\rVert
          &\le C L^{-20},
      \label{eq:prop71_step_error}\\
      r_n
          &= \Omega \otimes \Xi,
      \label{eq:prop71_terminal_factorization}\\
      1 - C L^{-57}
          &\le \lVert \Xi\rVert \le 1.
      \label{eq:prop71_junk_norm}
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
From these properties and (\ref{eq:prop71_step_error})--(\ref{eq:prop71_junk_norm}),
the formal development proves the gate-density estimate of Section~8.1, the
link-separation hypothesis of Lemma~8.2, and the lifetime bound of at most
$64 G$ gates per block party when each change has at most $G$
gates.\footnote{The protocol predicate is
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.DyadicProtocol.IsDistributionProtocol};
    the large-size existence hypothesis is
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.LargeSizeDyadicProtocolConsequences}.}

\section{Omitted clauses}

Four clauses of Proposition~7.1 are omitted from the formal predicate:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every square of the hierarchy, including the root, carries at least one
  party (\path{06-geometry.tex}, lines~64--67). The formal placement bounds the
  number of parties per square from above only.
\item One party holds the main copy of $\Omega$, and every auxiliary copy is
  initialized privately at its designated party (\path{06-geometry.tex},
  lines~22--23). The formal initialization is an arbitrary tensor product of
  normalized party vectors.
\item Each reference vector $r_t$ is formed from the main encoded frame and the
  auxiliary encoded frames, and all its factors have norm at most one
  (\path{06-geometry.tex}, lines~39--41). The step error
  (\ref{eq:prop71_step_error}) and endpoint relations
  (\ref{eq:prop71_terminal_factorization})--(\ref{eq:prop71_junk_norm}) alone
  do not imply that the intermediate reference vectors $r_t$ have norm at most
  one.
\item Each additional party takes the dyadic scale and grid address of its
  repainting event (\path{06-geometry.tex}, lines~36--37).
\end{enumerate}
None of these four clauses is needed for the Section~8 assembly: the gate-density
bound, the routing separation hypothesis, and the lifetime gate count depend
only on the retained bounds, while the message and replacement-slot bounds used
in compression follow from the message expansion and the nonprivate gate count
and arity. The formal statement is therefore a weaker consequence of
Proposition~7.1 sufficient for Section~8, while Proposition~7.1 itself remains
unproved.

\section{Clauses carried by the formal statement}

Two further clauses of Proposition~7.1 hold for the formal predicate.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Item~3 of Theorem~5.2 (\path{04-compression.tex}, lines~143--147) bounds
  the pair sources, the pair effects and the polynomial-dimensional messages of
  each monomial by one constant $r$. The pair-source bound is a field of the
  formal predicate,\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.Approximation.DyadicProtocol.IsDistributionProtocol.sourceBounded}.}
  with the constant $r$ of the pair effects. The recorded
  monomials of the formal gates contain no messages: messages are expanded
  before a gate is recorded (\path{04-compression.tex}, lines~42--45), and the
  formal monomials are built from local contractions, pair sources and pair
  effects only.
\item Item~2 of Proposition~7.1 provides expansions accurate to any fixed inverse power of $L$ in
  operator norm where an approximation is needed (\path{06-geometry.tex},
  lines~29--30), with constants that may depend on the accuracy exponent
  (\path{04-compression.tex}, line~17). The gates $W_t$ of the formal protocol
  are the selected contractions, as in the instantiation of Section~8
  (\path{07-assembly.tex}, lines~29--34), and each formal nonprivate gate $G$
  equals its recorded expansion,\footnote{By the definition of
    \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.PairEffect.OriginalCircuit.eval} on a nonprivate gate,
    which evaluates the sum of its recorded monomials.}
  $G = \sum_{\xi} c_{\xi} M_{\xi}$, so
  $\lVert G - \sum_{\xi} c_{\xi} M_{\xi}\rVert = 0 \le L^{-a}$ for every
  $a$. These expansions are therefore exact at every accuracy,
  with bounds independent of the accuracy exponent. The selected gates are
  contractions acting on their participating parties, with contractive
  constituent maps, which are the only conditions the source places on the
  gates of a change (\path{06-geometry.tex}, lines~25--27;
  \path{04-compression.tex}, lines~28 and~143--147). The source asserts no
  smaller reference error and no separate exact gate: the reference error of
  each change is as in (\ref{eq:prop71_step_error}) (\path{06-geometry.tex},
  lines~41--42 and~580--583).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Elimination}

Formalize the full statement of Proposition~7.1 including the map from
hierarchy squares to parties, the registers of the main and auxiliary copies of
$\Omega$, the frame factorization and unit-norm bounds of the intermediate
reference vectors $r_t$, and the assignment of repainting scales and addresses. Proving Proposition~7.1 in that stronger form then implies the
present existence hypothesis by projection onto the retained fields.
