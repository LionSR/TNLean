\section{The source definitions}
Garre-Rubio, Lootens and Moln\'ar define a matrix product operator
algebra through the closedness of the arbitrary-boundary operators
$\mathcal A_T^n=\{O^n_{T,B}: B\in\mathcal M_\chi\}$ under multiplication,
and a symmetric matrix product state through the invariance
$\mathcal A_T\cdot\mathcal S_A\subset\mathcal S_A$ of the arbitrary-boundary
subspace $\mathcal S_A^n=\{\lvert\psi^n_{A,X}\rangle: X\in\mathcal M_D\}$ for every
system size~$n$~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym}.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/2203.12563/REsubmission.tex}, lines 311--345 (the subspace
    $\mathcal S_A^n$ and the closedness condition) and lines 431--434
    (the definition of a symmetric state).}
The periodic operators $O_a=O_{T_a,\mathbb 1}$ of the blocks $a$ then
satisfy
\begin{align}
    O_aO_b & =\sum_c N_{ab}^c O_c
    \label{eq:glm23_fusion}
\end{align}
for every system size, with $N_{ab}^c\in\mathbb N$, and the action of a block
$a$ on a block $x$ of the state decomposes into blocks $y$ with multiplicities
$M_{a,x}^y\in\mathbb N$ through the action tensors. Associativity of the action
gives
\begin{align}
    \sum_c N_{ab}^c M_{c,x}^y & =\sum_z M_{b,x}^z M_{a,z}^y .
    \label{eq:glm23_nimrep}
\end{align}
\footnote{Source traceability: line 361 for
    (\ref{eq:glm23_fusion}); lines 458--460 for the multiplicities; lines
    491--492 for associativity; lines 564--565 for
    (\ref{eq:glm23_nimrep}); line 683 for the group case
    $M_{gh,x}^y=\sum_z M_{g,z}^y M_{h,x}^z$.}
Evaluating the invariance with periodic boundaries, the source obtains
\begin{align}
    O_a\lvert\psi_{A_x}\rangle & =\sum_y M_{a,x}^y\lvert\psi_{A_y}\rangle
    \label{eq:glm23_periodic_symmetry}
\end{align}
for general fusion rules, and recalls this form when it constructs boundary
states.\footnote{Source traceability: lines 567--568 and line 1801.}
For groups the source also formulates a weaker, periodic-boundary version
of both conditions, in which the boundaries are proportional to the identity
in each block.\footnote{Source traceability: lines 994--1000 and 1062--1064.}

\section{What is formalized}

The formalization has several layers. They have different hypotheses and
must not be conflated with the arbitrary-boundary existence theorem.

\paragraph{Length-independent arbitrary-boundary transport.}
The closedness and compatibility predicates have the order
$\forall X,Y\,\exists Z\,\forall N\geq1$; one output boundary works for
all lengths. Stacking sends $(X,Y)$ to $X\otimes Y$.
Choosing output boundaries on the matrix-unit basis and extending linearly
proves that each predicate is equivalent to one linear boundary map on
the entire stacked matrix space, independent of the positive length.
This proves the first assertion of Appendix A without assuming linearity
of an arbitrary choice of output boundary.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.IsBoundaryClosed},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.IsBoundaryCompatible},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.isBoundaryClosed_iff_exists_linearMap}, and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.isBoundaryCompatible_iff_exists_linearMap}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/BoundaryClosedness}.}
For supplied exact decompositions
$B^i=\sum_cW_cA_c^iV_c$ with $V_cW_b=\delta_{cb}1$, the transported
boundaries are $V_cXW_c$ at every positive length. The empty-word identity
additionally requires $\sum_cW_cV_c=1$ on the ambient bond space.
These forward formulas do not construct the decomposition from
closedness.\footnote{\leanid{MPSTensor.IsBiorthogonalDecomposition.mpvWithBoundary}
    and \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBiorthogonalDecomposition.evalWord_of_complete}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/BoundaryTransport}.}

\paragraph{Periodic fusion and action.}
The periodic fusion hypothesis is (\ref{eq:glm23_fusion}) at every positive
length. The periodic symmetry hypothesis is
(\ref{eq:glm23_periodic_symmetry}), initially with complex coefficients
independent of length. For normal state blocks whose periodic vectors are
linearly independent at one positive length, the formalization proves that
these coefficients are nonnegative integers and satisfy
(\ref{eq:glm23_nimrep}). The source obtains integrality instead from its
exact action tensors. Its standing assumption that distinct blocks become
orthogonal in the thermodynamic limit supplies eventual linear
independence; it is not to be replaced by a new unrelated independence
assumption in the source theorem.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPOTensor.IsMPOFusionAlgebra},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.IsMPOSymmetricFamily},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.IsMPOSymmetric}, and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_isNIMRep_of_isMPOSymmetricFamily}.
    The integer-coefficient action of an invertible label permutes the blocks,
    as expressed by
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_equiv_mpo_mulVec_eq_of_isInvertibleLabel}.}
The predicate for a single symmetric block does not itself require that
all its action coefficients are one; that extra clause is essential to
the source's single-block obstruction at line 610.

\paragraph{Fusion and action reductions with multiplicity.}
The periodic identities produce mutually biorthogonal fusion and action
reductions, one pair for every copy of each output channel. They reconstruct
the stacked tensor only up to a nilpotent remainder.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_fusionTensors_of_mpo_mul_eq_sum} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_actionTensors_of_mpo_mulVec_eq_sum}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/FusionTensors}.}
This is not the source's exact letterwise decomposition from
arbitrary-boundary closedness and invariance. For a single normal target,
two reductions are proportional after multiplication by sufficiently long
words. For several targets, periodic data alone does not force a pure
change of basis in the multiplicity spaces: upper-triangular changes can
mix the chosen maps with residual directions.

When an exact biorthogonal zipper decomposition is supplied, uniqueness up
to an invertible matrix on each multiplicity space is proved.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_unique_zipperFusionGauge} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_unique_zipperActionGauge}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/ZipperUniqueness}.}
Thus multiplicity-space uniqueness is available in the exact setting; it
is not an assertion about unrestricted periodic reductions.

\paragraph{Multiplicity F-symbols for complete zipper families.}
Complete zipper fusion families carry independently specified synthesis
and analysis maps, their biorthogonality and exact reconstruction
identities, and a simultaneous inverse separating the labelled block
letters. Their support projector need not be the identity on the full
product bond space. For such a family the formalization constructs the
invertible F-matrix, proves the F-move and pentagon, and proves covariance
under the full multiplicity gauge.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.CompleteZipperFusionFamily},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.CompleteZipperFusionFamily.printedFMatrix},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.CompleteZipperFusionFamily.rightTripleSynthesis_mul_printedFMatrix},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.CompleteZipperFusionFamily.inversePrintedFMatrix_pentagon}, and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.CompleteZipperFusionFamily.printedFMatrix_regauge}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/CompleteZipperFusionDefs},
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/CompleteZipperFusion},
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/CompleteZipperFusionPentagon}, and
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/CompleteZipperFusionGauge}.}
The printed F-matrix and its inverse have opposite orientations; the
pentagon must use the stated orientation. Constructions from a compression
with zero remainder and from a star-closed word algebra are also available.
Neither construction proves that the arbitrary-boundary hypotheses of
Ref.~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym} produce the required exact data.
Star closure is not a standing hypothesis of the source's Sections 2--3.

\paragraph{Group cocycles, L-symbols, and the group obstruction.}
For a normal periodic group representation, the two fusion trees define a
nonzero boundary-dressed associator scalar. It is a three-cocycle; changing
the fusion tensors changes it by a coboundary. Its class is expressed in
Mathlib's third group cohomology.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.FusionData.omega},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.FusionData.isCocycle_omega},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.FusionData.omega_eq_fusionGauge}, and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.IsNormalRepresentation.anomalyClass}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/Associator} and
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/AssociatorCohomology}.}
For an action permuting normal state blocks, the fusion and action
reductions similarly construct scalar L-symbols and prove their coupled
pentagon with that associator. Restriction to a fixed block trivializes
the anomaly on its stabilizer. In particular, a nontrivial group anomaly
excludes an invariant normal single-block state with action multiplicity
one. This group cohomological obstruction is already formalized; it must
not be described as absent.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.BlockActionData.lSymbol},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.BlockActionData.isCompatible_lSymbol},
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.IsNormalRepresentation.isTrivialGaugeClass_comap_omega_of_fixed},
    and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.IsNormalRepresentation.not_exists_invariant_of_not_isTrivialGaugeClass},
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/PermutedBlocks} and
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/AnomalyObstruction}.}
The scalar gauge operation and its preservation of compatibility are
proved. Deriving the full L-symbol gauge law from every change of
fusion and action tensors remains distinct from these scalar identities.

For a fixed transitive group action, the scalar reconstruction formula and
the torsor of action-gauge solution classes under
$H^2(H,\mathbb C^\times)$ are proved. The formula is first used in a fusion
gauge in which the anomaly is identically one on the stabilizer and then
transported back; the required gauge exists whenever the restricted class
is trivial. The correction is recorded in
\cite{gap:glm23_eq20_fusion_gauge}. This is an algebraic classification of
scalar solutions with the fusion gauge held fixed, not the physical
Hamiltonian-path classification.

The source's lemma that exact closed fusion and $U_e=\mathbb 1$ force an
injective group representation to be on-site, with trivial anomaly, is
also proved from the exact fusion identities. The equivalence of those
identities with the original arbitrary-boundary closedness still needs
its own construction.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.isOnSite_of_closed_fusion} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.anomalyClass_eq_zero_of_closed_fusion}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/ClosedFusion}.}

\paragraph{Other periodic consequences.}
The fusion-ring obstruction to a single normal symmetric block is also
proved: its eigenvalues define a nonnegative integer fusion character.
This excludes Fibonacci, but it is not the general F-symbol obstruction
of source lines 610--636. Separately, for a fusion ring and a unital
nonnegative integer representation, a strictly positive common eigenvector
produces the symmetric boundary state of source lines 1795--1805.
That construction requires neither commutativity of the fusion ring nor
indecomposability of the action.\footnote{
    \leanid{MPOTensor.not_isMPOSymmetric_of_forall_not_isFusionCharacter} and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.exists_pos_symmetric_boundary_state}, in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/Character} and
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/SymmetricBoundary}.}

\section{Remaining boundary}

The elementary arbitrary-boundary contractions themselves are already
available as \leanid{MPOTensor.mpoWithBoundary} and
\leanid{MPSTensor.groundSpaceMap}, with
\leanid{MPSTensor.groundSpace} their state range. Length-independent arbitrary-boundary closedness and compatibility are
now stated directly. The remaining implication is from these source
hypotheses to exact fusion and action decompositions. Merely closing the operators
separately at each length, with an output boundary that depends on the
length, would not be the source's condition.

In particular, the following claims remain open here:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The minimal two-sided matrix decomposition of the Appendix A
          boundary map, deriving the exact
          fusion and action tensors and their orthogonality from the source's
          closedness and invariance, without a star-closure or zero-remainder
          hypothesis.
    \item General L-symbols with fusion and action multiplicities, their
          invertible change-of-basis construction, full coupled pentagon and joint
          multiplicity gauge law, derived from those exact tensors.
    \item The source's general single-block, multiplicity-one obstruction
          that trivializes the F-symbols, beyond the already proved scalar group
          case and the different fusion-ring obstruction.
\end{enumerate}
These are tracked by issues \#7968 and \#8045. Once the constructions are
proved, the periodic predicates remain useful as explicitly weaker
hypotheses; they need not be renamed as the arbitrary-boundary ones.

The rest of the paper has further independent work: tensor-derived time
reversal constraints; the general physical phase classification of
Section 5, including non-fixed-point and varying-bond paths; and the
remaining exact constructions and completeness claims in Section 6.
Their status must not be inferred from the algebraic scalar classification.
The printed time-reversal L relation is subject to the resolved correction
in \path{docs/paper-gaps/glm23_time_reversal_l_symbol.tex}.

\section{Elimination plan}

Starting from the proved linear boundary map, derive its minimal
two-sided matrix decomposition and the exact fusion decomposition of
Appendix A, using the source's simultaneous block injectivity. Apply
the same finite-dimensional argument to the state action. Derive the
existing periodic fusion and symmetry formulas by taking identity
boundaries. Instantiate the existing complete zipper F theory from the
constructed exact fusion data; do not add its conclusions as hypotheses.
Then construct multiplicity L-symbols from the two action-tree bases,
prove their coupled pentagon and gauges, and derive the general
multiplicity-one obstruction. Keep the group and conditional F results
above as reusable proved ingredients while these source-level implications
remain open.
