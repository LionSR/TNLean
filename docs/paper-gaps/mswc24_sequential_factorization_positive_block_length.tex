\section{What the source states}
Let $A$ be a tensor of physical dimension $d$ and bond dimension $D$, and let
$\mathcal B_q\colon\C^D\otimes\C^D\to(\C^d)^{\otimes q}$ be its map blocked
over $q$ sites, with polar decomposition $\mathcal B_q=VP$. Equations~(13)--(15)
of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation} write the isometry as an ordered product of $q$
isometries acting on one site and a bond each,
\begin{align}
    V
     & =V_q\cdots V_1,
    \label{eq:mswc24_sequential_product}
\end{align}
where $V_k\colon\C^{D'_k}\to\C^{D'_{k+1}}\otimes\C^d$, $D'_1=D^2$,
$D'_{q+1}=1$ and $D'_k\leq D^2$. The source states no lower bound on the block
length $q$; the construction factors the blocked map site by site and blocks
at least one site.

\section{The empty block}
For $q=0$ the blocked map has a single matrix, the empty product
$\Id_D$, and the output space $(\C^d)^{\otimes0}$ is $\C$. The one matrix
$\Id_D$ spans the full matrix algebra only when $D\leq1$. When $D=1$,
$V=1$ and (\ref{eq:mswc24_sequential_product}) is the empty product with
$D'_1=D'_{q+1}=1$, so the statement holds trivially. When $D=0$ the space
$\C^D\otimes\C^D$ is zero, and the requirement $D'_1=D^2=0$ contradicts
$D'_{q+1}=1$ at $q=0$, where these are the same bond. The empty block
carries no content of the preparation scheme, which blocks at least one site.

\section{The restriction adopted}
The sequential factorization is stated for block lengths $q\geq1$ and injective
$\mathcal B_q$.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isometric_chain_polarIsoMatrix} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/SequentialFactorization}.}
The same bound $q\geq1$ is assumed by the variant for a blocked map injective on
a set $S$ of bond pairs, whose isometric factor is factored on the inputs of
$S$, with last bond $D'_1=\lvert S\rvert$.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isometric_chain_polarIsoMatrix_of_isInjectiveOn}
    in the same module.}
At $q=0$ the map $\Id_D$ vanishes outside $S$ and spans the matrix units in $S$
only when $D=0$ and $S$ is empty, or $D=1$ and $S$ is the one pair; the
conclusion requires $\lvert S\rvert=1$ and fails in the first case.
The bound $q\geq1$ is also assumed by the variant with no injectivity, which
factors the partial isometry $V$ composed with a unitary $T$ on $\C^{D^2}$ whose
first $r$ columns span the range of $V^\dagger V$, with last bond
$D'_1=r$.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isometric_chain_polarIsoMatrix_mul_unitary}
    in the same module.}
At $q=0$ this conclusion fails for every $D$. When $D=0$, $r\leq D^2=0$
contradicts $D'_1=D'_{q+1}=1$. When $D\geq1$, $V^\dagger V$ is the projector
onto the line of $\Id_D$, so $r=1$, and the conclusion asks that the scalar
$VT\ket0$ equal the empty product $1$. This scalar has modulus one, and
multiplying the first column of $T$ by $-1$ keeps the hypotheses and changes its
sign, so the conclusion fails for one of the two unitaries.
For every $q\geq1$ the conclusion is exactly
(\ref{eq:mswc24_sequential_product}) in matrix product form, with no further
hypothesis. Elimination for the two injective variants: replace $q\geq1$ by a
positive bond dimension $D\geq1$, which the source takes for granted, and treat
the case $q=0$, $D=1$ directly as the empty product. This does not cover the
variant with no injectivity, whose conclusion fails at $q=0$ for every $D\geq1$.
Its restriction can be removed only by changing the statement: either let the
boundary carry a phase, concluding
$\bra{\sigma}VT\ket x=c\,(Q_0(\sigma_0)\cdots Q_{q-1}(\sigma_{q-1}))_{0x}$
for a scalar $c$ of modulus one, or keep $q\geq1$ and state the empty block
separately as the scalar $VT\ket0$ of modulus one. Since the source blocks at
least one site, the second option is the planned one.
