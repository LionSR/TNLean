\section{What the source states}
The paragraph ``Inhomogeneous short-range correlated MPS''
of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation} considers a sequence of matrix product states
$\ket{\phi_N}$ ``with bond dimension at most $D$'' that are not translation
invariant. The sequence has finite correlation length if, after blocking
$q=O(\log N)$ sites, the state $\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}$ of the positive parts
$P_i$ of the polar decompositions of the blocked tensors (eq.~(18)) is
approximated by a product of nearest-neighbour pairs
$\ket\Omega=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N/q}\ket{\omega^i}_{R_iL_{i+1}}$ with
$\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})\to0$. The paragraph then asserts that
preparing $\ket\Omega$ and applying the isometries of the polar decompositions
gives depth $O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$ with error
$\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$.

\section{The restriction adopted}
The formal statements\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_isometricExtension}
    and \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_of_isPairApproximable}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/PartialIsometryPreparation}.}
work on a ring of $N$ sites cut into blocks of lengths $\ell_k$, with bond
dimensions $D_0,\dots,D_{N-1}\le D$ varying along the ring as in the source,
and blocked tensors that need not be injective. They assume that every block
has at least $3D$ sites, which the circuit needs to hold the two bond indices of
a block in registers of $D$ sites each and to route them together. The first
statement, with error exactly $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$, also
assumes $D^2\le d^{3D}$, which holds for every $D$ when $d\ge2$. For blocks of
$q\ge3D\ge1$ sites it follows from the source's requirement $d^q\ge D^2$
recalled below, since $d^q\ge1$ forces $d\ge1$ and for $d=1$ both conditions
read $D\le1$; it is therefore no further restriction. The second statement,
with error at most $\delta$ under the approximation hypothesis, holds for
every $d$.

The source has no bound $3D$, but it is not silent on the block length. Its
polar decompositions need ``$q$ large enough that $d^q\geq D^2$'' (footnote to
the paragraph ``Approximation through the fixed-point state''), and its circuit
holds the legs $R_i$ and $L_{i+1}$, of dimension $D$ each, on the sites of the
blocks, with the central sites in $\ket0$ (eq.~(10) and the paragraph
``Preparing the approximate state''). A block must therefore hold two registers
of dimension at least $D$, that is, at least $2\lceil\log_dD\rceil$ sites. The
bound $3D$ is a coarser sufficient condition: each register uses $D$ sites, as
$D\le d^D$, and a third stretch of $D$ sites leaves room for routing the left
register next to the right one. For $q=O(\log N)$ growing with $N$ both bounds
hold for large $N$.

The finite-correlation assumption is stated for one ring: some unit pairs
$\omega^k$ on $\C^{D_j}\otimes\C^{D_j}$, for the bond $j$ joining block $k$ to
block $k+1$, have $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})\le\delta$. The
formal statements prove depth at most $CL$ for blocks of lengths at most $L$,
with $C$ depending only on $d$ and $D$. The source's total depth
$O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$ is the choice $L=O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$, which is not
made here; the source's definition fixes blocks of length $q=O(\log N)$.

\section{Partial isometries}
Without injectivity the isometric factor $V_k$ of the polar decomposition
$B_k=V_kP_k$ is a partial isometry, $V_k^\dagger V_k=\Pi_k$ with $\Pi_k$ the
projector onto the range of $P_k$, as in the Supplemental Material, ``Proof of
Lemma~1 and extension to non-normal tensors'' of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation}; the
footnote to the paragraph ``The sequential-RG circuit'' states that the
derivation of the circuit ``remains valid also for non-injective tensors $B$'',
with $P^{-1}$ read as the pseudo-inverse. A unitary circuit cannot prepare
$(\bigotimes_kV_k)\ket\Omega$ itself, whose norm is that of
$(\bigotimes_k\Pi_k)\ket\Omega$ and may be smaller than $1$. The formal
statements prepare instead $(\bigotimes_kW_k)\ket\Omega$ for isometries $W_k$
with $W_k\Pi_k=V_k$, isometric extensions of $V_k$. They agree with $V_k$ on the
range of $\Pi_k$, and $W_kP_k=V_kP_k=B_k$, so
$\ket{\phi_N}=(\bigotimes_kW_k)\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}$ and the overlap of
$(\bigotimes_kW_k)\ket\Omega$ with the normalized $\ket{\phi_N}$ is that of
$\ket\Omega$ with the normalized $\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}$. The error is
therefore exactly $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$, the error the
source states. The block unitary implementing $W_k$ applies, after routing the
two input registers together, a unitary $T_k^\dagger$ on them whose
conjugate $T_k$ has its first columns spanning the range of $\Pi_k$, and then
the staircase of the sequential factorization of $V_kT_k$ on those columns.
This is a choice of the prepared state, not a restriction: the source names the
isometry $V_k$ and its circuit, and $W_k$ is the isometry that this circuit
implements.

When every blocked tensor is injective in the sense of the next section,
$\Pi_k$ is the coordinate projector onto the rectangle of the bonds of block $k$,
$\ket\Omega$ lies in the range of $\bigotimes_k\Pi_k$, and
$(\bigotimes_kW_k)\ket\Omega=(\bigotimes_kV_k)\ket\Omega$; the corresponding
statements\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousPreparation}, and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_chainBlockIsometryState_of_isInjectiveOn}
    and \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_chainBlockIsometryState}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/DepthUpperBound}.}
prepare $(\bigotimes_kV_k)\ket\Omega$ itself and keep the injectivity
hypothesis, together with the block length $3D$, for that purpose.

\section{Varying bond dimensions by zero padding}
The rectangular matrices $A_k^i$ of size $D_k\times D_{k+1}$ are padded with
zeros to $D\times D$ matrices\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{VaryingBondChain.zeroPad} and
    \leanid{VaryingBondChain.coeff_zeroPad}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Chain/VaryingBondChain}.}.
The padded chain has the same state, which is proved. For a block of at least
one site, its blocked tensor $B_k$ vanishes outside the upper-left corner
$[0,D_{o_k})\times[0,D_{o_{k+1}})$, which is also proved, and its entries on
that corner are those of the rectangular blocked tensor. That last
identification is not formalized: the injectivity hypothesis of the statement
for injective blocked tensors is stated directly for $B_k$, as spanning every
matrix unit in the corner, and its reading as injectivity of the rectangular
blocked tensor is the observation just made. For a block of length zero the
two readings differ, since $B_k$ is then the identity matrix of size $D$; the
statement using the hypothesis assumes blocks of at least one site. Under the hypothesis, the
support projector of the polar decomposition $B_k=V_kP_k$ is the coordinate
projector $\Pi_k$ onto the corner, so $V_k$ is an isometry on it and vanishes
on the other inputs. The pairs $\omega^k$ are padded in the same way,
and $\ket\Omega$ is supported on the rectangles. The objects
$\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}$, $(\bigotimes_kV_k)\ket\Omega$ and
$\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$ of the formal statements are those of
the padded chain; they agree with the source's after the zero coordinates are
dropped. This is the reading adopted, not a restriction: the theorems are stated
for every chain with bond dimensions at most $D$.

\section{Nonzero state of the positive parts}
The error $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$ compares $\ket\Omega$ with
the normalized state $\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}/\lVert\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}\rVert$,
which presupposes $\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}\neq0$. The formal approximation
condition\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{VaryingBondChain.IsPairApproximable}.}
states $\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{pos}}}\neq0$ explicitly; without it the normalized
vector would be read as $0$ and the condition would hold for every
$\delta\ge1$. This is a local fix of the intended reading, not a restriction.

\section{Elimination}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Varying bond dimensions.} Done by zero padding, as described
          above; the earlier restriction to a common bond dimension $D$ is
          removed.
    \item \emph{Injectivity.} Done, as described in the section on partial
          isometries: the block unitary implements an isometric extension
          $W_k$ of the partial isometry $V_k$, and the error is exactly
          $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})$ without injectivity.
    \item \emph{Block length.} The bound $3D\le\ell_k$ is needed only by
          the circuit, not by the state. Registers of
          $\lceil\log_dD\rceil$ sites in place of $D$ sites would lower it to
          a multiple of $\log_dD$, the order of the source's own requirement
          $d^q\ge D^2$; this is not done. The upper bound $q=O(\log N)$ alone
          allows bounded block lengths, so the sequence-level statement reads
          the source's blocking as $q_N\ge c\log N$ for some $c>0$, a block
          length growing with $N$ as in the translation-invariant case. Then
          $q_N\ge3D$ once $N\ge N_0$, with $N_0$ depending only on $D$ and
          $c$; each of the finitely many rings with $N<N_0$ is prepared exactly
          by a circuit of depth bounded in terms of $N_0$ and $d$, and the
          largest of these depths is absorbed into the constant of the depth
          bound. This argument is formalized in
          \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_of_log_lower}
          in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousSequence}, including
          the one-site ring with depth zero. The positive lower-growth
          hypothesis remains explicit; it is not inferred from the upper bound.
    \item \emph{Sequence-level statement.} With unit pairs satisfying
          $\varepsilon(\Omega,\phi_{\mathrm{pos}})\le\delta_N$ and
          $\delta_N\to0$, together with the preceding lower-growth hypothesis,
          \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_sequence}
          gives prepared unit states on every positive ring length with depth
          at most $CL_N$ and error tending to zero. Thus $L_N=O(\log N)$ gives
          logarithmic depth. This removes the restriction to one ring under
          that hypothesis, but it does not give the depth $O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$: the
          source supplies no rate for $\delta_N$, and the choice of a block
          length $L$ with error at most $\varepsilon$ and $L=O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$
          needs one. In the translation-invariant case the rate is proved: the
          error after blocking $q$ sites of a ring of $N$ sites is
          $O((N/q)\,e^{-\gamma q/\xi})$ for every $\gamma<1/2$, for one fixed
          normal tensor, with constants depending on that tensor (\cite{Malz2024Preparation}, Lemma~1 and
          eq.~(17))\footnote{Formal declaration:
              \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_approximationError_le_mul}, which proves
              the faster rate $e^{-2\gamma q/\xi}$ for $\gamma<1$; it gives the
              homogeneous depth in
              \path{docs/paper-gaps/mswc24_depth_upper_bound_divisible_length.tex}.}.
          For the inhomogeneous sequence the tensors change with $N$, and the
          same choice of $L$ requires a bound
          $\delta(N,q)\le C\,N^{k}e^{-cq}$ with $C$, $c>0$ and $k$ independent of
          $N$. The source states no such uniform bound and does not derive one
          from $\delta_N\to0$. A formal statement with this uniform rate as a
          hypothesis would be a further scope-restricted theorem, not the
          source's; with $\delta_N\to0$ alone the sequence-level depth
          $O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$ remains open.

          A conditional variant is now formalized in
          \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_of_uniform_rate}
          in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousUniformRate}.
          It assumes a rate $KN^ke^{-rq}$, with $K,r>0$ and $k\ge0$
          independent of $N$, at every scale $1\le q\le N$ for some
          partition with $q\le\ell_j\le2q$.
          Choosing $q=\lceil (\max(k,1)/r)\log(N/\varepsilon)+b\rceil$
          gives the accuracy-dependent depth for every positive ring length.
          If $q>N$, the ring is prepared exactly in depth $O(N)$ by
          \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_normalizedChainState}
          in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousExactPreparation}:
          the whole ring is one block, and its normalized positive-part state
          is the input pair. Generic finite-dimensional synthesis is used
          only for the fixed finite set $N<3D$. This proves the stated
          conditional consequence; it does not derive the uniform rate from
          the source's definition.
\end{enumerate}


\section{A quantitative sufficient condition on actual blocks}
A separate structural result assumes a common faithful fixed state $\sigma$
of trace one and normalized Choi domination
$J(F_j)\ge(\eta/D)(\sigma\otimes I)$, $0\le\eta\le1$, for the
trace-preserving transfer maps $F_j$ of actual contiguous blocks of at most $s$ sites. It proves
\begin{align}
    \lVert B^\dagger B-\sigma^\mathsf{T}\otimes I\rVert
     & \le2D(1-\eta)^{\lfloor N/s\rfloor},\notag  \\
    \lVert P_B-(\sqrt\sigma)^\mathsf{T}\otimes I\rVert
     & \le K_\sigma(1-\eta)^{\lfloor N/s\rfloor},
\end{align}
for the whole blocked chain, with $K_\sigma$ independent of its tensors and
length. The declarations are
\leanid{MPSChainTensor.transferMap_blockTensor},
\leanid{MPSChainTensor.traceNorm_transferMap_blockTensor_sub_le_of_choi_domination},
and
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_norm_polarPos_chainBlockTensor_sub_le_of_choi_domination}
in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Chain/Transfer},
\doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousDoeblin}, and
\doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousPositivePartRate}.

This condition is imposed on ordered products over actual intervals, not on
powers of the individual site maps. Separate sitewise spectral gaps are
insufficient to justify that replacement. The result is restricted to a
common bond space and a common faithful fixed state. It gives a uniform
Gram and polar estimate under those additional structural hypotheses, not
the full source preparation theorem: varying rectangular bond spaces,
varying fixed states, and passage from qualitative convergence to a uniform
quantitative hypothesis remain separate requirements.

A second sufficient-condition result now starts with actual ordered transfer
products on each nonwrapping contiguous interval in the chosen ring cut:
\[
    \lVert T_{E_{A_i}\circ\cdots\circ E_{A_{i+\ell-1}}}
    -T_\sigma\rVert \le K e^{-r\ell},
\]
with $K,r>0$ independent of the ring length and interval, in the $L^2$
operator norm on the transfer matrices. The common limiting state is
faithful and has trace one. The declarations
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_norm_trace_prod_polarPos_sub_one_le} and
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_one_sub_norm_inner_chainPosState_le}
prove the ordered mixed-overlap and normalized pair estimates: transfer
error at most $\delta$ on every selected block and on the whole ring
gives normalized pair error at most $C_\sigma M\delta$. The constants
are chosen before the tensors, physical dimension, ring, and partition.
The whole-ring transfer controls the actual normalization
$c_N^2=\operatorname{Tr}(T_{B_{\mathrm{whole}}})$; the local Gram
estimates are not used as a substitute for that normalization.

Consequently
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_of_ordered_mixing}
in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousMixingPreparation}
derives pair error at most $C_\sigma KN e^{-rq}$ and physical depth
$O(\log(N/\varepsilon))$. It assumes nonzero periodic targets at every
positive ring length, as required to interpret the source's normalized
quantum states. Mixing alone guarantees nonvanishing only eventually;
it does not silently remove the finite short-ring condition. Exact
preparation covers scales longer than the ring. This closes the uniform
overlap and normalization step under the stated ordered mixing hypothesis.
It does not derive that hypothesis from qualitative finite correlation,
and it does not cover varying reference states or rectangular bond spaces.

The direct consumer
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_of_choi_domination}
in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/InhomogeneousDoeblinPreparation}
now connects the actual-site sufficient condition above to physical
preparation. With trace-preserving site maps fixing the same faithful
$\sigma$, and uniform Choi domination $0<\eta\le1$, inverse Gram
reshuffling yields ordered-transfer error $K(1-\eta)^{\ell}$ on each
actual interval. Weakening to $\eta/2$ retains the endpoint $\eta=1$
while giving the finite positive exponent $-\log(1-\eta/2)$.
Strict domination itself proves nonvanishing on every positive ring:
\leanid{MPSPreparation.le_norm_chainState_sq_of_choi_domination}
gives $\|\phi_N\|^2\ge\eta>0$ by splitting off the first site and
using nonnegativity of the supertrace of the completely positive residual.
Thus this concrete corollary needs no additional nonzero-target hypothesis.
The general ordered-mixing theorem retains that physical normalization
premise. This is a quantitative sufficient condition, not a replacement
of ordered mixing by separate sitewise spectral gaps.

\section{Short rings and undersized physical blocks}
The algebraic pair estimate at a small blocking scale does not assert an
isometry from $\mathbb C^{D^2}$ into an undersized physical space.
The physical compiler chooses $q\ge3D$ first. For $d\ge2$, this implies
$D^2\le d^q$, and it constructs support-preserving isometric extensions
$W_j\Pi_j=V_j$ even for rank-deficient Gram matrices. For $d\le1$ it
prepares the normalized target directly in depth zero. When the selected
$q>N$, exact preparation handles the whole ring; generic synthesis is
needed only for the fixed finite range $N<3D$.

For example, AKLT dimensions $d=3,D=2$ give $d<D^2$, so no full-input
one-site block isometry exists. The general ordered-mixing estimate
permits this; circuit synthesis uses longer blocks or the exact short-ring
branch. By contrast, strict faithful Choi domination at a microscopic
site forces Choi rank $D^2$, whereas its Kraus rank is at most $d$.
The direct actual-site Choi corollary therefore does not apply to unblocked
AKLT. Such domination may hold after a fixed blocking; it is not supplied
by separate sitewise eigenvalue gaps.

The usual AKLT transfer spectrum is $1,-1/3,-1/3,-1/3$, giving periodic
squared norm $1+3(-1/3)^N$. In particular the one-site periodic vector
vanishes. The all-positive-length normalized theorem correctly keeps its
explicit nonzero premise. The separate result
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_eventually_of_ordered_mixing}
removes it from an eventual-length conclusion. Whole-ring convergence
chooses a uniform $N_0$ with $\|\phi_N\|^2\ge1/2$ for $N\ge N_0$,
then proves physical preparation for the original family on that range.
It uses
\leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_of_uniform_rate_from};
the old all-positive-length rate interface is its $N_0=0$ specialization.
No finite short-ring replacement is made in this proof. This is an
application under uniform quantitative ordered mixing, not a derivation
of that assumption from qualitative finite correlation.

\section{Varying positive semidefinite references on a square bond space}
A separate sufficient-condition result permits density matrices
$\sigma_{N,i}$ that vary with the ring and site and may be singular, while
keeping the common square bond dimension $D$. It assumes the actual ordered
transfer matrix on every positive nonwrapping interval starting at $a$ is
within $K e^{-r\ell}$ of $X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma_{N,a}$,
with $K,r>0$ independent of all intervals and rings. There is no separate
transport or stationarity hypothesis: the global estimate is proved directly
from these quantitative tensor-product bounds.

The global square-root H\"older bound replaces a reference-dependent
Lipschitz constant by a dimension-dependent factor and halves the exponential
rate. Compactness of the set of density matrices makes the mixed-transfer
constants uniform, including at singular references. Since
$R_\sigma R_\tau=R_\sigma$ for trace-one references, ordered reference
products stay bounded. The positive reference tensor of block $j$ contributes
the pair with coefficient $(\sqrt{\sigma_j})_{r_{j-1},l_j}$, so the outgoing
pair from block $j$ uses $\sigma_{j+1}$. A two-block regression with distinct
rank-one references detects this shift.

The resulting normalized pair error is at most $C_D M\sqrt\delta$.
For exponential transfer error this gives
$C_D\sqrt K\,N e^{-rq/2}$ and logarithmic physical preparation of the
original family beyond a uniform ring cutoff. Whole-ring transfer
convergence derives eventual nonvanishing; no assumption is made on short
zero rings. The common faithful-reference result retains its sharper
exponent $r$.

These declarations\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_norm_trace_prod_polarPos_sub_one_le_varying_reference},
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_one_sub_norm_inner_chainPosState_le_varying_reference}, and
    \leanid{MPSPreparation.exists_isPreparedInDepth_inhomogeneous_le_log_eventually_of_varying_reference}.}
remove the common-reference and faithfulness restrictions from this
fixed-square-bond sufficient condition. They still do not derive a uniform
rate from the paper's qualitative finite-correlation definition. Rectangular
bonds require the corresponding corner-supported reset maps; zero-padded
rectangular site maps are not generally trace preserving on the full square
space, so the square-space hypotheses are not silently applied to them.
