\section{The source statement}

Landau, Vazirani and Vidick~\cite{Landau2015Polynomial} consider
$H=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}H_i$ on $n$ qudits of dimension $d$, with $H_i$ acting on
the sites $i,i+1$ and $0\le H_i\le 1$, ground state $\Gamma$ and gap
$\varepsilon$ (arXiv:1307.5143v1, \path{algo53.tex}, line~283). Quoting
Hastings~\cite{Hastings2007AreaLaw} and Arad, Kitaev, Landau and
Vazirani~\cite{Arad2013AreaLaw}, they state (lines~342--345) that for every
cut $(i,i+1)$ and every $\delta$ there is a constant
\begin{align}
    B_\delta=\exp\Bigl(O\bigl(\tfrac1\varepsilon\log^3d\,\log\tfrac1\delta\bigr)\Bigr)
    \label{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_source}
\end{align}
such that the normalized trimmed vector
$v=\operatorname{trim}^i_{B_\delta}\Gamma/
    \lVert\operatorname{trim}^i_{B_\delta}\Gamma\rVert$ satisfies
$\lvert\langle\Gamma,v\rangle\rvert\ge1-\delta$. No proof is given in the
source.

\section{What is formalized}

The formalized statement concerns a chain Hamiltonian $H=\sum_Xh_X$ on $L$
sites of dimension $q\ge1$, with Hermitian terms supported on sets of
diameter at most $R$ and $\lVert h_X\rVert\le J$, and a gapped ground state
$\Omega$ with gap $\Delta>0$. The source Hamiltonian is the case $R=1$,
$J=1$. Let $C$ be the bound on the entropy of an interval given by the
one-dimensional area law of the library, let $b$ be its cut budget constant,
which depends on $q$ and $R$ only, and let
$s=\sqrt{(1+J/\Delta)\,b}$. For $\delta>0$ put
\begin{align}
    B_\delta=\Bigl\lfloor\exp\Bigl(C+32\,s\log\frac{2e^{e/2}}{\delta}\Bigr)
    \Bigr\rfloor .
    \label{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_formal}
\end{align}
Then for every cut $k$ and every choice of the kept Schmidt vectors, the
normalized trimmed vector $v$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\langle v,\Omega\rangle\ge1-\delta ;
    \label{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_overlap}
\end{align}
the overlap is real and nonnegative.\footnote{\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ChainHamiltonian.one_sub_le_re_inner_chainTrim}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/ViableSet/ConstantRankCut}.}

The proof is short. The marginal tail bound of the area law gives, at every
cut and every $w\ge0$, a spectral projection of the left marginal $\rho$ of
rank at most $e^{C+w}$ discarding mass at most $2e^{e/2}e^{-w/(32s)}$. The
choice $w=32\,s\log(2e^{e/2}/\delta)$ makes this mass equal to $\delta$ and
the rank at most $B_\delta$. By Ky Fan's maximum principle the sum of the
$B_\delta$ largest eigenvalues of $\rho$ is at least $1-\delta$; this sum is
$\lVert\operatorname{trim}\Omega\rVert^2$, and the overlap in
(\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_overlap}) is $\lVert\operatorname{trim}\Omega
    \rVert\ge\sqrt{1-\delta}\ge1-\delta$.

\section{The constant}

The bound (\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_formal}) has the structure of
(\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_source}): it depends on neither the length of
the chain nor the cut, and it is the exponential of an affine function of
$\log(1/\delta)$, rounded down. Its constants are different. The slope is $32s$ and the
intercept involves $C$, both taken from the area law proved in the library,
not the dependence $\varepsilon^{-1}\log^3d$ of
(\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_source}). The later steps of the source use
only that $B_\delta$ is independent of $n$ and of the cut, so the
different constant affects the explicit running time, not the conclusions.
The theorems hold under the source's hypotheses: its nearest-neighbour
Hamiltonian with $0\le H_i\le1$ is a chain Hamiltonian of range $1$ and
strength $1$.

\section{The range of \texorpdfstring{$\delta$}{delta}}

The source states Lemma \path{constantbondapprox} and Corollary
\path{c:1} for any constant $\delta$, and both are formalized for every
$\delta>0$. In the lemma, for $\delta\ge1$ the conclusion holds because the
overlap is nonnegative. No upper bound on $\delta$ is needed in the
corollary, because the approximation can be chosen at a smaller error.

For Corollary \path{c:1} of the source (lines~361--364), let $\delta>0$
and apply the constant bond dimension at $\delta_0=\min\{\delta,1/12\}$.
Writing $E_0$ for the ground-state energy, the corrected trimming-energy
estimate gives
\begin{align}
    \langle v,Hv\rangle
    \le E_0+\frac{4\sqrt{\delta_0}}{1-4\delta_0}
    \le E_0+6\sqrt{\delta_0}\le E_0+6\sqrt\delta.
    \label{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_corollary_energy}
\end{align}
The overlap is at least $1-\delta_0\ge1-\delta$, and the bond dimension
$B_{\delta_0}$ remains independent of the chain length and cut. Thus the
corollary retains its stated energy and overlap bounds for every
$\delta>0$, despite the correction needed in Lemma \path{l:2}.

\section{The actual bond spaces in size trimming}

In size trimming, put $c=(\Delta/169)^2$ and $D=B_c$. A retained bond of
actual dimension $m\le D$ supplies an isometry
$W:\mathbb C^m\to\mathcal H_R$. It does not in general supply an isometry
from $\mathbb C^D$ when the right physical space has dimension less than
$D$. Choosing the optimization families separately for every
$0\le m\le D$ and taking the union of their left columns avoids this
extension. If each boundary net $N_m$ has at most $K$ points, the resulting
family $T$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    |T|\le\sum_{m=0}^{D}m|N_m|\le(D+1)DK.
    \label{eq:lvv13_actual_bond_union}
\end{align}
Thus the prefactor $(D+1)D$ replaces the single-space prefactor $D$ in
\cite[Claim~2.7]{Landau2015Polynomial}. Both $D$ and the corrected net
exponent $2(qD)^2$ are independent of $L$, so this change preserves
polynomial growth in the chain length. It does not establish the sharper
quantitative AKLV dependence in
(\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_source}).

\section{The source's constant}

The explicit dependence $\varepsilon^{-1}\log^3d$ is not formalized, which is
why this note remains open as a scope restriction: the formalized lemma has the
source's hypotheses and the source's form of $B_\delta$, but a weaker explicit
constant. It would
follow from the bound of Arad, Kitaev, Landau and Vazirani,
arXiv:1301.1162~\cite{Arad2013AreaLaw}, which gives the Schmidt rank in
(\ref{eq:lvv13_constant_bond_source}), by the argument of the previous
sections.

The later AKLV development now proves the sharper physical per-cut estimate
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_aklv\_sharp\_physical\_per\_cut\_approximation}.
With $u=1+\log d+\log(1/\varepsilon)$ and the accuracy scale $z=z(\delta)$,
it supplies a normalized vector of cut rank $D$ satisfying
\begin{align}
 \log D\le C\left(
   \frac{u^3}{\varepsilon}
   +\left(\frac{z^3}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}
      \bigl(u+\log(z+1)\bigr)\right)
\end{align}
at every cut.  Thus the remaining work is no longer the physical AKLV
construction or the passage to overlap: the same theorem proves
$|\langle y,\Omega\rangle|\ge1-\rho/2$ from squared norm error at most
$\rho$.  Moreover,
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.aklvAccuracyRankBound\_log\_le\_lvv}
absorbs the fourth-root term and proves
\begin{align}
  \log D\le 11C\,\frac{u^3}{\varepsilon}
     \log\frac{128}{\rho}.
\end{align}
Thus the logarithmic accuracy dependence quoted by LVV is formalized.  What
remains is the comparison of $u^3=(1+\log d+\log(1/\varepsilon))^3$ with the
particular $\log^3d$ dependence printed by LVV.  This is a genuine remaining
gap, rather than a matter of suppressed notation: AKLV Theorem~4.3 states
$O(\log^3 d/\varepsilon)$, and its proof chooses
$s=O(\log^2 d/\varepsilon)$ before deriving
$\log D=O(\log^3 d/\varepsilon)$.  Immediately before that conclusion, however,
the displayed estimate is
\begin{align*}
  \log D=O\bigl(s(\log d+\log(s^2+s))\bigr).
\end{align*}
Substitution of the stated value of $s$ retains a term proportional to
$(\log^2d/\varepsilon)\log(1/\varepsilon)$ unless the gap is regarded as
fixed.  Thus the printed calculation does not itself provide the missing
uniform comparison.  Closing the gap requires a sharper rank estimate or
parameter choice that removes the additional $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ terms from
$u$, or an explicit restriction of the claimed asymptotic regime.

This distinction is formalized by
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.aklv\_gap\_logarithm\_not\_uniformly\_absorbable}.
For every proposed universal coefficient $K\ge0$, it constructs
$0<g\le1$ such that
\begin{align*}
  K\log^3 2<\bigl(1+\log2+\log(1/g)\bigr)^3.
\end{align*}
Thus the currently proved $u^3/g$ coefficient cannot be converted to the
printed $\log^3d/g$ coefficient by enlarging its universal constant.  This
does not refute the existence of a sharper rank argument; it proves that such
an argument must remove the gap logarithm before the final comparison.

For a gap bounded away from zero, the printed cubic dependence on the local
dimension does follow from the present estimate.  More precisely,
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.aklv\_cubic\_cost\_of\_fixed\_gap}
proves that for every fixed $0<g_0\le1$, all $g_0\le g\le1$ and $d\ge2$ satisfy
\begin{align*}
 \frac{(1+\log d+\log(1/g))^3}{g}
 \le \left(1+\frac1{\log2}\right)^3
      (1+\log(1/g_0))^3\frac{\log^3d}{g}.
\end{align*}
Thus the physical theorem already has the source's $\log^3d$ dependence
uniformly on every fixed-gap interval.  This consequence, including the
normalized same-cut approximants and their overlap, is stated directly as
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_aklv\_fixed\_gap\_physical\_per\_cut\_approximation}.
The corresponding actual sequentially truncated matrix product state, with
the same cubic local-dimension coefficient and final norm accuracy, is
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_aklv\_fixed\_gap\_physical\_sequential\_mps\_approximation}.
The remaining gap is precisely
uniformity as the lower gap bound tends to zero.

There is a sharper scalar rank calculation.  If
$v=1+\log d$, $w=\log(1/g)$, and one selects
$s_0=\lceil Av^2/g\rceil$, then
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.aklvAccuracyRankBound\_log\_le\_lvv\_separated}
proves
\begin{align*}
  \log B\le 11C(A)\frac{v^3+v^2w}{g}\log\frac{128}{\rho}.
\end{align*}
This removes two of the three powers of the gap logarithm from the preceding
formal bound, but it is not yet a physical approximation theorem.  Indeed,
the favorable-filter construction requires the product of the rank budget
and the shrinking coefficient to be uniformly small.  At this smaller width,
the logarithm of the rank budget contains a contribution of order
$s_0\log(1/g)$, whereas the available lower bound for the Chebyshev decay
exponent is only of order $s_0v$.  For fixed $d$ and $g\downarrow0$, the
former therefore dominates the latter.  Consequently the present polynomial
filter argument cannot establish the favorable tradeoff at this smaller
width with a universal $A$.  A physical proof of the source coefficient needs
either a rank count without the $s_0\log(1/g)$ contribution or a different
filter whose decay compensates for it.

The same estimate now passes to the actual sequentially truncated matrix
product state in
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.exists\_aklv\_sharp\_physical\_sequential\_mps\_approximation}.
For final norm error $\epsilon$, the auxiliary accuracy is
$\rho=\epsilon^2/(16(L+1))$, and
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.aklvAccuracyLog\_sequential} identifies its
logarithmic scale exactly as
\begin{align*}
  z=\log\frac{2048(L+1)}{\epsilon^2}.
\end{align*}
Consequently the normalized output MPS itself satisfies
$\log D\le11C(u^3/\varepsilon)z$; this is not merely a collection of
independent cutwise approximants.
