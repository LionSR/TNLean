\section{What the source states}
Let $A^i=\bigoplus_{j=1}^b\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{j,1},\dots,\mu_{j,m_j})
    \otimes A_j^i$ with normal $A_j$ forming a basis of normal tensors
\cite[eq.~(S2)]{Malz2024Preparation}, block $q$ sites, and write the polar
decomposition of the blocked map as $B=VP$. The source argues ``Since $V$ is
an isometry, $P$ inherits the block structure of $A$'',
\begin{align}
    P & =\bigoplus_{j=1}^b\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{j,1}^q,\dots,\mu_{j,m_j}^q)
    \otimes P_j
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}
\end{align}
with normal $P_j$ \cite[eq.~(S5)]{Malz2024Preparation}. It then approximates the
state on $N=qM$ sites by
$\ket{\widetilde\phi_N}\propto V^{\otimes M}\sum_j\beta_j\ket{\Omega_j}$ with
$\beta_j=\sum_k\mu_{j,k}^N$ \cite[eq.~(S7)]{Malz2024Preparation}, and part~(ii)
of the approximation-error lemma bounds
$\varepsilon=1-\lvert\braket{\widetilde\phi_N}{\phi_N}\rvert$ by
$O\bigl((N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}\bigr)$ for $q=o(N)$
\cite[eq.~(S12)]{Malz2024Preparation}, where
$\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}=\max_j\xi_{jj}$ is the largest correlation length of the
blocks. In the proof, the overlaps of distinct blocks enter only through a
term $O(e^{-N/\xi_{\mathrm{off\text{-}diag}}})$, which $q=o(N)$ removes.

\section{Where the argument fails}
The positive part is $P=(B^\dagger B)^{1/2}$, and $B^\dagger B$ is the transfer
matrix of $B$ with its legs regrouped. Its block between two distinct blocks
$A_j$ and $A_{j'}$ is the $q$-th power of the mixed transfer matrix
$E_{jj'}=\sum_iA_j^{i*}\otimes A_{j'}^i$. The source uses that
$E_{jj'}$ has spectral radius $\tau<1$ \cite[proof of Lemma~1$'$]{Malz2024Preparation},
but $E_{jj'}^q$ is not zero, so $B^\dagger B$ and its square root are not block
diagonal. The block form (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}) holds only when the
$q$-site states of distinct blocks are orthogonal. This failure is separate from
the one for repeated blocks \cite{gap:mswc24_multiplicity_fixed_point}: it occurs
when every multiplicity is one.

\section{The counterexample}
Take $d=2$ and the two one-dimensional normal blocks $A_1=(1,0)$ and
$A_2=(3/5,4/5)$, each of multiplicity one with weight one, so that
$A^0=\operatorname{diag}(1,3/5)$ and $A^1=\operatorname{diag}(0,4/5)$. Both
blocks satisfy $\sum_i\lvert a_i\rvert^2=1$ and are not proportional, so they form a
basis of normal tensors, and $\beta=(1,1)$. The $q$-site states of the blocks are
$\ket{0\cdots0}$ and $(\tfrac35\ket0+\tfrac45\ket1)^{\otimes q}$, with overlap
$s=(3/5)^q$. On the diagonal bond coordinates $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$, the only ones
on which $B$ does not vanish,
\begin{align}
    B^\dagger B=\begin{pmatrix}1&s\\s&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    P=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&u\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    u=\frac{\sqrt{1+s}+\sqrt{1-s}}2,\quad
    v=\frac{\sqrt{1+s}-\sqrt{1-s}}2,
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_positive_part}
\end{align}
and the support projector of $P$ is the projector onto these two coordinates. The
fixed point of a one-dimensional block is $\sigma=1$, so the pairs are
$\omega_1=e_{00}$ and $\omega_2=e_{11}$. Since $V^\dagger B=P$ and
$V^\dagger V$ fixes the pairs, for every $q,M\geq1$,
\begin{align}
    \braket{\widetilde\phi_N}{\phi_N}
     & =\frac{u^M+v^M}{(1+s^M)^{1/2}}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_overlap}
\end{align}
For $q=1$ this is $(3^M+1)/(10^M+6^M)^{1/2}$, which tends to zero. Since
$u^2+v^2=1$, $u^2=(1+\sqrt{1-s^2})/2\geq1/2$, and $1-u\geq s^2/8$, one has
$u^M+v^M\leq u^3+v^2=1-u^2(1-u)\leq1-s^2/16$ for $M\geq3$, so
\begin{align}
    \varepsilon & \geq\frac{s^2}{16}=\frac1{16}\Bigl(\frac9{25}\Bigr)^q
    \qquad(M\geq3).
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_lower_bound}
\end{align}
The construction nevertheless converges. For $M=1$ the overlap
(\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_overlap}) is one, since $u+v=\sqrt{1+s}$. For $M\geq2$,
$u\geq u^2=(1+\sqrt{1-s^2})/2\geq1-s^2/2$, so $u^M\geq1-Ms^2/2$ by Bernoulli's
inequality, and $(1+s^M)^{1/2}\leq1+s^2/2$; hence
\begin{align}
    \varepsilon & \leq Ms^2=M\Bigl(\frac9{25}\Bigr)^q
    \qquad(q,M\geq1).
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_upper_bound}
\end{align}
The error of this example therefore lies between $s^2/16$ (for $M\geq3$) and $Ms^2$,
and it tends to zero whenever $(N/q)\tau^{2q}\to0$ with $\tau=3/5$, for instance when
$q\geq c\log N$ with $c>1/(2\log(5/3))$.

The transfer maps of one-dimensional blocks are the identity and have no
eigenvalue other than one. The source defines
$\xi_{jj}=-1/\ln\lvert\lambda_2^{(j)}\rvert$ with $\lambda_2^{(j)}$ the subleading
eigenvalue of the transfer matrix of $A_j$ \cite{Malz2024Preparation}; here there
is none, so $\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is degenerate. Under the usual convention
$\lambda_2^{(j)}=0$ it is $0$, and the printed bound then asserts $\varepsilon=0$,
which (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_lower_bound}) refutes directly. The formal statement
avoids the convention: it reads $\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ as the correlation length at any
$\lambda_2$ with $0<\lvert\lambda_2\rvert<1$ bounding the subleading eigenvalues,
which here is every such $\lambda_2$, and covers those with
$r=e^{-\gamma/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}=\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^\gamma<9/25$. Take the
sequence $q=M$, $N=q^2$, which satisfies $q=o(N)$.
The printed bound $C(N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}=Cqr^q$ is eventually
smaller than $(9/25)^q/16$ for every constant $C$, and so is
$Cqr^q\exp(Cqr^q)$. The error is governed by the overlap $s=\tau^q$ of the blocks,
with $\tau=3/5$ the eigenvalue of the mixed transfer matrix: it is at least $s^2/16$
and at most $Ms^2=(N/q)\tau^{2q}$, and numerically close to $Ms^2/8$, a contribution
that the source's estimate does not contain. What fails is the rate, not the
construction.

\section{The construction converges}
Let every block have multiplicity one and weight one, so that $\beta_j=1$ and
$\alpha_j=b^{-1/2}$, and let the blocks $A_j$ be normal in the gauge
$\sum_i(A_j^i)^\dagger A_j^i=\mathbf 1$ with positive definite fixed points
$\sigma_j$ of trace one. Let $\lambda_2$ bound the moduli of the eigenvalues other than
one of every transfer matrix $E_{jj}$ and the moduli of all eigenvalues of the mixed
transfer matrices $E_{jj'}$, $j\neq j'$, so that $\xi=-1/\ln\lvert\lambda_2\rvert$ is at
least $\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $\xi_{\mathrm{off\text{-}diag}}$, and let $0<\gamma<1/2$.
Then there is $C$ with
\begin{align}
    \varepsilon\leq Cy\,e^{Cy}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon\leq Cy,\qquad
    y=\frac Nq\,e^{-\gamma q/\xi},
    \label{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}
\end{align}
for every $q\geq1$ and $M\geq1$, for the source's state (S7) itself: the same $V$, the
same pairs and the same weights. No orthogonality and no condition $q=o(N)$ is needed.
This is the statement of part~(ii) with $\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}$ replaced by
$\max(\xi_{\mathrm{diag}},\xi_{\mathrm{off\text{-}diag}})$.

The proof replaces the block form (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}) by an estimate.
With $K_j$ the isometries placing the bond pairs of block $j$, the blocked map is
$B=\sum_jB_jK_j^\dagger$ and
$B^\dagger B=\sum_{j,j'}K_jB_j^\dagger B_{j'}K_{j'}^\dagger$. The diagonal blocks satisfy
$\lVert B_j^\dagger B_j-\sigma_j^T\otimes\mathbf 1\rVert\leq Ke^{-\gamma q/\xi}$ as in the
normal case, and the off-diagonal blocks are the entries of $E_{j'j}^q$, so
$\lVert B_j^\dagger B_{j'}\rVert\leq Ke^{-\gamma q/\xi}$. Their common limit
$G_\infty=\sum_jK_j(\sigma_j^T\otimes\mathbf 1)K_j^\dagger$ vanishes on the bond pairs
$(a,b)$ with $a$ and $b$ in different blocks, and so does $B^\dagger B$. Adding the
projector $Q$ onto these pairs makes $G_\infty+Q$ positive definite, and $PQ=0$ gives
$\sqrt{B^\dagger B+Q}=P+Q$. The square root is Lipschitz at a positive definite point,
so $\lVert P-P_\infty\rVert\leq Ke^{-\gamma q/\xi}$ with
$P_\infty=\sum_jK_j((\sqrt{\sigma_j})^T\otimes\mathbf 1)K_j^\dagger$.

Since $V^\dagger B=P$, the overlap of the unnormalized state
$V^{\otimes M}\sum_j\alpha_j\ket{\Omega_j}$ with $\ket{\phi_N(A)}$ is
$b^{-1/2}\sum_jz_j$ with $z_j=\braket{\Omega_j}{\phi_M(P)}$, and $V^{\otimes M}$ does not
increase norms, so normalizing the approximating state only increases the overlap.
The mixed transfer matrix of $P_\infty$ against the fixed-point tensor of block $j$,
placed in the full bond space, is $\rho\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_j\rho)\,\tilde\sigma_j$,
with $\Pi_j$ the projector onto the bond coordinates of block $j$ and
$\tilde\sigma_j$ the embedded fixed point; it is idempotent with trace one. The
telescoping estimate of the normal case then gives
$\lvert z_j-1\rvert\leq Cy\,e^{Cy}$. Finally
$\lVert\phi_N(A)\rVert^2=\sum_{j,j'}\braket{\phi_N(A_j)}{\phi_N(A_{j'})}$, whose diagonal
terms are one up to $O(e^{-2\gamma N/\xi})$ and whose other terms are
$O(e^{-\gamma N/\xi})$. With $S=\sum_jz_j$ and $t=\lVert\phi_N(A)\rVert$, the
inequality $1-\lvert S\rvert/(\sqrt b\,t)\leq\lvert S-b\rvert+\lvert t^2-b\rvert$ for
$b\geq1$ gives (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}).

The first-order Lipschitz estimate gives the rate $e^{-\gamma q/\xi}$; the
counterexample and the numerical checks below show the error to be of order
$(N/q)\tau^{2q}$, of second order in the overlaps, which the argument does not
capture.

\section{Weights of different moduli}
Let every block still have multiplicity one, now with weight $\mu_j$. For multiplicity one the
phase of $\mu_j$ can be absorbed into $A_j$, which stays normal with the same transfer map; the
weight of block $j$ in (S7) is then $\beta_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$. So let $0\leq w_j\leq1$ be real
with $\max_jw_j=1$, the normalization of the source \cite[after eq.~(S2)]{Malz2024Preparation},
let $A=\bigoplus_jw_jA_j$ and $\beta_j=w_j^N$, and keep the hypotheses of the previous section on
the blocks, on $\lambda_2$ and on $\gamma$. Then (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}) holds for the
source's state (S7), with a constant $C$ that does not depend on $q$ or $M$, again for every
$q\geq1$ and $M\geq1$.

The Lipschitz step of the previous section fails: the limit of the Gram matrices is now
$G_\infty^{(q)}=\sum_jw_j^{2q}K_j(\sigma_j^T\otimes\mathbf 1)K_j^\dagger$, whose smallest
eigenvalue on the blocks of weight $w_j<1$ tends to zero as $q$ grows. Three changes repair the
argument.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Writing $B=\sum_jw_j^qB_jK_j^\dagger$, every block of $B^\dagger B-G_\infty^{(q)}$ is a
          block of the unit-weight case multiplied by $w_j^qw_{j'}^q\leq1$, so
          $\lVert B^\dagger B-G_\infty^{(q)}\rVert\leq Ke^{-\gamma'q/\xi}$ for every
          $0<\gamma'<1$, with $K$ independent of the weights.
    \item The square root is $\tfrac12$-Hölder on positive semidefinite matrices,
          $\lVert\sqrt X-\sqrt Y\rVert\leq\lVert X-Y\rVert^{1/2}$, the bound used in
          \cite[Supplemental Material, eq.~(26)]{Piroli2021LOCC}. With $\gamma'=2\gamma<1$ it gives
          $\lVert P-P_\infty^{(q)}\rVert\leq K^{1/2}e^{-\gamma q/\xi}$, where
          $P_\infty^{(q)}=\sum_jw_j^qK_j((\sqrt{\sigma_j})^T\otimes\mathbf 1)K_j^\dagger$. The
          halving of the exponent is absorbed by the range $0<\gamma<1/2$ of the source.
    \item The mixed transfer matrix of $P_\infty^{(q)}$ against the fixed-point tensor of block
          $j$ is $w_j^q$ times the rank-one idempotent $R_j:\rho\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_j\rho)
              \tilde\sigma_j$ of trace one. Its powers $w_j^{qk}R_j$ are bounded by
          $\lVert R_j\rVert$, uniformly in $q$, so the telescoping estimate gives
          $\lvert z_j-w_j^N\rvert\leq C_jye^{C_jy}$.
\end{enumerate}
With $S=\sum_j\beta_jz_j$ and $b=\sum_j\beta_j^2\geq1$ one has
$\lvert S-b\rvert\leq\sum_j\lvert z_j-\beta_j\rvert$, and as before
$\bigl\lvert\lVert\phi_N(A)\rVert^2-b\bigr\rvert\leq Ke^{-\gamma N/\xi}$; the inequality of the
previous section gives (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}).

The phase cannot be kept in $\beta_j$. Write $\mu_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert u_j$ with
$u_j=e^{i\theta_j}$, let $B_0$ be the blocked map for the weights $\lvert\mu_j\rvert$, and let
$U$ be the diagonal unitary that multiplies the bond coordinates of block $j$ by $u_j^q$. Then
$B=B_0U$, so $B^\dagger B=U^\dagger B_0^\dagger B_0U$, $P=U^\dagger P_0U$ and $V=V_0U$. The
diagonal blocks of $P$ are those of $P_0$ and carry no phase, contrary to
(\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}); only the mixed blocks of $P$, which vanish for orthogonal
blocks, carry the phases $\overline{u_j}^{\,q}u_{j'}^q$. Since $\ket{\Omega_j}$ lies on the bond
coordinates of block $j$, $V^{\otimes M}\ket{\Omega_j}=e^{iN\theta_j}V_0^{\otimes M}\ket{\Omega_j}$,
and with $\beta_j=\mu_j^N$ the state (S7) has the block coefficients
$\lvert\mu_j\rvert^Ne^{2iN\theta_j}$ relative to $V_0^{\otimes M}\ket{\Omega_j}$, in which the
phase of the weight appears twice. For the random blocks of the numerical check below with $\mu=(1,e^{0.9i})$ and
$M=8$, the error with $\beta_j=\mu_j^N$ is $0.794$, $0.191$, $0.301$ at $q=4,8,12$, against
$0.355$, $6.53\cdot10^{-2}$, $1.47\cdot10^{-2}$ with $\beta_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$. The printed
form $\beta_j=\mu_j^N$ is consistent with the block form (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}),
which assigns the phase to the diagonal blocks of $P$. With the printed coefficients (S6) and
(S7) fail already for orthogonal blocks of multiplicity one
(Section~\ref{sec:mswc24_phase_counterexample}), and
$\beta_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$ is used, with the phases absorbed into the blocks. The
coefficients $\beta_j=\mu_j^N$ of (S4) remain correct in the expansion (S3) of
$\ket{\phi_N}$.

\section{The phase of a weight: a counterexample}\label{sec:mswc24_phase_counterexample}
The doubled phase is a separate failure of (S7), which occurs already for orthogonal blocks of
multiplicity one. Take $d=2$, the one-dimensional normal blocks $A_1=(1,0)$ and $A_2=(0,1)$, each
of multiplicity one, with weights $1$ and $\mu$, $\lvert\mu\rvert=1$, so that
$A^0=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $A^1=\operatorname{diag}(0,\mu)$ and
$\ket{\phi_N}\propto\ket{0\cdots0}+\mu^N\ket{1\cdots1}$. The blocks form a basis of normal tensors
in canonical form~II, and both weights have modulus one. For $q\geq1$ the Gram matrix of the
blocked map on the diagonal bond coordinates is the identity, so $P=JJ^\dagger$ and $V=BJJ^\dagger$,
with $J$ the isometry placing the coordinate $e$ on the pair $(e,e)$; $V$ maps the pair of the
second block to $\mu^q\ket{1\cdots1}$. For coefficients $\alpha$ with
$\lvert\alpha_1\rvert^2+\lvert\alpha_2\rvert^2=1$ the overlap is
$(\overline{\alpha_1}+\overline{\alpha_2})/\sqrt2$, so for every $q,M\geq1$
\begin{align}
    \braket{\widetilde\phi_N}{\phi_N}=\frac{1+\overline\mu^{\,N}}2
    \quad\bigl(\beta=(1,\mu^N)\bigr),\qquad
    \braket{\widetilde\phi_N}{\phi_N}=1
    \quad\bigl(\beta=(1,\lvert\mu\rvert^N)\bigr).
    \label{eq:mswc24_phase_overlap}
\end{align}
For $\mu=-1$ and odd $N$ the state (S7) is $\propto\ket{0\cdots0}+\ket{1\cdots1}$, orthogonal to
$\ket{\phi_N}\propto\ket{0\cdots0}-\ket{1\cdots1}$, and $\varepsilon=1$. The transfer maps of the
blocks have no eigenvalue other than one, so every $\lambda_2$ with $0<\lvert\lambda_2\rvert<1$
bounds their subleading eigenvalues, and along $q=M$ odd, $N=q^2$, the printed bound
$C(N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}}\exp(C(N/q)e^{-\gamma q/\xi_{\mathrm{diag}}})$ tends to zero
while $\varepsilon=1$. A script evaluating the overlap from the eigendecomposition of $B^\dagger B$
reproduces (\ref{eq:mswc24_phase_overlap}) for $\mu\in\{-1,i,e^{0.9i}\}$ and
$(q,M)\in\{(1,1),(1,3),(2,3),(3,3),(3,5),(5,5)\}$.

The same tensor is the direct sum $A_1\oplus A_2'$ with the block $A_2'=(0,\mu)$ and weights
$(1,1)$, for which (S4) gives $\beta=(1,1)$ and (S7) is exact: the printed coefficients depend on
how the phase is split between the block and the weight. The coefficients
$\beta_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$ do not.

\section{Numerical check}
A script evaluates the overlap through $P$, using $V^\dagger B=P$ and the support
projector of $P$, from the eigendecomposition of $B^\dagger B$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item For the counterexample it reproduces (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_overlap}), and
          $\varepsilon$ is $3.324\cdot10^{-2}$, $4.348\cdot10^{-3}$, $5.641\cdot10^{-4}$ at
          $M=16$ and $q=4,6,8$, against $Ms^2/8=3.359\cdot10^{-2}$, $4.354\cdot10^{-3}$,
          $5.642\cdot10^{-4}$.
    \item For two random normal blocks of bond dimension two on $d=2$ with subleading
          eigenvalues $0.4312$ and $0.4481$ and mixed spectral radius $\tau=0.780$, with
          unit weights, $\varepsilon/(M\tau^{2q})$ is $0.176$, $0.167$, $0.154$, $0.155$ at
          $M=8$ and $q=6,8,10,12$: the error is of order $(N/q)\tau^{2q}$ and tends to
          zero.
    \item For the same blocks with weights $(1,0.7)$ the error is $3.52\cdot10^{-3}$,
          $3.45\cdot10^{-4}$, $2.89\cdot10^{-5}$ at $M=8$ and $q=6,8,10$.
    \item For two other random normal blocks of bond dimension two on $d=2$, with subleading
          eigenvalues $0.515$ and $0.850$ and mixed spectral radius $\tau=0.757$, so that
          $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert=0.850$, and weights $(1,w_2)$ with
          $w_2\in\{1,0.99,0.9,0.7,0.4,0.1\}$, the ratio $\varepsilon/(M\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^q)$
          stays below $0.14$ for all $M\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and $q\in\{2,4,\dots,12\}$. For
          $w_2=0.9$ and $0.7$, $M\geq2$, and $q\geq4$ the error is close to $cM(w_2\tau)^{2q}$

          constant of (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}) does not grow as $w_2$ decreases.

\section{Elimination}
Two statements would repair part~(ii) for multiplicity one. The first adds the
hypothesis that the $q$-site states of distinct blocks are orthogonal,
$\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_q}\overline{(A_j^{i_1}\cdots A_j^{i_q})_{ab}}\,
    (A_{j'}^{i_1}\cdots A_{j'}^{i_q})_{a'b'}=0$ for $j\neq j'$. Under it
(\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_block_form}) holds with $\lvert\mu_j\rvert^q$ in place of
$\mu_j^q$: writing the blocked map as $B=\sum_j\mu_j^qB_jK_j^\dagger$ with the
isometries $K_j$ that place the bond pairs of block $j$, the polar decomposition of
$B$ is $V=\sum_jV_jK_j^\dagger$, $P=\sum_j\mu_j^qK_jP_jK_j^\dagger$ for $\mu_j>0$.
The states of distinct blocks, before and after the replacement by the fixed
points, are then exactly orthogonal, the term $O(e^{-N/\xi_{\mathrm{off\text{-}diag}}})$
vanishes, and part~(ii) reduces to part~(i) for each block, with no condition
$q=o(N)$. For multiplicity one the phase of $\mu_j$ can be absorbed into $A_j$,
which stays normal with the same transfer map, so $\mu_j>0$ is no loss. This
repair is formalized.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{Matrix.polarIso_sum_of_orthogonal} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OrthogonalSumPolar};
    \leanid{MPSTensor.nonNormalApproxVector_blockSum} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OrthogonalBlockSum};
    \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_approximationError_le_blockSum} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_approximationError_le_mul_blockSum} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OrthogonalBlockError}.}
The orthogonality holds for instance when distinct blocks use disjoint sets of
physical indices.

The second keeps the source's hypotheses and construction, apart from the phases of the
weights, and corrects the rate: it
is (\ref{eq:mswc24_mixed_corrected}), with the correlation lengths of the mixed
transfer matrices included in $\xi$. It is formalized for multiplicity one and complex
weights $\lvert\mu_j\rvert\leq1$ with $\max_j\lvert\mu_j\rvert=1$, with
$\beta_j=\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$ in place of the coefficients $\mu_j^N$ that (S6) and (S7) take
from (S4); with those coefficients the counterexample (\ref{eq:mswc24_phase_overlap}) refutes
(S7). Blocks with multiplicity combined with overlaps are formalized for the corrected state of
\cite{gap:mswc24_repeated_block_corrected_state}, which replaces (S7), with complex weights of
modulus at most one and the coefficients $\beta_j=\sum_k\mu_{j,k}^N$ of (S4), up to a factor
$\min(1,\sum_j\lvert\beta_j\rvert^2)^{-1/2}$ that is one when $\sum_j\lvert\beta_j\rvert^2\geq1$,
and without that factor for real weights in $[0,1]$ one of which is one. The state (S7) itself,
with the pairs of block $j$ on one copy $k_j$, is the corrected state when its coefficients are
$\beta_j(c_j/\mu_{j,k_j}^q)^M$ with $c_j=(\sum_k\lvert\mu_{j,k}\rvert^{2q})^{1/2}$, which is
$\lvert\mu_j\rvert^N$ for multiplicity one; with these coefficients the bounds above hold for
(S7) and weights of any phase \cite{gap:mswc24_repeated_block_corrected_state}. Two extensions are not
formalized: the bound without that factor when $\sum_j\lvert\beta_j\rvert^2<1$, as for cancelling
weights or weights all of modulus below one \cite{gap:mswc24_repeated_overlap_small_weight_sum},
and the sharp rate $(N/q)\tau^{2q}$; both need a second-order perturbation bound for the square
root of
$B^\dagger B$ around its block-diagonal part.
