\section{The source statement}

Theorem~A of the one-dimensional note~\cite{TNLean2026OneDimensional} is
stated for every finite set $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z$ with its induced path
graph: for an admissible Hamiltonian $H=\sum_X h_X$ on $\Lambda$, one Hermitian
term of norm at most $J$ per nonempty support $X\subseteq\Lambda$ of graph
diameter at most $R$, and a unit ground vector $\Omega$ with
$H-E_0\ge\Delta(1-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)$, every $A\subseteq\Lambda$
satisfies $S_\Omega(A)\le C_{\mathrm{AL}}\,|\partial_\Lambda A|$ with
$C_{\mathrm{AL}}\le C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$. The edge boundary
$\partial_\Lambda A$ counts the edges $\{x,x+1\}\subseteq\Lambda$ with exactly
one endpoint in $A$.

The note's first reduction (Section~2) disposes of disconnected domains in a
sentence: the Hamiltonian is a sum over the components of $\Lambda$, each
component is an interval of consecutive integers, the unique gapped ground
vector is a product of the component ground vectors, and both the entropy and
the edge boundary add over components. The theorem is then proved for
connected $\Lambda$, that is, for a chain of $L$ consecutive sites.

\section{What is proved and the restriction}

The formalization states the model on the chain of $L$ sites, with sites
$\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$, supports of range $R$ the nonempty sets of sites of
diameter at most $R$, and the Hamiltonian, gapped ground vector, regional
entropies and boundary edges as in the note.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsChainSupport},
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ChainHamiltonian},
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.boundaryEdges}.} Theorem~A is proved on this
chain for every subset $A$, with the explicit constant
$C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$.\footnote{Declarations:
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.regionEntropy_interval_le},
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.regionEntropy_le_mul_card_boundaryEdges},
\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.chainAreaLaw}.} Theorem~B of the note is stated on
the chain of $L$ sites in the note itself, so it carries no restriction.

The restriction is to connected domains. The finite-dimensional spectral
step is proved for two finite-dimensional tensor factors: let $H_1,H_2$ be
Hermitian, put $H=H_1\otimes I+I\otimes H_2$, and suppose that a unit vector
$\Omega$ satisfies $H\Omega=E_0\Omega$ and
$H-E_0I\ge\Delta(I-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)$ with $\Delta>0$, then there
are unit vectors $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$ and real numbers $E_1,E_2$ such that
$\Omega=\Omega_1\otimes\Omega_2$, $E_0=E_1+E_2$, and each factor satisfies
$H_i\Omega_i=E_i\Omega_i$ and
$H_i-E_iI\ge\Delta(I-|\Omega_i\rangle\langle\Omega_i|)$.
\footnote{Declaration: \leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.exists_isGappedGroundState_factors_of_kronecker_sum}.}
The phase is included in one normalized factor, so the product equality is
exact and the gap does not decrease.

The induced integer geometry is also established: two vertices of a subset
of $\mathbb Z$ are connected precisely when every integer between them is
present, and the coordinate image of every connected component is order-convex.
\footnote{Declarations: \leanid{SimpleGraph.hasse_int_induce_reachable_iff},
\leanid{SimpleGraph.ConnectedComponent.ordConnected_image_hasse_int_induce}.}
This geometric statement requires no finiteness hypothesis. It does not yet
provide the finite chain coordinate maps or the decomposition of the actual
Hamiltonian into component Hamiltonians.

For an arbitrary finite $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z$, the identification of the
actual Hamiltonian with a tensor sum over its connected components, and the
transport of each component to the chain model, remain to be formalized.
The entropy bound $S_\Omega(A)\le C|\partial_\Lambda A|$ therefore remains
restricted to connected domains.

\section{Elimination plan}

Express the Hamiltonian on an arbitrary finite domain as an iterated tensor
sum of the Hamiltonians on its connected components. Admissible supports have
finite diameter in the induced path graph, so each support lies in one
component. Identify each component, a finite interval, with a chain of its
cardinality, and verify that the transported Hamiltonian has the same
interaction range and norm bound. Apply the binary spectral theorem above
repeatedly to obtain normalized component ground vectors with the same gap.
Finally identify the actual regional reduced states under the component
coordinate equivalence. Reuse the existing product reduced-state identity,
entropy additivity for normalized product vectors, and covariance under site
relabelling, applying them by finite induction over the components. Prove
separately that the edge boundary is the disjoint union of the component
boundaries. The empty domain is handled
separately; its regional entropy and boundary are both zero. Until then the
restriction is recorded in the module docstring of \texttt{TNLean/MPS/AreaLaw/AreaLaw.lean} and in the blueprint
statement of Theorem~A.
