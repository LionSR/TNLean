\section{The source statement}

Theorem~A of the one-dimensional note~\cite{TNLean2026OneDimensional} is
stated for every finite set $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z$ with its induced path
graph: for an admissible Hamiltonian $H=\sum_X h_X$ on $\Lambda$, one Hermitian
term of norm at most $J$ per nonempty support $X\subseteq\Lambda$ of graph
diameter at most $R$, and a unit ground vector $\Omega$ with
$H-E_0\ge\Delta(1-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)$, every $A\subseteq\Lambda$
satisfies $S_\Omega(A)\le C_{\mathrm{AL}}\,|\partial_\Lambda A|$ with
$C_{\mathrm{AL}}\le C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$. The edge boundary
$\partial_\Lambda A$ counts the edges $\{x,x+1\}\subseteq\Lambda$ with exactly
one endpoint in $A$.

The note's first reduction (Section~2) disposes of disconnected domains in a
sentence: the Hamiltonian is a sum over the components of $\Lambda$, each
component is an interval of consecutive integers, the unique gapped ground
vector is a product of the component ground vectors, and both the entropy and
the edge boundary add over components. The theorem is then proved for
connected $\Lambda$, that is, for a chain of $L$ consecutive sites.

\section{The chain case}

The formalization first states the model on the chain of $L$ sites, with sites
$\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$, supports of range $R$ the nonempty sets of sites of
diameter at most $R$, and the Hamiltonian, gapped ground vector, regional
entropies and boundary edges as in the note.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.IsChainSupport},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ChainHamiltonian},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.boundaryEdges}.} Theorem~A is proved on this
chain for every subset $A$, with the explicit constant
$C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.regionEntropy_interval_le},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.regionEntropy_le_mul_card_boundaryEdges},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.chainAreaLaw}.} Theorem~B of the note is stated on
the chain of $L$ sites in the note itself, so it carries no restriction.

\section{Resolution: arbitrary finite domains}

Theorem~A is now formalized for every finite $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z$, with
no hypothesis beyond the source's and with the constant
$C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$ of the chain case.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.DomainHamiltonian},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.domainBoundaryEdges},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.regionEntropy_le_mul_card_domainBoundaryEdges},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.domainAreaLaw}.} The sites of $\Lambda$ are
labelled $0,\ldots,|\Lambda|-1$ in increasing order, the supports are the
nonempty sets of graph diameter at most $R$ in the induced path graph, and
$\partial_\Lambda A$ is the set of edges $\{x,x+1\}\subseteq\Lambda$ with
exactly one endpoint in $A$, as in the note.

The proof takes a shorter route than the factorization sketched in the note.
A support of range $R$ in $\Lambda$ is a support of range $R$ on the chain of
$|\Lambda|$ sites, so the Hamiltonian is a chain Hamiltonian and the chain
theorem bounds $S_\Omega(A)$ by $C_{\mathrm{int}}$ times the number of chain
boundary edges of $A$. These include the chain edges at the gaps of
$\Lambda$, which are not edges of $\Lambda$. At a gap no term crosses, so the
Hamiltonian is $H_1\otimes1+1\otimes H_2$ across the prefix $P$ of the chain
ending at the gap, the gapped ground vector is a product vector across that
cut, and $S_\Omega(P)=0$. For $S_\Omega(P)=0$, the Araki--Lieb inequality and
strong subadditivity give $S_\Omega(A\triangle P)=S_\Omega(A)$ for every
region $A$, while replacing $A$ by $A\triangle P$ toggles exactly the chain
edge at the end of $P$. Flipping the prefixes at the gaps where $A$ changes
yields a region with the entropy of $A$ whose chain boundary edges are the
edges of $\partial_\Lambda A$. The full factorization of the ground vector
over the components is therefore not needed.

\section{Locality in the matrix product state argument}

Part~II of the note applies its Lieb--Robinson lemma on the contiguous chain
of Theorem~B, namely $\Lambda=\{1,\ldots,L\}$. A formulation on this chain
therefore suffices for that application. The printed local lemma
(\path{part2a_mps_information.tex}, lines~260--309) does not separately
reset the standing notation for $\Lambda$, however, so its broader reading
also includes arbitrary finite induced-path domains. The contiguous
formulation is not a formalization of that unrestricted reading.

Both estimates are now proved for arbitrary finite induced integer domains,
with the same uniform constants as on the contiguous chain. Put
\begin{align}
    C   & = 2+4|b_0|e^{2R}, & v  & = 2(R+1)|b_0|e^{2R}+1, \notag \\
    \mu & = \tfrac12,       & C' & = \frac{2Ce}{1-e^{-1/2}}.
    \label{eq:tnlean_one_dimensional_connected_domain_scope_domain_constants}
\end{align}
For every finite labelled family of Hermitian terms on nonempty supports
of induced-graph diameter at most $R$, with per-site norm sum at most $b_0$,
the commutator bound holds on every finite initial support $S$. The
localization bound holds for $q\ge1$ and every real radius $\ell\ge0$, with
neighborhood
$N_\ell(S)=\bigcup_{x\in S}\{y:d_\Lambda(x,y)\le\lfloor\ell\rfloor\}$.
Neither estimate assumes connectedness, a ground state or a spectral gap;
empty $S$ is allowed.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.norm_heisenberg_commutator_le_domainDistance},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.norm_heisenberg_sub_siteExpectation_domainNeighborhood_le}.}

The increasing enumeration identifies the domain graph with the induced
integer Hasse graph. Each component is a finite path, and its inclusion
preserves extended graph distance. A sphere of finite radius therefore has
at most two vertices, while a support of diameter at most $R$ has at most
$R+1$ sites.\footnote{Declarations:
    \leanid{SimpleGraph.ConnectedComponent.nonempty_pathGraph_iso_hasse_int_induce_finset},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.domainGraphIsoHasse},
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.domainGraph_card_sphere_le_two}.}
The general graph estimates apply with sphere coefficient $2$ and exponent
zero. Passing from the natural radius $\lfloor\ell\rfloor$ to $\ell$
contributes the factor $e^{1/2}$ in the coefficient $C'$ of
(\ref{eq:tnlean_one_dimensional_connected_domain_scope_domain_constants}). Operators at
infinite separation commute exactly; infinite distances are not replaced
by coordinate distances across a missing integer. This resolves the broader
reading of the local propagation and localization lemma without using any
ground-state decomposition.

\section{Information reset on the contiguous chain}

Part~II, Lemma~\texttt{lem:reset}
(\path{part2a_mps_information.tex}, lines~319--435), is proved on the
contiguous chain with the source hypotheses. For every $p>0$ there are
$C_p>0$ and a natural $L_0\ge1$, chosen before all system and region data,
such that the following holds for $L\ge L_0$. If $D,C$ are disjoint,
$R_0\subseteq D$, $C_0\subseteq C$, $I_\Omega(D:C)\le b$, and both
cores have distance greater than $w=C_p(1+b+\log L)$ from their respective
outer-region complements, then $I_\Omega(R_0:C_0)\le L^{-p}$.
Nonnegativity of $b$ is derived, and only the original full-system gap is
assumed. The Gaussian filter, supported approximation, polar unitary and
independence after the regional replica swap are constructed within the
proof.\footnote{Declaration:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ChainHamiltonian.information_reset}.}

\section{Resolution: information reset on finite domains}

The information-reset lemma is now proved on every finite induced integer
 domain, with the same hypothesis set. The gap remains the original global
 gap, and the proof constructs the physical buffer, Gaussian approximation
 and supported polar unitary for the actual doubled and regionally swapped
 domain operators. No factorization or product-marginal hypothesis is added.
 The constants $C_p,L_0$ precede the domain and all instance data.\footnote{
 Declaration: \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.DomainHamiltonian.information_reset}.}

Distances in the margins are intrinsic extended graph distances. Equivalently,
 every finite distance $d$ from a core to its outer-region complement must
 satisfy $w<d$; infinite distances impose no condition. For the chosen natural
 localization radius $\ell$, the inequality $\ell+R<w$ places closed graph
 balls of radius $\ell+R$ inside the two outer regions. Since each original
 interaction support has diameter at most $R$, its crossing contribution
 cannot reach a core after enlargement by $\ell$. The supported unitary
 therefore acts away from both cores. The regional swap itself yields the
 required product marginal, and entropy continuity completes the proof.\footnote{
 Declaration: \leanid{TNLean.Lattice.mutualInformation_cores_le_of_reset_unitary}.}

\section{Information at logarithmic separation}

Part~II, Lemma~\texttt{lem:logsep}
(\path{part2a_mps_information.tex}, lines~472--488), is also proved on
the contiguous chain. For fixed $p>0$ and $k_0\ge1$, positive $D_p$ and a
natural cutoff are chosen before all system data. If $T$ is a union of at
most $k_0$ intervals and every pair $x\in T$, $y\in E$ satisfies
$|x-y|\ge D_p\log L$, then $I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-p}$ for sufficiently
large $L$. The interval family may overlap or contain empty members.
Neither disjointness nor a sign hypothesis on the original real $J$ is
added. The sign needed for the interval area law follows from the norm
bound of an actual singleton interaction.\footnote{Declaration:
    \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.ChainHamiltonian.mutualInformation_le_of_logarithmic_separation}.}

\section{Resolution: logarithmic separation on finite domains}

The logarithmic-separation lemma is also proved on arbitrary finite induced
 integer domains. Relative intervals are intervals in their increasing
 order, while separation is measured in the original integer positions,
 not in compressed labels. All constants precede the domain and instance
 data; no sign condition on $J$ is added.\footnote{Declaration:
 \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.DomainHamiltonian.mutualInformation_le_of_logarithmic_separation}.}

For a finite intrinsic distance, a shortest walk bounds the physical
 coordinate difference by that distance. Take $n=\lceil w+1\rceil$,
 $D=N_n(T)$ and $C=\Lambda\setminus N_{2n}(T)$, using intrinsic graph
 neighborhoods. The coefficient $D_p=3C_p(2+b)+4$ ensures
 $2n+w<D_p\log L$ once $\log L\ge1$. The coordinate comparison and triangle
 inequality give the core inclusions and strict reset margins.
 Each intrinsic neighborhood of a relative interval is again a relative
 interval. Subadditivity and the interval area law bound $S_\Omega(D)$,
 and hence $I_\Omega(D:C)$. The finite-domain reset theorem then gives
 $I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-p}$.

These results resolve the broader finite-domain readings of the two local
 information lemmas. They do not assert completion of the separate
 preparation protocol.
