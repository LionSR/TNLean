The theorem
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.Scan.exists_template_final_mutualInformation_of_boxEntropy}
constructs the actual scanner of every separated template and derives all
its geometric and original-state target/prefix entropy inputs.  Its
conclusion concerns the original state and the literal template regions
$X=A\cap T$ and $S_0=A\cap T_L$, with
\[
  2I_\Omega(X:S_0^c)\leq Cn^{62497/62500},\qquad
  2I_\Omega(X:S_0^c)\leq Cn^{1-1/100000}.
\]

The remaining analytic hypothesis is precisely the global uniform
safe-box estimate at the fixed exponent $e_*=2\cdot10^{-6}$:
\[
  \forall r\geq1,\qquad
  F_{\rm box}(r)\leq C_{\rm box}r^{1+e_*}.
\]
It is not replaced by a package asserting template entropy, scanner
probability, a selected low-energy point, a norm comparison or the desired
information bound.  The scanner is the accepted actual-history data type,
with its induced graph, ambient depth, support-member anchors and literal
floor/ceiling scales assigned explicitly.  The physical source theorem is
invoked once on that scanner.

The constants $\mu_R$ and $C_1=3\mu_R$ are fixed before the source entropy
coefficient.  The radius coefficient precedes that coefficient as well.
The information constant and one enlarged size threshold precede the
domain, Hamiltonian, ground vector, cut, template and scale parameters.
The premise $L\leq s_0$ is the source template-collar scale condition.
The fixed choices $C_{\rm tpl}\geq24$ and $D_0\geq1$ are sufficient
conditions for the accepted geometric estimates.  No positive core-size
premise is added.

The exact scanner-to-template truncation-set identity is exported for
later use by the existing geometric packaging theorem.  The accepted
cut bounds and compact-volume packaging are not duplicated or changed.
The actual uniform safe-box one-step improvement and its physical finite
iteration remain open in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/8756}{\#8756}.
Consequently this theorem does not complete
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/9334}{Proposition 9.6}
from the physical hypotheses alone and does not prove the full area law.
