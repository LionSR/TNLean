\section{The source claim}
For a matrix product operator representing the Fibonacci fusion category
$\{1,\tau;\ \tau\times\tau=1+\tau\}$, Garre-Rubio, Lootens and Moln\'ar state
that ``the only solution for an invariant MPS subspace is characterized by two
blocks $\{x_1,x_\tau\}$'' with $\tau\cdot x_1=x_\tau$ and
$\tau\cdot x_\tau=x_1+x_\tau$~\cite{GarreRubio2023MPOSym}.\footnote{Source
    traceability: \path{Papers/2203.12563/REsubmission.tex}, lines 1991--1993.}
The action multiplicities form a nonnegative integer representation of the
fusion ring. When the unit acts as the identity, its Fibonacci matrix
$T=M_\tau$ satisfies $T^2=I+T$.

The printed two-block uniqueness statement does not assume
indecomposability. Taken literally without that hypothesis, it is false:
$F\oplus F$ is a four-block solution whenever $F$ is the displayed regular
action, since $(F\oplus F)^2=I+(F\oplus F)$.
The local correction proved here is that every nonempty indecomposable
family has two blocks. Indecomposability is an added hypothesis correcting
the source claim, not a condition stated in the cited passage. The
unrestricted direct-sum theorem below records all decomposable solutions.

\section{The earlier restriction and its removal}
The earlier classification treated only one or two blocks. The finite-rank
restriction is now removed: every natural matrix $T$ satisfying $T^2=I+T$
is a direct sum of copies of
\begin{align}
    F & =\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:glm23_fib_regular}
\end{align}
No symmetry or irreducibility of $T$ is assumed. More explicitly, let
$K$ be its finite index set and $R=\{x\in K:T_{xx}=0\}$. There is a bijection
$\sigma:K\longrightarrow R\times\{0,1\}$ such that
\begin{align}
    T_{xy} & =
    \begin{cases}
        F_{ij}, & \sigma(x)=(r,i),\ \sigma(y)=(r,j),          \\
        0,      & \sigma(x)=(r,i),\ \sigma(y)=(s,j),\ r\ne s.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:glm23_fib_direct_sum}
\end{align}
Consequently, every unital Fibonacci nonnegative integer representation is a
sum of regular representations, and every nonempty indecomposable such
representation has exactly two blocks.\footnote{Results:

\section{An elementary proof}
The diagonal equation implies $T_{xx}^2\leq1+T_{xx}$, hence
$T_{xx}\in\{0,1\}$ and $\sum_{z\ne x}T_{xz}T_{zx}=1$.
There is therefore a unique partner $y\ne x$ with
$T_{xy}=T_{yx}=1$, and $T_{xz}T_{zx}=0$ for $z\notin\{x,y\}$.
For such $z$, the $(z,x)$ equation gives $T_{zy}\leq T_{zx}$, so
$T_{xz}T_{zy}=0$. The $(x,y)$ equation reduces to
$T_{xx}+T_{yy}=1$.

Orient the pair so that $T_{xx}=0$ and $T_{yy}=1$. For $z\notin\{x,y\}$,
the $(x,z)$ and $(y,z)$ equations give
\begin{align}
    T_{yz} & \leq T_{xz}, & T_{xz}+T_{yz} & \leq T_{yz}.
    \label{eq:glm23_fib_isolation}
\end{align}
Thus both entries vanish. The partner operation is an involution: the
partner of $y$ must be $x$, because its outgoing row has no other
nonzero off-diagonal entry. The isolated pairs partition $K$, proving
(\ref{eq:glm23_fib_direct_sum}). Symmetry follows from this decomposition.
Each pair is a nonempty invariant subset; indecomposability forces it to be
all of $K$.

\section{Remaining distinctions}
The resolved restriction concerns the rank of the nonnegative integer
representation. It does not assert that an arbitrary decomposable invariant
family has two blocks, nor does the matrix classification by itself identify
its associativity coefficients with the Fibonacci $F$-symbols. The
periodic-boundary reading of the symmetry hypothesis is recorded
separately~\cite{gap:glm23_mpo_symmetric_mps_scope}.
