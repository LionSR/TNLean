\section{The source passage}

Hastings' proof of the one-dimensional area law~\cite{Hastings2007AreaLaw}
(arXiv:0705.2024v4) reduces the strengthened doubling inequality
(lines~201--204) to the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relent}
  S(\rho_{j-l+1,j})+S(\rho_{j+1,j+l})-S(\rho_{j-l+1,j+l})
  \ge\bigl(1-2\epsilon\bigr)\frac{l}{\xi'}-\ln C_1-C_2,
  \qquad\epsilon=C_1e^{-l/\xi'},
\end{equation}
for adjacent intervals of $l$ sites (lines~238--242). Lines~413--503 prove it.
Here $\Psi_0$ is the unique ground state of the chain, $P_0=|\Psi_0\rangle
\langle\Psi_0|$ its projector, and $\rho_{a,b}$ the marginal of $P_0$ on the
sites $a,\dots,b$. The operators $O_B,O_L,O_R$ of Lemma~1 satisfy
$\lVert O_BO_LO_R-P_0\rVert\le\epsilon$. Let $\rho$ be the ground-state
marginal on the $2l$ sites, $\sigma=\rho_{1,j}\otimes\rho_{j+1,N}$,
$x=\operatorname{tr}(O_B\sigma)$ and $y=\operatorname{tr}(O_LO_R\sigma)$.
The proof shows $\operatorname{tr}(O_B\rho)\ge1-2\epsilon$, bounds $x$
(eq.~\texttt{xbd}), and bounds the mutual information below by the binary
relative entropy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LU}
  (1-2\epsilon)\ln\frac{1-2\epsilon}{x}+2\epsilon\ln\frac{2\epsilon}{1-x}
\end{equation}
(eq.~\texttt{LU}). It ends with ``$C_2$ is a numeric constant of order unity''
(line~500).

\section{Route 1 and the bound on $x$}

Hastings' note added (lines~912--914) observes that the operator $O_B$ of
Lemma~1 need not be positive, and runs the Lindblad--Uhlmann step with the
effect $E=O_B^\dagger O_B$ (route~1, lines~916--925). With $Z=O_LO_R$, the product of the two
commuting projections of Lemma~1, the bound
$\lVert O_BZ-P_0\rVert\le\epsilon$ gives:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\lVert(1-Z)\Psi_0\rVert\le\epsilon$, since $ZO_B^\dagger\Psi_0$ is
    within $\epsilon$ of $\Psi_0$;
  \item $\langle\Psi_0,E\Psi_0\rangle=\lVert O_B\Psi_0\rVert^2\ge(1-2\epsilon)^2
    \ge1-4\epsilon$;
  \item $y=\langle O_L\rangle\langle O_R\rangle\ge1-2\epsilon^2$ for the product
    state, since each factor is at least
    $\lVert Z\Psi_0\rVert^2\ge1-\epsilon^2$. This is stronger than the
    $1-2\epsilon$ of line~446.
\end{itemize}
Route~1 also gives $\lVert ZEZ-P_0\rVert\le2\epsilon+\epsilon^2$. Writing
$E=ZEZ+(1-Z)E+ZE(1-Z)$ and bounding the cross terms by the Cauchy--Schwarz
inequality for the expectation in $\sigma$ gives
\[
  x\le p+\delta+\sqrt{1-y}\,\bigl(\sqrt x+\sqrt{p+\delta}\bigr),
  \qquad\delta=2\epsilon+\epsilon^2,\quad p=\operatorname{tr}(\sigma P_0).
\]
With $p\le2\epsilon$ (lines~484--490) and $1-y\le2\epsilon^2$, this gives
$x\le20\,\epsilon$ for $\epsilon\le1/136$. The case analysis below is
therefore run with $\epsilon'=2\epsilon=2C_1e^{-l/\xi'}$ in place of
$\epsilon$. Then $1-2\epsilon'$ bounds the ground-state expectation from below
and $x\le36\,\epsilon'$. Replacing $C_1$ by $2C_1$ keeps the rate
$e^{-l/\xi'}$ and the coefficient $l/\xi'$ of the strengthened doubling
inequality.

\section{The value of $C_2$}

The formalization proves~\eqref{eq:relent} with $C_2=7$, using only $2C_1>1$
and the nonnegativity of the mutual information. It splits on $\epsilon$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item For $\epsilon\le1/68$, $x\le36\epsilon$ and $1-2\epsilon\ge1/2$ give
    \[
      (1-2\epsilon)\ln\frac{1-2\epsilon}{x}
      \ge(1-2\epsilon)\ln\frac1\epsilon-\ln 72
      \ge(1-2\epsilon)\ln\frac1\epsilon-5 .
    \]
    The inequality $\ln u\ge1-1/u$ gives
    $2\epsilon\ln(2\epsilon/(1-x))\ge-1$. Since
    $\ln(1/\epsilon)=l/\xi'-\ln C_1$ and $\ln C_1>-\ln2>-1$,
    the bound~\eqref{eq:LU} is at least
    $(1-2\epsilon)l/\xi'-\ln C_1-7$.
  \item For $1/68<\epsilon<1/2$, the right side of~\eqref{eq:relent} equals
    $(1-2\epsilon)\ln(1/\epsilon)-2\epsilon\ln C_1-C_2$. This is at most
    $\ln68+\ln2-7<0$.
  \item For $\epsilon\ge1/2$, the right side is at most $-\ln C_1-7<0$.
\end{itemize}
In the last two cases the nonnegativity of the mutual information suffices.
The doubling iteration needs $\ln C_1+C_2\ge0$, which holds since
$\ln C_1>-1$.

\section{Formalization}

\leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.Hastings.le_of_lindbladUhlmann} proves the case
analysis above for real numbers. Its hypotheses are $\Delta\ge0$ and, for
$\epsilon\le1/68$, a point $x\in(0,36\epsilon]$ at which~\eqref{eq:LU} is at
most $\Delta$. \leanid{TNLean.MPS.AreaLaw.Hastings.relentConst} is $C_2=7$.
To reach that form from the effect bound, note that the binary relative
entropy of $a$ with respect to $b$ is increasing in $a$ and decreasing in $b$
for $b\le a$. So $a=\operatorname{tr}(\rho E)\ge1-2\epsilon'$ may be lowered
to $1-2\epsilon'$, and $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma E)$ may be raised to
$x=36\,\epsilon'\le1-2\epsilon'$. That choice of $x$ also avoids proving
$x>0$.
