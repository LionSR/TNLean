\section{The source lemmas}

Lemmas~6.5 and~6.6 of the September~24, 2026 polynomial-PEPS manuscript
(\path{05-frames.tex}, lines~396--570) bound the error and circuit complexity of
operations that transfer register ownership in encoded frames on a finite
lattice $\Lambda$ of side length $L$ with site dimension $q$ and ground vector
$\Omega$.

\emph{Lemma~6.5 (homogeneous birth and death).} In a one-sheet encoded frame
$\mathcal F$, let $H^+(\mathcal F)\subseteq\Lambda$ be the union of the outer
hole footprints, let $T\subseteq\Lambda\setminus H^+(\mathcal F)$ be a set of
raw sites owned by a party $P_\circ$, and set
$E=\{x\in\Lambda:\text{the raw owner of }x\neq P_\circ\}\cup H^+(\mathcal F)$
and $U=\Lambda\setminus(T\cup E)$. If the mutual information in $\Omega$
satisfies $I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-60}$, then reassigning the raw owner of $T$ to
a party $Q_\circ$ while keeping every hole, encoding, and tag owner fixed is a
bounded change in the sense of Theorem~5.2 involving only $P_\circ$ and
$Q_\circ$, with reference-vector error at most $L^{-30}$. The reverse operation
(a death) obeys the same bound when the hypotheses hold in the frame after the
death.

\emph{Lemma~6.6 (two-sheet exchange).} Let $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2$ be two
encoded frames and $Y\subset\mathbb R^2$ a region such that every hole's outer
square lies strictly on one side of $\partial Y$. Define
$Z=\{x\in\Lambda:\text{the old raw owners of }x\text{ in }\mathcal F_1,\mathcal
F_2\text{ are not both }P_\circ\}\cup H^+(\mathcal F_1)\cup H^+(\mathcal F_2)$,
$T=Z\cap Y$, $E=Z\setminus Y$, and $U=\Lambda\setminus Z$. If
$I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-60}$, then exchanging the raw ownership assignments and
the encoded holes inside $Y$ between the two sheets can be implemented by
private contractions and register renaming using only $P_\circ$ and the owners
of the renamed registers and tags, with reference-vector error at most
$4L^{-30}$, and is therefore a bounded change in the sense of Theorem~5.2.

In both lemmas, the mutual-information bound $I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-60}$ is an
explicit hypothesis that the paper later verifies using Proposition~3.4
(\texttt{prop:angular}).

\section{What is proved}

Both lemmas are proved in Lean under their exact paper hypotheses, including
the mutual-information bound $I_\Omega(T:E)\le L^{-60}$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.birth},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.death},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.TwoSheetExchange.exchange}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.TwoSheetExchange.exchange_ofFrames}.}
At the level of the frame Hilbert spaces, the formalization establishes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a birth or death, let $\mathcal V$ be the decoupling isometry on
  $T\cup E$ with purifying spaces $\mathcal B_T,\mathcal B_E$ and decoupled
  unit vector $s$ on $T\mathcal B_T$. The transition operator
  $B=\mathcal V^\dagger(\ket s\bra s_{T\mathcal B_T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E\mathcal
  B_E})\mathcal V$ is a Hermitian contraction that acts as the identity on $E$
  and on all tag registers, commutes with every hole encoder, and approximates
  the encoded reference vector within $L^{-30}$. It factors as
  $B=(\mathcal V^\dagger S)(S^\dagger\mathcal V)$, where $S$ inserts $s$ on
  $T\mathcal B_T$, acting only on registers outside $E$ (which belong to
  $P_\circ$ before the birth and to $P_\circ$ or $Q_\circ$ after it).
\item For a two-sheet exchange with frame encodings $K_{\rm in},K_{\rm out}$
  and swap operators $F$, the register-renaming operator $\mathcal R$ satisfies
  $\mathcal RK_{\rm in}=K_{\rm out}F_{Y\cap\Lambda}$, the decoupling operator
  $D_U$ satisfies
  $D_UF_T=(\mathcal V^{\otimes2})^\dagger F_{T\mathcal B_T}\mathcal V^{\otimes2}$,
  and the composite $C=D_UF_A\mathcal R$ (where $F_A$ swaps the purifying
  factor on $U$) is a contraction satisfying $CK_{\rm in}=K_{\rm out}D_UF_T$
  that approximates the tensor product of the two encoded reference vectors
  within $4L^{-30}$. Both $D_U$ and $F_A$ act only on $U$, which is owned by
  $P_\circ$ on both sheets before and after the exchange.
\end{enumerate}

The bounded-change decompositions are proved directly on the paper's register
layout: one raw register $\mathbb C^q$ per lattice site and one register per
hole tag, each assigned to its owner party (\path{05-frames.tex}, lines~15--20
and Definition~6.1), identified by a unitary isomorphism with the block tensor
product of tags and sheets on which the frame encodings act.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.layoutRegs},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.layoutIso},
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.regs}, and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.regIso}.}
An allowed monomial (as in Lemma~5.1) is a composition of single-party
contractions, normalized two-party pair sources, normalized two-party pair
effects, and permutations of tensor factors:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A birth is implemented by a single allowed monomial from the registers of
  $\mathcal F$ to those of the updated frame, using only $P_\circ$ and
  $Q_\circ$ and at most one normalized pair source, with operator
  $\mathrm{id}_{\rm tags}\otimes B$ and reference-vector error at most
  $L^{-30}$. It permutes the site registers of $T$ and $U$ to the front, groups
  each block into one register at $P_\circ$, applies the private contraction
  $\bra s(\mathrm{id}_T\otimes V)$ at $P_\circ$, prepares the pair source $s$
  between $T$ (at $Q_\circ$) and $\mathcal B_T$ (at $P_\circ$), applies the
  private contraction $V^\dagger$ at $P_\circ$, and ungroups $T$ at $Q_\circ$
  and $U$ at $P_\circ$ (reducing to a single private contraction at $P_\circ$
  when $Q_\circ=P_\circ$).\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.birth_bounded}.}
\item A death is the adjoint sequence using only $P_\circ$ and $Q_\circ$, one
  normalized pair effect $\bra s$ between $T$ (at $Q_\circ$) and $\mathcal B_T$
  (at $P_\circ$), and no pair source.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.Frame.death_bounded}.}
\item A two-sheet exchange is implemented by a word of tensor-factor
  permutations representing $\mathcal R$ (which swaps the raw registers in
  $Y\cap\Lambda$ and the tags of holes inside $Y$ between the two sheets
  without changing any register's owner party), followed by one private
  contraction at $P_\circ$ on the raw registers of $U$ in both sheets. It uses
  only $P_\circ$ and no pair sources or pair effects, realizes $C=D_UF_A\mathcal R$
  (or $CG$ after the tag-reordering permutation $G$ when holes are listed in an
  arbitrary order), and has reference-vector error at most $4L^{-30}$.\footnote{%
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.TwoSheetExchange.exchange_bounded} and
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.EncodedFrame.TwoSheetExchange.exchange_ofFrames_bounded}.}
\end{enumerate}

In the formalization, each hole stores its discrete lattice footprint
$H^+\subseteq\Lambda$ rather than its continuous square in $\mathbb R^2$. For
Lemma~6.6, the paper's condition that every hole's outer square avoids
$\partial Y$ is represented by partitioning the holes into those inside $Y$
(whose lattice footprint lies in $Y\cap\Lambda$) and those outside $Y$ (whose
lattice footprint is disjoint from $Y\cap\Lambda$). When a hole's outer square
contains no lattice sites, the plane geometry determines which side of
$\partial Y$ it lies on, and that classification is given as part of the datum.

\section{Effect on the paper and what remains}

An earlier version of the formalization proved the bounded-change clauses only
after pre-grouping the raw registers of $T$ and $U$ into single registers. That
restriction has been removed by constructing the site-and-tag register layout
and the factor-permutation words explicitly. Lemmas~6.5 and~6.6 are now
formalized as stated in the paper, and nothing in these two lemmas remains
open.
