\section{What the sources print}
Definition~``def:2d-Ug-inj'' of \cite{Schuch2010PEPS} calls a PEPS tensor
$A$ $G$-injective for a semi-regular representation $g\mapsto U_g$, one
containing every irreducible representation of the finite group $G$, if
$\mathcal P(A)U_g=\mathcal P(A)$ and $\mathcal P(A)$ has a left inverse
$L$ with $L\,\mathcal P(A)=\Pi_U$, the projector onto the invariant
subspace.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1010--1013 and
    1278--1296.}
Definition~``def:iso:isopeps'' calls it $G$-isometric if moreover $U_g$ is
the left-regular representation $L_g\ket h=\ket{gh}$ and $\mathcal P(A)$,
restricted to its domain and range, is unitary.\footnote{Source
    traceability: \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines
    1692--1700.}
The quantum-double tensor
\begin{align}
    K=\sum_{p,q,r,s\in G}\ket{pq^{-1},qr^{-1},rs^{-1},sp^{-1}}\otimes
    \ket{p,q}\bra{r,s}
    \label{eq:rmp_peps_qd_tensor}
\end{align}
is then called $G$-isometric.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 2904--2911.}
Appendix~A of \cite{Cirac2021Matrix}, under ``The Toric Code and quantum
double models'', gives the same tensor with plaquette colors on the
virtual legs and the differences of adjacent colors as physical spins,
states that it is $G$-isometric for the regular representation, and
states that the virtual labels of the primal tensor fuse to the identity,
so that the primal tensor has a symmetry under every irreducible
representation.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}, lines 2450--2465.}

\section{Normalization}
Neither tensor is normalized. Each physical configuration of differences
arises from exactly $|G|$ colorings, those related by one global shift
$h_i\mapsto h_ig$. For vectors $x,y$ invariant under the shift,
\begin{align}
    \langle Kx,Ky\rangle=|G|\,\langle x,y\rangle .
    \label{eq:rmp_peps_qd_isometry}
\end{align}
So $K$ restricted to the invariant subspace is $\sqrt{|G|}$ times a
unitary onto its range, and $K/\sqrt{|G|}$ satisfies the printed
definition. The formal notion of $G$-isometry asks for
$\langle Kx,Ky\rangle=c\,\langle x,y\rangle$ with one constant $c>0$,
which the rescaling by $c^{-1/2}$ turns into the printed notion; the
quantum-double tensor satisfies it with $c=|G|$. For a matrix product
tensor with the left-regular representation on its bond the formal notion
is the first form of Definition~``def:iso:isopeps'',
$\mathcal P(A^\dagger)\mathcal P(A)=c\,\Pi$; under concatenation
$C^{ij}=A^iB^j$ the constants multiply, $c_C=c_Ac_B$, and for $c_A=c_B=1$
this is the source's Lemma~``lemma:iso:iso-stable-under-concat''.\footnote{Source
    traceability: \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines
    1704--1716.}

\section{Representations}
The formal notions of $G$-injectivity and $G$-isometry take the
representation as a parameter and do not require it to be semi-regular
or left-regular; each instance names the representation it uses. The
quantum-double instance shifts every color by right multiplication,
$h\mapsto hg$, which leaves the differences $hh'^{-1}$ unchanged also for
non-abelian $G$. On each leg this is the right-regular representation
$R_g\ket h=\ket{hg^{-1}}$, and the unitary $\ket h\mapsto\ket{h^{-1}}$ intertwines it with the left-regular
one. Both are semi-regular. The one-dimensional notion and the stability
under concatenation fix the left-regular representation, since their
proof uses $\Delta=|G|^{-1}\mathbb 1$. The correspondence between
virtual unitaries commuting with the symmetry and physical unitaries,
Lemma~``lemma:iso:sym-virt-can-be-done-on-phys'', uses only the isometry
of $\mathcal P(A)$ on the invariant subspace, and is stated for the
representation parameter.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1729--1761.}

\section{Concatenation in one and two dimensions}
Lemma~``lemma:iso:iso-stable-under-concat'' states that $G$-isometry is
stable under concatenation of PEPS; its proof is written for the
one-dimensional left inverse of Lemma~4.7.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1688--1689 and 1704--1716.}
The formalization proves the one-dimensional case for matrix product
tensors with the left-regular representation on the bond, and the
two-dimensional case for the contraction of one link of two PEPS tensors
with the left-regular representation on that link. In two dimensions the
virtual system of the first tensor is $W_A\otimes\mathbb C[G]^*$ and that
of the second is $\mathbb C[G]\otimes W_B$; in the basis of group elements
on the link and coordinate bases of the remaining legs, the representations
are $\sigma_A\otimes L$ and $L\otimes\sigma_B$, the contragredient of the
permutation representation $L$ having the same matrices in the dual basis.
For a unitary representation, $G$-isometry with constant $c$ is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal P(A)^\dagger\mathcal P(A)=c\,\Pi ,
    \label{eq:rmp_peps_qd_gram}
\end{align}
where $\Pi$ is the projector onto the invariant subspace. The Gram matrix of
the contracted tensor is the contraction of the two Gram matrices, and on the
link the group elements $g$, $h$ of the two projectors meet in
$\sum_{k,k'}(L_g)_{kk'}(L_h)_{kk'}=|G|\,\delta_{g,h}$, which is the step
$\Delta=|G|^{-1}\mathbb 1$ of the source. Hence
$\mathcal P(C)^\dagger\mathcal P(C)=c_Ac_B\,\Pi$: the left inverse of the
contracted tensor is $(c_Ac_B)^{-1}\mathcal P(C)^\dagger$. Entrywise
$\mathcal P(C)^\dagger_{(a,b),(k,l)}
    =\sum_g\mathcal P(A)^\dagger_{(a,g),k}\,\mathcal P(B)^\dagger_{(g,b),l}$,
so this adjoint is the contraction of the adjoints of the two factors. The remaining legs are required to carry
unitary representations; in the source every leg of a $G$-isometric tensor
carries the left-regular representation or its contragredient, so this
follows from the source's hypotheses. This item is resolved.

\section{Scope: statements about the whole network}
Two statements of the review concern the contracted lattice rather than
one tensor.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The primal tensor's virtual symmetry under every irreducible
    representation $\pi$ is formalized as the single-tensor identity
    $A_{t,r,b,l}\,\pi(t)\pi(r)\pi(b)^{-1}\pi(l)^{-1}=A_{t,r,b,l}\,\mathbb 1$
    for every entry, which follows from $trb^{-1}l^{-1}=1$ on the support,
    and for the contracted network on the torus: the virtual string that
    multiplies $\pi(g)$ for each bond with label $g$ it crosses can be
    pulled through every rectangle of sites, since on the support
    $\pi(t)\pi(r)=\pi(l)\pi(b)$ at each site and the two boundary paths
    of a grid of such squares have the same product. This item is
    resolved.
    \item The review states that the equal-weight superposition of
    plaquette colorings is the equal-weight superposition of the
    configurations obeying Gauss' law. The primary source reads the
    contracted dual network as the superposition over all color
    patterns, with a Hamiltonian whose local term makes the four spins
    of each tensor multiply to the identity, whose plaquette term does
    the same for the spins of four tensors around a corner, and whose
    two-body term enforces equal weight.\footnote{Source traceability:
        \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 2824--2827 and
        2914--2923; \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}, lines
        2460--2461.}
    On a plane, or on a disc with open boundary, every Gauss-law
    configuration is a difference of colorings. On a torus the
    differences of colorings are exactly the Gauss-law configurations of
    trivial holonomy around the two non-contractible cycles, so the
    statement holds for that sector only. This is formalized: with the
    dual tensor at every site of a torus of width and height at least
    three, the coefficient of the contracted network is $|G|$ at every
    spin configuration obeying the vertex and plaquette rules with
    trivial holonomy around one row and one column, and $0$ elsewhere.
    The factor $|G|$ counts the colorings with given differences, which
    differ by one global shift. The proof writes a coloring through the
    left color $f_v$ of each site; the spins then prescribe
    $f_{v+(1,0)}=a_vf_v$ and $f_{v+(0,1)}=b_vf_v$ for elements $a_v,b_v$
    built from the spins, and such $f$ exist exactly when the $a,b$ are
    flat around every elementary square, which is the plaquette rule, and
    have trivial holonomy around one row and one column. The review also
    prints the Gauss law as $g_1g_2g_3^{-1}g_4^{-1}=0$, where the
    identity element $1$ is meant.\footnote{Source traceability:
        \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}, line 2452.}
    This item is resolved within the trivial-holonomy sector, which is
    the whole state of the network.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Torus cut entropy: interior rectangles and region lifts}
Theorem~6.9 of \cite{Schuch2010PEPS} computes the reduced density of a
topologically trivial region for every coherent superposition of the
commuting $(g,h)$-closure states of a regular $G$-isometric tensor:
its rank is $|G|^{\partial R-1}$, its nonzero spectrum is flat, and its
entropy is $(\partial R-1)\log|G|$.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1935--1990 and 2043--2076.}
Three restricted statements toward this theorem are now formalized.

\paragraph{Strictly interior rectangles.}
Let both torus periods be at least three, so that the simple torus graph
distinguishes the four virtual legs of a site. Let $R$ be a coordinate
rectangle of positive side lengths lying strictly between the two
coordinate seams. Then the closure seams have no edge incident to $R$,
the rectangle is connected, and its complement remains connected after
the seams are removed. These connectivity facts are elementary lemmas
proved for this purpose; the source uses the rectangular partition but
does not state them. Consequently the finite twisted-cut calculation
applies, and every nonzero superposition of closure states, commuting or
not, has the same normalized reduced density on $R$, with the rank,
flat spectrum and entropy above. This is a scope restriction of the
source theorem to one family of regions, not its formalization.

\paragraph{Supplied integer lifts.}
For a region $R$ with an integer lift $L:R\to\mathbb Z^2$ that projects to
each site and preserves each internal unit step, and for commuting
closure labels $(g,h)$, the gauge
$k_v=h^{\lfloor L_x(v)/w\rfloor}g^{-\lfloor L_y(v)/H\rfloor}$ has the
closure operator as its gradient on every internal edge, including seam
edges. The internal cycle operators therefore become identities, and the
region block of each single closure state has the transported Gram
identity and rank $|G|^{\partial R-1}$ when the boundary is nonempty and
the region is graph connected. The lift is arithmetic data independent
of the group; annular regions may also admit one.

\paragraph{Lift existence under simple connectedness.}
Realize $R$ as the union of the closed unit cells centred at its sites on
the continuous torus. If this union is simply connected, the covering of
the torus by the plane gives a continuous lift, which takes integer
values at sites and preserves unit steps. It is a closed embedding onto
the corresponding union of planar squares, distinct period translates of
this planar union are disjoint, and an exterior collar of the planar
union projects outside $R$. Hence the integer lift above is derived, not
assumed, for simply connected cell realizations. Graph connectivity of
$R$ remains an explicit hypothesis of the rank statement, since cells
meeting only at corners can have a simply connected union.

\paragraph{Remaining work.}
The integer-lift gauge transports the boundary configuration of $R$ by
an amount that depends on the closure sector. Separate single-sector Gram
identities therefore do not yet give a common reduced density for
coherent superpositions. The source removes this dependence by one
controlled operation on the complement of $R$, chosen independently of
the sector. Formalizing it requires complementary loops with prescribed
seam-crossing numbers, constructed from the exterior collar of the
simply connected realization, together with the corresponding
sector-independent removal of the boundary transport. Until then,
Theorem~6.9 is formalized only for the strictly interior rectangles
above; this item remains open and is tracked in issue~\#8271.

\section{Verdict and elimination plan}
The note is a scope restriction: the review's identification of the
contracted dual network with the equal-weight superposition of all
Gauss-law configurations holds on the torus for the trivial-holonomy
sector, which is the sector formalized; the configurations of nontrivial
holonomy have coefficient $0$. The normalization and the representation
are local corrections within that restriction and are conventions that
need no further work. The pulling-through identity of the representation
operators across the contracted primal network is formalized on the torus.
For these torus statements no elimination is planned: the unrestricted
reading is false on the torus. Stability of $G$-isometry under
concatenation is formalized in one dimension and for the contraction of one
link in two dimensions. The torus cut entropy of Theorem~6.9 is formalized
only for strictly interior rectangles; its extension to simply connected
regions awaits the complementary routing described above.
