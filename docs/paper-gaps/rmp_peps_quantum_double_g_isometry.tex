\section{What the sources print}
Definition~``def:2d-Ug-inj'' of \cite{Schuch2010PEPS} calls a PEPS tensor
$A$ $G$-injective for a semi-regular representation $g\mapsto U_g$, one
containing every irreducible representation of the finite group $G$, if
$\mathcal P(A)U_g=\mathcal P(A)$ and $\mathcal P(A)$ has a left inverse
$L$ with $L\,\mathcal P(A)=\Pi_U$, the projector onto the invariant
subspace.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1010--1013 and
    1278--1296.}
Definition~``def:iso:isopeps'' calls it $G$-isometric if moreover $U_g$ is
the left-regular representation $L_g\ket h=\ket{gh}$ and $\mathcal P(A)$,
restricted to its domain and range, is unitary.\footnote{Source
    traceability: \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines
    1692--1700.}
The quantum-double tensor
\begin{align}
    K=\sum_{p,q,r,s\in G}\ket{pq^{-1},qr^{-1},rs^{-1},sp^{-1}}\otimes
    \ket{p,q}\bra{r,s}
    \label{eq:rmp_peps_qd_tensor}
\end{align}
is then called $G$-isometric.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 2904--2911.}
Appendix~A of \cite{Cirac2021Matrix}, under ``The Toric Code and quantum
double models'', gives the same tensor with plaquette colors on the
virtual legs and the differences of adjacent colors as physical spins,
states that it is $G$-isometric for the regular representation, and
states that the virtual labels of the primal tensor fuse to the identity,
so that the primal tensor has a symmetry under every irreducible
representation.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}, lines 2450--2465.}

\section{Normalization}
Neither tensor is normalized. Each physical configuration of differences
arises from exactly $|G|$ colorings, those related by one global shift
$h_i\mapsto h_ig$. For vectors $x,y$ invariant under the shift,
\begin{align}
    \langle Kx,Ky\rangle=|G|\,\langle x,y\rangle .
    \label{eq:rmp_peps_qd_isometry}
\end{align}
So $K$ restricted to the invariant subspace is $\sqrt{|G|}$ times a
unitary onto its range, and $K/\sqrt{|G|}$ satisfies the printed
definition. The formal notion of $G$-isometry asks for
$\langle Kx,Ky\rangle=c\,\langle x,y\rangle$ with one constant $c>0$,
which the rescaling by $c^{-1/2}$ turns into the printed notion; the
quantum-double tensor satisfies it with $c=|G|$. For a matrix product
tensor with the left-regular representation on its bond the formal notion
is the first form of Definition~``def:iso:isopeps'',
$\mathcal P(A^\dagger)\mathcal P(A)=c\,\Pi$; under concatenation
$C^{ij}=A^iB^j$ the constants multiply, $c_C=c_Ac_B$, and for $c_A=c_B=1$
this is the source's Lemma~``lemma:iso:iso-stable-under-concat''.\footnote{Source
    traceability: \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines
    1704--1716.}

\section{Representations}
The formal notions of $G$-injectivity and $G$-isometry take the
representation as a parameter and do not require it to be semi-regular
or left-regular; each instance names the representation it uses. The
quantum-double instance shifts every color by right multiplication,
$h\mapsto hg$, which leaves the differences $hh'^{-1}$ unchanged also for
non-abelian $G$. On each leg this is the right-regular representation
$R_g\ket h=\ket{hg^{-1}}$, and the unitary $\ket h\mapsto\ket{h^{-1}}$ intertwines it with the left-regular
one. Both are semi-regular. The one-dimensional notion and the stability
under concatenation fix the left-regular representation, since their
proof uses $\Delta=|G|^{-1}\mathbb 1$. The correspondence between
virtual unitaries commuting with the symmetry and physical unitaries,
Lemma~``lemma:iso:sym-virt-can-be-done-on-phys'', uses only the isometry
of $\mathcal P(A)$ on the invariant subspace, and is stated for the
representation parameter.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1729--1761.}

\section{Concatenation in one and two dimensions}
Lemma~``lemma:iso:iso-stable-under-concat'' states that $G$-isometry is
stable under concatenation of PEPS; its proof is written for the
one-dimensional left inverse of Lemma~4.7.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1688--1689 and 1704--1716.}
The one-dimensional case and the elementary two-dimensional contraction
of one regular bond are formalized. For the latter, use
orthonormal bases on the remaining virtual and physical spaces and the
group basis of $\mathbb C[G]$ on the contracted leg, with its dual basis
on the other end. The coefficients of the contracted map are
\begin{align}
    C_{(i,j),(a,b)}=\sum_{k\in G}A_{i,(a,k)}B_{j,(k,b)}.
\end{align}
Complex conjugation gives the adjoint by contracting the adjoints of
$A$ and $B$. Since $\Delta=|G|^{-1}\mathbb 1$, the previously constructed
left inverse is $(c_Ac_B)^{-1}C^\dagger$, and hence
$C^\dagger C=c_Ac_B\Pi$. The square-lattice statement has four regular
virtual legs at each input site; unitarity of the two remaining
three-leg actions follows from tensor-product and dual unitarity.
The same argument applies to two tensors with arbitrary unitary actions
on the remaining virtual spaces, joined along one regular bond. This resolves the
previous one-dimensional scope restriction, tracked in issue~\#8448.
The untwisted contraction of an arbitrary nonempty connected finite
region is also formalized, including regions containing closed virtual
cycles. If $V_R$ and $I_R$ count its vertices and internal edges, and
$A_v^\dagger A_v=c_v\Pi_v$, its genuine open tensor satisfies
\begin{align}
    T_R^\dagger T_R
      =\left(\prod_{v\in R}c_v\right)
        \frac{|G|^{I_R+1}}{|G|^{V_R}}\Pi_{\partial R}.
\end{align}
The bonds are unnormalized, and no group operators are inserted on
them. Expanding the local Gram identities yields a group label at
each vertex. Equality along every internal edge and connectedness
make these labels one common translation. Each internal label remains
free, contributing one factor $|G|$; the remaining boundary average
is $|G|\Pi_{\partial R}$. The boundary may be empty.

The actual block also satisfies the defining invariant-subspace isometry
relation, with the positive factor displayed above. Its accessible physical
coordinates are obtained explicitly. Applying the local map
$c_v^{-1}A_v^\dagger$ at every vertex turns the original open contraction
into the open contraction $B_R$ of the averaging projectors $\Pi_v$.
Applying the original $A_v$ to these projector coordinates recovers the
original contraction, since $A_v\Pi_v=A_v$. These are identities of
the original finite sums, for arbitrary finite regions. They do not
assert that the two rectangular maps are inverse on unused physical
subspaces.

For a connected region, choose a spanning tree and a root. There is a
reversible change from its physical half-edge labels to boundary labels
$y$, one reference label $a_e$ per internal edge, normalized vertex
labels $r_v$ with $r_o=1$, and one residual label $z_e$ per edge outside
the tree. In these coordinates the actual canonical block is
\begin{align}
 B_R(y,a,r,z;\theta)
   =|G|^{1-|R|}\Pi_{\partial R}(y,\theta)
     \mathbf 1_{\{z_e=1\text{ for all }e\notin T\}}.
\end{align}
Thus the reference and normalized vertex labels give uniform factors,
while the residual cycle labels give fixed identity factors. This
factorization is derived from the internal-bond summation, without
assuming a block decomposition. It gives an explicit form of the
accessible-coordinate and blocking arguments of
\cite{Schuch2010PEPS}.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1765--1920.}

The same coefficient formula proves $G$-injectivity of a connected
regular block from local $G$-injectivity alone. Choose local inverses
$L_vA_v=\Pi_v$ and apply their product to the actual block. In the
canonical coordinates, the row with $a_e=r_v=z_e=1$ is the nonzero
multiple $|G|^{1-|R|}\Pi_{\partial R}$. It therefore recovers every
invariant boundary vector. This proves the regular-representation
connected-region consequence of Lemma~5.2 and the following
observation of \cite{Schuch2010PEPS}, including internal cycles and an
empty boundary. The general semi-regular connected-region conclusion
is not asserted by this argument.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1318--1358.}

For arbitrary inserted bond operators $u_e$, the same physical maps
relate the actual open tensor to the canonical contraction
$B_R^u(\alpha,\theta)$ in both directions. A normalized tree gauge
$k_o=1$, $k_hk_t^{-1}=u_e$ on tree edges, makes the corresponding
residual operators $\omega_e=k_h^{-1}u_ek_t$ equal to the identity on
the tree. The residual operators on the other internal edges remain
unrestricted. The transported boundary label is
$k_v^{-1}\theta_e$ at a tail endpoint and
$k_v^{-1}u_e\theta_e$ at a head endpoint. These are actual
coordinate identities; they do not assert that arbitrary internal
twists preserve the untwisted boundary projector or its rank.

In spanning-tree coordinates, the twisted canonical coefficient is
\begin{align}
 B_R^u(y,a,r,z;\theta)
 = |G|^{-|R|}\sum_{x\in G}
   \mathbf 1_{\{y=x\theta'\}}
   \mathbf 1_{\{z_e=x\omega_e x^{-1}\text{ for all }e\notin T\}}.
\end{align}
The internal reference and normalized vertex coordinates remain free.
This identity includes arbitrary crossing operators, and follows from a
bijection between compatible internal labelings and the surviving
common translations $x$.

More generally, an arbitrary vertex gauge changes the actual open
tensor to the tensor with internal operators $k_t^{-1}u_ek_s$ and
transported crossing labels. If all these internal residuals are
identities, its ordered matrix has the form $T_R^u=T_RF$, where $F$ is
the boundary basis permutation. Local regular isometry and connectedness
then give
\begin{align}
 (T_R^u)^\dagger T_R^u
   =c_R F^\dagger\Pi_{\partial R}F,\qquad c_R>0.
\end{align}
The transported projector has rank and trace $|G|^{|\partial R|-1}$
for a nonempty boundary. The residual-flatness condition here is an
explicit hypothesis; it is not a substitute for the source's geometric
disk condition. On the closed lattice, the same endpoint gauge change
leaves the original physical vector exactly unchanged, without any
isometry assumption, by local regular invariance and reindexing the
summed bond labels.

For a native torus region with circumferences $w,H\geq3$, a supplied
integer lift $L:R\to\mathbb Z^2$ is required to project to each original
vertex and to preserve every internal rightward and upward unit step.
These are arithmetic equations independent of the group. For commuting
closure labels, the explicit gauge
\begin{align}
 k_v=h^{\lfloor L_x(v)/w\rfloor}
      g^{-\lfloor L_y(v)/H\rfloor}
\end{align}
has the native closure operator as its gradient on every internal edge,
including seam edges. Thus identity residuals, the positive transported
Gram formula, and the boundary rank follow without an additional
group-valued flatness hypothesis. Normalizing this gauge at the root
identifies it with the spanning-tree gauge. The actual canonical tensor
then has the same normalized internal ancillary vector in every commuting
sector; only its transported boundary configuration depends on the sector.

The supplied lift does not define a disk: some annular regions also
admit lifts. Conversely, a filled plaquette is a disk whose induced
vertex graph has a cycle, so acyclicity of that graph would impose the
wrong geometric restriction. A native region is realized as the finite
union of closed unit cells centered at its sites on the continuous torus.
This union is compact and locally path connected: the finite coproduct
of its compact convex squares maps onto it by a closed quotient map.
Every internal native unit-edge path lies in the two endpoint cells,
including across a periodic seam. If the realization is simply
connected, the two additive-circle covering maps give a continuous
lift to the real plane. Its values at native sites are integers, and
uniqueness of path lifts gives exactly the required integer unit-step
equations. Thus no arithmetic lift is an additional hypothesis in
this simply connected case.

The continuous lift is a closed embedding. On each native cell, it is
translation of the closed unit square by the lifted integer site, and
its image is exactly the finite union of these planar squares. Thus
the original torus cell domain is homeomorphic to this actual planar
cell union. This identifies the domain for an exterior path argument;
it does not supply such a path.

For a graph-connected native region with simply connected cell
realization, local regular isometry and commuting closure labels
therefore imply that its actual open-region matrix has rank
$|G|^{|\partial R|-1}$ whenever the boundary is nonempty. Graph
connectivity remains explicit: cells touching only at diagonal points
can be connected as a geometric union without connecting the native
induced graph. This auxiliary rank statement concerns one closure
state; it is not the source's full disk entropy theorem. The source's
complement-only controlled permutations must then remove the
sector-dependent boundary transports simultaneously. Separate
single-sector Gram identities do not establish a common reduced density
operator for coherent superpositions.

The untwisted region Gram formula is the identity-operator case of these
calculations. For arbitrary twisted open regions, the cycle and crossing
compatibility constraints remain essential. The actual torus overlap
calculation below retains the noncontractible closure operators, and
does not justify discarding those constraints on an arbitrary open region.
The residual small-period torus and stripe restrictions, together with the
remaining source results, are tracked in issue~\#8271.

\section{Actual torus closure overlaps}
The regular $G$-isometric torus closure vectors are contracted with the
horizontal $h$-seam and the downward-oriented vertical $g$-seam of
\cite[equation~``eq:2d:peps-with-ug-uh'']{Schuch2010PEPS}. For $N$ sites,
allowing site-dependent normalization $c_v>0$, their overlap is
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi_{g,h},\psi_{g',h'}\rangle
      =\left(\prod_v c_v\right)|G|^N
        \#\{x\in G:hx=xh',\ gx=xg'\}.
\end{align}
This identity is derived from the actual tensor contraction. The local
Gram averages contribute $|G|^{-N}$; the two families of free bond
labels contribute $|G|^{2N}$. Every vertex translation is constant off
the seams, and the seam equations are the two displayed intertwining
conditions. The proof includes tori with a circumference of one.

For a self-overlap the count is $|Z_G(g)\cap Z_G(h)|>0$, since it
contains the identity. Different pair-conjugacy classes give orthogonal
nonzero vectors. Consequently the family indexed by all pair-conjugacy
classes is linearly independent, as is its commuting subfamily.
Noncommuting closures are not asserted to be ground states.

The isometry assumption is now removed for regular virtual representations.
Let $T=\mathcal P(A)$ and let $L$ be the local inverse furnished by
$G$-injectivity, so that $LT=\Pi$. Applying $L$ at every physical site
sends the actual closure vector of $A$ to that of the canonical site
map $\Pi$. The latter is $G$-isometric, with factor one, so its
class-indexed closures are linearly independent. Applying the same
physical product map to a linear relation proves independence of the
original closures. In particular the commuting family satisfies the
regular-representation independence assertion in
\cite[Theorem~5.9]{Schuch2010PEPS}, with no isometry or physical
surjectivity hypothesis. The auxiliary extension to all pair classes
still makes no ground-state assertion.

Conversely, applying $T$ at every physical site to a projector closure
recovers the original closure, since $T\Pi=T$. These two rectangular
physical maps recover the corresponding actual vectors; they need not
be inverses on the full physical spaces. A physical product map changes
the coefficient matrix across any partition by
$C\mapsto F_R C F_{R^c}^{\mathsf T}$, so its rank cannot increase.
Recovery in both directions therefore preserves the Schmidt rank.
This is the local deformation argument required in
\cite[Corollary~6.10]{Schuch2010PEPS}; it does not yet supply the
source's boundary-rank formula on arbitrary contractible regions.

Independence is now also established for general semi-regular virtual
representations. After the same local inverse operation, expanding the
actual projector contraction gives one group label $q_v$ per vertex.
On every bond away from the seams, the weighted trace pairing with
$\Delta$ gives $\delta_{q_v,q_{v'}}$. Thus all vertex labels coincide.
On each seam retain one operator, and contract the remaining seam
operators with the sum of the trace-dual functionals, which takes value
one on every $U_g$. The resulting linear map $E$ satisfies
\[
 E\psi^\Pi_{g,h}=|G|^{-N}
   \sum_{x\in G}U_{xhx^{-1}}\otimes U_{xgx^{-1}}.
\]
These operator sums are independent when indexed by pair-conjugacy
classes: the tensor product of the two trace-dual functionals vanishes
on different classes and gives $|Z_G(g)\cap Z_G(h)|>0$ on its own class.
Since $|G|^{-N}\ne0$, the actual closure vectors are independent.
This proves the independence component of
\cite[Theorem~5.9]{Schuch2010PEPS}, with its semi-regular hypothesis,
without a local isometry or a contracted-region Gram hypothesis.
The construction includes noncommuting pairs as an auxiliary extension
and tori of circumference one. Ground-space membership and spanning
remain separate steps.

\section{Scope of the physical entropy calculation}
The normalized actual finite-graph PEPS has entropy
$S(\rho_R)=(\partial R-1)\log|G|$, rank $|G|^{\partial R-1}$,
and a flat reduced spectrum when every local tensor is
$G$-isometric in its regular group coordinates, the induced graphs on
$R$ and its complement are nonempty and connected, the boundary is
nonempty, and the contraction has no inserted bond operators.
The two region Gram identities are derived from the local isometries;
they are not additional hypotheses. Their positive factors give an
explicit positive normalization of the actual coefficient vector.

Theorem~6.9 of \cite{Schuch2010PEPS} concerns the torus states with
$(g,h)$-closures, including their superpositions. The finite untwisted
statement above is therefore a restriction, not the formalization of
that source theorem. The local controlled permutation
$(a,b,c)\mapsto(cb^{-1}a,b,c)$ and its inverse have been established,
including the cancellation of a twist $t$ when $c=tb$. Accessible
coordinates, exact twisted coefficients and mixed Gram matrices, and
the factorization on supplied integer-lifted regions are established above.
Simple connectedness of the cell realization now yields the geometric
lift and eliminates the region's internal cycle operators. The remaining boundary transport
must be removed by one operation on the complement, independent of the
closure sector.

The complement's spanning-tree coordinates provide a concrete route.
Write its cycle tuple as $z=x\omega x^{-1}$ on the support of the
actual coefficient. Let $m_f$ be the combined transport on crossing
bond $f$, and fix a reference bond $f_0$. The actual two boundary
equivalences compose as $F_S\circ F_R^{-1}$, and their multiplier is
$m_f=k_S(w_f)^{-1}D_fk_R(v_f)$, where $v_f\in R$, $w_f\in S$, and
$D_f$ transports from $v_f$ to $w_f$. The actual closed route from
the complementary root through $f_0$ backwards, the region tree,
$f$ forwards, and the complementary tree back to the root has
holonomy $m_fm_{f_0}^{-1}$. This identity is established for arbitrary
bond operators; the route passes through $R$.

For each actual induced complementary walk, assigning free generators
to the non-tree edges constructs a word whose evaluation at $\omega$
is its reduced holonomy. At the normalized root it is its original
holonomy. Simultaneous conjugation of the tuple conjugates the word's
evaluation. The required geometric step is to replace the route through
$R$ by a complementary loop with the same torus seam crossing numbers.
For actual commuting closure operators, a walk with crossing numbers
$(n,m)$ has holonomy $h^n(g^{-1})^m$, so equality of these numbers
gives equality of the required transports. Inside an integer-lifted
region, the crossing numbers are the differences of the endpoint
integer deck coordinates. Simple connectedness of the actual cell
domain derives the lift and hence makes those numbers independent of
the interior path with fixed endpoints.

The exterior loop with the required numbers is now constructed from the
actual simply connected cell domain. The full plane preimage of the region
is the union of integer-period translates of its planar lift; distinct copies
are disjoint even at sides and corners. The exterior collar of radius $3/4$
projects wholly outside the actual torus region. Each native crossing edge
supplies an exterior integer center in that collar, and the difference of
the two exterior deck coordinates is exactly the winding pair of the
interior boundary route.

The collar paths are derived rather than assumed. Simple connectedness
excludes finite four-neighbor components of missing integer cells. A finite
box then shows that the entire missing-cell lattice is connected. The
occupied-square contact graph is also connected, retaining shared corners.
Orient each exposed cell edge and give the finite set of these edges its
boundary-successor permutation. For a successor-invariant weight, the finite
horizontal-ray potential is constant on missing cells and on occupied cells;
its jump across an exposed edge is that edge's weight. Taking the indicator
of one successor cycle shows that every exposed edge belongs to that cycle.
Each successor transition yields an exterior four-neighbor walk supported
by a common occupied square, and every nearby missing center joins such an
exposed edge. Thus the safe exterior graph is connected. Its straight edge
segments lie entirely in the genuine collar, giving the required continuous
paths. Lattice-path approximation projects these paths to actual
induced-complement walks with their exact deck-coordinate differences as
crossing numbers. Appending the two complementary tree paths gives the
fixed root loops for all boundary routes, independently of every
closure label. No condition on group holonomies replaces the geometry.

The occupied connectivity condition must be distinguished from simple
connectedness of the closed-cell realization. Two squares meeting only at
a corner have a contractible union but no occupied four-neighbor path.
The source asks for a contiguous block of spins (lines 1931--1933), and its
blocking construction concerns an already connected block (lines 1349--1354).
Accordingly, the contiguous-region statement uses connectivity of the
actual induced occupied graph. This is the source's block condition;
it is not inferred from the topological hypothesis. The contour and collar
arguments themselves allow corner contacts.

The constructed loop word evaluates to
$m_fm_{f_0}^{-1}$. Evaluating this word on $z$ then gives
$W_f(z)=x m_fm_{f_0}^{-1}x^{-1}$. The single controlled permutation
$y_f\mapsto W_f(z)^{-1}y_f$, leaving the cycle coordinates fixed,
turns $y_f=xm_f\theta_f$ into $x m_{f_0}\theta_f$ at every crossing
bond. Its reversible coordinate permutation and unitary matrix on
the native physical half-edge space are established, as is its exact
action on the actual twisted canonical coefficient sum. The resulting
common-transport formula at the reindexed boundary input is established.
For fixed actual complementary loops with the prescribed geometric
crossing numbers, the relative-word identity is now derived for every
commuting closure pair. The resulting unitary is chosen before the
closure labels; it depends only on complement coordinates and those
fixed walks, whose existence follows from the geometric argument above.
The common remaining translation is separated by the boundary reference
coordinates in the actual canonical cut contraction. Its exact coefficient
formula factors every coherent finite sector sum into the same normalized
maximally entangled relative-boundary vector, the same normalized region
ancillary vector, and a complementary cycle-orbit amplitude. All dependence
on the reference crossing multiplier cancels in the boundary sum.
Region cycle residuals and relative-word identities are explicit premises
of the finite-graph formula; the torus specialization derives them from
the geometric lift and constructed complementary winding loops.

A canonical unitary
commuting with the product of the local invariant projectors is proved
to have a unitary implementation on the original physical region,
without requiring surjectivity of the site maps. Its product Gram
identity is derived from local isometry. For actual walk-word controls,
the commutation identity is also proved: independent vertex translations
act on the cycle labels by conjugation by the root translation, and
evaluation of a free-group word respects that conjugation. Hence the
controlled permutation commutes with each vertex translation and with
their average, the actual product of local invariant projectors. It
preserves the supported canonical physical space and has a unitary
implementation on the original physical region.
The resulting common reduced matrix is now computed in the native canonical
half-edge coordinates. For every coherent sum $C$, it satisfies
$CC^\dagger=\operatorname{tr}(CC^\dagger)\rho_R^0$, with $\rho_R^0$ fixed
before the closure labels and coefficients. The actual original physical cut
is the image of $C$ under the two products of local site maps; their positive
Gram factors and canonical support identities are derived from local isometry.
Normalizing therefore gives one fixed density on the original physical region
for every nonzero coherent commuting-closure sum. Its rank is $|G|^{b-1}$,
its nonzero spectrum is flat, and every finite nonnegative R\'enyi entropy is
$(b-1)\log|G|$. The positive-order calculation uses the genuine spectral trace
of a real power, with the rank convention at zero and von Neumann entropy at
one. For a contiguous block with actually simply connected closed-cell realization,
the induced complement is connected. Indeed, every complementary component
has a crossing bond to the nonempty occupied region, and the collar argument
joins all exterior crossing endpoints. The two spanning trees, their roots,
the boundary numbering, and the winding loops can therefore be chosen within
the proof. The common-density and entropy statements require none of these
choices as hypotheses.

The regular-torus statements retain periods at least three. Periods one or two
require a model retaining multiple bonds between the same sites and self-bonds;
the simple-graph statements do not cover them. The operation on the two
width-one coordinate-zero stripes is established for actual coherent closure
states: their complementary rectangle projects injectively from a compact
convex planar rectangle, so the common-density theorem gives a physical
complementary unitary relating any two normalized states. Identification
with the entire two-dimensional parent-Hamiltonian ground space remains
separate. This is
the general-coordinate form of the controlled operation in
equation~``eq:iso:tweezer-duplicate-operation'', leading to
equation~``eq:iso:block-disent-decomposition'' and the common reduced
state for commuting closure superpositions.\footnote{Source traceability:
    \path{Papers/1001.3807/paper_v3.tex}, lines 1935--1990 and 2027--2072.}

\section{Superpositions with complementary twists}
The Gram calculation now includes actual group operators on internal
bonds. For assignments $u,w$ that are the identity on crossing bonds,
suppose their common identity bonds contain a connected spanning
subgraph of the region. The local translations are then constant.
Let $\kappa_R$ be the untwisted region factor and put
\[
    F_{u,w}=\{x\in G:u_ex=xw_e\text{ for every internal bond }e\}.
\]
Then the actual cross-Gram operator satisfies
\begin{align}
    (T_R^u)^\dagger T_R^w
      &=\frac{\kappa_R}{|G|}\sum_{x\in F_{u,w}}L_x^{\otimes b},\\
    P(T_R^u)^\dagger T_R^wP
      &=\kappa_R\frac{|F_{u,w}|}{|G|}P.
\end{align}
The second identity uses only that every common translation acts as
the identity on the invariant boundary.

Consequently, let the original region $D$ be connected, and consider
any finite family of twists supported entirely inside $D^c$, with a
common connected spanning subgraph of identity bonds in $D^c$.
Every nonzero superposition of their actual PEPS contractions has the
same normalized reduced density on $D$:
\begin{align}
    \rho_D=\frac{1}{c_D|G|^{b-1}}T_DPT_D^\dagger,
    \qquad T_D^\dagger T_D=c_DP,\quad c_D>0.
\end{align}
Its rank is $|G|^{b-1}$ and its nonzero spectrum is flat, so its
von Neumann entropy is $(b-1)\log|G|$. The region and mixed Gram
identities are derived from local regular isometry. Positivity of the
combined complementary Gram factor follows from nonvanishing of the
actual superposition, with no extra condition on its coefficients.

This removes the absence-of-twists restriction for this finite-cut
setting. It still requires the twists to avoid every edge incident to
$D$. The native torus closure states are now identified with these
twisted finite-graph contractions. On a torus of width and height at
least three, a positive-size coordinate rectangle strictly inside the
fundamental domain has connected interior and connected complement
even after removing the closure seams. The seams have no edge incident
to the rectangle. Thus local regular isometry alone gives the same
reduced density, rank, flat spectrum, and entropy for every nonzero
superposition of native closures on such a rectangle. The boundary
count and these connectivity conditions are proved from the coordinate
bounds, rather than supplied as additional hypotheses. This restricted
calculation permits noncommuting closure pairs as well.

Commuting closure seams may also be placed at arbitrary rows and columns:
local virtual invariance removes the corresponding vertex changes of
basis, while commutation preserves the transverse closure. This deformation
identity is established for the actual torus contraction, without
