\section{Source passage}

Lemma \texttt{propSN} of Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete\footnote{Source:
\path{Papers/1606.00608/MPDO-22-12-17-2.tex:1406--1471}.} states that if
$\mathcal K$ fulfills the saturation of the area law (SAL), there exists an
isometry $U$ and tensors $l_k$, $r_k$ such that
\begin{align}
  U\mathcal K_{\beta,\alpha}U^* = \bigoplus_k
  (l_k)_\beta \otimes (r_k)_\alpha
  \notag
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
  \eta_{k,h} = \sum_a (r_k)_a \otimes (l_h)_a \ge 0,
  \notag
\end{align}
and the non-negative matrix $T_{k,h} = \operatorname{tr}(\eta_{k,h})$ is
primitive.

The passage defines $U$, $r_k$, $l_k$, and $\eta_{k,h}$ at
lines~1436--1450.  It defines $T_{k,h}$ at lines~1452--1455 and proves its
primitivity through line~1471.  It does
\textbf{not} construct the active physical support compression: choosing
isometries onto the joint column supports of the left and right factor
families per sector, giving inactive sectors zero-dimensional coordinate
spaces, and assembling the compressed injective tensor.

Proposition~\texttt{3to4}, at lines~1570--1594, uses the uncompressed
neighboring operators on the full factor spaces to form commuting positive
bonds.  The later Proposition~\texttt{prop3to4}, at lines~1786--1797,
concerns a simple tensor with a basis of normal tensors and invokes the
preceding injective result.  These are distinct source statements.

\section{The compression construction}

The three files
\doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/PhysicalSectorActiveRestriction},
\doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/PhysicalSectorActiveNeighboring}, and
\doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPDO/PhysicalSectorCoordinateTransport}
(roughly 95~declarations total) build the following auxiliary construction
after Lemma~\texttt{propSN} (lines~1406--1471).  It is used in the formal
argument corresponding to Proposition~\texttt{prop3to4} (line~1787):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Factor-support coordinates} (\texttt{SectorActiveFactorSupportData},
    \texttt{ActiveFactorSupportData}): For each active sector, choose isometries
    $V^L_k$, $V^R_k$ whose ranges are the joint column supports of the left and
    right factor families $\{l_{k,\beta}\}_\beta$ and
    $\{r_{k,\alpha}\}_\alpha$.  For inactive sectors, set both coordinate
    dimensions to zero.  The product range of each pair is exactly the
    family-support projection of the sector product family.

  \item \textbf{Compressed factors} (\texttt{compressedLeftTensor},
    \texttt{compressedRightTensor}): Form $\tilde l_{k,\beta} =
    (V^L_k)^\dagger l_{k,\beta} V^L_k$ and similarly for $\tilde r_{k,\alpha}$.
    These are the factor matrices restricted to the active coordinate spaces.

  \item \textbf{Physical inclusion} (\texttt{physicalSupportInclusion}):
    Assemble a block-diagonal isometry $V$ from the sector support isometries,
    transport it back to original physical coordinates via $U^\dagger$, and
    obtain $V : \C^e \to \C^d$ with $VV^\dagger$ equal to the canonical joint
    column support of the original physical slices.

  \item \textbf{Compressed tensor} (\texttt{activePhysicalSupportRestriction}):
    Define $K_{\mathrm{act}} = V^\dagger K V$.  This tensor lives on
    $\C^e$ where $e$ is the total dimension of the retained active-factor
    supports.  Its injectivity is proved from the injectivity of $K$ in
    \texttt{activePhysicalSupportRestriction\_isInjective}.

  \item \textbf{Neighboring congruence} (\texttt{compressedNeighboringOperator}):
    The compressed neighboring operator is the congruence of the ambient one by
    the Kronecker product of the source right and target left support isometries.
    Positive semidefiniteness follows from preservation under matrix congruence.

  \item \textbf{Coordinate transport} (\texttt{changePhysicalBasis}): The
    generic operation $M \mapsto V M V^\dagger$ changes physical coordinates of
    any MPO tensor, together with its two-site and four-site closures.
\end{enumerate}

None of these steps appears in lines~1406--1471 of CPSV16.  The source has
the block factorization and the $\eta$ construction; the compression removes
inactive dimensions as an auxiliary input preparation in the formal route to
the theorem corresponding to Proposition~\texttt{prop3to4}.  The argument is
a standard finite-dimensional linear algebra construction: the joint column
support projections absorb every physical slice on both sides, so compression
by their conjugate transposes preserves exact two-sided recovery.
