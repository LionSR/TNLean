\section{Source statement}

Section~II.B.3 of Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}
notes that the transfer matrix $E$ need not be Hermitian and ``may have some
Jordan blocks'', and assumes only that the leading eigenvalue is simple.
It then states that a connected correlator at distance $n$ has the form
\begin{align}
    C(X,Y;n) & =\sum_{i=2}^{D^2} c_{XY}(i)\,\lambda_i^n,
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_pure_exponentials}
\end{align}
a sum of $D^2-1$ pure exponentials, with decay rate set by
$|\lambda_2|$.\footnote{Source location:
\path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}:433--440.}

\section{The deviation}

Gauge the tensor so that its transfer map is trace preserving with fixed state
$\rho$; the traceless matrices are then invariant.
Equation~(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_pure_exponentials}) holds when the
transfer map, restricted to the traceless matrices, is diagonalizable. If that
restriction has a Jordan block $J=\lambda I+N$ of size $k+1$, then
\begin{align}
    J^n & =\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,n)}\binom nj\lambda^{n-j}N^j,
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_jordan_power}
\end{align}
and a correlator whose insertions detect $N$ is a polynomial-exponential
sequence rather than a sum of pure exponentials. Likewise, a uniform bound $|C(X,Y;n)|\le C_{XY}|\lambda_2|^n$
can fail at a defective subleading eigenvalue. A bound $C_{XY}r^n$ still holds
for every rate $r$ strictly above the spectral radius of the complementary map,
by the Gelfand formula.

\paragraph{Explicit example.}
On $2\times2$ matrices, with Pauli matrices $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$, fix
a real $\lambda\neq0$ and let $J$ be the linear map with
$J(1)=0$, $J(\sigma_x)=\lambda\sigma_x$, $J(\sigma_y)=\sigma_x+\lambda\sigma_y$,
$J(\sigma_z)=\lambda\sigma_z$. For $\varepsilon>0$ put
$E(Z)=\tr(Z)\,\tfrac12 1+\varepsilon J(Z)$. At $\varepsilon=0$ the Choi
matrix of $E$ is a positive multiple of the identity, so for small
$\varepsilon$ it is positive definite. Then $E$ is completely positive and
trace preserving with full Kraus rank, hence primitive, with fixed state
$\rho=\tfrac12 1$, and its Kraus operators define a tensor with $D=2$. On the
traceless matrices $E=\varepsilon J$ has a Jordan block of size two at
$\varepsilon\lambda$. For $X=\sigma_y$ and $Y=\sigma_x$ one has
$Z_\rho(X)=\tfrac12\sigma_y$ and
\begin{align}
    C(X,Y;n) & =\tr\bigl(\sigma_x\,E^n(\tfrac12\sigma_y)\bigr)
    =n\,\varepsilon^n\lambda^{n-1},
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_explicit_counterexample}
\end{align}
which is not a finite sum of pure exponentials. It also exceeds every bound
$C_{XY}(\varepsilon|\lambda|)^n$ for large $n$.

The same sequence arises from physical operators. Full Kraus rank means
that the four Kraus operators $A^1,\ldots,A^4$ of $E$ are linearly
independent, so they span the $2\times2$ matrices. Hence the maps
$Z\mapsto\sum_{i,j}O_{ij}A^iZ(A^j)^\dagger$, for $O$ a $4\times4$ physical
operator, exhaust all linear maps of the $2\times2$ matrices. Choose physical
operators $O_X$ and $O_Y$ whose insertion maps are $Z\mapsto\sigma_yZ$ and
$Z\mapsto\sigma_xZ$. Because $E$ is trace preserving, its left fixed point is
the identity. The one-point expectation of $O_X$ is
$\tr(\sigma_y\rho)=0$, so the connected two-point function of $O_X$ and $O_Y$
at distance $n\ge1$ is
\begin{align}
    \tr\bigl(\sigma_x\,E^{n-1}(\tfrac12\sigma_y)\bigr)
     & =(n-1)\,\varepsilon^{n-1}\lambda^{n-2},
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_physical_counterexample}
\end{align}
which is again not a finite sum of pure exponentials in $n$.

These $O_X$ and $O_Y$ need not be Hermitian. Hermitian observables suffice as
well. Write $O_X=H_1+iH_2$ and $O_Y=H_3+iH_4$ with $H_k$ Hermitian. The
connected two-point function is bilinear in the two operators. If it were a
finite combination of the sequences $\lambda_i^n$ for every pair of Hermitian
observables, with $\lambda_i$ the eigenvalues of $E$, then the same would hold
for $O_X$ and $O_Y$. That contradicts
(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_physical_counterexample}), so some pair of
Hermitian observables already has a connected correlator that is not a sum of
pure exponentials.

\section{Corrected statements}

The blueprint chapter on correlations states the expansion under the explicit
hypothesis that the restriction of the transfer map to the traceless matrices
is diagonalizable, and states the decay bound at every rate $r$ with
$\operatorname{spr}(E-P)<r<1$, with $\operatorname{spr}$ the spectral radius, where $P$ is the projection onto the fixed state; the rate $|\lambda_2|$ is recorded only for
the diagonalizable case. Neither statement has a formal declaration yet.

\section{Elimination plan}

Formalize the eigenbasis expansion of the centered insertion in the
diagonalizable case and the Gelfand-formula bound for the connected correlator.
The general polynomial-exponential expansion is a corrected replacement for
the source statement, not a proof of its unqualified pure-exponential form.
