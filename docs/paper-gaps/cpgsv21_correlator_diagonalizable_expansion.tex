\section{Source statement}

Section~II.B.3 of Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--Schuch--Verstraete~\cite{Cirac2021Matrix}
notes that the transfer matrix $E$ need not be Hermitian and ``may have some
Jordan blocks'', and assumes a unique eigenvalue of largest modulus which
is algebraically simple.
It then states that a connected correlator at distance $n$ has the form
\begin{align}
    C(X,Y;n) & =\sum_{i=2}^{D^2} c_{XY}(i)\,\lambda_i^n,
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_pure_exponentials}
\end{align}
a sum of $D^2-1$ pure exponentials, with decay rate set by
$|\lambda_2|$.\footnote{Source location:
\path{Papers/2011.12127/TN-Review-main.tex}:433--440.}

\section{The deviation}

In trace-preserving gauge with fixed state $\rho$, the traceless matrices
are invariant.
Equation~(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_pure_exponentials}) holds when the
transfer map, restricted to the traceless matrices, is diagonalizable. If that
restriction has a Jordan block $J=\lambda I+N$ of size $k+1$, then
\begin{align}
    J^n & =\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,n)}\binom nj\lambda^{n-j}N^j,
    \label{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_jordan_power}
\end{align}
and a correlator whose insertions detect $N$ is a polynomial-exponential
sequence rather than a sum of pure exponentials. Likewise, a uniform bound $|C(X,Y;n)|\le C_{XY}|\lambda_2|^n$
can fail at a defective subleading eigenvalue. A bound $C_{XY}r^n$ still holds
for every rate $r$ strictly above the spectral radius of the complementary map,
by the Gelfand formula.

\paragraph{Explicit Hermitian physical example.}
Let
\[
 P=\frac1{16}\begin{pmatrix}9&5&2\\3&7&6\\4&4&8\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad u=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad v=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every entry of $P$ is strictly positive, and every row and column sums to one.
Put $Q=\frac13\boldsymbol 1\boldsymbol 1^{\mathsf T}$ and
$N=uv^{\mathsf T}/16$. Direct calculation gives
\[
 P=Q+\tfrac14(I-Q)+N,\qquad QN=NQ=N^2=0.
\]
Here $u^{\mathsf T}v=0$, $u^{\mathsf T}u=2$, and $v^{\mathsf T}v=6$.
Consequently, for every $n\geq0$,
\[
 P^n v=4^{-n}\bigl(v+\tfrac32 n u\bigr),\qquad
 \tfrac13u^{\mathsf T}P^n v=n4^{-n}.
\]
The channel on $3\times3$ matrices with Kraus operators
$A^{(a,b)}=\sqrt{P_{ab}}\,|a\rangle\langle b|$ is trace preserving and unital,
with fixed state $\rho=I/3$. It annihilates off-diagonal matrices and acts as
$P$ on diagonal matrices. It is positivity improving in one step: for every
nonzero positive semidefinite matrix $Z$, the diagonal of $Z$ has a positive
entry, so every entry of $P\operatorname{diag}(Z)$ is positive.
Moreover, its nine Kraus operators span the full matrix algebra; thus the
associated tensor is already one-site injective. Its spectrum is
$\{1,\tfrac14,0\}$, with a size-two Jordan block at $\tfrac14$.

Define physical observables on the nine-dimensional one-site space by
\[
 X_{(a,b),(a,b)}=u_b,\qquad Y_{(a,b),(a,b)}=v_a,
\]
with all off-diagonal entries zero. These are Hermitian. Writing
$\Phi_O(Z)=\sum_{a,b}P_{ab}O_{(a,b),(a,b)}Z_{bb}|a\rangle\langle a|$
for the insertion map of a diagonal observable, bistochasticity gives
\[
 \Phi_Y(\rho)=\tfrac13\operatorname{diag}(v),\qquad
 \tr\Phi_X(\operatorname{diag}(z))=u^{\mathsf T}z.
\]
Both one-point expectations vanish. The fixed-point infinite-chain connected
correlator with $n$ unobserved intermediate sites therefore is exactly
\[
 \tr\!\bigl(\Phi_X(E^n(\Phi_Y(\rho)))\bigr)
 -\tr\Phi_X(\rho)\,\tr\Phi_Y(\rho)=n4^{-n}.
\]
The physical spacing of the one-site observables is $n+1$. This sequence
cannot be a finite linear combination of pure exponentials with the
transfer-map eigenvalues, nor can it satisfy a bound $C4^{-n}$ for all $n$.
Every matrix unit occurs with a strictly positive coefficient, and the
fixed state is positive definite.

\paragraph{Zero eigenvalues.}
Formula~(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_jordan_power}) is valid also at
$\lambda=0$, with the usual convention $0^0=1$ for integer powers.
A nonzero nilpotent Jordan block contributes a finite transient. Such a
contribution cannot in general be written as $p(n)0^n$: that expression
vanishes for every $n>0$, whereas the nilpotent block can remain nonzero
for several positive powers. Thus an all-distance corrected expansion
uses the binomial terms in~(\ref{eq:cpgsv21_correlator_jordan_power}), or
separates the nonzero-eigenvalue polynomial-exponential terms from the
finite nilpotent transient.

\section{Corrected statements}

The blueprint chapter on correlations states the expansion under the explicit
hypothesis that the restriction of the transfer map to the traceless matrices
is diagonalizable. It states the decay bound at every rate $r$ with
$\operatorname{spr}(E-P)<r<1$, where $P$ is the projection onto the fixed state.
Normality and trace preservation imply this complementary spectral gap; no
gap certificate or diagonalizability hypothesis is supplied separately.
The eigenbasis expansion remains unformalized.

\paragraph{Gauge covariance.}
Physical insertions and the connected left-right contraction are now gauge
covariant, by
\leanid{MPSTensor.physicalObservableTransfer_congruence_of_gauge} and
\leanid{MPSTensor.physicalLeftRightConnectedCorrelator_eq_of_gauge}.
For a normal tensor, a trace-preserving gauge $B^i=UA^iU^{-1}$ with fixed
state $\rho$ of trace one gives positive definite fixed matrices
$\rho^L=U^\dagger U$ and $\rho^R=U^{-1}\rho U^{-\dagger}$, with
$\tr(\rho^L\rho^R)=1$. The theorem
\leanid{MPSTensor.IsNormalTensor.exists_physicalLeftRightCorrelation_decay}
proves exponential decay in the original representation on independent
finite supports at all separations, including adjacent blocks.

\paragraph{Remaining normality scope.}
The source also considers tensors whose unique eigenvalue of largest modulus
is algebraically simple and whose fixed matrices may be singular. The
arbitrary-gauge decay theorem is presently proved for normal tensors, with
leading eigenvalue one. Extending it to singular fixed matrices requires the
complementary spectral projection directly, rather than an invertible
Perron gauge.

\section{Elimination plan}

Formalize the diagonalizable eigenbasis expansion and the corrected Jordan
expansion, including finite nilpotent transients. The latter replaces the
source's false pure-exponential formula. For the broader class with a unique,
algebraically simple eigenvalue of largest modulus, construct the left-right
spectral projection and prove decay on its complement without an invertible
Perron gauge.
