\section{Corrected general support-algebra statement}

The printed statement of GNVW Lemma~7 assumes only that
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B_1\otimes\mathcal B_2$ is a subalgebra.  Its
$C^*$-generated support and commutant clauses require $\mathcal A$ to be
closed under the involution.  The corrected statement used here therefore
lets $\mathcal B_1$ and $\mathcal B_2$ be finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebras
and $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B_1\otimes\mathcal B_2$ be a
$C^*$-subalgebra.  There is then a smallest $C^*$-subalgebra
$\mathcal C_1\subseteq\mathcal B_1$ for which
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A&\subseteq\mathcal C_1\otimes\mathcal B_2,
    &
    \mathcal C_1&=\operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_1).
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_least}
\end{align}
If $(e_\mu)_\mu$ is a basis of $\mathcal B_2$ and
\begin{align}
    a&=\sum_\mu a_\mu\otimes e_\mu,
\end{align}
then $\operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_1)$ is generated by all
coefficients $a_\mu$ obtained from all $a\in\mathcal A$. Its commutant inside
$\mathcal B_1$ is
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_1)'
    &=\{b\in\mathcal B_1\mid b\otimes\Id\in\mathcal A'\}.
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_commutant}
\end{align}
Apart from the necessary star-closure correction, these are the three
assertions printed in arXiv:0910.3675v2, Section~7.1, Lemma~7
\cite{Gross2012IndexTheory}.  In the v2 source, the statement and leastness
argument occupy lines 1181--1191, coefficient generation is at lines
1188--1191, and the commutant assertion is at lines 1192--1208.  The published
DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-012-1423-1}
{10.1007/s00220-012-1423-1} and arXiv v2 source are the
third-party-verifiable anchors.  The lemma has source label \path{lem:spp}.

Applying the same construction to the second factor gives the separate
two-factor containment
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A
    &\subseteq
      \operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_1)
      \otimes
      \operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_2)
      \subseteq\mathcal B_1\otimes\mathcal B_2.
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_two_factor}
\end{align}
This is GNVW equation~(22), which follows after Lemma~7 and is not itself one
of the lemma's three clauses.  It appears at lines 1211--1219 of the arXiv v2
source, with source label \path{a2Supp}. Schumacher and Werner give the
preceding coefficient-space and generated-support-algebra constructions in Section~4.3
\cite{SchumacherWerner2004QCA}; see
\path{Papers/quant-ph_0405174/qca.tex}, lines 1137--1169, with source labels
\path{a2supp} and \path{a2Supp}.

\subsection*{Local fix (star-closed input)}

The printed word ``subalgebra'' cannot be read literally as allowing an
arbitrary algebra not closed under the involution.  For example, if
$\mathcal B_2=\mathbb C$ and
$\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}\{\Id,N\}\subset M_2(\mathbb C)$ for a nonzero
nilpotent matrix unit $N$, then the $C^*$-subalgebra generated by the
coefficients is $M_2(\mathbb C)$, whose commutant is $\mathbb C\Id$, whereas
$\mathcal A'=\operatorname{span}\{\Id,N\}$.  Hence the printed commutant
identity is false without involution closure.

Schumacher and Werner distinguish the two relevant statements.  They define
the support algebra as the ordinary subalgebra generated by the coefficient
support in lines 1161--1165, and then deduce that this algebra is closed under
adjoints because its observable-algebra input is closed under adjoints in
lines 1170--1171 \cite{SchumacherWerner2004QCA}.  This is a conditional
adjoint-closure argument, not a convention that the word ``subalgebra'' itself
means ``$C^*$-subalgebra.''  The GNVW QCA application likewise applies the
construction to the image of a local observable algebra under a
$*$-automorphism in arXiv:0910.3675v2, lines 1251--1265.

Thus the star-subalgebra hypothesis is an additional hypothesis relative to
the printed sentence of Lemma~7, but it is a necessary local correction to
that sentence rather than an open mathematical restriction.  The QCA and MPU
applications both supply it.

\section{The full-matrix formal statement}

Let $I$ and $J$ be finite sets and let
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A&\subseteq M_{I\times J}(\mathbb C)
      \cong M_I(\mathbb C)\otimes M_J(\mathbb C)
\end{align}
be a star-subalgebra. Thus the formal statement makes one restriction: both
tensor factors are full complex matrix algebras.  The star-subalgebra type
records the necessary local correction above.  The open formalization scope
is the passage from full matrix factors to arbitrary finite-dimensional
$C^*$-algebra factors. For $X\in\mathcal A$, define its
two coefficient families by
\begin{align}
    X^{\mathrm L}_{rs}(i,j)&=X((i,r),(j,s)), &
    X^{\mathrm R}_{ij}(r,s)&=X((i,r),(j,s)).
\end{align}
The formal left and right support algebras are
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm L}(\mathcal A)
      &=\operatorname{alg}^*
        \{X^{\mathrm L}_{rs}\mid X\in\mathcal A,\ r,s\in J\}, \\
    \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm R}(\mathcal A)
      &=\operatorname{alg}^*
        \{X^{\mathrm R}_{ij}\mid X\in\mathcal A,\ i,j\in I\}.
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_supports}
\end{align}
They satisfy the coefficient form of leastness
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm L}(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal D
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    X^{\mathrm L}_{rs}&\in\mathcal D
    \quad
    (X\in\mathcal A,\ r,s\in J),
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_least}
\end{align}
and, when $I=\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ and $J=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$, this is equivalently
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm L}(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal D
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \mathcal A&\subseteq\mathcal D\otimes M_q(\mathbb C).
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_tensor_least}
\end{align}
The commutant assertion is
\begin{align}
    B\otimes\Id_J\in\mathcal A'
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    B&\in\operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm L}(\mathcal A)',
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_commutant}
\end{align}
with the analogous right-factor statement.

For subspaces $S\subseteq M_I(\mathbb C)$ and
$T\subseteq M_J(\mathbb C)$, write
\begin{align}
    S\boxtimes T
    &=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
      \{A\otimes B\mid A\in S,\ B\in T\}.
\end{align}
The formal counterpart of GNVW equation~(22) is the literal containment
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A
    &\subseteq
      \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm L}(\mathcal A)
      \boxtimes
      \operatorname{Spp}_{\mathrm R}(\mathcal A).
    \label{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_two_factor}
\end{align}
This is membership in the span of simple tensors with one factor in each
support algebra, rather than only the conjunction of two one-sided
containments.

\section{Why the specialization suffices for the MPU application}

The MPU appendix recalls the GNVW support algebra only after observing that
every one-site algebra $\mathcal A_z$ is isomorphic to $M_d(\mathbb C)$
\cite[lines~2320--2334]{Cirac2017MPU}.
Consequently, each two-site factor in the inclusion used there is isomorphic
to $M_{d^2}(\mathbb C)$. After choosing these matrix coordinates, the support
algebra $\mathcal R_{2x}$ is exactly an instance of
(\ref{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_least}) and
(\ref{eq:gnvw_scope_matrix_tensor_least}). Thus the full-matrix theorem is
sufficient for the support-algebra step used by the Cirac--P\'erez-Garc\'ia--
Schuch--Verstraete spin-chain argument.

The use of a star-subalgebra also agrees directly with this application.  The
map $\omega$ preserves every operation of the local star-algebra
\cite[lines~2300--2306]{Cirac2017MPU}, and the algebra whose support is taken
in lines~2313--2328 is
\begin{align}
    \omega(\mathcal A_{2x}\otimes\mathcal A_{2x+1}).
\end{align}
It is the image, under the star-automorphism $\omega$, of a local full matrix
star-algebra.  It is therefore a star-subalgebra and satisfies the corrected
input hypothesis of Lemma~7.

The restriction to full matrix factors is an open scope boundary in TNLean.
It is not a mathematical gap in GNVW Lemma~7 or equation~(22).

\section{Elimination plan}

The star-closure hypothesis is not to be removed: without it the printed
$C^*$-generated-support/commutant package is false.  To remove the open
full-matrix restriction, one should formalize the same construction for
arbitrary finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebras $\mathcal B_1$ and $\mathcal B_2$
and a $C^*$-subalgebra
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B_1\otimes\mathcal B_2$.
Choose a basis of $\mathcal B_2$ and its dual basis, extract the coefficients
$a_\mu$ with the maps $\operatorname{id}\otimes\omega_\mu$, and define
$\operatorname{Spp}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B_1)$ as the $C^*$-subalgebra generated
by those coefficients. The dual-basis expansion proves the leastness in
(\ref{eq:gnvw_scope_least}); expanding
$[b\otimes\Id,a]$ proves the commutant identity
(\ref{eq:gnvw_scope_commutant}); repeating the construction on the other
factor proves (\ref{eq:gnvw_scope_two_factor}). The present full-matrix
statements should then be derived by identifying matrix units with the chosen
basis and their coefficient functionals. This is the direct coefficient route
of the source and requires no bicommutant or density theorem.

Until this generic construction is formalized, source-labelled entries may
carry completion markers only when they explicitly state the full complex
matrix-factor restriction.
