\section{What the source states}
Let $A^i=\bigoplus_{j=1}^b\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{j,1},\dots,\mu_{j,m_j})
    \otimes A_j^i$ be a basis of normal tensors, let $\ket{\omega_j}$ be the
fixed-point pair of the normal block $A_j$, embedded in the bond space
$\C^D\otimes\C^D$ of $A$, and put
\begin{align}
    \ket{\Omega'}
     & =\sum_{j=1}^b\alpha_j\bigotimes_{k=1}^{M}\ket{\omega_j}_{R_kL_{k+1}}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_fixed_point_state}
\end{align}
After eq.~(S7) of~\cite{Malz2024Preparation} the pairs are asserted to be
locally orthogonal,
\begin{align}
    \braket{\omega_j}{\omega_{j'}} & =\delta_{jj'},
    \label{eq:mswc24_local_orthogonality}
\end{align}
with a reference to the canonical-form theory of matrix product density
operators. The paragraph after eq.~(19) then concludes that
$W\colon\ket{j}\mapsto\ket{\omega_j}$ is an isometry and that
$\ket{\Omega'}=W^{\otimes M}\ket{\chi_M}$ with
$\ket{\chi_M}=\sum_j\alpha_j\ket{j}^{\otimes M}$. In the source,
(\ref{eq:mswc24_local_orthogonality}) is a property of these particular pairs,
not a hypothesis.

\section{What is already formalized}
For an arbitrary pair family the identity
$\ket{\Omega'}=W^{\otimes M}\ket{\chi_M}$ is algebraic. The isometry of $W$
and unit norm of the state require orthonormal pairs, and normalization also
requires $M\geq1$ and a nonzero coefficient vector.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.isIsometry_pairIsometry},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.nonNormalFixedPointState_eq_tensorPower_mulVec_ghzState}, and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.nonNormalFixedPointState_norm_sq} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/NonNormalFixedPoint}.}

For a left-canonical basis of normal tensors these pair hypotheses have
already been discharged using the actual stationary matrices of the blocks.
Each block has a unique density matrix $\sigma_j$ with
$\mathcal E_{A_j}(\sigma_j)=\sigma_j$, and irreducibility gives $\sigma_j>0$.
Place $\operatorname{vec}(\sqrt{\sigma_j})$ on both virtual coordinates of one
chosen copy $k_j$ of block $j$. Its squared norm is
$\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_j=1$, and distinct blocks occupy disjoint virtual
coordinates. Thus these actual canonical pairs are orthonormal and $W$ is an
isometry, with no supplied fixed-point or pair-normalization
hypothesis.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.basisFixedPoint_spec},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.inner_basisFixedPointPair}, and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.isIsometry_pairIsometry_basisFixedPointPair}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/CanonicalFixedPoint}.}
This does not require a block form for a finite positive part.

The corrected one-copy state at $N=qM$ uses
\begin{align}
    c_j&=\left(\sum_k|\mu_{j,k}|^{2q}\right)^{1/2},
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_copy_norm}\\
    \beta'_j&=\left(\sum_k\mu_{j,k}^{qM}\right)
        \left(\frac{c_j}{\mu_{j,k_j}^{q}}\right)^M,
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_copy_weight}\\
    \alpha'_j&=\frac{\beta'_j}{(\sum_l|\beta'_l|^2)^{1/2}}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_normalized_weight}
\end{align}
With the coefficients (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_copy_norm})--(\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_normalized_weight}),
its GHZ identity is formalized, and its unit norm is proved for $M\geq1$
when $(\sum_k\mu_{j,k}^{qM})_j\neq0$. For $q\geq1$ its image under the
polar factor of the actual blocked canonical tensor equals the corrected
copy-isometry construction before normalization, and the normalized
physical overlaps agree.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.oneCopyFixedPointState_eq_tensorPower_mulVec_ghzState},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.oneCopyFixedPointState_norm_sq},
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.nonNormalApproxVector_oneCopyFixedPoint}, and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.IsBNTCanonicalForm.nonNormalApproxOverlap_oneCopyFixedPoint}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/CanonicalOneCopyState}.}
For multiplicity one, (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_copy_weight}) gives
$\beta'_j=|\mu_j|^N$. Replacing these coefficients by
those printed in (S7) would be a different statement and is refuted by the
phase and multiplicity examples in
\cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap,gap:mswc24_repeated_block_corrected_state}.

\section{Why a finite positive-part block form cannot discharge the gap}
Consider multiplicity one and unit weights. Let $B_q$ be the map from the two
virtual bond coordinates to the $q$ physical sites whose matrix entries are
the coefficients of $A^{i_1}\cdots A^{i_q}$, and write its polar decomposition
as $B_q=V_qP_q$. Define $B_{j,q}$ in the same way from the block $A_j$.
Let $E_j\colon\mathbb C^{D_j}\to\mathbb C^D$ be the coordinate inclusion of
block $j$, and put $K_j=E_j\otimes E_j$. These pair embeddings satisfy
$K_j^\dagger K_j=I$ and $K_j^\dagger K_{j'}=0$ for $j\neq j'$.
The direct sum gives $B_q=\sum_j B_{j,q}K_j^\dagger$, so
\begin{align}
    K_j^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_q K_{j'}&=B_{j,q}^\dagger B_{j',q}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_kernel_identity}
\end{align}
If $P_q=(B_q^\dagger B_q)^{1/2}$ were block diagonal along these embeddings,
then $P_q^2$ would be too. By (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_kernel_identity}), this forces
every mixed Gram matrix $B_{j,q}^\dagger B_{j',q}$ to vanish for $j\neq j'$.
Conversely, vanishing mixed Gram matrices gives
$P_q=\sum_jK_j(B_{j,q}^\dagger B_{j,q})^{1/2}K_j^\dagger$ by uniqueness of
the positive square root. The sufficient direction is formalized for
orthogonal summands; orthogonality is an additional hypothesis, not a
consequence of normality.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{Matrix.polarIso_sum_of_orthogonal} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OrthogonalSumPolar}.}

For $A_1=(1,0)$ and $A_2=(3/5,4/5)$, both of multiplicity one and weight one,
put $s_q=(3/5)^q>0$. The active Gram matrix is
\begin{align}
    G_q&=\begin{pmatrix}1&s_q\\s_q&1\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_active_gram}
\end{align}
The nonzero off-diagonal entries in (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_active_gram}) show that
the finite block form fails at every $q\geq1$, while the canonical
pairs $e_{00},e_{11}$ are exactly orthonormal. The existing formal
counterexample also gives
$\|P_q-JJ^\dagger\|\geq(3/5)^q/2>0$, where $J\ket j=\ket{j,j}$;
see \cite{gap:mswc24_block_form_mixed_overlap}.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.diagGram_overlappingBlockDiag} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.overlappingBlockOverlap_div_two_le_norm_polarPos_sub_limit}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OverlappingBlockCounterexample}.}
This is a nondegenerate obstruction to the proposed finite block form,
not a counterexample to local orthogonality of the limiting canonical pairs.

\section{Corrected limiting scope and remaining identification}
For multiplicity one and unit weights, let
\begin{align}
    P_\infty&=\sum_jK_j\bigl((\sqrt{\sigma_j})^T\otimes I\bigr)K_j^\dagger.
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_limiting_positive_part}
\end{align}
For normal left-canonical blocks with faithful trace-one fixed points, choose
$0<|\lambda_2|<1$ bounding the nontrivial eigenvalues of each diagonal transfer
map and all eigenvalues of each mixed transfer map. For $0<\gamma<1$ the
formalized estimate, for every $q\geq1$, is
\begin{align}
    \|P_q-P_\infty\|&\leq K e^{-\gamma q/\xi},
    \label{eq:mswc24_ghz_polar_limit_rate}
\end{align}
where $\xi$ is the correlation length of $\lambda_2$ and $K$ is independent
of $q$.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPSTensor.blockSumPosLimit} and
    \leanid{MPSTensor.exists_norm_polarPos_blockTensor_blockSum_sub_le} in
    \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Preparation/OverlappingBlockGram}.}
The estimate (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_polar_limit_rate}) concerns matrices. It does
not assert equality at finite $q$ or convergence of states uniformly over an
increasing number of sites.

A separate explicit identification remains to be formalized: read
$P_\infty$ from (\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_limiting_positive_part}) as a tensor with
physical index in $\mathbb C^D\otimes\mathbb C^D$
and show that its unnormalized periodic vector on $M\geq1$ sites is
$\sum_j\bigotimes_{k=1}^M\ket{\omega_j}_{R_kL_{k+1}}$, with the same pair
embeddings and cyclic regrouping. Its normalized GHZ coefficients in the
unit-weight case would be $b^{-1/2}$ for $b\geq1$. This is an identification
of the limiting positive-part tensor; the already proved canonical-pair
GHZ identity does not itself establish it. Weighted or repeated blocks
require their weight-dependent construction and the corrected coefficients
(\ref{eq:mswc24_ghz_normalized_weight}), rather than this unit-weight limit.

Issue \#10575 therefore remains open for reconciliation of the source and
this corrected limiting statement. Its proposed finite positive-part block
form must not be an acceptance criterion. Local orthogonality of the actual
canonical pairs and the corrected one-copy GHZ identity are already proved;
neither the false finite block form nor the uncorrected coefficients of
(S7) are established by those results.
