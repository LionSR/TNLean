\section{The printed claim}

Let $U$ be a matrix product unitary with $U^2=\mathbf 1$ exchanging two
symmetry-broken ground states $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ and
$\lvert\psi_B\rangle$, and let $\omega$ be its anomaly. For an interval
$[i,j]$, Ref.~\cite{GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall} obtains a truncation
$U^{[i,j]}$ by writing $U$, after blocking, as a unitary circuit of depth two
with nearest-neighbour gates~\cite{Cirac2017MPU} and dropping the gates outside
the region. It asserts that $U^{[i,j]}$ is unitary, equals $U$ inside the region,
and creates a pair of domain walls around $i$ and $j$. For sites
$i_2<i_1<j_1<j_2$ at mutual distances much larger than the correlation length,
it then states
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi_A\rvert\,(U^{[i_2,j_2]})^\dagger(U^{[i_1,j_1]})^\dagger
    U^{[i_2,j_2]}U^{[i_1,j_1]}\,\lvert\psi_A\rangle
     & =\omega,
    \label{eq:gs24td_detector}
\end{align}
and, for an on-site symmetry $U=u^{\otimes N}$ with the truncation
$U^{[a,b]}=\bigotimes_{i\in[a,b]}u^{[i]}$, that the left side
is~$1$.\footnote{Source traceability: \path{Papers/2405.00439/MPU-DW.tex},
    lines 2077--2106; the display is \texttt{detecZ2}.}

\section{A counterexample}

Let $X$ and $Z$ be the Pauli matrices on $\mathbb C^2$, so that
\begin{align}
    X^2 & =Z^2=\mathbf 1, & ZXZ & =-X.
    \label{eq:gs24td_pauli}
\end{align}
Take the on-site symmetry $U=X^{\otimes N}$ on a periodic chain of even length
$N$. Its anomaly is trivial, $\omega=1$, and $\lvert\psi_A\rangle=\lvert0\cdots0\rangle$,
$\lvert\psi_B\rangle=\lvert1\cdots1\rangle$ are the two symmetry-broken ground
states of $H=-\sum_iZ_iZ_{i+1}$.

\paragraph{A depth-two circuit for $U$.} Let the first layer apply the gate
$X\otimes ZX$ to every pair of sites $(2k,2k+1)$, and let the second layer apply
the gate $Z\otimes\mathbf 1$ to every pair $(2k+1,2k+2)$. Since $Z\cdot ZX=X$, the
product of the two layers is
\begin{align}
    \Bigl(\prod_kZ_{2k+1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_kX_{2k}(ZX)_{2k+1}\Bigr)
     & =X^{\otimes N}.
    \label{eq:gs24td_circuit}
\end{align}
Let $i$ be even and $j$ odd. The gates contained in $[i,j]$ are the first-layer
gates on all pairs $(2k,2k+1)$ in $[i,j]$ and the second-layer gates on the pairs
$(2k+1,2k+2)$ with $2k+2\leq j$. Every odd site of $[i,j]$ except $j$ keeps both
factors $Z\cdot ZX=X$, while the site $j$ keeps only $ZX$. Dropping all other
gates therefore gives
\begin{align}
    U^{[i,j]}
     & =Z_j\prod_{k\in[i,j]}X_k.
    \label{eq:gs24td_truncation}
\end{align}
It is unitary, equals $U$ on $[i,j-1]$, and acts trivially outside $[i,j]$.

\paragraph{The detector.} Let $i_2<i_1<j_1<j_2$ with $i_1,i_2$ even and
$j_1,j_2$ odd, and write $P_r=\prod_{k\in[i_r,j_r]}X_k$, so that
$U^{[i_r,j_r]}=Z_{j_r}P_r$. The operators $P_1$ and $P_2$ commute and square to
the identity. The site $j_2$ lies outside $[i_1,j_1]$, so $Z_{j_2}$ commutes with
$P_1$ and $Z_{j_1}$; the site $j_1$ lies in $[i_2,j_2]$, so
$Z_{j_1}P_2Z_{j_1}=-P_2$ by (\ref{eq:gs24td_pauli}). Hence
\begin{align}
    (U^{[i_2,j_2]})^\dagger(U^{[i_1,j_1]})^\dagger U^{[i_2,j_2]}U^{[i_1,j_1]}
     & =P_2Z_{j_2}\,P_1Z_{j_1}\,Z_{j_2}P_2\,Z_{j_1}P_1
    \notag                                                     \\
     & =P_2P_1\,(Z_{j_1}P_2Z_{j_1})\,P_1
    \notag                                                     \\
     & =-\mathbf 1.
    \label{eq:gs24td_minus_one}
\end{align}
The expectation value on every normalized state, $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ included,
is $-1\neq\omega$. The truncations are circuit truncations of $U$ in the sense of
the source, the four sites can be arbitrarily far apart, and the data are
nondegenerate, so (\ref{eq:gs24td_detector}) is false as printed. The plain truncation
$X^{\otimes[a,b]}$ of the source's on-site remark comes from the circuit with
first layer $X\otimes X$ and trivial second layer; the two circuits differ only
by the unitary $Z$ on the leg between the layers.

\paragraph{The general mechanism.} The circuit of a matrix product unitary is
determined only up to unitaries inserted between the layers, and the
endpoint-coordinate truncation of Ref.~\cite{FrancoRubio2025MPUGauging} is
determined only up to the identifications of its endpoint
spaces~\cite{gap:fbc25_truncated_symmetry_endpoint_unitarity_scope}. Either
freedom replaces $U^{[i_1,j_1]}$ by $WU^{[i_1,j_1]}$ for a unitary $W$ near an
endpoint. If $W$ is near $j_1$, where $U^{[i_2,j_2]}$ acts as $U$, then
$U^{[i_2,j_2]}W=\widetilde WU^{[i_2,j_2]}$ with $\widetilde W=UWU^\dagger$, also
near $j_1$. The detector then becomes
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi_A\rvert\,(U^{[i_2,j_2]})^\dagger(U^{[i_1,j_1]})^\dagger
    \,W^\dagger\widetilde W\,U^{[i_2,j_2]}U^{[i_1,j_1]}\,\lvert\psi_A\rangle,
    \label{eq:gs24td_dressing}
\end{align}
and the local operator $W^\dagger\widetilde W$ need not act by $1$. In
the counterexample, $W=Z_{j_1}$ and $W^\dagger\widetilde W=Z(XZX)=-\mathbf 1$.

\section{What the argument needs}

The source's argument treats $U^{[i,j]}$ on the states it meets as the
domain-wall string $O^{[i,j]}$ of its Section~III.E, whose endpoint tensors
create the walls $e_{AB}$ and $e_{BA}$ on $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ and the walls
$e_{BA}$ and $e_{AB}$ on $\lvert\psi_B\rangle$, the second pair being the images
of the first under $U$ with the phases $c_{AB}$ and $c_{BA}$. For a truncation the
needed property is: \emph{the walls that $U^{[i,j]}$ creates on
    $\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ are the images under $U$ of the walls it creates on
    $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$.} With this property the two orders
$U^{[i_2,j_2]}U^{[i_1,j_1]}\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ and
$U^{[i_1,j_1]}U^{[i_2,j_2]}\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ are both multiples of one state
with four domain walls, the first acquiring $c_{AB}c_{BA}=\omega$ when the outer
truncation passes over the walls of the inner one, and
(\ref{eq:gs24td_detector}) follows from unitarity. Without it the two states are
built from different walls, and the detector compares them.

In the counterexample the property fails. The truncation
$U^{[i,j]}=Z_jP$ sends $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ to $-\lvert0\cdots01\cdots10\cdots0\rangle$
and $\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ to $+\lvert1\cdots10\cdots01\cdots1\rangle$, whereas
$U$ sends the first state to $-\lvert1\cdots10\cdots01\cdots1\rangle$: the walls
created on $\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ differ from the images of those created on
$\lvert\psi_A\rangle$ by the sign of the wall at~$j$.

\section{The formal statements}

The counterexample is formalized for any two sets of sites $S_1$, $S_2$ and
sites $j_1\in S_2$, $j_2\notin S_1$, $j_1\neq j_2$: the commutator of
$Z_{j_1}X^{S_1}$ and $Z_{j_2}X^{S_2}$ is
$-\mathbf 1$.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{Matrix.groupCommutator_dressed_onSiteTruncation_pauliX} and
    \leanid{Matrix.star_dotProduct_groupCommutator_dressed_onSiteTruncation_pauliX}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/MPU/TruncationCommutator}.}

The detection is formalized as a scope restriction that makes the needed
property a hypothesis. Let $O_1=O^{[i_1,j_1]}$ and $O_2=O^{[i_2,j_2]}$ be the
domain-wall strings of Ref.~\cite{GarreRubioSchuch2024DomainWall} with the three
inner regions longer than the buffer of the local
action~\cite{gap:gs24_domain_wall_nondegenerate}. If $U_1$ and $U_2$ are unitary with
\begin{align}
    U_2U_1\lvert\psi_A\rangle & =O_2O_1\lvert\psi_A\rangle, &
    U_1U_2\lvert\psi_A\rangle & =O_1O_2\lvert\psi_A\rangle,
    \label{eq:gs24td_hypothesis}
\end{align}
then
\begin{align}
    \langle\psi_A\rvert\,U_2^\dagger U_1^\dagger U_2U_1\,\lvert\psi_A\rangle
     & =c_{AB}c_{BA}\,\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_A\rangle
    =\omega(g,g,g)\,\omega(g,e,g)\,\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_A\rangle,
    \label{eq:gs24td_restricted}
\end{align}
where the second equality holds for an involution $g$ of a normal
representation, and $\omega(g,e,g)=1$ for fusion tensors trivial on the
identity, as in the source.\footnote{Formal declarations:
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.BlockActionData.star_dotProduct_groupCommutator_mulVec_mpv_of_wallString}
    and
    \leanid{MPOTensor.GroupFamily.BlockActionData.star_dotProduct_groupCommutator_mulVec_mpv_eq_omega}
    in \doclink{TNLean/MPS/Symmetry/MPOSymmetry/DomainWallDetector}.}
The hypotheses (\ref{eq:gs24td_hypothesis}) hold for truncations with the
property of the previous section, and fail in the counterexample.

\textbf{Verdict: false as printed, open.} The value of
(\ref{eq:gs24td_detector}) depends on the choice of truncation; it equals
$\omega$ for truncations whose walls on $\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ are the images
under $U$ of their walls on $\lvert\psi_A\rangle$. Elimination plan: identify a
class of circuit or endpoint-coordinate truncations with this property and prove
(\ref{eq:gs24td_hypothesis}) for it. The same dependence must be examined for
the detection of the general-group phases \texttt{detectG} (lines 2108--2114).
