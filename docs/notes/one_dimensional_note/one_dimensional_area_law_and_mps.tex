\title{A one-dimensional area law and a matrix product state
approximation from a global spectral gap:\\
the collar-free route}
\author{Prepared for Sirui Lu (MPQ)}
\date{October 16, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We take the two September 2026 manuscripts \emph{A two-dimensional area
law from a global spectral gap} \cite{AL} and \emph{Polynomial PEPS
approximation of gapped square-grid ground states} \cite{PP} and run
their arguments in one dimension. Two things happen. First, the area-law
proof collapses: the marginal tail bound and the buffered conditional-entropy
discount of \cite[Section~2]{AL}, together with the Araki--Lieb inequality,
already prove $S(X)\le C(q,R)\,(1+J/\Delta)$ for every interval $X$. The
sub-volume recursion, the positive quasi-local constraints, the replica
scanner, the amplification and the two-family geometry of \cite{AL} are
not needed. The reason is geometric: a shell around an interval has a
bounded number of crossing interactions, so the discount costs a constant
instead of a boundary-sized error, and the buffer whose entropy must be
controlled is a constant number of sites. Second, the tensor-network
theorem survives as a linear \emph{sweep} instead of a dyadic hierarchy.
The encoded holes, adaptive patches, angular information estimates and
row-column routing of \cite{PP} disappear; what remains are the
Uhlmann-type birth and exchange moves, the information reset by a
Gaussian filter, the dimension-free density compression and the
column-extraction lemma. The result is a matrix product state of bond
dimension $CL^{c}$ with vector error $L^{-1}$. We also record that in one
dimension the tail bound makes the matrix product state theorem a
two-line corollary of the area law by sequential Schmidt truncation.
Every statement in this note is written twice: once in words, once
precisely. The area-law proof uses none of the techniques of Hastings or
of Arad--Kitaev--Landau--Vazirani; the tensor-network proof uses a
Lieb--Robinson bound in exactly one lemma, and we indicate how to avoid
it at the price of a quasi-polynomial bond dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}

\begin{inwords}
Both source papers are long because two dimensions is hard. The
entanglement of a region is produced along its boundary, and in two
dimensions the boundary of a region of linear size $r$ has $r$ sites: every
estimate that costs ``a boundary'' costs $r$, and every buffer that one
puts around a region to isolate it is itself a region whose entanglement
must be controlled. The whole replica, scanner and amplification apparatus
exists to break this circularity. In one dimension the boundary of an
interval is two points. An estimate that costs a boundary costs a
constant, and a buffer of constant width has, trivially, constant
entropy. So the circularity never arises, and one short section of the
two-dimensional paper proves the whole area law. This is the content of
Part~\ref{part:arealaw}.

The tensor-network theorem is a different story. It does not follow from
entropy bounds alone (the papers explain why: an entropy bound on each cut
does not give a global vector approximation). Its two-dimensional proof
distributes the ground vector among ``parties'' attached to dyadic
squares, by local changes of ownership whose errors are controlled by
information estimates, then compresses the resulting distributed density
into a tensor network. In one dimension a dyadic hierarchy is the wrong
geometry: a binary tree drawn on a line has cutwidth $\log L$, which turns
polynomial bond dimension into quasi-polynomial. The right geometry is a
sweep from left to right with a moving ``remainder'' party. All the
genuinely one-dimensional difficulties (what to do at the ends of a band
when a third owner is adjacent) are solved by exactly the seven-move word
trick that the two-dimensional paper uses along an edge. This is
Part~\ref{part:mps}. We also show that, in one dimension, the tail bound
of Part~\ref{part:arealaw} makes the tensor-network theorem a corollary
of the area law by the classical sequential-truncation argument; the
sweep protocol is the faithful one-dimensional analogue of the
two-dimensional construction, and it is what generalizes.

Table~\ref{tab:map} is a map from the two-dimensional proofs to this
note. The reader who wants only the one-dimensional area law can read
Sections~\ref{sec:setting}--\ref{sec:closing}; this is about four pages.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
Throughout, $q\ge 2$ and $R\ge 1$ are integers and $J,\Delta>0$. A finite
set $\Lam\subset\Z$ carries the induced path graph (edges between
consecutive integers both in $\Lam$) and graph distance $d_\Lam$.
Each site carries $\C^q$ and $\cH_\Lam=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lam}\C^q$.
An admissible Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ham}
 H=\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne X\subseteq\Lam\\ \diam_{d_\Lam}X\le R}}h_X,
 \qquad h_X=h_X^*,\qquad \norm{h_X}\le J,
\end{equation}
one summand per support, zero summands allowed. The standing spectral
hypothesis is that $H$ has a unique normalized ground vector $\Om$ (up to
phase), ground energy $E_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 H-E_0\id\ \ge\ \Delta\bigl(\id-\ket\Om\bra\Om\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $A\subseteq\Lam$, $\rho_A=\Tr_{\Lam\setminus A}\ket\Om\bra\Om$,
$S(A)=S_\Om(A)=-\Tr\rho_A\log\rho_A$ with natural logarithms and
$0\log 0=0$, $S(A|B)=S(AB)-S(B)$ and $I(A:B)=S(A)+S(B)-S(AB)$ for disjoint
$A,B$. The edge boundary is
$\partial_\Lam A=\{\{x,x+1\}\subseteq\Lam:\ \text{exactly one of }x,x+1\text{ lies in }A\}$.
Constants $C,c$ depend only on $q,R,J,\Delta$ unless stated otherwise and
may change from line to line; where a dependence is tracked it is said so.
The two theorems of the note are the following.

\begin{theorem}[One-dimensional area law]\label{thm:A}
There is $C_{\mathrm{AL}}=C_{\mathrm{AL}}(q,R,J,\Delta)$, with
\[
 C_{\mathrm{AL}}\le C_0(q,R)\,\Bigl(1+\frac J\Delta\Bigr),
\]
such that for every finite $\Lam\subset\Z$, every admissible Hamiltonian
satisfying \eqref{eq:gap}, and every $A\subseteq\Lam$,
\[
 S_\Om(A)\le C_{\mathrm{AL}}\,\abs{\partial_\Lam A}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[One-dimensional matrix product state approximation]\label{thm:B}
Fix $q,R,J,\Delta$. There are $C,c>0$ depending only on these parameters
such that for every $L\ge2$, every admissible Hamiltonian on
$\Lam=\{1,\dots,L\}$ satisfying \eqref{eq:gap}, there is a nonzero matrix
product state $\Phi$ on $\Lam$ with open boundary conditions and bond
dimension at most $CL^{c}$ such that
\[
 \min_{\theta\in\R}\Bigl\|\frac{\Phi}{\norm\Phi}-\e^{i\theta}\Om\Bigr\|\le L^{-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The constants never depend on $\Lam$, on $A$, or on the particular
Hamiltonian. No gap for a restricted Hamiltonian, no frustration
freeness, no translation invariance is assumed.
\part{The one-dimensional area law}\label{part:arealaw}

\section{Setting, reductions, and counting}\label{sec:setting}

\begin{inwords}
Three small reductions make the rest clean. If the domain $\Lam$ has
several components, the Hamiltonian is a sum over components (every
support has finite graph diameter, so it lies in one component), the
unique ground vector is a product over components, and entropy and edge
boundary both add over components. So we may take $\Lam$ to be an
interval. A subset $A$ of an interval is a union of maximal subintervals,
each of which contributes at least one boundary edge, and subadditivity
reduces the theorem to intervals. Finally, the only two counting facts
we need are these: an interval is cut at two points at most, and an
interaction of diameter at most $R$ that crosses a cut lives in a window
of $2R$ sites around that cut. So the number of interactions crossing
the boundary of an interval, and the logarithm of their joint dimension,
are bounded by constants depending only on $R$ and $q$. This is the
entire ``area'' input of the one-dimensional proof.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
For an interval $X\subseteq\Lam$ and a support $Y$ with
$\diam_{d_\Lam}Y\le R$, say that $Y$ is \emph{split} by $X$ if
$Y\cap X\ne\varnothing$ and $Y\setminus X\ne\varnothing$. Since $Y$ is
contained in $R+1$ consecutive integers and contains a point on each
side of one of the two cut edges of $X$, it lies in one of the two windows
of $2R$ consecutive sites centered at a cut edge. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 \#\{Y:\ Y\text{ split by }X\}\le 2\cdot 2^{2R}=:N_R,
 \qquad
 \dim\cH_Y\le q^{R+1},\qquad
 \ell_R:=\log(\e q^{R+1}),
\end{equation}
and a term $h_Y$ split by $X$ can be expanded by matrix units on
$Y\cap X$ as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrixunits}
 h_Y=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_Y}c_\alpha\otimes d_\alpha,\qquad
 N_Y\le q^{2(R+1)},\qquad \norm{c_\alpha}\,\norm{d_\alpha}\le J,
\end{equation}
with $c_\alpha$ supported in $X$ and $d_\alpha$ in $\Lam\setminus X$.
(Each matrix unit $E_{ab}$ on $\cH_{Y\cap X}$ has norm one, and the
coefficient operator $d_\alpha=\Tr_{Y\cap X}[(E_{ba}\otimes\id)h_Y]$ has
norm at most $\norm{h_Y}$.) Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BR}
 \cB_R:=1+N_R\,\ell_R^2 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Reductions]\label{lem:reductions}
It suffices to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:A} for $\Lam$ an interval and $A$
an interval with $\partial_\Lam A\ne\varnothing$, with a constant
$C_{\mathrm{int}}$ bounding $S_\Om(A)$; then Theorem~\ref{thm:A} holds
with $C_{\mathrm{AL}}=C_{\mathrm{int}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Lam_1,\dots,\Lam_k$ be the components of $\Lam$ (maximal intervals).
Every support of finite graph diameter lies in one component, so
$H=\sum_iH_i$ with $H_i$ acting on $\cH_{\Lam_i}$. The ground eigenspace
of $H$ is the tensor product of the ground eigenspaces of the $H_i$; if
the former is one-dimensional so is each latter, hence
$\Om=\bigotimes_i\Om_i$ and $S_\Om(A)=\sum_iS_{\Om_i}(A\cap\Lam_i)$,
while $\partial_\Lam A=\bigsqcup_i\partial_{\Lam_i}(A\cap\Lam_i)$. Each
$H_i$ inherits \eqref{eq:gap} with the same $\Delta$ (restrict the
inequality to vectors of the form $\xi\otimes\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\Om_j$).
So assume $\Lam$ is an interval. Write $A=\bigsqcup_{a=1}^mA_a$ as a union
of maximal subintervals. If some $A_a=\Lam$ then $A=\Lam$ and $S(A)=0$.
Otherwise each $A_a$ has at least one boundary edge, and distinct $A_a$
have distinct boundary edges (two maximal subintervals are separated by at
least one site, so their outer cut edges differ). Thus $m\le\abs{\partial_\Lam A}$
and subadditivity gives $S(A)\le\sum_aS(A_a)\le C_{\mathrm{int}}\abs{\partial_\Lam A}$.
\end{proof}

From now on $\Lam=\{1,\dots,N\}$ and $X\subseteq\Lam$ is an interval. For
an integer $j\ge0$ write $X^{+j}=\{x\in\Lam:\dist(x,X)\le j\}$, again an
interval.

\section{The marginal tail bound}\label{sec:tail}

\begin{inwords}
Fix an interval $X$ and write $\Om=\sum_j\sqrt{p_j}\,u_j\otimes w_j$ for its
Schmidt decomposition across $X$ and the rest. The entanglement spectrum
is the probability law of the \emph{surprisal} $K=-\log p_j$ drawn with
probability $p_j$; its mean is $S(X)$. The lemma says that, because of
the global gap, $K$ has exponential tails around its mean with a width
that depends only on $q,R,J/\Delta$: the spectrum of an interval is
\emph{concentrated}, whatever its entropy is.

The mechanism is a tilt. Multiply $\Om$ by $\rho_X^{-u/2}$ (acting on
$X$) and renormalize: this reweights the Schmidt probabilities to
$p_j^{1-u}/M(u)$, with $M(u)=\sum_jp_j^{1-u}$ the moment generating
function of $K$. The tilted vector $\Theta$ is an exact eigenvector of the
conjugated Hamiltonian $FHF^{-1}$. Interactions inside $X$ or inside its
complement do not see the conjugation in expectation (they commute with
the marginal, or with $F$). Only the boundedly many interactions crossing
a cut of $X$ do, and for each of them the conjugated expectation
$f(z)=\langle\Theta,\sigma^{z}h\sigma^{-z}\Theta\rangle$ is an entire
function of the tilt parameter which is bounded on a strip and satisfies
$f(-\bar z)=\overline{f(z)}$. That symmetry kills the linear term of the
\emph{real part}, so the excitation energy of $\Theta$ is quadratic in
$u$. The gap converts a quadratic energy into a quadratic loss of overlap
with $\Om$; that overlap is the ratio $M(u/2)^2/M(u)$. Iterating the
resulting inequality $\log M(u)\le 2\log M(u/2)+Cu^2$ down to $u\to0$,
where $\log M$ is linear with slope $S(X)$, gives
$\log M(u)\le uS(X)+Cu^2$: a sub-Gaussian moment bound. Exponential
Markov turns it into the tail bound.

In two dimensions the width of the tail grows like the square root of the
boundary. In one dimension it is a constant. This constant is what makes
both halves of this note work.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
Let $X\subseteq\Lam$ be an interval, $\rho=\rho_X$ with positive
eigenvalues $p_j$, let $K$ be the random variable taking the value
$-\log p_j$ with probability $p_j$, so that $\E K=S(X)$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \vartheta=\frac J\Delta,\qquad
 u_0=\frac{1}{32\sqrt{(1+\vartheta)\cB_R}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Marginal tail bound]\label{lem:tail}
For every interval $X\subseteq\Lam$ and every real $u$ with $\abs u\le u_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mgf}
 \log\Tr\rho_X^{\,1-u}=\log\E\,\e^{uK}\le u\,S(X)+512\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_R\,u^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $\rho_X^{1-u}$ is zero on the kernel of $\rho_X$. Consequently, for
every $w\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \Pr\{\abs{K-S(X)}>w\}\le 2\e^{\e/2}\exp(-u_0w).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $M(u)=\sum_jp_j^{1-u}$, $L(u)=\log M(u)$; note $L(0)=0$ and
$L'(0)=S(X)$. Let $P$ be the support projection of $\rho$, let
$F=\rho^{-u/2}$ on $P\cH_X$ and $F=\id$ on the kernel, and define
\[
 \Theta=\frac{F\Om}{\sqrt{M(u)}},\qquad
 \sigma=\Tr_{\Lam\setminus X}\ket\Theta\bra\Theta=\frac{\rho^{1-u}}{M(u)} .
\]
$F$ is invertible on $\cH_\Lam$, so $FHF^{-1}\Theta=E_0\Theta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tiltenergy}
 \langle\Theta,(H-E_0)\Theta\rangle
 =\sum_Y\bigl(\langle\Theta,h_Y\Theta\rangle-\langle\Theta,Fh_YF^{-1}\Theta\rangle\bigr).
\end{equation}
A term supported in $\Lam\setminus X$ commutes with $F$ and contributes
zero. A term supported in $X$ contributes zero because
$\Tr\sigma Fh_YF^{-1}=\Tr\sigma h_Y$ by $[F,\sigma]=0$ and cyclicity. So only
terms split by $X$ remain, and there are at most $N_R$ of them by
\eqref{eq:count}.

Fix such a term $h=h_Y$. For complex $z$ let
$\sigma^z=P\exp(z\log(\sigma|_{P\cH_X}))P$ and
$f(z)=\langle\Theta,\sigma^zh\sigma^{-z}\Theta\rangle$, an entire function
with $f(0)=\langle\Theta,h\Theta\rangle$ (because $\Theta$ lies in the
support of $\sigma$). At $z_u=-u/(2(1-u))$ one has, on the support,
$\sigma^{z_u}=M(u)^{-z_u}F$ and $\sigma^{-z_u}=M(u)^{z_u}F^{-1}$, so
$f(z_u)=\langle\Theta,Fh F^{-1}\Theta\rangle$. On the imaginary axis the
powers are unitary on the support and $\abs{f(iy)}\le J$. For
$\abs{\Re z}\le\tfrac14$ expand $h$ as in \eqref{eq:matrixunits} and let
$\Theta=\sum_j\sqrt{s_j}\,e_j\otimes f_j$ be its Schmidt decomposition
across $X$. Then
\[
 f(z)=\sum_\alpha\sum_{j,k}s_j^{1/2+z}s_k^{1/2-z}\,
 \langle e_j,c_\alpha e_k\rangle\langle f_j,d_\alpha f_k\rangle .
\]
For $\abs{\Re z}\le\frac14$, $\abs{s_j^{1/2+z}s_k^{1/2-z}}\le s_j+s_k$ by the
weighted arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, and for any operators
$c,d$ the row and column Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rowcolumn}
 \sum_{j,k}(s_j+s_k)\,\abs{c_{jk}}\,\abs{d_{jk}}\le 2\norm c\,\norm d ,
\end{equation}
because $\sum_k\abs{c_{jk}}\abs{d_{jk}}\le\norm c\norm d$ for each row $j$ and
likewise for each column. Hence $\abs{f(z)}\le 2Jq^{2(R+1)}$ on
$\abs{\Re z}\le\frac14$. Hadamard's three-lines theorem on each of the
strips $0\le\pm\Re z\le\frac14$ gives
$\log\abs{f(x+iy)}\le\log J+4\abs x\log(2q^{2(R+1)})\le \log J+8\abs x\,\ell_R$,
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:narrow}
 \abs{f(z)}\le \e J\qquad\text{for }\abs{\Re z}\le\delta_R:=\frac1{8\ell_R}.
\end{equation}
Since $h=h^*$ and $(\sigma^z)^*=\sigma^{\bar z}$, one has
$f(-\bar z)=\overline{f(z)}$. Writing $f(z)=\sum_nc_nz^n$, this says
$c_n$ is real for even $n$ and purely imaginary for odd $n$; so for real
$x$, $\Re(f(x)-f(0))=\sum_{n\ge2\text{ even}}c_nx^n$, and Cauchy's estimate
$\abs{c_n}\le\e J\delta_R^{-n}$ from \eqref{eq:narrow} gives, for
$\abs x\le\delta_R/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
 \abs{\Re(f(x)-f(0))}\le 2\e J\,x^2\delta_R^{-2}=128\,\e J\,\ell_R^2\,x^2 .
\end{equation}
For $\abs u\le u_0$ we have $\abs u\le\frac1{32}$, $\abs{z_u}\le\abs u$ and
$\ell_R\le\sqrt{\cB_R}$, so $\abs{z_u}\le\delta_R/2$. Since
\eqref{eq:tiltenergy} is real, applying \eqref{eq:quadratic} to each of the
at most $N_R$ split terms yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energyquad}
 0\le\langle\Theta,(H-E_0)\Theta\rangle\le 128\,\e J\,N_R\ell_R^2\,u^2
 \le 128\,\e J\,\cB_R\,u^2 .
\end{equation}
The gap \eqref{eq:gap} now gives
$1-\abs{\langle\Om,\Theta\rangle}^2\le 128\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_Ru^2\le\frac12$,
and $\langle\Om,\Theta\rangle=M(u/2)/\sqrt{M(u)}$, so
\[
 \frac{M(u/2)^2}{M(u)}\ge 1-128\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_R u^2,
 \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
 L(u)\le 2L(u/2)+256\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_Ru^2,
\]
using $-\log(1-v)\le2v$ for $0\le v\le\frac12$. Every halving $u/2^j$
stays in the admissible interval, so by induction
\[
 L(u)\le 2^jL(u/2^j)+256\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_Ru^2\sum_{i<j}2^{-i}
 \xrightarrow[j\to\infty]{} uS(X)+512\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_Ru^2 ,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:mgf}. For \eqref{eq:tail}, apply Markov's inequality
to $\e^{\pm u_0(K-S)}$: the quadratic term at $u=\pm u_0$ equals
$512\e\vartheta\cB_R/(1024(1+\vartheta)\cB_R)\le\e/2$, so each one-sided
tail is at most $\e^{\e/2}\e^{-u_0w}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Rényi entropies near one]\label{rem:renyi}
For $0<u\le u_0$ and $\alpha=1-u$, \eqref{eq:mgf} reads
$S_\alpha(X)=\frac{1}{u}\log\Tr\rho_X^{\alpha}\le S(X)+512\e\vartheta\cB_R(1-\alpha)$.
Since $S_\alpha\ge S$ for $\alpha<1$, all Rényi entropies of order
$\alpha\in[1-u_0,1]$ of an interval lie within $16\e\sqrt{(1+\vartheta)\cB_R}$ of its von
Neumann entropy. This is used in Section~\ref{sec:shortcut}.
\end{remark}

\section{The buffered discount with a constant-width buffer}\label{sec:buffer}

\begin{inwords}
Here is the one lemma that does the work. Take an interval $X$ and
surround it by $m$ nested shells: $X_j=X^{+jD}$ for $j=1,\dots,m$, each
one step of width $D=\max(R,1)$ thicker than the previous one, and let
$T_j=X_j\setminus X$ be the two flanks. The number $m$ will be a large
constant (of order $1+J/\Delta$), so the total buffer $T=T_m$ has
\emph{constant} width $mD$ on each side of $X$. The claim is
\[
 S(X\,|\,T)\ \le\ \tfrac12\,S(X)+C .
\]
Conditioning $X$ on a constant number of neighboring sites halves its
entropy, up to a constant.

Why should that be? Apply to $\Om$ a product $K=L_m\cdots L_1$ of positive
operators, $L_j$ acting on $X_j$, each normalized by
$\Tr L_j^{2/a_j}=1$ with $a_j=2/m$ (so the weights sum to $2$), and
maximize the norm $N=\norm{K\Om}$. Two estimates bound the optimum from
opposite sides.

\emph{The optimum is small.} At the optimum, each filter commutes with the
marginal of the filtered vector on its own region (a stationarity
computation), which lets one strip the outer filters from any expectation
of an operator living inside them. Then the energy of the normalized
filtered vector is at most a constant times $\sum_ja_j^2\times(\text{number of
interactions split by }X_j)$: an interaction is split by at most one shell
because consecutive shells are $D\ge R$ apart, and the conjugation of a
split term by one filter changes its expectation only quadratically in
$a_j$, by the same kind of row--column Cauchy--Schwarz estimate as in the
tail bound. In one dimension the number of split terms is bounded, so the
energy is at most $C/m$. The gap then says the filtered vector overlaps
$\Om$ to $1-C/(m\Delta)$. Using that overlap, one peels the filters off one
at a time, and each peeled filter $L_j$ can shrink the norm by at most
$\norm{L_j\Om}\le\exp(-a_jS(X_j)/2+Ca_j^2)$ by Hölder and the tail bound. So
$-2\log N\ge\sum_ja_jS(X_j)-C$.

\emph{The optimum is large.} Pin a Schmidt vector $u_i$ of $X$, that is,
use the trial filters $L_j=\ket{u_i}\bra{u_i}\otimes M_j$ with $M_j$ a
filter on the flank $T_j$ only. The output is $\sqrt{p_i}$ times a filtered
version of the partner vector $w_i$, and an interpolation argument shows
the flank filters can be chosen so that the norm is at least
$\exp(-\sum_ja_jS_{w_i}(T_j)/2)$. Averaging over $i$ and using concavity
of entropy, $-2\log N\le S(X)+\sum_ja_jS(T_j)$.

Subtracting, $\sum_ja_j\,[S(X_j)-S(T_j)]\le S(X)+C$, and $S(X_j)-S(T_j)$ is
the conditional entropy $S(X|T_j)$, which decreases as the flank grows.
With $\sum_ja_j=2$ this is the discount. Notice what did not happen: no
property of the state on the flanks was used beyond their being a
constant number of sites, and no gap of any restricted Hamiltonian was
invoked.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
Fix $D=\max(R,1)$ and an integer $m\ge4$. For an interval $X\subseteq\Lam$
put
\[
 X_0=X,\qquad X_j=X^{+jD}\ (1\le j\le m),\qquad T_j=X_j\setminus X,
 \qquad a_j=\frac2m .
\]
Then $T_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq T_m$, each $T_j$ consists of at most two
intervals of length at most $jD$, and $\sum_ja_j=2$. Define the constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CE}
 C_E:=8\,N_R\,J\,q^{2(R+1)},\qquad
 m:=\Bigl\lceil\max\Bigl\{4,\ \frac{4C_E}{\Delta},\ 128\sqrt{(1+\vartheta)\cB_R},\
 4096\,\e\,(1+\vartheta)\,\cB_R\Bigr\}\Bigr\rceil .
\end{equation}
Every entry is at most a constant depending on $q,R$ times $(1+J/\Delta)$,
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mbound}
 m\le C_1(q,R)\,(1+\vartheta).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Single splitting]\label{lem:singlesplit}
A support $Y$ with $\diam_{d_\Lam}Y\le R$ is split by at most one of the
intervals $X_1,\dots,X_m$. If it is split by $X_j$, it is contained in
$X_i$ for every $i>j$ and disjoint from $X_i$ for every $i<j$. The same
holds for the family $X_0,\dots,X_m$ with the convention that $X_0$ may
also be split.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $Y$ is split by $X_j$. It contains a point $y\notin X_j$ and a point
$x\in X_j$, and all its points are within graph distance $R\le D$ of $x$
and of $y$. Points within distance $D$ of $X_j$ lie in $X_{j+1}$, so
$Y\subseteq X_{j+1}\subseteq X_i$ for $i>j$. Points of $X_{j-1}$ are at
distance more than $D$ from every point outside $X_j$ (because
$X_j=X_{j-1}^{+D}$), so $Y\cap X_{j-1}=\varnothing$, and likewise for
$i<j$. A set contained in $X_i$ or disjoint from $X_i$ is not split by
$X_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Buffered discount]\label{lem:buffer}
With $m$ as in \eqref{eq:CE}, every interval $X\subseteq\Lam$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discount}
 S(X\,|\,T_m)\ \le\ \frac12\,S(X)+\frac12+\frac{C_E}{\Delta}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $X=\varnothing$ both sides vanish. Assume $X\ne\varnothing$.

\paragraph{The optimization.}
Fix $\epsilon>0$ so small that $(\dim\cH_{X_j})\epsilon^{2/a_j}<1$ for all
$j$, and maximize
\[
 N=\norm{L_m\cdots L_1\Om}
\]
over positive operators $L_j$ on $\cH_{X_j}$ (extended by the identity)
with $L_j\ge\epsilon\id$ and $\Tr_{\cH_{X_j}}L_j^{2/a_j}=1$. The feasible
sets are nonempty and compact, every feasible product is invertible, so a
maximum $N>0$ exists. At an optimum put $K=L_m\cdots L_1$, $\phi=K\Om/N$,
and $\rho_j=\Tr_{\Lam\setminus X_j}\ket\phi\bra\phi$.

\paragraph{Stationarity.}
We claim $[L_j,\rho_j]=0$ for all $j$, by descending induction on $j$. The
tool is the stripping identity: if $[L_k,\rho_k]=0$ and $T$ is supported
in $X_k$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strip}
 \langle\phi,L_kTL_k^{-1}\phi\rangle=\Tr(L_k^{-1}\rho_kL_kT)=\Tr(\rho_kT)=\langle\phi,T\phi\rangle .
\end{equation}
Assume the claim for $k>j$ and let $B$ be anti-Hermitian on $\cH_{X_j}$.
Replacing $L_j$ by $\e^{tB}L_j\e^{-tB}$ preserves both constraints, so the
derivative of $\log\norm{K\Om}$ at $t=0$ vanishes. That derivative equals
$\Re\langle\phi,K_{>j}(B-L_jBL_j^{-1})K_{>j}^{-1}\phi\rangle$ with
$K_{>j}=L_m\cdots L_{j+1}$. The operator inside is supported in $X_j$,
hence in $X_m$; strip $L_m$ by \eqref{eq:strip}, then $L_{m-1}$, and so on
down to $L_{j+1}$. Since $\Tr(\rho_jB)$ is purely imaginary, stationarity
gives $\Re\Tr(L_j^{-1}\rho_jL_jB)=0$ for all anti-Hermitian $B$, so
$L_j^{-1}\rho_jL_j$ is Hermitian, i.e.\ equals $L_j\rho_jL_j^{-1}$, so
$\rho_j$ commutes with $L_j^2$ and hence with $L_j\ge0$. No commutation
between distinct filters is claimed or used.

In a common eigenbasis let $l_i$, $p_i$ be the eigenvalues of $L_j$,
$\rho_j$, and $x_i=l_i^{2/a_j}$, so $\sum_ix_i=1$, $x_i\ge\epsilon^{2/a_j}$.
A diagonal variation and the same stripping give
$d\log N=\frac{a_j}2\sum_ip_i\,dx_i/x_i$. The Kuhn--Tucker conditions on the
simplex with the floor say that $p_i/x_i$ is a common value $\lambda>0$ on
coordinates above the floor and at most $\lambda$ on floored coordinates
(there is a free coordinate by the dimensional slack, and $\lambda>0$
because moving mass from a zero-probability free coordinate to a
positive-probability floored one would increase $N$). Hence
$x_i=\max\{\epsilon^{2/a_j},p_i/\lambda\}$ and, since taking a maximum with
a common floor contracts differences of logarithms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clip}
 \Bigl|\log\frac{l_i}{l_k}\Bigr|\le\frac{a_j}{2}\Bigl|\log\frac{p_i}{p_k}\Bigr|
 \qquad(p_i,p_k>0).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Energy of the optimized vector.}
Since $KHK^{-1}\phi=E_0\phi$,
\[
 \langle\phi,(H-E_0)\phi\rangle=\sum_Y\Re\bigl(\langle\phi,h_Y\phi\rangle-\langle\phi,Kh_YK^{-1}\phi\rangle\bigr).
\]
If $Y$ is split by no $X_j$ ($1\le j\le m$), then by Lemma~\ref{lem:singlesplit}
each $X_j$ either contains $Y$ or is disjoint from it; strip the
containing filters by \eqref{eq:strip} from the outside in, and commute
the disjoint ones through $h_Y$: the term vanishes. If $Y$ is split by
$X_j$, the same procedure leaves exactly
$\langle\phi,h_Y\phi\rangle-\langle\phi,L_jh_YL_j^{-1}\phi\rangle$. Let
$\phi=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}\,e_i\otimes f_i$ be the Schmidt decomposition across
$X_j$ with $e_i$ in the common eigenbasis, and $h_{ik}=\langle e_i\otimes f_i,h_Y(e_k\otimes f_k)\rangle$.
Pairing $(i,k)$ with $(k,i)$ and using $h_{ki}=\overline{h_{ik}}$,
\[
 \Re\langle\phi,L_jh_YL_j^{-1}\phi\rangle-\langle\phi,h_Y\phi\rangle
 =\sum_{i,k:\,p_ip_k>0}\sqrt{p_ip_k}\,\bigl(\cosh(\log(l_i/l_k))-1\bigr)\Re h_{ik}.
\]
Put $z=\frac12\log(p_i/p_k)$. By \eqref{eq:clip} and $a_j\le\frac12$,
$\cosh(\log(l_i/l_k))-1\le\cosh(a_jz)-1\le\frac12a_j^2z^2\cosh(a_jz)\le\frac12a_j^2z^2\cosh(z/2)$,
while $\sqrt{p_ip_k}=(p_i+p_k)/(2\cosh z)$. Since
$\sup_z z^2\cosh(z/2)/(4\cosh z)\le1$, the coefficient is at most
$a_j^2(p_i+p_k)$. Expanding $h_Y$ by \eqref{eq:matrixunits} and using
\eqref{eq:rowcolumn} for each product term gives
\[
 \bigl|\Re\langle\phi,L_jh_YL_j^{-1}\phi\rangle-\langle\phi,h_Y\phi\rangle\bigr|
 \le 2a_j^2\,q^{2(R+1)}J .
\]
Summing over the at most $N_R$ terms split by each $X_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 0\le\langle\phi,(H-E_0)\phi\rangle\le\sum_{j=1}^m N_R\cdot 2a_j^2q^{2(R+1)}J
 =\frac{8N_RJq^{2(R+1)}}{m}=\frac{C_E}{m}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:gap} and \eqref{eq:CE},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 1-\abs{\langle\Om,\phi\rangle}^2\le\frac{C_E}{m\Delta}\le\frac14 .
\end{equation}
The same bound holds for the optimizer of every prefix $L_j\cdots L_1$,
$1\le j\le m$, since its energy is bounded by the same sum restricted to
$i\le j$.

\paragraph{Upper bound on the optimum.}
Let $N_j$ be the optimum for the prefix $1,\dots,j$ at the same floor,
$N_0=1$, and let $L_j\cdots L_1$ be an optimizer for prefix $j$ with
$K_{<j}=L_{j-1}\cdots L_1$. By \eqref{eq:overlap} for that prefix and
Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \sqrt{1-C_E/(m\Delta)}\;N_j\le\abs{\langle\Om,L_jK_{<j}\Om\rangle}
 =\abs{\langle L_j\Om,K_{<j}\Om\rangle}\le\norm{L_j\Om}\,N_{j-1}.
\]
For any feasible $L_j$, Schatten--Hölder with exponents $\frac1{1-a_j}$ and
$\frac1{a_j}$ and the constraint $\Tr L_j^{2/a_j}=1$ give
\[
 \norm{L_j\Om}^2=\Tr\rho_{X_j}L_j^2\le\bigl(\Tr\rho_{X_j}^{1/(1-a_j)}\bigr)^{1-a_j}.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} to the interval $X_j$ with $u=-a_j/(1-a_j)$,
which is admissible because $\abs u\le 2a_j=4/m\le u_0$ by \eqref{eq:CE}.
Using $(1-a_j)u^2\le 2a_j^2$,
\[
 \log\norm{L_j\Om}^2\le -a_jS(X_j)+1024\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_R\,a_j^2 .
\]
Multiplying the recursion over $j$ and using $-\frac12\log(1-v)\le v$ for
$v\le\frac12$,
\[
 \log N\le-\frac12\sum_ja_jS(X_j)+512\,\e\,\vartheta\,\cB_R\sum_ja_j^2+\frac{mC_E}{m\Delta}
 \le -\frac12\sum_ja_jS(X_j)+\frac12+\frac{C_E}{\Delta},
\]
because $\sum_ja_j^2=4/m\le 1/(1024\e\vartheta\cB_R)$ by \eqref{eq:CE}. That is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowerN}
 -2\log N\ \ge\ \sum_{j=1}^ma_jS(X_j)-1-\frac{2C_E}{\Delta}.
\end{equation}
The constants in \eqref{eq:lowerN} do not depend on the floor $\epsilon$.

\paragraph{Lower bound on the optimum by Schmidt pinning.}
Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The optima converge to the optimum $N$ over
nonnegative filters with the same trace constraints (the feasible sets
are compact and every nonnegative feasible operator is a limit of
strictly positive feasible ones), and \eqref{eq:lowerN} persists. Write
$\Om=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}\,u_i\otimes w_i$ with $p_i>0$ for the Schmidt
decomposition across $X$. For fixed $i$ use the trial filters
\[
 L_j=\ket{u_i}\bra{u_i}\otimes M_j,\qquad M_j\ge0\text{ on }\cH_{T_j},\qquad\Tr M_j^{2/a_j}=1,
\]
which are feasible (as $\Tr_{X_j}L_j^{2/a_j}=\Tr M_j^{2/a_j}=1$) and give
$N\ge\sqrt{p_i}\,N_i$, where $N_i=\sup\norm{M_m\cdots M_1w_i}$ over such $M_j$.
We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trial}
 \log N_i\ge-\frac12\sum_ja_jS_{w_i}(T_j).
\end{equation}
Fix invertible feasible $M_j$ and consider $F_i(z)=M_m^z\cdots M_1^zw_i$ on
$0\le\Re z\le1$, bounded and analytic. On $\Re z=0$ the factors are
unitary and $\norm{F_i(iy)}=1$. On $\Re z=1$ put $U_j=M_j^{iy}$,
$V_j=U_j\cdots U_1$, $V_0=\id$; moving the unitaries left,
\[
 M_m^{1+iy}\cdots M_1^{1+iy}=(U_m\cdots U_1)\,(V_{m-1}^*M_mV_{m-1})\cdots(V_0^*M_1V_0).
\]
Each conjugated factor is positive, supported in $T_j$ (the conjugating
unitaries live on the smaller nested flanks), and has the same
trace-power normalization, so $\norm{F_i(1+iy)}\le N_i$. Testing against
unit vectors and applying the scalar three-lines theorem gives
$\log\norm{F_i(x)}\le x\log N_i$ for $0\le x\le1$; differentiating at
$x=0^+$ yields $\sum_j\langle w_i,\log M_j\,w_i\rangle\le\log N_i$. Taking
$M_j=\bigl((1-\delta)\rho_{w_i,T_j}+\delta\id/\dim\cH_{T_j}\bigr)^{a_j/2}$ and
letting $\delta\downarrow0$, the left side tends to
$-\frac12\sum_ja_jS_{w_i}(T_j)$, proving \eqref{eq:trial}. Hence
$-2\log N\le-\log p_i+\sum_ja_jS_{w_i}(T_j)$ for every $i$; average with
weights $p_i$ and use concavity of the entropy (the $p_i$-average of
$\rho_{w_i,T_j}$ is $\rho_{T_j}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upperN}
 -2\log N\le S(X)+\sum_{j=1}^ma_jS(T_j).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Subtract \eqref{eq:upperN} from \eqref{eq:lowerN}:
$\sum_ja_j\bigl(S(X_j)-S(T_j)\bigr)\le S(X)+1+2C_E/\Delta$. Here
$S(X_j)-S(T_j)=S(XT_j)-S(T_j)=S(X|T_j)$, and $S(X|T_j)\ge S(X|T_m)$ for
$j\le m$ by strong subadditivity (conditioning on the larger flank
$T_m\supseteq T_j$ decreases conditional entropy). With $\sum_ja_j=2$ this
gives $2S(X|T_m)\le S(X)+1+2C_E/\Delta$.
\end{proof}

\section{Closing the argument}\label{sec:closing}

\begin{inwords}
The discount says that $S(X|T)\le\frac12S(X)+C$ where $T$ is a buffer of a
constant number of sites. But a conditional entropy cannot be very
negative: by the Araki--Lieb triangle inequality $S(XT)\ge S(X)-S(T)$, so
$S(X|T)\ge S(X)-2S(T)$. Putting the two together,
$S(X)-2S(T)\le\frac12S(X)+C$, hence $S(X)\le 4S(T)+2C$, and $S(T)$ is at
most the number of sites of $T$ times $\log q$. That is the area law.

Nothing else is needed. In two dimensions the same two inequalities give
$S(X)\le 4S(T)+Cr$ for a box $X$ of side $r$ with a buffer $T$ of width
proportional to $r$; the buffer is an annulus of area $\sim r^2$ whose
entropy is as unknown as that of $X$, and the inequality is useless.
That is the precise point at which the two-dimensional proof must bring
in everything else.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reductions} it suffices to bound $S(X)$ for an interval
$X$ in an interval domain. Let $T=T_m$ be the buffer of
Lemma~\ref{lem:buffer}; it has at most $2mD$ sites. The Araki--Lieb
inequality $S(XT)\ge S(X)-S(T)$ gives $S(X|T)\ge S(X)-2S(T)$, and
\eqref{eq:discount} gives $S(X|T)\le\frac12S(X)+\frac12+C_E/\Delta$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final}
 S(X)\le 4S(T)+1+\frac{2C_E}{\Delta}\le 8mD\log q+1+\frac{2C_E}{\Delta}=:C_{\mathrm{int}}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:mbound} and \eqref{eq:CE}, $C_{\mathrm{int}}\le C_0(q,R)(1+J/\Delta)$
with, for instance,
$C_0(q,R)=8C_1(q,R)\max(R,1)\log q+1+16N_Rq^{2(R+1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the constant depends on]
The gap enters only through $\vartheta=J/\Delta$, linearly. The dependence
on the local dimension and range is through $N_R\,q^{2(R+1)}$, which for
nearest-neighbor interactions ($R=1$) is of order $q^4$; no attempt has
been made to optimize it. By comparison, the bound of
Arad--Kitaev--Landau--Vazirani is $\tilde O(\log^3(q)/\Delta)$ for $R=1$, so
the present argument matches the linear dependence on the inverse gap
and is worse in $q$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What was used]
The proof used the global gap in exactly two places: in
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} applied at the cuts of the shells $X_j$, and to
convert the energy bound \eqref{eq:energy} into the overlap bound
\eqref{eq:overlap}. No Lieb--Robinson bound, no spectral filter, no
approximate ground-state projector, no Chebyshev polynomial, no
bootstrapping of a Schmidt-rank bound appears. The entropy itself enters
only through operator norms of filtered vectors.
\end{remark}

\section{The long route, for comparison}\label{sec:longroute}

\begin{inwords}
It is instructive to see what the two-dimensional proof would do in one
dimension if one refused to use the Araki--Lieb shortcut, because this
is where each piece of the big machine would be used, and because the
amplification and two-family steps reappear, in spirit, in
Part~\ref{part:mps}.

\emph{Sub-volume exponent.} Split an interval of length $Mr$ into $M$
children of length $r$ and add them up in a random order. A child whose
constant-width buffer has already been added gets the discount, which
happens with a fixed probability $p_0$. This gives
$F(Mr)\le(1-p_0/4)M\,F(r)+C M$ for the maximal interval entropy $F$, hence
$F(r)\le Cr^{e_0}$ with $e_0<1$. In two dimensions the analogous recursion
gives $r^{1+e_0}$, which is \emph{above} the boundary $r$; in one
dimension $r^{e_0}$ is already below the diameter $r$, which is all the
next step needs.

\emph{Amplification.} The amplification proposition of the source
converts an information bound $I(X:\text{far})\le n^{1-\varepsilon}$ into
$I(X:J)\le n^{-M}$ for all $J$ beyond a graph distance $2r$ with
$r=O((n^{1-\varepsilon}+\log n)^{1/\alpha})$, $\alpha<1$ as close to one
as desired, using positive quasi-local constraints built from Kitaev's
spectral filter, a random product of the corresponding contractions, and
a polar decomposition. In one dimension the trivial bound
$I(X:\text{anything})\le2S(X)\le Cn^{e_0}$ for an interval $X$ of length
$n$ already is such an input, with no collar at all; one gets
$I(X:J)\le n^{-M}$ beyond distance $Cn^{e_0/\alpha}=o(n)$ once
$\alpha>e_0$.

\emph{Two families.} Cut the half-line to the right of the cut into
dyadic intervals $X_i=[2^i,2^{i+1})$ and alternate their family labels.
Each $X_i$ is separated from all earlier same-family intervals and from
the other side of the cut by the gap $X_{i-1}$, of length $2^{i-1}\gg
2^{ie_0/\alpha}$. The two-family cancellation lemma then gives
$S(A)\le|D|\log q+\frac12\sum_i2^{-iM}$ with $|D|=O(1)$.

So the long route also works, with the entire replica scanner replaced by
the trivial inequality $I\le 2S$. We do not pursue it; the direct proof is
shorter and gives better constants.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
We only record the recursion, since the other two steps are literally
the source propositions with the stated inputs. Let $F(r)$ be the supremum
of $S(X)$ over intervals $X\subseteq\Lam$ of length at most $r$ (over all
admissible instances), and let $w_0=mD$ be the buffer width of
Lemma~\ref{lem:buffer} with discount constant $C_{\mathrm{buf}}=\frac12+C_E/\Delta$.
Let $M\ge 8(2w_0/r+2)$ and let $X$ be an interval of length at most $Mr$.
Cut $X$ into at most $M$ consecutive children of length at most $r$ and
order them by a uniformly random permutation. Call a child interior if
its buffer of width $w_0$ on each side lies in $X$; at most
$B:=2\lceil w_0/r\rceil+2$ children are not interior. For an interior
child, the event that all children meeting its buffer precede it has
probability at least $p_0=(2\lceil w_0/r\rceil+3)^{-1}$; on that event the
chain-rule term is at most $S(X_i|T_i)\le\frac12S(X_i)+C_{\mathrm{buf}}$,
and in every case it is at most $S(X_i)$. Taking the expectation of the
chain rule,
\[
 F(Mr)\le\Bigl[(1-\tfrac{p_0}2)M+\tfrac{p_0}2B\Bigr]F(r)+p_0MC_{\mathrm{buf}}
 \le\bigl(1-\tfrac{p_0}4\bigr)M\,F(r)+C M ,
\]
using $B\le M/2$. With $\lambda=(1-p_0/4)M\in(1,M)$ for $r\ge w_0$, iterating
at $r=w_0,Mw_0,M^2w_0,\dots$ gives $F(M^jw_0)\le C\lambda^j$ and hence
$F(r)\le Cr^{e_0}$ with $e_0=\log_M\lambda<1$.

\part{The one-dimensional matrix product state approximation}\label{part:mps}

\section{Statement, and the two-line route}\label{sec:shortcut}

\begin{inwords}
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} asks for one global vector, not for a bound on each
cut. In two dimensions this is a serious gap between hypothesis and
conclusion, and the source closes it with a distributed protocol. In one
dimension there is a classical bridge: if the Schmidt spectrum at every
cut has a fast-decaying tail, then truncating the cuts one after another
produces a matrix product state whose error is the sum of the tail
masses (Verstraete--Cirac). The marginal tail bound of Section~\ref{sec:tail}
gives exactly such a tail, with an exponential rate $u_0$ that depends
only on $q,R,J/\Delta$, and the area law fixes the location of the
spectrum. So Theorem~\ref{thm:B} follows in a few lines, with bond
dimension $CL^{4/u_0}$.

We record this first because it is true and short. The rest of
Part~\ref{part:mps} then presents the one-dimensional version of the
\emph{protocol} proof, which is the construction that generalizes to two
dimensions and which uses none of this sequential truncation.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
\begin{proposition}[Sequential truncation]\label{prop:VC}
Let $\Om$ be a unit vector on $\cH_\Lam$, $\Lam=\{1,\dots,L\}$. For
$1\le k\le L-1$ let $\Pi_k$ be the projection of $\cH_{[1,k]}$ onto the
span of the $D$ eigenvectors of $\rho_{[1,k]}$ with largest eigenvalues,
let $\epsilon_k=\langle\Om,(\id-\Pi_k\otimes\id)\Om\rangle$ be the discarded
mass, and put
\[
 \Phi=(\Pi_1\otimes\id)(\Pi_2\otimes\id)\cdots(\Pi_{L-1}\otimes\id)\,\Om .
\]
Then $\Phi$ is a matrix product state with open boundary conditions and
bond dimension at most $qD$, and $\norm{\Phi-\Om}\le\sum_k\sqrt{\epsilon_k}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $P_k=\Pi_k\otimes\id$. Telescoping,
$\Phi-\Om=\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}P_1\cdots P_{k-1}(P_k-\id)\Om$ and each prefix is
a contraction, which gives the error bound. For the bond dimension fix a
cut $k$. The vector $\Xi=P_{k+1}\cdots P_{L-1}\Om$ is in the range of
$P_{k+1}$, so $\Xi=\sum_{b=1}^D\ket b_{[1,k+1]}\otimes\xi_b$; expanding
$\ket b=\sum_{s=1}^q\beta_{b,s}\otimes\ket s_{k+1}$ shows that $\Xi$ has
Schmidt rank at most $qD$ across the cut $k$. The remaining factors
$P_1,\dots,P_k$ act on $[1,k]$ and do not increase that rank. A vector
with Schmidt rank at most $qD$ across every cut is a matrix product
state of bond dimension at most $qD$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, two-line route]\label{thm:Bshort}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, for every $L\ge2$ there is
a matrix product state $\Phi$ with bond dimension at most
$q\,\e^{C_{\mathrm{int}}}\,(32\e^{\e/2}L^4)^{1/u_0}$ and
$\min_\theta\norm{\Phi/\norm\Phi-\e^{i\theta}\Om}\le L^{-1}$, where $u_0$ is
\eqref{eq:theta} and $C_{\mathrm{int}}$ is \eqref{eq:final}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cut $k$ and $X=[1,k]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, the eigenvalues
$p$ of $\rho_X$ with $-\log p>S(X)+w$ have total mass at most
$2\e^{\e/2}\e^{-u_0w}$; the eigenvalues with $-\log p\le S(X)+w$ number at
most $\e^{S(X)+w}$. Choose $w$ with $2\e^{\e/2}\e^{-u_0w}=(16L^4)^{-1}$ and
$D=\lceil\e^{C_{\mathrm{int}}+w}\rceil\ge\e^{S(X)+w}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}).
Then $\epsilon_k\le(16L^4)^{-1}$ for every $k$, Proposition~\ref{prop:VC}
gives $\norm{\Phi-\Om}\le(L-1)/(4L^2)\le1/(4L)$, hence $\Phi\ne0$ and
$\norm{\Phi/\norm\Phi-\Om}\le2\norm{\Phi-\Om}\le L^{-1}$. The bond
dimension is $qD$ with $\e^{w}=(32\e^{\e/2}L^4)^{1/u_0}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The exponent $4/u_0$ is of order $\sqrt{(1+J/\Delta)\cB_R}$. Hastings'
original construction gives a polynomial bond dimension with an exponent
that is exponential in $J/\Delta$; here the exponent is a square root.
This is a byproduct of the tail bound, not of the protocol below.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Plan of the protocol route}

\begin{inwords}
The protocol proof has four layers, and we keep all four.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Information at logarithmic separation.} Two regions of the
chain separated by $D\log L$ sites share at most $L^{-p}$ nats of mutual
information. This is where the gap is used. In two dimensions it took the
coarse collar bound, the cell conditional-information lemma and the
``angular'' induction; here it takes the area law plus one lemma, the
information reset (Section~\ref{sec:logsep}).
\item \emph{Moves.} Three elementary operations change who holds which
registers while keeping the global vector close to a reference: a birth
(give an interior interval to a new owner), its reverse, a death, and an
exchange of assignments between two copies of the ground state inside a
window. Each is a contraction built from private maps and one normalized
bipartite vector or covector, and each costs $O(L^{-p/2})$ when the moved
region is informationally independent of the other owners
(Section~\ref{sec:moves}).
\item \emph{The schedule.} A left-to-right sweep with a moving remainder
party creates the block owners one by one. The only difficulty is a band
owned by the remainder that is wedged between two different owners; it is
dissolved by a fixed sequence of nine moves using an auxiliary copy of the
ground state, copied from the edge construction of the two-dimensional
paper. Every party takes part in a bounded number of moves
(Section~\ref{sec:sweep}).
\item \emph{Compression and assembly.} The distributed construction is a
pure linear calculation with unbounded private dimensions. Replacing each
bipartite resource by an average of polynomially many random product
operators approximates the physical density in trace norm, and the error
analysis is dimension-free because each party participates in boundedly
many operations. One column of the resulting operator network is a ket
network; placing its nodes on the chain gives the matrix product state
(Sections~\ref{sec:compression}, \ref{sec:routing}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{inwords}

\section{Information at logarithmic separation}\label{sec:logsep}

\subsection{Purifications, fidelity, and splitting}

\begin{inwords}
Small mutual information $I(T:E)$ means the joint state of $T$ and $E$
is close, in relative entropy, to the product of its marginals. Relative
entropy controls fidelity, and fidelity between two mixed states is the
largest overlap between their purifications (Uhlmann). So if the third
system $U$ purifies $TE$, there is an isometry on $U$ alone that turns the
global vector into nearly a product of a purification of $T$ and a
purification of $E$. Whoever holds $U$ can do this privately. This is
the single mechanism behind births and exchanges.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
For density operators $\rho,\sigma$ put $F(\rho,\sigma)=\norm{\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma}_1$
and $D(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)$, infinite when the
support of $\rho$ is not contained in that of $\sigma$.

\begin{lemma}[Fidelity, information, Uhlmann]\label{lem:fidelity}
For normalized finite-dimensional states, $F(\rho,\sigma)\ge\e^{-D(\rho\|\sigma)/2}$,
and $D(\rho_{AC}\|\rho_A\otimes\rho_C)=I(A:C)$. If $\xi$ purifies $\rho$ on
$\cH\otimes\cK$ and $\eta$ purifies $\sigma$ on $\cH\otimes\cK'$, then after
enlarging $\cK'$ if necessary there is an isometry $V:\cK\to\cK'$ with
$\abs{\langle\eta,(\id\otimes V)\xi\rangle}=F(\rho,\sigma)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $(p_i,\ket i)$, $(q_j,\ket{j'})$ be eigenpairs of $\rho,\sigma$. If
$D(\rho\|\sigma)<\infty$, $w_{ij}=p_i\abs{\langle i|j'\rangle}^2$ is a
probability on the pairs with $p_iq_j>0$ and
$D(\rho\|\sigma)=\sum w_{ij}\log(p_i/q_j)$. By convexity of the exponential,
$\Tr\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma=\sum_{ij}w_{ij}\exp(-\tfrac12\log(p_i/q_j))\ge\e^{-D(\rho\|\sigma)/2}$,
and the trace norm dominates the trace. The identity for $I(A:C)$ is the
expansion of $\log(\rho_A\otimes\rho_C)$. The purification statement is
Uhlmann's theorem: the overlaps $\Tr(\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma U)$ over unitaries
$U$ on a common large purifying space have maximum modulus
$\norm{\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma}_1$ by polar decomposition, and any purification
is obtained from the canonical one by an isometry on the purifying factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Splitting]\label{lem:splitting}
Let $T,U,E$ partition $\Lam$ and $b=I_\Om(T:E)$. There are finite-dimensional
spaces $\cB_T,\cB_E$, an isometry $V:\cH_U\to\cB_T\otimes\cB_E$ and unit vectors
$s\in\cH_T\otimes\cB_T$, $s'\in\cH_E\otimes\cB_E$ with
\[
 \norm{(\id_{TE}\otimes V)\Om-s\otimes s'}\le\sqrt{2(1-\e^{-b/2})}\le\sqrt b .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\rho_{TE}$ is supported in $\supp\rho_T\otimes\supp\rho_E$ (the projector onto
the kernel of a marginal, tensored with the identity, has zero expectation
in the positive joint state), so $D(\rho_{TE}\|\rho_T\otimes\rho_E)=b$ is
finite. Purify $\rho_T$ by $s$ and $\rho_E$ by $s'$, enlarge the purifying
factors so that $\cB_T\otimes\cB_E$ contains an isometric copy of $\cH_U$,
and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:fidelity}: an isometry $V$ on $\cH_U$ attains
overlap at least $\e^{-b/2}$ with $s\otimes s'$, with real positive phase.
Two unit vectors with overlap $F$ are at distance $\sqrt{2(1-F)}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The information reset}\label{sec:reset}

\begin{inwords}
The area law gives $I(R:C)=O(1)$ between an interval $R$ and anything
else, never $o(1)$. The reset lemma turns a constant into an arbitrary
inverse power of $L$ by shrinking the two regions inward by a margin of
order $\log L$. The idea is a two-replica swap. Take two copies of the
ground vector, $\Psi=\Om\otimes\Om$, and swap the two copies on $R$ to get
$\Phi=F_R\Psi$. In $\Phi$, the core $R_0\subseteq R$ of one copy and the
core $C_0\subseteq C$ of the same copy come from different original copies,
so they are exactly independent. If we can find a unitary $U$ acting only
on the buffer between the cores and sending $\Psi$ to $\Phi$ up to
$L^{-p}$, then the joint marginal of the cores in $\Psi$ equals the
product marginal up to $L^{-p}$, which is the claim.

The unitary is built in three steps. First, $I(R:C)\le b$ and the
splitting lemma give an operator $W$ on the buffer $Q$ of both copies
with $\langle\Phi,W\Psi\rangle\ge\e^{-2b}$. Second, a Gaussian spectral
filter applied in both directions, $M=\int g_h(t)\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H}dt$,
projects $W\Psi$ onto $\Phi$ and $W^\dagger\Phi$ onto $\Psi$ with error
$\e^{-h\Delta^2/2}$, because $\Psi,\Phi$ are the unique gapped ground
vectors of the two doubled Hamiltonians $\mathbb H$, $\mathbb H'=F_R\mathbb HF_R$.
Third, a Lieb--Robinson bound localizes $M$ to within $\ell=O(1+b+\log L)$
of the buffer and of the edges crossing $R$, where $\mathbb H$ and
$\mathbb H'$ differ. The localized operator is almost isometric on
$\Psi$, and its polar unitary does the job. The lemma is proved exactly as
in the source; we only insert the one-dimensional locality bound.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
We need the propagation estimate for a doubled chain. Let
$\mathbb H$ be a Hamiltonian on $\bigotimes_{x\in\Lam}(\C^q\otimes\C^q)$
which is a sum of terms $k_Z$ supported on sets $Z$ of diameter at most
$R$ with $\sum_{Z\ni x}\norm{k_Z}\le b_0$ for every $x$, and
$\tau_t(A)=\e^{it\mathbb H}A\e^{-it\mathbb H}$.

\begin{lemma}[Lieb--Robinson bound on a chain]\label{lem:LR}
There are $C,v,\mu>0$ depending only on $R$ and $b_0$ such that for every
operator $A$ supported in $S\subseteq\Lam$, every site $y$ and every
operator $B_y$ supported at $y$,
\[
 \norm{[\tau_t(A),B_y]}\le C\abs S\,\norm A\,\norm{B_y}\,\e^{v\abs t-\mu\,\dist(S,y)} .
\]
Consequently, if $\mathcal P_D$ denotes the conditional expectation onto
operators supported in $D$ (the average over independent Haar unitaries at
the sites outside $D$), then for every $\ell\ge0$
\[
 \norm{\tau_t(A)-\mathcal P_{N_\ell(S)}\tau_t(A)}\le C'\abs S\,\norm A\,\e^{v\abs t-\mu\ell}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $t\ge0$ put $c_y(S,t)=\sup\{\norm{[\tau_t(A),B_y]}:\supp A\subseteq S,\norm A\le1\}$.
Differentiating and using the Jacobi identity, the derivative of
$[\tau_t(A),B_y]$ equals $i[\sum_{Z\cap S\ne\varnothing}\tau_t(k_Z),[\tau_t(A),B_y]]$
plus a sum over $Z\cap S\ne\varnothing$ of $-i[\tau_t(A),[\tau_t(k_Z),B_y]]$; the
first part is removed by a unitary change of frame, so
$c_y(S,t)\le c_y(S,0)+2\sum_{Z\cap S\ne\varnothing}\norm{k_Z}\int_0^tc_y(Z,u)\,du$.
Iterating, and using $c_y(Z,u)\le2\norm{B_y}$ to see that the remainder
after $m$ iterations tends to zero, gives
\[
 c_y(S,t)\le 2\norm{B_y}\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(2t)^m}{m!}
 \sum_{\substack{Z_1\cap S\ne\varnothing,\ Z_{i+1}\cap Z_i\ne\varnothing\\ y\in Z_m}}
 \prod_{i=1}^m\norm{k_{Z_i}},
\]
with the $m=0$ term equal to $\mathbf 1_{\{y\in S\}}$. Summing the norms of
the terms meeting a set of $n$ sites gives at most $nb_0$, so the inner
sum is at most $\abs S\,b_0\,((R+1)b_0)^{m-1}\le\abs S\kappa^m$ with
$\kappa=(R+1)b_0$; and a chain reaching $y$ needs $mR\ge\dist(S,y)$. Hence
for every $\mu>0$,
$c_y(S,t)\le 2\abs S\norm{B_y}\e^{-\mu\dist(S,y)}\sum_m(2\kappa\e^{\mu R}t)^m/m!$,
which is the first bound with $v=2\kappa\e^{\mu R}$. Negative times are
identical. For the second
bound, $\mathcal P_D$ is a product of commuting single-site twirls
$T_y$, telescoping gives
$\norm{B-\mathcal P_DB}\le\sum_{y\notin D}\sup_{U_y}\norm{[B,U_y]}$, and there are
at most two sites at each distance $d>\ell$ from $S$, so the sum is at most
$2C\abs S\norm A\e^{vt}\sum_{d>\ell}\e^{-\mu d}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Information reset]\label{lem:reset}
For every $p>0$ there is $C_p<\infty$, depending only on $p,q,R,J,\Delta$,
with the following property for all sufficiently large $L$. Let
$R,C\subseteq\Lam$ be disjoint, $R_0\subseteq R$, $C_0\subseteq C$, and
suppose $I_\Om(R:C)\le b$. If
\[
 \dist(R_0,\Lam\setminus R)>w,\qquad \dist(C_0,\Lam\setminus C)>w,
 \qquad w=C_p(1+b+\log L),
\]
then $I_\Om(R_0:C_0)\le L^{-p}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a core is empty there is nothing to prove. Let $\Psi=\Om\otimes\Om$ on
two replicas, $F_R$ the unitary exchanging the two replicas at the sites
of $R$, $\Phi=F_R\Psi$, and $Q=\Lam\setminus(R\cup C)$.

\emph{Overlap.} Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:splitting} to $(T,U,E)=(R,Q,C)$: an
isometry $V:\cH_Q\to\cB_R\otimes\cB_C$ and unit vectors $a\in\cH_R\otimes\cB_R$,
$a'\in\cH_C\otimes\cB_C$ with
$\abs{\langle a\otimes a',\widetilde\Om\rangle}\ge\e^{-b/2}$,
$\widetilde\Om=(\id\otimes V)\Om$. The largest Schmidt coefficient of
$\widetilde\Om$ across $R\cB_R\,|\,C\cB_C$ is therefore at least $\e^{-b}$,
so $\Tr\rho_{\widetilde\Om,R\cB_R}^2\ge\e^{-2b}$. Define on the two
replicas of $Q$ the Hermitian contraction
$W=(V^\dagger\otimes V^\dagger)F_{\cB_R}(V\otimes V)$, where $F_{\cB_R}$ swaps
the two copies of $\cB_R$. Since $V$ acts outside $R$, the swap identity
$\langle\xi^{\otimes2},F_{A}\xi^{\otimes2}\rangle=\Tr\rho_{\xi,A}^2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zoverlap}
 z:=\langle\Phi,W\Psi\rangle=\langle\widetilde\Om^{\otimes2},F_RF_{\cB_R}\widetilde\Om^{\otimes2}\rangle
 =\Tr\rho_{\widetilde\Om,R\cB_R}^2\ \ge\ \e^{-2b}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Two-sided Gaussian filtering.} Let $\mathbb H=H\otimes\id+\id\otimes H$
and $\mathbb H'=F_R\mathbb HF_R$. Both are range-$R$ Hamiltonians on the
doubled chain with uniformly bounded interaction sums, with unique ground
vectors $\Psi$, $\Phi$, common ground energy $\mathcal E=2E_0$, and gap at
least $\Delta$. Their difference $\mathbb H'-\mathbb H$ is supported on the
endpoints $Z_R$ of the edges crossing $R$ (interior terms of $R$ are merely
exchanged between the two identical replica copies). Put
$B_*=1+b+\log L$, $r_*=2p+10$, $h=AB_*$ with $A\Delta^2/2\ge r_*+30$, and
\[
 g_h(t)=(2\pi h)^{-1/2}\e^{-t^2/(2h)},\qquad
 M=\int_{\R}g_h(t)\,\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H}\,dt .
\]
$M$ is a contraction, and by the spectral theorem
$M\Psi=\e^{-h(\mathbb H'-\mathcal E)^2/2}W\Psi$, $M^\dagger\Phi=\e^{-h(\mathbb H-\mathcal E)^2/2}W^\dagger\Phi$,
so by the gaps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twosided}
 \norm{M\Psi-z\Phi},\ \norm{M^\dagger\Phi-z\Psi}\le\e^{-h\Delta^2/2}\le\e^{-10b}L^{-r_*}.
\end{equation}
Truncating the integral to $\abs t\le T=T_0B_*$ costs at most
$2\e^{-T_0^2B_*/(2A)}$, which is also at most $\e^{-10b}L^{-r_*}$ once
$T_0^2/(2A)\ge r_*+30$.

\emph{Localization.} Write
$\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H}=(\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H'})\,V(t)$
with $V(t)=\e^{it\mathbb H'}\e^{-it\mathbb H}$, whose left generator
$G(t)=\e^{it\mathbb H'}(\mathbb H'-\mathbb H)\e^{-it\mathbb H'}$ is a sum of
at most $N_R\abs{\partial R}$ terms of norm at most $2J$, each supported
within distance $R$ of a cut edge of $R$. Lemma~\ref{lem:LR} applied to $W$ (support $Q$, norm one)
and to the terms of $G(t)$ gives Hermitian localized approximants
$W_\ell(t)=\mathcal P_{N_\ell(Q)}\bigl(\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H'}\bigr)$
and $G_\ell(t)=\mathcal P_{N_\ell(Z_R)}G(t)$ with
\[
 \norm{\e^{it\mathbb H'}W\e^{-it\mathbb H'}-W_\ell(t)}+\norm{G(t)-G_\ell(t)}\le CL\,\e^{v\abs t-\mu\ell},
\]
where the single factor $L$ bounds $\abs Q$ and $\abs{\partial R}$. Let
$V_\ell(t)$ be the unitary evolution generated by $G_\ell(t)$, supported in
$N_\ell(Z_R)$; Duhamel's formula gives $\norm{V(t)-V_\ell(t)}\le CL\abs t\e^{v\abs t-\mu\ell}$.
Hence $W_\ell(t)V_\ell(t)$ is a contraction supported in
$S:=N_\ell(Q\cup Z_R)$ which approximates the integrand uniformly for
$\abs t\le T$ with error $CL(1+T)\e^{vT-\mu\ell}$. Take
$\ell=\lceil K_{\mathrm{loc}}B_*\rceil$ with $K_{\mathrm{loc}}$ fixed large
enough (after $T_0$) that this error is at most $\e^{-10b}L^{-r_*}$,
using $\log L\le B_*$ and $\log(1+T)\le C+B_*$. Averaging the localized
integrands against the truncated Gaussian gives a contraction
$M_{\mathrm{loc}}$ supported in $S$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loctwosided}
 \norm{M_{\mathrm{loc}}\Psi-z\Phi},\ \norm{M_{\mathrm{loc}}^\dagger\Phi-z\Psi}\le C\e^{-10b}L^{-r_*}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Polar decomposition.} Put $D=M_{\mathrm{loc}}/z$. Then $\norm D\le\e^{2b}$,
$\norm{D\Psi-\Phi},\norm{D^\dagger\Phi-\Psi}\le C\e^{-8b}L^{-r_*}$, and writing
$(D^\dagger D-\id)\Psi=D^\dagger(D\Psi-\Phi)+(D^\dagger\Phi-\Psi)$,
$\norm{(D^\dagger D-\id)\Psi}\le C\e^{-6b}L^{-r_*}$. The polar partial
isometry of the square operator $D$ on $\cH_S$ extends to a unitary $U$ on
$\cH_S$ (its two support spaces have equal dimension), tensored with the
identity elsewhere. Since $(\sqrt x-1)^2\le(x-1)^2$ for $x\ge0$,
$\norm{(U-D)\Psi}=\norm{(\id-\abs D)\Psi}\le\norm{(\id-D^\dagger D)\Psi}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Uerror}
 \norm{U\Psi-\Phi}\le C\e^{-6b}L^{-r_*}\le CL^{-r_*}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Support and conclusion.} Every site of $Q$ is more than $w$ from
$R_0$ and from $C_0$. If $\{x,y\}$ crosses $R$ with $x\in R$, $y\notin R$,
then $y$ is more than $w$ from $R_0$ so $x$ is more than $w-1$ from
$R_0$; and $x\notin C$ is more than $w$ from $C_0$, so $y$ is more than $w-1$
from $C_0$. Choosing $C_p>K_{\mathrm{loc}}+3$ makes $S=N_\ell(Q\cup Z_R)$
disjoint from both cores in both replicas. Thus $U$ does not change the
joint two-replica marginal on $R_0C_0$. In $\Phi=F_R\Psi$, the marginal on
$R_0$ and $C_0$ of the \emph{first} replica is $\rho_{R_0}\otimes\rho_{C_0}$,
because $R_0$ was swapped in from the second replica and $C_0$ was not. By
\eqref{eq:Uerror} and contractivity of the partial trace, the original
marginal $\rho_{R_0C_0}$ is within trace distance $CL^{-r_*}$ of
$\rho_{R_0}\otimes\rho_{C_0}$. The continuity bound
$\abs{S(A|B)_\rho-S(A|B)_\sigma}\le C\sqrt\delta\log(\e\dim\cH_A)$ for
$\delta=\frac12\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1$ \cite{Winter2016}, applied to
$I(R_0:C_0)=S(R_0)-S(R_0|C_0)$ with $\log\dim\cH_{R_0}\le L\log q$, gives
$I_\Om(R_0:C_0)\le CL^{1-r_*/2}=CL^{-p-4}\le L^{-p}$ for large $L$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The one-dimensional separation lemma}\label{sec:logsep2}

\begin{inwords}
Combine the two previous facts. An interval $R$ has entropy at most
$C_{\mathrm{int}}$, so its mutual information with anything is at most
$2C_{\mathrm{int}}$. Feed this constant into the reset lemma: any
interval $T$ and any set $E$ at distance of order $\log L$ from $T$ are
independent up to $L^{-p}$. This replaces the entire Section~3 of the
two-dimensional paper (coarse collar, cell conditional information,
angular induction), and it holds for intervals of every length, from a
few sites to the whole chain.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
\begin{lemma}[Information at logarithmic separation]\label{lem:logsep}
For every $p>0$ and $k_0\ge1$ there is $D_p=D_p(q,R,J,\Delta,k_0)$ such that for
all sufficiently large $L$, every $T\subseteq\Lam$ that is a union of at most
$k_0$ intervals, and every $E\subseteq\Lam$ with $\dist(T,E)\ge D_p\log L$,
\[
 I_\Om(T:E)\le L^{-p}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $b=4k_0C_{\mathrm{int}}$, $w=C_p(1+b+\log L)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:reset},
and $D_p\log L\ge 3w+3$. Put $R=T^{+(w+1)}$, a union of at most $k_0$
intervals, $C=\Lam\setminus T^{+(2w+2)}$, $R_0=T$, $C_0=E$. Then
$\dist(R_0,\Lam\setminus R)\ge w+1$, $\dist(C_0,\Lam\setminus C)\ge w+1$, and
$I(R:C)\le 2S(R)\le 2k_0C_{\mathrm{int}}\le b$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}
(subadditivity over the at most $k_0$ intervals of $R$, and
$I(R:C)=S(R)+S(C)-S(RC)\le 2S(R)$ by the Araki--Lieb inequality).
Lemma~\ref{lem:reset} gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A route without Lieb--Robinson bounds]\label{rem:LRfree}
The reset lemma is the only place in Part~\ref{part:mps} where a
Lieb--Robinson bound is used. It can be replaced by the amplification
argument of \cite[Section~10]{AL}, which uses exact positive quasi-local
constraints with stretched-exponential tails $\e^{-cl^\alpha}$,
$\alpha=p/(p+1)$, random products of the associated contractions, and a
polar decomposition, and needs no propagation bound for the unitary
dynamics. With the area law as input, that argument gives
$I(T:E)\le L^{-M}$ for $E$ beyond distance $C(\log L)^{1/\alpha}$ of an
interval $T$. Running the protocol below with blocks of that size yields
a matrix product state with bond dimension $\exp(O((\log L)^{1/\alpha}))$,
quasi-polynomial for every fixed $p$, and polynomial only in the limit.
Exactly polynomial bond dimension through the protocol requires the
logarithmic separation of Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep}.
\end{remark}
\section{Distributed constructions and the three moves}\label{sec:moves}

\begin{inwords}
Think of a bookkeeping game. There are \emph{parties}; each owns a set
of registers (tensor factors), which may include whole copies of the
ground vector held privately. A \emph{gate} is a linear contraction
applied to the registers of a few parties. A gate is \emph{private} if it
touches one party only; private gates are free. A non-private gate is
charged only through two numbers: how many parties it involves, and how
many such gates each party ever takes part in. In addition every
non-private gate must have a \emph{polynomial expansion}: it must be a
sum of polynomially many terms, each a composition of private maps,
preparations of normalized vectors shared by two parties, contractions
by normalized covectors shared by two parties, and transfers of
polynomial-dimensional registers. The dimensions of the shared vectors
are unrestricted.

The point of the game is that its final physical density admits a tensor
network with polynomial links on the party graph (Section~\ref{sec:compression}),
whatever the private dimensions were. So we are free to prepare the
ground vector exactly at one party and to use arbitrarily entangled
bipartite resources; the price is only paid in participation counts.

A \emph{sheet} is a full set of site registers with an assignment of
owners; its reference vector is a copy of the ground vector placed on
those registers in canonical site coordinates (ownership does not permute
sites). The three moves change the ownership of a sheet or of two sheets
while keeping the actual vector within $O(L^{-p/2})$ of the product of the
reference vectors.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
A \emph{distributed construction} of size parameter $L$ consists of a
finite set of parties, each with an initial finite-dimensional register
space in a normalized product initial vector, and a sequence of gates,
each a linear contraction between specified input and output register
spaces of the participating parties, the other registers being
spectators. Registers to be discarded are retained until a final partial
trace. An \emph{allowed monomial} is a composition of private
contractions, preparations of normalized vectors shared between two
participating parties, contractions by normalized covectors shared
between two participating parties, and identity transfers of registers of
uniformly polynomial dimension. A non-private gate is \emph{admissible} if
it is a sum of uniformly polynomially many allowed monomials with
uniformly polynomially bounded scalar coefficients, each monomial
containing a bounded number of shared vectors, shared covectors and
transfers; a contraction within $L^{-a}$ of such a sum, for a fixed $a>0$,
is also admissible after rescaling by $1+L^{-a}$. A \emph{bounded change}
is a sequence of a bounded number of admissible gates on a bounded number
of parties.

A sheet is $\cH_\Lam=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lam}\C^q$ with an owner assigned to
each site; its reference vector is $\Om$ in these registers.

\begin{lemma}[Birth and death]\label{lem:birth}
In a sheet, let $T$ be a set of sites owned by $P_\circ$, and let
$E=\{x:\text{the owner of }x\text{ is not }P_\circ\}$,
$U=\Lam\setminus(T\cup E)$. If $I_\Om(T:E)\le L^{-2p}$, then the change of
owner of $T$ from $P_\circ$ to any party $Q_\circ$ (a \emph{birth}) is a bounded
change involving $P_\circ,Q_\circ$ only, consisting of private maps and one
shared normalized vector, with reference-vector error at most $L^{-p}$.
The reverse change (a \emph{death}: $T$ returns from $Q_\circ$ to
$P_\circ$) is a bounded change with the same error whenever the
ownership and information conditions hold in the sheet \emph{after} the
death; it uses one shared normalized covector instead.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $V,s,s'$ be given by Lemma~\ref{lem:splitting} for $(T,U,E)$, put
$\mathcal V=\id_{TE}\otimes V$ and $\Pi=\ket s\bra s_{T\cB_T}\otimes\id_{E\cB_E}$.
All of $T,U$ belong to $P_\circ$. At $P_\circ$ apply $V$ on $U$; contract
$T\cB_T$ with $\bra s$ (private, both at $P_\circ$); supply the shared
vector $s$ on $T\cB_T$ with $T$ now owned by $Q_\circ$ and $\cB_T$ by
$P_\circ$; apply $V^\dagger$ at $P_\circ$. The composite is the Hermitian
contraction $B=\mathcal V^\dagger\Pi\mathcal V$ and
$\norm{(B-\id)\Om}\le\norm{(\Pi-\id)\mathcal V\Om}=\norm{(\Pi-\id)(\mathcal V\Om-s\otimes s')}\le L^{-p}$.
For the death, apply $V$ at $P_\circ$, use the shared covector $\bra s$ on
$T\cB_T$ held by $Q_\circ,P_\circ$, reinsert $s$ wholly at $P_\circ$, and
apply $V^\dagger$: the same operator $B$ with reversed ownership.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-sheet exchange]\label{lem:exchange}
Consider two sheets and an interval $Y\subseteq\Lam$. Inside $Y$ exchange
the two ownership assignments (sheet 1 at $x\in Y$ gets the owner sheet 2
had, and conversely); outside $Y$ keep both. Let $P_\circ$ be a party,
\[
 Z=\{x:\text{the two owners of }x\text{ are not both }P_\circ\},\qquad
 T=Z\cap Y,\quad E=Z\setminus Y,\quad U=\Lam\setminus Z .
\]
If $I_\Om(T:E)\le L^{-2p}$, the exchange is implemented by renaming of
registers (no register changes party) and private contractions at
$P_\circ$, with error at most $4L^{-p}$ between the products of the old and
of the new reference vectors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal R$ rename the two sheets inside $Y$: a private identification
of tensor factors, since the new owner of sheet 1 at $x\in Y$ is the old
owner of sheet 2 there. In canonical coordinates $\mathcal R$ acts as the
swap $F_{Y}$ of the two sheets on $Y$. All registers of $U$ on both sheets
are held by $P_\circ$ before and after. Let $V,s,s'$ be given by
Lemma~\ref{lem:splitting} for $(T,U,E)$, $\mathcal V=\id_{TE}\otimes V$, and
let $D_U=(V^{\otimes2})^\dagger F_{\cB_T}V^{\otimes2}$ on the two copies of
$U$, a private contraction at $P_\circ$ (apply $V$ to both copies, swap the
two $\cB_T$ factors, apply the adjoints). With $A=Y\cap U$, after
$\mathcal R$ apply the private swap $F_A$ and then $D_U$. Since
$Y=T\sqcup A$, the net action on the two copies is
\[
 D_UF_AF_Y=D_UF_T=(\mathcal V^{\otimes2})^\dagger F_{T\cB_T}\mathcal V^{\otimes2},
\]
using that $F_T$ acts outside $U$ and commutes through $V^{\otimes2}$. Put
$\zeta=s\otimes s'$ and $\delta=\norm{\mathcal V\Om-\zeta}\le L^{-p}$. Then
$\norm{(\mathcal V\Om)^{\otimes2}-\zeta^{\otimes2}}\le2\delta$, $\zeta^{\otimes2}$
is fixed by $F_{T\cB_T}$ (it swaps two identical factors $s$), and
$(\mathcal V^{\otimes2})^\dagger\mathcal V^{\otimes2}=\id$, so
\[
 \bigl\|(\mathcal V^{\otimes2})^\dagger F_{T\cB_T}\mathcal V^{\otimes2}\Om^{\otimes2}-\Om^{\otimes2}\bigr\|
 \le\norm{(F_{T\cB_T}-\id)\bigl((\mathcal V\Om)^{\otimes2}-\zeta^{\otimes2}\bigr)}\le4\delta .
\]
In canonical coordinates both the old and the new reference vector are
$\Om\otimes\Om$ (the sheets are relabeled, not moved), which is the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In the two-dimensional source these lemmas carry encoded holes and tags at
the points where three owners meet. On a line, the closure of two
intervals with different owners meets at most two owners at any point, so
there are no such points and no encodings. This is the first of the two
reasons why the adaptive patch constraints of \cite[Section~4]{PP} are
not needed in one dimension.
\end{remark}

\section{The sweep protocol}\label{sec:sweep}

\begin{inwords}
Here is the schedule. Divide the chain into consecutive blocks of $t$
sites, where $t=20w$ and $w$ is the logarithmic separation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep}; the last block is whatever is left over, of length
less than $3t$. At the start, a single party $R_0$ holds everything. At
step $k$ the picture is
\[
 P_1\,|\,P_2\,|\cdots|\,P_k\,|\,R_k ,
\]
with $P_i$ owning block $i$ and the \emph{remainder} party $R_k$ owning the
rest of the chain. Two births, both allowed because they leave a margin
of $w$ to any other owner, create the next remainder $R_{k+1}$ on
everything beyond block $k+1$ plus a margin, and the next block owner
$P_{k+1}$ on the interior of block $k+1$. What remains of $R_k$ are two
bands of width $w$: one wedged between $P_k$ and $P_{k+1}$, one between
$P_{k+1}$ and $R_{k+1}$. Each band must be dissolved, and this is the only
nontrivial step.

A band $A$ wedged between two different owners $C$ (left) and $B$ (right)
cannot simply die into $B$: the band touches $C$, so it is not
informationally independent of the non-$B$ owners. The two-dimensional
paper solves this along every edge of every square with a fixed sequence
of moves using an auxiliary copy of the ground vector, and the sequence
transplants verbatim. In units of the band width, with the band at
$0<x<1$, $C$ to the left and $B$ to the right:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item Create a fresh copy of the ground vector, all of it owned by $C$.
On it, birth $B$ on $(-6,-1)$, then $A$ on $(-5,-2)$, then $C$ on $(-4,-3)$.
The auxiliary word is $C\,|\,B\,|\,A\,|\,C\,|\,A\,|\,B\,|\,C$.
\item Exchange the two sheets inside the window $(-7,-\tfrac72)$. Both
ends of the window are in places where both sheets are owned by $C$, and
the only non-$C$ material inside the window is separated from the
non-$C$ material outside it by a margin $w$. The main sheet now reads
$C\,|\,B\,|\,A\,|\,C\,|\,A\,|\,B$ with interfaces at $-6,-5,-4,0,1$, and the
auxiliary sheet is retired.
\item On the main sheet: the $C$ band $(-4,0)$ is flanked by $A$ on both
sides, so it dies into $A$; the $A$ band $(-5,1)$ is then flanked by $B$ on
both sides and dies into $B$. The word is $C\,|\,B$ with the interface
at $-6$. Finally birth $C$ on $(-3,0)$ inside $B$ and let the $B$ band
$(-6,-3)$ die into $C$: the interface is back at $0$ and the band belongs
to $B$.
\end{enumerate}
Each move in this list has its changed region at distance at least $w$
from every other owner, so Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep} supplies the information
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:birth} or~\ref{lem:exchange}. Only three
parties take part. Since a party is born once, dissolves the bands on its
two sides once, and is otherwise a spectator, every party participates in
a bounded number of moves.

Why a sweep rather than the dyadic hierarchy of the source? Because the
final network must be placed on the chain. A link of the party network
joins two parties, and every link crossing a given bond of the chain
contributes a factor to that bond's dimension. In a dyadic hierarchy,
every scale has a link crossing every bond (the tree of intervals has
cutwidth $\log L$), so the bond dimension would be polynomial to the power
$\log L$. In the sweep every link joins parties attached to blocks at
most two apart, and a bounded number of links cross each bond.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
Fix $p=12$. Let $D_{24}$ be the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep} with
exponent $2p=24$ and $k_0=1$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocks}
 w=\lceil D_{24}\log L\rceil,\qquad t=20w,\qquad
 k_*=\max\{k\ge0:\ L-kt\ge 3t\},
\end{equation}
with the blocks $B_i=[(i-1)t+1,\,it]$ for $1\le i\le k_*$ and the last block
$B_{k_*+1}=[k_*t+1,L]$, of length in $[3t,4t)$. We assume $L$ large enough
that $t\ge 20$ and $k_*\ge0$ (small $L$ are handled in Section~\ref{sec:routing}).
For a band $[a,a+w)$ with $C$ owning $[a-8w,a)$ and $B$ owning
$[a+w,a+2w)$ (and possibly more), write $x=(\cdot-a)/w$ for the scaled
coordinate.

\begin{construction}[Band dissolution]\label{con:band}
Let the main sheet read $C\,|\,A\,|\,B$ near $a$: $C$ on $x<0$ down to at
least $x=-8$, $A$ on $0<x<1$, $B$ on $x>1$ up to at least $x=2$, where
$A,B,C$ are three distinct parties. Perform:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(i)] Prepare an auxiliary sheet, a fresh copy of $\Om$ held entirely
by $C$ (a private initialization). Birth $B$ on $(-6,-1)$; birth $A$ on
$(-5,-2)$; birth $C$ on $(-4,-3)$.
\item[(ii)] Exchange the two sheets inside $Y=(-7,-\tfrac72)$ with
$P_\circ=C$. Retire the auxiliary sheet (its registers stay idle at their
owners).
\item[(iii)] On the main sheet: death of the $C$ band $(-4,0)$ into $A$;
death of the $A$ band $(-5,1)$ into $B$; birth of $C$ on $(-3,0)$ inside
$B$; death of the $B$ band $(-6,-3)$ into $C$.
\end{enumerate}
If there is no left owner $C$ (the band starts at site $1$), replace
the whole construction by a single death of the band into $B$.
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[The band dissolution is a bounded change]\label{lem:band}
For all sufficiently large $L$, each of the nine moves in
Construction~\ref{con:band} satisfies the information hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:birth} or Lemma~\ref{lem:exchange} with exponent $2p=24$,
so that the construction is a bounded change on the parties $A,B,C$ with
total reference-vector error at most $12L^{-12}$. Afterwards the main
sheet reads $C\,|\,B$ with the interface at $x=0$, and all other ownership
is unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We check each move; distances are in units of $w\ge D_{24}\log L$, and
every changed region is an interval, so Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep} applies.
(i) The first auxiliary birth has $E=\varnothing$ and needs no
information bound. The second has changed region $(-5,-2)$ inside the
$B$ band $(-6,-1)$, and $E$, the non-$B$ sites, is at distance $1$. The
third has changed region $(-4,-3)$ inside $(-5,-2)$, distance $1$. The
auxiliary word is now $C|B|A|C|A|B|C$ at $-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1$.
(ii) The main sheet is $C$ on $x<0$ and the auxiliary sheet is $C$ on
$x<-6$, $(-4,-3)$ and $x>-1$. Hence $Z\cap Y=(-6,-4)$ (auxiliary $B$ and
$A$) and $Z\setminus Y=(-3,-1)\cup[0,\infty)$, at distance $1$ from $T$.
After the exchange the main sheet inside $Y$ carries the auxiliary
assignment, giving $C|B|A|C|A|B$ at $-6,-5,-4,0,1$; the auxiliary sheet
inside $Y$ is $C$.
(iii) The $C$ band $(-4,0)$ is surrounded by $A$ on $(-5,-4)\cup(0,1)$,
and the non-$A$ sites after the death are $x\le-5$ and $x\ge1$, at
distance $1$. The $A$ band $(-5,1)$ is surrounded by $B$ on
$(-6,-5)\cup(1,2)$; non-$B$ sites after the death are $x\le-6$ and $x\ge2$,
distance $1$. The birth of $C$ on $(-3,0)$ inside $B$ has non-$B$ sites
at distance $\ge2$. The death of $(-6,-3)$ into $C$ has non-$C$ sites
$x\ge0$ at distance $3$. The counts of moves and parties are as stated;
each birth or death costs at most $L^{-12}$ and the exchange at most
$4L^{-12}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{construction}[The sweep]\label{con:sweep}
Initially one party $R_0$ holds the sheet. For $k=0,1,\dots,k_*-1$, with
$a=kt+1$ the first site of block $k+1$ and the word $P_1|\cdots|P_k|R_k$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(1)] Birth $R_{k+1}$ on $[a+t+w,L]$ inside $R_k$.
\item[(2)] Birth $P_{k+1}$ on $[a+w,a+t-1]$ inside $R_k$.
\item[(3)] Dissolve the band $[a,a+w-1]$ (owner $R_k$) between $C=P_k$
and $B=P_{k+1}$ by Construction~\ref{con:band}; if $k=0$ let the band die
into $P_1$.
\item[(4)] Dissolve the band $[a+t,a+t+w-1]$ (owner $R_k$) between
$C=P_{k+1}$ and $B=R_{k+1}$ by Construction~\ref{con:band}.
\end{enumerate}
At the end set $P_{k_*+1}=R_{k_*}$, which owns exactly the last block.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Properties of the sweep]\label{prop:sweep}
For all sufficiently large $L$, Construction~\ref{con:sweep} is a
distributed construction with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item There are $2k_*+2\le 2L$ parties. The initial vector is a product by
party: $R_0$ holds $\Om$, and every auxiliary copy of $\Om$ is initialized
privately at the party $C$ of its band dissolution.
\item There are at most $20k_*\le 2L$ non-private gates. Each involves at
most three parties and is admissible with one shared vector or covector
per monomial. Each party participates in at most $40$ non-private gates
over its lifetime.
\item The final vector $\Psi$ satisfies $\norm\Psi\le1$ and
$\norm{\Psi-\Om\otimes\Xi}\le 26\,L^{-11}$, where $\Xi$ is the tensor
product of the retired auxiliary copies of $\Om$ in their final registers
and $\norm\Xi=1$. At the end, party $P_i$ owns exactly the raw registers
of block $B_i$, of dimension $q^{\abs{B_i}}\le q^{4t}=L^{O(1)}$, and no other
party owns a raw register.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At step $k$ the word is $P_1|\cdots|P_k|R_k$ with $R_k$ owning
$[a,L]$, $a=kt+1$. Move (1) has changed region $[a+t+w,L]$ and
$E=[1,a-1]$ at distance $t+w$; move (2) has changed region $[a+w,a+t-1]$
and $E=[1,a-1]\cup[a+t+w,L]$ at distance $w$. Both are births by
Lemma~\ref{lem:birth} with Lemma~\ref{lem:logsep}. After them $R_k$ owns
the two bands $[a,a+w-1]$ and $[a+t,a+t+w-1]$. For (3), $C=P_k$ owns
block $k$, of length $t=20w\ge8w$ to the left of $a$, and $B=P_{k+1}$ owns
$[a+w,a+t-1]$, of length $19w\ge2w$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:band} applies
(for $k=0$, the death of $[1,w]$ into $P_1$ has $E=[t+1,L]$ at distance
$t-w$). For (4), $C=P_{k+1}$ owns $[a+w,a+t-1]$ of length $19w\ge8w$ and
$B=R_{k+1}$ owns $[a+t+w,L]$ of length at least $3t-w\ge2w$. After (4)
the word is $P_1|\cdots|P_{k+1}|R_{k+1}$. The parties are $R_0,\dots,R_{k_*}$
and $P_1,\dots,P_{k_*}$, with $P_{k_*+1}=R_{k_*}$. The number of moves per
step is at most $2+2\cdot9=20$, so at most $20k_*$ in total, and the last
block has length in $[3t,4t)$.

Participation: $R_k$ takes part in steps $k-1$ (as the born party in (1)
and as $B$ in (4)) and $k$ (as $P_\circ$ in (1), (2) and as $A$ in (3),
(4)); $P_k$ takes part in step $k-1$ (born in (2), $B$ in (3), $C$ in (4))
and step $k$ ($C$ in (3)). Each band dissolution has nine moves and an
exchange that is private; so no party exceeds $2\cdot(2+2\cdot9)=40$ gates.
Every birth or death is a single gate on two parties with one shared
vector or covector, and every exchange consists of private gates only.

Errors: every move takes the current actual vector within the stated
error of the next reference vector, which is the tensor product of $\Om$
on the main sheet and $\Om$ on each auxiliary sheet in canonical
coordinates (Lemmas~\ref{lem:birth}, \ref{lem:exchange}; retired sheets
are spectators of norm one). Telescoping a product of contractions bounds
the accumulated error by the sum of the per-move errors: two births and
two dissolutions per step cost at most $(2+2\cdot12)L^{-12}$, so the total
is at most $26k_*L^{-12}\le 26L^{-11}$. The initial vector has norm one and every gate is a
contraction, so $\norm\Psi\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why no patches]
In the source, squares of size below $K_0\log L$ are repainted by
``small-patch rewrites'' that use finite-rank cylinder projections of
polynomial rank. Here the final owners hold blocks of $O(\log L)$ sites,
whose register dimension $q^{O(\log L)}$ is polynomial, and the compression
theorem below accepts polynomial-dimensional physical outputs. Converting
a block tensor into single-site tensors is then linear algebra
(Section~\ref{sec:routing}). This is the second reason the adaptive patch
constraint is not needed.
\end{remark}

\section{Compression and column extraction}\label{sec:compression}

\begin{inwords}
The sweep is a pure linear calculation ending in a partial trace over
everything but the block registers. Its gates involve shared vectors of
enormous dimension (purifications of half-chain marginals). The
compression theorem says that if we replace, in the \emph{density}
calculation $\rho=\Tr_{\mathrm{private}}\ket\Psi\bra\Psi$, each shared
vector $\ket\eta\bra{\tilde\eta}$ by an average of $k$ independent random
product operators $U_j\otimes V_j$ built from its Schmidt data and
Gaussian coefficients, then for polynomial $k$ the physical density
changes by at most $L^{-p}$ in trace norm. Expanding the error by which
positions use a centered correction, each correction position involves
only the two endpoint parties, and by bounded participation only
boundedly many gates have to be opened for each corrected position; a
Gaussian second-moment computation then shows that the expected trace
norm of a term with $s$ corrections is $k^{-s/2}$ times a fixed
polynomial per correction. Nothing in this argument depends on the
private dimensions or on the Schmidt ranks of the shared vectors, which
is the whole point. The output is an operator tensor network with one
node per party and polynomial links between parties that shared a gate.
A column of it is a ket network with the same links, close to the ground
vector after normalization.

The theorem and its proof are not geometric and we do not change a word
of them; we restate the theorem in the form used here and sketch the
estimate.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
\begin{theorem}[Density compression, {\cite[Theorem~5.2]{PP}}]\label{thm:compression}
Consider a family of distributed constructions indexed by $L\ge2$ such
that: the numbers of parties and gates are uniformly polynomial; the
initial vector is a product of normalized private vectors; every gate is
a contraction; each non-private gate involves at most $b$ parties and each
party participates in at most $b$ non-private gates; each non-private gate
is admissible with uniformly polynomial bounds; and the intended physical
output of each party has uniformly polynomial dimension, everything else
being traced out. Let $\rho$ be the physical output density (positive,
trace at most one). For every fixed $p>0$ there is an operator tensor
network $\sigma$ with one node per party, a bounded number of links at
each node, each link joining two parties that participate in a common
non-private gate, uniformly polynomial link dimensions, and
\[
 \norm{\sigma-\rho}_1\le L^{-p}.
\]
The constants depend only on $p$, $b$ and the uniform polynomial bounds,
not on private dimensions or on the Schmidt ranks of shared vectors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
First eliminate shared covectors: a covector $\bra\eta$ on a pair of
registers is replaced by appending $m-1$ copies of $\eta$ and averaging the
$m$ cyclic permutations of the $m$ pair registers, a contraction $T_m$ with
$\norm{T_m-\ket\eta^{\otimes m}\bra\eta}=m^{-1/2}$; with a common fixed
garbage vector in every branch, a gate with $K$ monomials, each with at
most $r$ covectors, is approximated in operator norm within
$rm^{-1/2}\sum\abs{c_\xi}$ by a gate with $Km^r$ monomials using only
shared vectors. Choosing $m$ polynomial makes this error at most
$\epsilon/(8M)$ per gate, $M$ the number of non-private gates.

Next, in the density calculation each source operator $X=\ket\eta\bra{\tilde\eta}$,
with Schmidt data $(\lambda_a,u_a,v_a)$ and $(\mu_c,\tilde u_c,\tilde v_c)$,
is replaced by $\widehat X=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kU_j\otimes V_j$ with
$U_j=\sum_{a,c}(\lambda_a\mu_c)^{1/4}g^{(j)}_{ac}\ket{u_a}\bra{\tilde u_c}$,
$V_j=\sum_{b,d}(\lambda_b\mu_d)^{1/4}\overline{g^{(j)}_{bd}}\ket{v_b}\bra{\tilde v_d}$
and independent standard complex Gaussians $g^{(j)}_{ac}$. Then
$\E\widehat X=X$, and the centered coefficients of $\Delta=\widehat X-X$ have
diagonal covariance $k^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda_a\lambda_b\mu_c\mu_d}$ by the
Gaussian pairing formula. Expanding the product over all $N$ slot
positions, $\widehat\rho-\rho'=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S}F_S$ where $S$ lists
the positions using a correction. For $\abs S=s$, the endpoint parties
number at most $2s$ and participate in at most $2bs$ gates; expand only
those gates into monomials and fix the physical basis entries at those
parties, at total weighted cost $Q(L)^s$ with $Q$ a fixed polynomial.
For a fixed choice, the calculation separates into an affected side,
holding the corrected endpoints as free inputs, and an exterior, joined
only by the exact shared vectors crossing between them, with Schmidt
probability matrices $\tau,\tilde\tau$. The affected contractions give a
block operator $O$ of norm at most one, the corrected coefficients $z_{il}$
have covariance $k^{-s}\sqrt{p_i\tilde p_l}\,\delta\delta$, and the exterior
input operator is $Z=\sum_{il}z_{il}\tau^{1/2}O_{li}^{\mathsf T}\tilde\tau^{1/2}$.
Schatten--Hölder with exponents $4,2,4$ and trace-one weights gives
$\norm Z_1\le\norm{\sum_{il}z_{il}\tilde\tau^{1/4}O_{li}\tau^{1/4}}_2$, whose expected
square is $k^{-s}\norm{\widetilde R^{1/4}OR^{1/4}}_2^2\le k^{-s}$ with
$R=\mathrm{diag}(p_i)\otimes\tau$, $\widetilde R=\mathrm{diag}(\tilde p_l)\otimes\tilde\tau$.
The exterior maps and the physical matrix unit do not increase the trace
norm. Hence $\E\norm{F_S}_1\le Q(L)^sk^{-s/2}$ and
$\E\norm{\widehat\rho-\rho'}_1\le(1+Q(L)k^{-1/2})^N-1\le\epsilon/4$ for a
polynomial $k$. Fix a realization. The link structure: the common monomial
label of a gate is shared along a star between its participants, and each
slot's sample index $j$ is shared between its two endpoints; all private
maps, private source preparations and final private traces are
contracted inside each party's node.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Column extraction, {\cite[Lemma~8.1]{PP}}]\label{lem:column}
Let $\Om$ be a unit vector on a finite tensor product and $\sigma$ an
operator with $\norm{\sigma-\ket\Om\bra\Om}_1\le\eta<1$. Then some
product-basis column $v=\sigma\ket z$ is nonzero and satisfies
$\min_\theta\norm{v/\norm v-\e^{i\theta}\Om}\le2\eta$. Fixing a column in an
operator tensor network leaves all virtual dimensions unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $E=\sigma-\ket\Om\bra\Om$ and $a_z=\langle\Om|z\rangle$. Then
$\sum_z\norm{E\ket z}^2=\norm E_2^2\le\eta^2$ and $\sum_z\abs{a_z}^2=1$, so
some $z$ with $a_z\ne0$ has $\norm{E\ket z}\le\eta\abs{a_z}$. For it,
$w=v/a_z$ satisfies $\norm{w-\Om}\le\eta$, hence $w\ne0$ and
$\norm{w/\norm w-\Om}\le2\eta$. Fixing the input index at every node is a
contraction of physical legs only.
\end{proof}

\section{Assembly on the chain}\label{sec:routing}

\begin{inwords}
Apply the compression theorem to the sweep. Its parties are the block
owners $P_i$, with physical output the $q^{\abs{B_i}}$-dimensional block
register, and the remainders $R_k$, with no physical output. Every link
joins two parties that shared a gate, and by construction those are
attached to blocks at most two apart. Put the node of $R_k$ next to block
$k+1$. Then a bond of the chain between two blocks is crossed only by
links among the boundedly many parties attached to the four nearest
blocks: constant congestion, and the dimension of a block-to-block bond
is a bounded product of polynomial link dimensions. Finally, split each
block node into one tensor per site by sequential singular value
decompositions; the internal bonds are at most $q^{\abs{B_i}}$ times the
crossing dimension, still polynomial. The column-extraction lemma turns
the operator network into a ket network first. Small chains are handled
by the trivial exact representation.
\end{inwords}

\precisely
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $L_0$ be large enough for Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep} and for the
inequalities below; for $2\le L<L_0$ every vector on $\Lam$ is an exact
matrix product state of bond dimension at most $q^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\le
q^{L_0/2}$, which is absorbed into $C$. Let $L\ge L_0$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep}(3) and
$\norm{\ket u\bra u-\ket v\bra v}_1\le2\norm{u-v}$ for vectors of norm at
most one, the physical output density
$\rho=\Tr_{\mathrm{private}}\ket\Psi\bra\Psi$ satisfies
$\norm{\rho-\ket\Om\bra\Om}_1\le 52L^{-11}$. The construction satisfies the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} with $b=40$ and physical
output dimensions at most $q^{4t}\le L^{80D_{24}\log q+1}$, so with
accuracy $L^{-5}$ there is an operator network $\sigma$ on the party
graph with $\norm{\sigma-\ket\Om\bra\Om}_1\le L^{-5}+52L^{-11}\le2L^{-5}$,
bounded degree, and link dimensions at most $C_1L^{r}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:column} some column is a nonzero ket network $v$ with the
same links and $\min_\theta\norm{v/\norm v-\e^{i\theta}\Om}\le4L^{-5}\le L^{-1}$.

Placement. Attach $P_i$ to block $B_i$ and $R_k$ to block $B_{k+1}$. Two
parties sharing a gate are, by the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep},
among $\{P_k,P_{k+1},R_k,R_{k+1}\}$ for some $k$, hence attached to blocks
at most two apart. A bond between blocks $i$ and $i+1$ is therefore
crossed only by links incident to parties attached to blocks
$i-1,\dots,i+2$; there are at most $8$ such parties, each of degree at most
$5\cdot40$ (at most two star links and three sample links per three-party
gate), so at most $\chi:=1600$ links cross any block bond. Route each link along the chain
between its two blocks; group the indices crossing a block bond into one
index of dimension at most $(C_1L^r)^{\chi}$. The tensor at block $i$ is
now a map from its left and right block-bond indices to the block's
physical register $\cH_{B_i}$, of dimension at most $q^{4t}$. Writing
$B_i=\{x_1<\dots<x_n\}$, perform singular value decompositions
successively across $x_1|x_2\cdots x_n$, $x_1x_2|x_3\cdots x_n$, \dots,
treating the two block-bond indices as additional legs attached to the
first and last site; the resulting single-site tensors have internal
bond dimensions at most $\min\{q^{j}(C_1L^r)^\chi,\ q^{n-j}(C_1L^r)^\chi\}\le
q^{2t}(C_1L^r)^{\chi}$ and the block bonds are unchanged. Thus $v$ is a
matrix product state on $\Lam$ with open boundary conditions and bond
dimension at most $q^{2t}(C_1L^r)^\chi\le CL^{c}$, with
$c=40D_{24}\log q+r\chi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Order of constants]
Fix $q,R,J,\Delta$. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives $C_{\mathrm{int}}$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:reset} at $p=24$ gives $C_{24}$, hence $D_{24}$ and the
block scale $t$; the number of parties, gates and the participation bound
$b=40$ are then fixed; Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} at accuracy $L^{-5}$
fixes the covector stack length $m$, the Gaussian sample count $k$, and
the link exponent $r$; the congestion $\chi$ is a numerical constant.
None of these depends on the Hamiltonian or on $L$. The protocol uses
$O(L/\log L)$ auxiliary copies of $\Om$, all private.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\begin{inwords}
\emph{What is different from Hastings.} Hastings' area law proves that
the half-chain marginal has an entanglement spectrum whose tail is
exponentially small beyond rank $\exp(O(1))$, by combining a
Lieb--Robinson bound, a spectral filter that approximately projects onto
the ground state, and a clever bound on the approximate factorization of
the filtered state. Arad--Kitaev--Landau--Vazirani replace the filter by
an approximate ground-state projector built from Chebyshev polynomials
with controlled Schmidt rank, and bootstrap. The present proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} has no dynamics in it at all: the gap is used only to
bound the overlap of a variationally tilted or filtered vector with the
ground vector, and the entropy is read off from operator norms by Hölder,
a three-lines interpolation and the Araki--Lieb inequality. Its
dependence on the gap is linear. The matrix product state theorem has two
one-dimensional proofs here: the two-line route, which is a direct
consequence of the tail bound and the area law and therefore equally free
of dynamics, and the protocol route, which uses a Lieb--Robinson bound in
one lemma (the reset) but in a way that has no analogue in Hastings'
construction (a two-replica swap undone by a local unitary), and which
otherwise consists of Uhlmann-type ownership changes, random Gaussian
compression and column extraction.

\emph{What one dimension buys.} Three things, each used once.
A shell around an interval is crossed by $O(1)$ interactions, so the
buffered discount costs a constant and needs a constant-width buffer.
No three owners ever meet at a point of a line, so no encodings are
needed when ownership changes. And a chain can be swept, so the party
graph has bounded cutwidth. Everything else in the source is either
unchanged or disappears.

\emph{What does not transfer back.} The Araki--Lieb closure fails in two
dimensions because the buffer is an annulus of unknown entropy; the
sweep fails in two dimensions because a region being repainted has a
one-dimensional boundary along which a third owner is adjacent, which
the edge bands and point treatments of the source are designed to handle;
and the two-line route fails because sequential truncation across
nested cuts does not produce a network on the lattice. So the
two-dimensional papers are not inefficient versions of this note; they
solve problems that do not exist on a line.
\end{inwords}
