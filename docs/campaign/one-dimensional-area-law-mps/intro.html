The two theorems

Each site of a finite set Λ of integers carries a quantum system of dimension q, and two sites are joined by an edge when they are consecutive integers. The Hamiltonian H is a sum of terms of norm at most J, each acting on a set of sites of diameter at most R. Its normalized ground state Ω is unique and separated from the rest of the spectrum by a gap Δ, so that H−E0≥Δ(1−|Ω⟩⟨Ω|) for the ground energy E0. Neither theorem assumes a gap on parts of the chain, frustration-freeness, or translation invariance. Both are proved by the one-dimensional argument directly, not as consequences of the two-dimensional theorems of the other tab, and every constant is explicit.

One-dimensional note · Theorem A

Entanglement obeys an area law

For every set A ⊆ Λ, the entanglement entropy of the ground state on A is at most a constant times the number of edges leaving A, and the constant is linear in 1 + J/Δ.

SΩ(A)≤C0(q,R)(1+JΔ)|∂ΛA|

Here |∂ΛA| is the number of edges of Λ with exactly one endpoint in A. The formalized constant is C0 = (8R log q + log 2)(5 + ER + 128BR) + 1024 e BR, where NR = 2 · 4R bounds the number of interaction terms crossing a cut, BR = 1 + NR log2(eqR+1), and ER = min{32NRq2(R+1), 64eNR log2(eqR+1)}. Every interval has entropy at most an explicit Cint ≤ C0(1 + J/Δ).

Hastings proved the one-dimensional area law in 2007 using Lieb–Robinson bounds; later proofs use approximate ground-state projectors. This proof uses no time evolution: the gap enters only through the energy cost of reweighting the ground state, and the constant is linear in J/Δ.

One-dimensional note · Theorem B

Ground states are polynomial matrix product states

On a chain of L ≥ 2 sites there is a normalized matrix product state Φ with open boundary conditions, of bond dimension polynomial in L, within distance 1/L of the ground state Ω.

‖Φ−Ω‖≤L−1,D≤eCint(2c0)64sL128s

Here D is the bond dimension of Φ, the largest Schmidt rank of Φ across a cut of the chain, s = √((1 + J/Δ)BR), and c0 = √(2ee/2). The note allows a global phase and normalizes afterwards; the formal statement needs neither.

Theorem A alone does not give a good matrix product state, since an entropy bound does not control the small Schmidt coefficients that a truncation discards. The marginal tail bound controls them, and truncating the cuts one after another then gives Theorem B with an exponent of order √(1 + J/Δ), where Hastings' construction has an exponent exponential in J/Δ.
