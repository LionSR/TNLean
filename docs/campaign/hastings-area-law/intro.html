The two results

A chain of N sites has local dimension D and nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian terms of norm at most J. Its ground state Ψ0 is unique, with gap ΔE; a finite interaction range is handled by grouping sites. The verified argument uses the common Lieb–Robinson pair v = 40cRJ, ξC = 80, with cR depending only on the range. Put s = max(80cRJ/ΔE, 80) and ξ′ = 6s. The area law, positive factorization, entropy and Rényi bootstrap bounds, matrix product state approximation and Schmidt-diagonal correlation bound all use these same source parameters. The prefactor in the factorization is polynomial in s, and no restriction on identifying the source parameters remains. TNLean also proves the two results by a different one-dimensional argument with better constants, described on the neighbouring tab.

Hastings 2007 · Main theorem

Entanglement of a half chain is bounded

For every cut of the chain, the entanglement entropy of the ground state on the sites to its left is at most a constant that depends on D and ξ′ but not on the length N.

S(ρ1,i0)≤c0ξ′lnξ′lnD2ξ′lnD

Here ρ01,i is the reduced density matrix of Ψ0 on sites 1, …, i. The constant c0 is numerical for nearest-neighbour interactions and depends only on the range in the finite-range statement. This is the printed closed form at the common source length ξ′ = 6s, proved by Hastings' factorization and doubling argument. The corrected iteration gives a larger exponential at the original fixed length; enlarging the admissible Lieb–Robinson pair absorbs that loss. The bound is independent of N and of the cut.

This was the first proof that gapped one-dimensional ground states obey an area law. Its key step is an approximate factorization of the ground-state projector into operators localized near a cut; later proofs replace it by approximate ground-state projectors and obtain constants polynomial in J/ΔE.

Hastings 2007 · Matrix product states

Ground states are efficiently approximated by matrix product states

The Schmidt coefficients of Ψ0 across any cut decay as a power law beyond the first k0 = e2S/2, so a matrix product state whose bond dimension is polynomial in N approximates Ψ0 to accuracy polynomially small in N.

∑α≥k′|A0(α)|2≤4C12e2/ξ′(k′k0)−1/(ξ′lnD)

Here A0(α) are the Schmidt coefficients in nonincreasing order and C1 is the factorization constant. The paper prints e1/ξ′ and a positive exponent; the formalization corrects both. For a gapped chain, take ξ′ = 6s, C1 = K s3 and the common integer threshold k0 = ⌈exp(2Smax)/2⌉ from the printed entropy bound above. Summing the discarded weights over internal cuts bounds the squared Hilbert-space error of the unnormalized sequential truncation Φ by (N − 1) times the displayed expression. The norm error is its square root. These constants do not depend on the chain length; for fewer than two sites the approximation is exact.

An entropy bound alone does not give a good matrix product state, because it does not control the small Schmidt coefficients a truncation discards. The approximate factorization controls them directly.
