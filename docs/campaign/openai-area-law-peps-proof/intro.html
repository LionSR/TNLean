The two theorems

Qudits of dimension q sit on the sites of a finite piece Λ of the square lattice. The Hamiltonian H is a sum of bounded local terms. Its ground vector Ω is unique and separated from the rest of the spectrum by a gap Δ, so that H−E0≥Δ(1−|Ω⟩⟨Ω|). Neither theorem assumes a gap on subregions, frustration freeness or translation invariance.

Area-law manuscript · Theorem 1.1

Entanglement obeys an area law

For every region A, the entanglement entropy of the ground state is at most a constant times the number of lattice edges leaving A.

SΩ(A)≤C(q,R,J,Δ)|∂ΛA|

Without such a bound, S could grow like the number of sites in A. The statement is the gap-to-area-law conjecture in two dimensions. Hastings proved the one-dimensional case in 2007. Earlier two-dimensional results assumed frustration-free models with local gaps and gave weaker bounds.

PEPS manuscript · Theorem 1.1

Ground states are polynomial PEPS

On an open L×L grid, a tensor network with one tensor per site and bond dimension polynomial in L reproduces the whole ground vector to error 1/L.

An area law bounds each cut separately, while a PEPS has to make all cuts compatible on the given lattice. The result addresses Questions 7–8 of the Cirac–Garre-Rubio–Pérez-García survey tracked in #8273. It asserts existence only, and the tensors need not be efficiently computable.
