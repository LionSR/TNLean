The result

A chain of n sites carries a quantum system of dimension d at each site. The Hamiltonian H = ∑i Hi is a sum of nearest-neighbour terms with 0 ≤ Hi ≤ 1, and its ground state Γ is unique, with ground energy ε0 and gap ε. Before this work, the area law showed that Γ is close to a matrix product state of polynomial bond dimension, but finding such a state was not known to be possible in polynomial time: for general chains without a gap the problem is as hard as any problem a quantum computer can verify.

Landau, Vazirani and Vidick 2013 · Theorem 1

Ground states of gapped chains can be found efficiently

There is a randomized algorithm that, given H and an accuracy η, outputs a matrix product state with overlap at least 1 − η with Γ, with probability at least 1 − 1/poly(n), in time

nc(d,ε0,ε)poly(n/η),c(d,ε0,ε)=2O(log3d/ε)+O(ε0/ε)

The ε0/ε term comes from the normalization (1 − ε0/n)−m of the approximate ground-state projector below; it is absent for frustration-free chains, where ε0 = 0, and the running time is polynomial whenever ε0/ε is bounded. The formalization targets the existence statement behind the algorithm: for every cut, a set of polynomially many matrix product states of polynomial bond dimension whose span contains the left half of a state close to Γ, built from the set at the previous cut. Running times are not formalized, since TNLean has no model of computation.

This was the first proof that gapped one-dimensional ground states are not only simple but also efficiently computable. Arad, Landau, Vazirani and Vidick later replaced the matrix concentration step by random subspaces and merged the sweep into a tree, which gives a more efficient algorithm and covers degenerate ground spaces.
