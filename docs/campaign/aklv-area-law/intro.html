The two results

A chain of n sites carries a quantum system of dimension d at each site. The Hamiltonian H = ∑i Hi is a sum of nearest-neighbour terms with 0 ≤ Hi ≤ 1, and its ground state Γ is unique, with ground energy ε0 and gap ε ≤ 1 to the next eigenvalue. Hastings proved that the entanglement entropy of Γ across any cut is bounded independently of n, by eO(log d/ε). Arad, Kitaev, Landau and Vazirani improve the bound exponentially and show that Γ is close to a matrix product state whose bond dimension grows more slowly than any power of n.

Arad, Kitaev, Landau and Vazirani 2013 · Theorems 4.3 and 6.2

An area law of order log³ d/ε

For every cut of the chain, the entanglement entropy of Γ across the cut satisfies

S≤O(log3dε)

Theorem 4.3 states this for frustration-free chains, whose terms all annihilate Γ, and Theorem 6.2 for all chains. In Lean both are proved with the bound C(1 + log d + log(1/ε))3/ε at every cut, for one universal constant C; for frustrated chains ε is the distance from ε0 to the next eigenvalue. The printed form, without the logarithm of 1/ε, is not yet derived; the introduction of the paper writes it with a tilde, which hides logarithmic factors.

The dependence on the gap is exponentially better than Hastings' and may be optimal up to logarithmic factors. The proof needs neither the Lieb–Robinson bound nor the detectability lemma: a Chebyshev polynomial of a truncated Hamiltonian does the work of both.

Arad, Kitaev, Landau and Vazirani 2013 · Sections 5 and 7

Matrix product states of sub-polynomial bond dimension

For accuracy 1/poly(n), Γ is approximated by a matrix product state of bond dimension

B=eO~(log3/4n/ε1/4)

For frustration-free chains the paper prints the smaller B = exp(O(ε−1/3 log2/3 n)). The general bound is proved in Lean, at every accuracy δ, with log B ≤ C[u3/ε + (z3/ε)1/4(u + log(z + 1))], where u = 1 + log d + log(1/ε) and z = log(2048(n + 1)/δ2). For frustration-free chains it is proved with (z2/ε)1/3 in place of (z3/ε)1/4, that is log B = O(log2/3 n log log n) at δ = 1/poly(n). The extra log log n comes from the rank of the paper's Lemma 4.2, which line 868 drops; the sign of the gap exponent at line 867 is a misprint. The paper also deduces that approximating the ground energy of such chains is not NP-hard unless 3-SAT has sub-exponential algorithms; complexity statements are outside the formalization.

A bond dimension smaller than any power of n means that the ground state can be found by a search over matrix product states in sub-exponential time, before a polynomial-time algorithm was known.
