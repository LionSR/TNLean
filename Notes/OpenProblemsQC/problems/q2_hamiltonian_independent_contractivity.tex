\section{Q2: Hamiltonian-independent contractivity --- resolved}
\label{sec:q2}
%======================================================================

We use the dissipator convention
\begin{equation}
  \Dd_L(X)=LXL^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,X\},
  \qquad
  \Dd=\sum_{j=1}^m\Dd_{L_j},
\end{equation}
and write $\M_d=M_d(\C)$. The algebra $\Alg(\{L_j\})$ is the unital
complex algebra generated by the jumps, without adjoining their adjoints.

\begin{definition}[Contractivity and HIC \cite{Wolff2026Contractivity}]
\label{def:q2-hic}
Let $\mathcal E^H_{t,s}$ be the propagator of
\begin{equation}
  \Ld^H_t=-i[H(t), \cdot\,]+\Dd,
\end{equation}
where $H=H^\dagger$ has locally integrable matrix entries. For a fixed $H$,
the evolution is \emph{exponentially contractive} if there are
$K_H,\gamma_H>0$ such that, for every pair of states $\rho,\sigma$ and every
$t\ge s\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(\rho-\sigma)}_1
 \le K_H e^{-\gamma_H(t-s)}\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1.
 \label{eq:q2-source-contractivity}
\end{equation}
The dissipator $\Dd$ is \emph{Hamiltonian-independently contractive} (HIC) if
\begin{equation}
  \forall\,H=H^\dagger\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}},\quad
  \exists K_H,\gamma_H>0\quad\text{such that
  \eqref{eq:q2-source-contractivity} holds.}
  \label{eq:q2-hic-quantifiers}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, one may quantify in \eqref{eq:q2-source-contractivity} over all
traceless Hermitian matrices: if $0\ne X=X^\dagger$ and $\tr X=0$, then
$X=c(\rho-\sigma)$ for states $\rho,\sigma$, where
$c=\norm{X}_1/2$ and $\rho=X_+/c$, $\sigma=X_-/c$.
\end{definition}

The quantifiers in \eqref{eq:q2-hic-quantifiers} are important: the definition
allows the constants to depend on the drive. The result below proves the
strictly stronger conclusion that one pair of constants works for every
locally integrable Hamiltonian.

\begin{theorem}[Resolution of Q2: uniform HIC]
\label{thm:q2-uniform-hic}
If
\begin{equation}
  \Alg(\{L_1,\ldots,L_m\})=\M_d,
  \label{eq:q2-full-jump-algebra}
\end{equation}
then there are constants $K\ge1$ and $\gamma>0$, depending only on the jump
family, such that for every $H=H^\dagger\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, all states
$\rho,\sigma$, and all $t\ge s\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(\rho-\sigma)}_1
 \le K e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1.
 \label{eq:q2-uniform-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\Dd$ is HIC in the sense of \cref{def:q2-hic}.
\end{theorem}

We give the compactness proof in detail. The case $d=1$ is immediate, since
there are no nonzero traceless Hermitian differences, so assume $d\ge2$. All
spaces below are finite-dimensional. Let
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf V=\{X\in\M_d:X=X^\dagger,\ \tr X=0\}
\end{equation}
be regarded as a real normed space with the trace norm. For $U\in\mathrm
U(d)$ define the interaction-frame dissipator
\begin{equation}
  \Dd_U(X)=U^\dagger\Dd(UXU^\dagger)U
          =\sum_j\Dd_{U^\dagger L_jU}(X),
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal K=\{\Dd_U:U\in\mathrm U(d)\}
  \subset\operatorname{End}_{\R}(\mathsf V),
  \qquad
  \mathcal C=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal K).
  \label{eq:q2-control-sets}
\end{equation}
The unitary group is compact and $U\mapsto\Dd_U$ is continuous, so
$\mathcal K$ is compact. Its convex hull $\mathcal C$ is compact as well.
By Carath\'eodory's theorem, every $A\in\mathcal C$ has a finite
representation
\begin{equation}
 A=\sum_{r=1}^R\alpha_r\Dd_{U_r}
   =\sum_{r=1}^R\sum_{j=1}^m
       \Dd_{\sqrt{\alpha_r}\,U_r^\dagger L_jU_r},
 \qquad \alpha_r>0,\quad\sum_r\alpha_r=1,
 \label{eq:q2-caratheodory}
\end{equation}
where zero weights have been removed. The first equality here is an equality
of real endomorphisms of $\mathsf V$. The expression on the right defines a
canonical dissipator on all matrices, whose restriction to $\mathsf V$ is
$A$: indeed $\alpha_r\Dd_{U_r}=\sum_j
\Dd_{\sqrt{\alpha_r}\,U_r^\dagger L_jU_r}$ on $\mathsf V$ (and in fact on all
matrices). Thus every relaxed generator has the finite weighted-jump extension
needed below.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-dimensional relaxed-control compactness]
\label{lem:q2-relaxed-compactness}
Fix $T>0$. Let $A_n:[0,T]\to\mathcal C$ be measurable and let
$X_n$ solve
\begin{equation}
 X_n(t)=X_n(0)+\int_0^t A_n(\tau)X_n(\tau)\,d\tau.
 \label{eq:q2-integral-equation-n}
\end{equation}
If $X_n(0)$ stays in a compact set, then a subsequence has
\begin{equation}
 A_n\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}A
 \quad\text{in }L^\infty([0,T];\operatorname{End}_{\R}(\mathsf V)),
 \qquad
 X_n\longrightarrow X\quad\text{uniformly},
 \label{eq:q2-compact-convergence}
\end{equation}
where $A(t)\in\mathcal C$ almost everywhere and
\begin{equation}
 X(t)=X(0)+\int_0^t A(\tau)X(\tau)\,d\tau.
 \label{eq:q2-limit-integral-equation}
\end{equation}
Moreover, every such relaxed trajectory satisfies
$\norm{X(t)}_1\le\norm{X(s)}_1$ for $t\ge s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First pass to a subsequence for which the compact initial data converge,
$X_n(0)\to X_0$. The controls are uniformly bounded because $\mathcal C$ is
compact. Banach--Alaoglu then gives weak-* compactness in $L^\infty$; here the
predual is a finite product of copies of $L^1[0,T]$, hence separable, so bounded
weak-* compact sets are metrizable and subsequences suffice.

We record why the pointwise constraint survives the limit. Choose a countable
dense set $\{\ell_q\}_{q\ge1}$ in the unit sphere of the dual of the
finite-dimensional superoperator space, and put
$h_{\mathcal C}(\ell)=\max_{B\in\mathcal C}\ell(B)$. Since
$\ell_q(A_n(t))\le h_{\mathcal C}(\ell_q)$, weak-* convergence tested against
every nonnegative $L^1$ function gives, for each $q$, a full-measure set $E_q$
on which $\ell_q(A(t))\le h_{\mathcal C}(\ell_q)$. On the common full-measure
set $E=\bigcap_qE_q$, density of the $\ell_q$ and continuity of both sides in
$\ell$ extend the inequality to every linear functional $\ell$. The closed
convex set $\mathcal C$ is the intersection of its supporting half-spaces, so
$A(t)\in\mathcal C$ for every $t\in E$. This proves weak-* closedness of the
convex-hull-valued selectors.

For completeness, trace-norm monotonicity does not require additional
regularity of $A$. Approximate a measurable $\mathcal C$-valued control in
$L^1$ by simple controls whose values remain in $\mathcal C$ (for example,
conditional averages on finer partitions). By
\eqref{eq:q2-caratheodory}, each constant piece has a full-matrix Lindblad
extension, so its exponential is completely positive and trace preserving and
contracts the trace norm on Hermitian matrices. Products of these maps have
the same property. If $A,B$ are two coefficients bounded in operator norm by
$M$ and $\Phi_A,\Phi_B$ are their fundamental solutions, Duhamel's formula and
Gr\"onwall's bound give the representative estimate
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\le s\le t\le T}\norm{\Phi_A(t,s)-\Phi_B(t,s)}
 \le e^{MT}\int_0^T\norm{A(\tau)-B(\tau)}\,d\tau.
 \label{eq:q2-duhamel-estimate}
\end{equation}
Thus the simple-control propagators converge uniformly under $L^1$
coefficient approximation, and trace-norm monotonicity passes to the original
control.

Consequently the $X_n$ are uniformly bounded, and
$\dot X_n=A_nX_n$ are uniformly bounded as well. They are therefore
equicontinuous. With the already established convergence $X_n(0)\to X_0$,
Arzel\`a--Ascoli gives a further subsequence converging uniformly to a curve
$X$ with $X(0)=X_0$. Passing to the integral equation is also explicit:
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^t A_nX_n-\int_0^t AX
 =\int_0^t A_n(X_n-X)+\int_0^t(A_n-A)X.
\end{equation}
The first term tends to zero by the uniform convergence and the uniform bound
on $A_n$. In each coordinate, the second tends to zero by weak-* convergence,
since the corresponding coordinate function of $X$ is continuous and hence
is an $L^1$ test function. This proves
\eqref{eq:q2-limit-integral-equation} and the claimed continuity of
trajectories under relaxed-control convergence.
\end{proof}

We next isolate two elementary finite-dimensional facts needed when a
trajectory meets the singular locus of the trace norm.

\begin{lemma}[Trace-norm directional derivative]
\label{lem:q2-trace-directional}
Let $X=X^\dagger$ and $Y=Y^\dagger$, and let $P_0$ be the orthogonal projection
onto $\ker X$. Then the right directional derivative exists and is
\begin{equation}
 D_+\norm{\cdot}_1(X;Y)
 :=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{\norm{X+hY}_1-\norm{X}_1}{h}
 =\tr(\operatorname{sgn}(X)Y)+\norm{P_0YP_0}_1.
 \label{eq:q2-trace-directional}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Diagonalize $X$ and separate its positive, negative, and zero spectral
subspaces. The variational formula for Hermitian matrices is
\begin{equation}
 \norm{Z}_1=\max\{\tr(SZ):S=S^\dagger,\ -1\le S\le1\}.
\end{equation}
Its maximizers at $X$ are precisely
$S=\operatorname{sgn}(X)+P_0CP_0$ with $C=C^\dagger$ and
$\norm{C}_\infty\le1$. The directional derivative of this finite-dimensional
support function is the maximum of $\tr(SY)$ over those maximizers. Maximizing
the kernel term gives
$\max_{\norm{C}_\infty\le1}\tr(CP_0YP_0)=\norm{P_0YP_0}_1$, proving
\eqref{eq:q2-trace-directional}. Equivalently, this is the first-order spectral
calculation in which nonzero eigenvalues contribute their signs while the
zero spectral cluster contributes the trace norm of its compressed
perturbation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-inertia density lemma]
\label{lem:q2-fixed-inertia}
Let $X:I\to\mathsf V$ be differentiable at an interior point $t_0$. Suppose
$t_0$ is a density point of the set of times at which the inertia
$(n_+,n_-,n_0)$ of $X(t)$ equals the inertia of $X(t_0)$. If $P_0$ is the
orthogonal projection onto $\ker X(t_0)$, then
\begin{equation}
  P_0\dot X(t_0)P_0=0.
  \label{eq:q2-kernel-block-zero}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $Y=\dot X(t_0)$ and let $E$ be the fixed-inertia set. Since $t_0$ is an
interior density point of $E$, choose nonzero signed increments $h_k\to0$ with
$t_0+h_k\in E$. Then
\begin{equation}
 X(t_0+h_k)=X(t_0)+h_kY+o(|h_k|).
\end{equation}
Choose a spectral gap $(-\delta,\delta)$ containing no nonzero eigenvalue of
$X(t_0)$. For large $k$, the eigenvalues outside this interval retain their
signs and already account for the $n_+$ positive and $n_-$ negative
eigenvalues prescribed by the common inertia. Hence the remaining $n_0$
eigenvalues---the entire spectral cluster converging to zero---must be
\emph{exactly zero} at every $t_0+h_k$.

The signed first-order degenerate perturbation statement says, as a multiset,
that these cluster eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
 h_k\mu_a+o(|h_k|),
\end{equation}
where the $\mu_a$ are the eigenvalues of $P_0YP_0$; the same assertion holds
for positive or negative $h_k$ (only their ordering may reverse). It follows
by dividing by the signed $h_k$ that every $\mu_a=0$. Since $P_0YP_0$ is
Hermitian, it is therefore zero, which is
\eqref{eq:q2-kernel-block-zero}. This cluster expansion follows directly from
the Schur complement in the splitting
$\ker X(t_0)\oplus\ker X(t_0)^\perp$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No nonzero constant-norm relaxed trajectory]
\label{lem:q2-no-constant-trajectory}
Under \eqref{eq:q2-full-jump-algebra}, there is no relaxed trajectory
$\dot X=A(t)X$, $A(t)\in\mathcal C$, on a nontrivial interval such that
$X\ne0$ and $\norm{X(t)}_1$ is constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume such a trajectory exists and write it in integral form. There are only
finitely many possible inertias, so one inertia stratum $E_\nu$ has positive
measure. The density points of $E_\nu$, the Lebesgue points of the
matrix-valued function $A$, and the points at which the absolutely continuous
integral solution is differentiable and satisfies $\dot X=AX$ are all
full-measure sets (the first relative to $E_\nu$). Choose $t_0$ in their common
full-measure intersection and in the interior of the time interval.
Continuity of $X$ and the Lebesgue-point property give directly from the
integral equation, for signed $h\to0$,
\begin{align}
 X(t_0+h)-X(t_0)
 &=\int_{t_0}^{t_0+h}A(\tau)X(\tau)\,d\tau \\
 &=hA(t_0)X(t_0)+o(|h|).
 \label{eq:q2-ode-expansion}
\end{align}
Put $X=X(t_0)$ and $Y=A(t_0)X$. Let $P_+,P_-,P_0$ be the positive, negative,
and zero spectral projections of $X$. Since $X$ is nonzero and traceless,
$P_+$ is a nonzero proper projection. By \cref{lem:q2-fixed-inertia},
$P_0YP_0=0$.

Because the scalar function $\norm{X(t)}_1$ is constant,
\eqref{eq:q2-ode-expansion} and \cref{lem:q2-trace-directional} yield
\begin{equation}
 0=D_+\norm{\cdot}_1(X;Y)
   =\tr(\operatorname{sgn}(X)Y)+\norm{P_0YP_0}_1
   =\tr(\operatorname{sgn}(X)Y).
 \label{eq:q2-directional-zero}
\end{equation}

Choose the finite representation \eqref{eq:q2-caratheodory} of $A(t_0)$ and
write
\begin{equation}
  M_{rj}=\sqrt{\alpha_r}\,U_r^\dagger L_jU_r.
\end{equation}
This is where the full-matrix weighted-jump extension following
\eqref{eq:q2-caratheodory} is used. Diagonalize
$X=\sum_k\lambda_k\ketbra{e_k}{e_k}$ and put
$s_k=\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_k)$. A direct expansion of the final trace in
\eqref{eq:q2-directional-zero} for the \emph{total finite jump list}
$\{M_{rj}\}_{r,j}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  0=-\sum_{k,l}|\lambda_k|(1-s_ks_l)
       \sum_{r,j}|\bra{e_l}M_{rj}\ket{e_k}|^2.
  \label{eq:q2-total-derivative}
\end{equation}
This algebraic evaluation is the computation underlying the trace-norm
right-derivative formula in Wolff--Malz--Trivedi, Proposition~19 of
\cite{Wolff2026Contractivity}; we are not applying that proposition directly
to a merely measurable relaxed trajectory. It is essential to perform the
calculation once for the total weighted-jump list. At a singular $X$, the
trace-norm directional derivative is not linear in the direction, so one must
not average componentwise directional derivatives of the separate
$\Dd_{U_r}$.
Every summand on the right is nonnegative before the minus sign. If
$\lambda_k>0$ and $\lambda_l\le0$, then $1-s_ks_l$ is respectively $2$ or
$1$. Hence \eqref{eq:q2-total-derivative} forces
\begin{equation}
  (1-P_+)M_{rj}P_+=0\qquad\text{for every }r,j.
  \label{eq:q2-positive-invariance}
\end{equation}
In particular, for any one $r$, the nontrivial subspace $P_+\C^d$ is invariant
under all $U_r^\dagger L_jU_r$. Conjugating by $U_r$, we obtain a nontrivial
common invariant subspace for all $L_j$. It is then invariant under the
unital algebra generated by the $L_j$, contradicting
$\Alg(\{L_j\})=\M_d$, since the full matrix algebra has no nontrivial invariant
subspace. Equivalently, this is precisely the contradiction supplied by
Burnside's theorem. This contradiction proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:q2-uniform-hic}]
\textbf{Step 1: interaction frame.}
Fix $s\ge0$ and let $W(t)$ be the absolutely continuous unitary solution of
$\dot W(t)=-iH(t)W(t)$, $W(s)=1$. If
$Z(t)=\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(Z_s)$ and $X(t)=W(t)^\dagger Z(t)W(t)$, then
\begin{equation}
  \dot X(t)=\Dd_{W(t)}(X(t)).
  \label{eq:q2-interaction-frame}
\end{equation}
Thus every driven evolution gives a control in the compact orbit
$\mathcal K$, and unitary conjugation preserves the trace norm. It is enough
to obtain a bound uniform over the larger relaxed class of measurable
$\mathcal C$-valued controls.

\textbf{Step 2: a strict fixed-window factor.}
For $T>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 q_T=\sup\{\norm{X(T)}_1:
       \dot X=A(t)X,\ A(t)\in\mathcal C\text{ a.e.},\ \norm{X(0)}_1=1\}.
 \label{eq:q2-window-factor}
\end{equation}
The unit sphere of $\mathsf V$ is compact. By
\cref{lem:q2-relaxed-compactness}, the admissible initial-control pairs are
compact in the topology relevant to the endpoint map, so the supremum is
attained. Trace-norm monotonicity gives $q_T\le1$. Also $q_T>0$: choose any
constant control $A_0\in\mathcal K$. Its fundamental solution
$e^{TA_0}:\mathsf V\to\mathsf V$ is an invertible matrix exponential, so it
maps every nonzero unit vector to a nonzero vector, and the supremum defining
$q_T$ is positive. If $q_T=1$, a maximizing trajectory would satisfy
$1=\norm{X(0)}_1\ge\norm{X(t)}_1\ge\norm{X(T)}_1=1$ for all $t\in[0,T]$,
contradicting \cref{lem:q2-no-constant-trajectory}. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  0<q_T<1.
  \label{eq:q2-q-strict}
\end{equation}
This is where weak-* control compactness, Arzel\`a--Ascoli, continuity of the
integral equation, the fixed-inertia lemma, and the algebraic hypothesis enter.

\textbf{Step 3: iteration.}
Let $A$ be any interaction-frame control and let $a\ge0$. The translated
control $A_a(\tau)=A(a+\tau)$ on $[0,T]$ is again a measurable
$\mathcal C$-valued control, so the definition of $q_T$ gives the same
operator-norm factor on every window $[a,a+T]$. By the cocycle identity for
fundamental solutions, $n=\lfloor(t-s)/T\rfloor$ consecutive windows
contribute the factor $q_T^n$. Trace-norm contractivity on the final interval
of length less than $T$ then gives, for $r=t-s$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X)}_1
 \le q_T^{\lfloor r/T\rfloor}\norm{X}_1
 \le q_T^{-1}\exp\!\left(\frac{\log q_T}{T}r\right)\norm{X}_1.
\end{equation}
Consequently \eqref{eq:q2-uniform-bound} holds with the explicit choices
\begin{equation}
  K=q_T^{-1},\qquad \gamma=-\frac{\log q_T}{T}>0.
\end{equation}
The equivalence in \cref{def:q2-hic} converts the bound on $\mathsf V$ to the
stated bound for differences of states.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why a pointwise Gr\"onwall proof cannot work]
\label{rem:q2-four-dimensional-example}
In $d=4$, let $E_{ij}$ denote the one-based matrix units and set
\begin{equation}
 A=E_{21}+E_{32}+E_{14},\qquad B=E_{43},\qquad
 X=E_{11}-E_{33}.
 \label{eq:q2-example-data}
\end{equation}
These two jumps generate all of $\M_4$. Indeed,
$A^3=E_{34}$, whence
\begin{equation}
 A^3B=E_{33},\qquad BA^3=E_{44},\qquad
 E_{33}A=E_{32},\qquad AE_{44}=E_{14},
\end{equation}
and therefore $E_{21}=A-E_{32}-E_{14}$. The four matrix units
$E_{21},E_{32},E_{43},E_{14}$ form a directed cycle through all four basis
vectors. Products along paths in that cycle give every off-diagonal matrix
unit, and products around cycles give every $E_{ii}$. Thus
$\Alg(A,B)=\M_4$.

A direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}
 (\Dd_A+\Dd_B)(X)
   =\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,-1).
 \label{eq:q2-example-dissipator}
\end{equation}
For $0<h<1$,
\begin{equation}
 X+h(\Dd_A+\Dd_B)(X)
  =\operatorname{diag}(1-h,h,-1+h,-h),
\end{equation}
whose trace norm is exactly $2=\norm{X}_1$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{d}{dh}\right|_{h=0+}
 \norm{X+h(\Dd_A+\Dd_B)(X)}_1=0.
\end{equation}
Thus full jump-algebra generation does not yield a pointwise differential
inequality $\partial_+\norm{X(t)}_1\le-c\norm{X(t)}_1$ with $c>0$. The theorem
nevertheless holds because the compactness argument rules out an entire
constant-norm trajectory, not an isolated zero derivative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact formalization status]
\label{rem:hic-lean}
The Lean kernel currently checks the algebraic obstruction and the explicit
$4\times4$ example, but it does \emph{not} check
\cref{thm:q2-uniform-hic}.
\par\smallskip\noindent
\leanstatus{Channel/\allowbreak Semigroup/\allowbreak HamiltonianIndependent\allowbreak Contractivity}
\par\smallskip\noindent
More precisely, the declarations
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \nolinkurl{full_jump_algebra_projection_eq_zero_or_one},
  \item \nolinkurl{exists_jump_leakage_of_full_algebra_generation},
  \item \nolinkurl{full_jump_algebra_implies_no_blockUpperTriangular},
  \item \nolinkurl{full_jump_algebra_implies_not_isReducibleQDS},
  \item \nolinkurl{HICFourDimensionalExample.adjoin_A_B_eq_top}, and
  \item \nolinkurl{HICFourDimensionalExample.dissipator_sum_x}
\end{itemize}
kernel-check, respectively: the absence of a nontrivial common invariant
projection under full adjoint-free generation; the equivalent leakage
statement; exclusion of a block-upper-triangular Lindblad form and of
reducibility for every frozen Hamiltonian; full algebra generation in the
example \eqref{eq:q2-example-data}; and the exact dissipator identity
\eqref{eq:q2-example-dissipator}.

The analytic relaxed-control theorem is not yet formalized. The present
library lacks the required integrated infrastructure for the matrix trace
norm, propagators with locally integrable coefficients, weak-* compactness of
control selectors, and degenerate Hermitian eigenvalue perturbation. The
formal file should therefore be read as a kernel-checked algebraic companion
to the proof above, not as a formal verification of the full theorem.
\end{remark}
