\section{Q1: Weighted lowering under arbitrary drive --- resolved}\label{sec:q1}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Background: Hamiltonian-independent contractivity}\label{sec:hic-background}

This background also underlies Q2 (\sharedref{Q2 in the primary-list document}{sec:q2}), which states the
Wolff--Malz--Trivedi conjecture directly implicated by items (b)--(d) below.

Spohn's theorem \cite{Spohn1977} and the Frigerio--Evans theory
\cite{Frigerio1978,Evans1977} settle the \emph{time-independent} question:
if $\Alg(\{L_\alpha\}\cup\{H\})=\M_d$ (Evans irreducibility), the semigroup
$e^{t\Ld}$ has a unique, full-rank, exponentially attracting fixed point.
The formalized relaxation conditions in TNLean
(\texttt{full\_algebra\_generation\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak blockUpperTriangular},
\texttt{not\_isReducible\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak generates\_\allowbreak full\_algebra}) are exactly this
circle of results. The problems below ask what survives when the Hamiltonian
becomes an \emph{adversarial, time-dependent} drive --- a question studied
systematically only very recently.

Wolff, Malz, and Trivedi \cite{Wolff2026Contractivity} (arXiv:2602.16067,
February 2026, unpublished as of July 2026) introduce the HIC notion of
\sharedref{the HIC definition in the primary-list introduction}{def:hic} and prove, for a dissipator
$\Dd=\sum_j\Dd_{L_j}$ on $\C^d$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
	\item \emph{Necessity:} $\Alg(\{L_j,L_j^\dagger\}_j)=\M_d$ is necessary for
	      HIC.
	\item \emph{Unital dissipators:} if all $\Dd_{L_j}$ are unital
	      (e.g.\ Hermitian jump operators), HIC is \emph{equivalent} to
	      $\Alg(\{L_j,L_j^\dagger\}_j)=\M_d$, equivalently to $\Dd$ alone having the
	      maximally mixed state as unique fixed point.
	\item \emph{$d=2$:} a complete characterization --- HIC iff
	      $\Alg(\{L_j,L_j^\dagger\}_j)=\M_2$.
	\item \emph{$d=3$:} the stronger condition $\Alg(\{L_j\}_j)=\M_3$ (jump
	      operators alone, no adjoints) is sufficient.
	\item \emph{Counterexamples:} an Evans-irreducible dissipator on
	      $\C^2\otimes\C^2$ (jump operators $(\sigma^z+2\sigma^-)\otimes\id$,
	      $\ketbra11\otimes\sigma^\pm$) that loses its unique-fixed-point property
	      when the \emph{constant} Hamiltonian $H=\sigma^y\otimes\id$ is added; and a
	      drive with uniformly gapped instantaneous spectrum that defeats
	      contractivity by varying fast in time. Thus the standard Davies/Lindblad
	      irreducibility condition
	      $\Alg(\{L_\alpha\}\cup\{\sum_\alpha L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha\})=\M_d$ does
	      \emph{not} imply HIC --- exactly the counterexample the internal file
	      alludes to.
\end{enumerate}
For the ladder jump considered below, Wolff, Malz, and Trivedi also apply
their spectral sufficient criterion directly. They prove HIC for uniform and
angular-momentum ladders through $d=5$, for the harmonic-oscillator ladder
through $d=3$, and, when $d=3$, for
$L=\ketbra01+\alpha\ketbra12$ whenever
$\alpha\in(\sqrt3-\sqrt2,\sqrt3+\sqrt2)$. These results leave arbitrary
positive weights and general dimension open.

Independently, Yoshida and Hamazaki \cite{Yoshida2026Steady} give, for
\emph{quasiperiodic} drives with Hermitian jump operators, a necessary and
sufficient algebraic criterion for steady-state uniqueness, and exhibit a
single-qubit example ($H_t=\tfrac\omega2\sigma^z$,
$L_t=\sqrt\kappa(\sigma^x\cos\omega t+\sigma^y\sin\omega t)$) whose
\emph{instantaneous} Spohn algebra is full at every time yet whose steady
state is not unique: instantaneous algebraic conditions cannot suffice.
Di~Meglio et al.\ \cite{DiMeglio2024Relaxation} give quantitative
relaxation criteria for \emph{fixed} (known) time-dependent generators.

\subsection{The problem}

We use the dissipator convention
$\Dd_L(X)=LXL^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,X\}$, write $\M_d=M_d(\C)$, and let
$\ket0,\dots,\ket{d-1}$ be the standard orthonormal basis of $\C^d$.

\begin{problem}[Q1: weighted lowering under arbitrary drive]\label{prob:lowering}
	On $\C^d$ with basis $\ket0,\dots,\ket{d-1}$, let
	\begin{equation}
		L=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_i} \ketbra{i-1}{i},
		\qquad \gamma_i>0.
		\label{eq:q1-lowering}
	\end{equation}
	Characterize the drives $H(t)$ for which the propagator of
	$\Ld(t)=-i[H(t),\cdot]+\Dd_L$ sends every traceless $X$ to $0$ in trace
	norm, and determine for which $\{\gamma_i\}$ this holds for every locally
	integrable drive $H(t)$.
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}[Corrected transcription]\label{rem:q1-correction}
	An earlier transcription incorrectly used the diagonal terms
	$\ketbra{i}{i}$. Rahul Trivedi clarified that the intended jump operator is
	the lowering operator \eqref{eq:q1-lowering}, with transitions
	$\ket{i}\mapsto\ket{i-1}$. Thus $\Dd_L$ describes weighted amplitude damping,
	not pure dephasing, and the previous diagonal-kernel objection does not apply.
	Because every weight is nonzero, $L$ and $L^\dagger$ generate the full matrix
	algebra $\M_d$, so the necessary algebraic condition of
	\cite{Wolff2026Contractivity} holds; for $d=2$ their characterization item~(c)
	already gives HIC. The theorem below settles all $d\ge2$ at once and, moreover,
	supplies a single uniform pair of constants.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Resolution}\label{sec:q1-resolution}

\begin{definition}[Contractivity and HIC]\label{def:q1-hic}
	Let $\mathcal E^H_{t,s}$ be the propagator of
	$\Ld^H_t=-i[H(t),\cdot\,]+\Dd_L$, where $H=H^\dagger$ has locally integrable
	matrix entries. For a fixed $H$ the evolution is \emph{exponentially
		contractive} if there are $K_H,\gamma_H>0$ such that for every pair of states
	$\rho,\sigma$ and all $t\ge s\ge0$,
	\begin{equation}
		\norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(\rho-\sigma)}_1
		\le K_H e^{-\gamma_H(t-s)}\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1.
		\label{eq:q1-contractivity}
	\end{equation}
	The dissipator $\Dd_L$ is \emph{Hamiltonian-independently contractive} (HIC)
	if such $K_H,\gamma_H$ exist for every $H=H^\dagger\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. As
	in \sharedref{the Q2 definition}{def:q2-hic}, one may equivalently quantify
	\eqref{eq:q1-contractivity} over all nonzero traceless Hermitian matrices,
	since such an $X$ equals $c(\rho-\sigma)$ with $c=\norm{X}_1/2$ and
	$\rho=X_+/c$, $\sigma=X_-/c$ for the Jordan decomposition $X=X_+-X_-$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Resolution of Q1]\label{thm:q1-main}
	Fix $d\ge2$ and let $L$ be the weighted lowering operator
	\eqref{eq:q1-lowering}.
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
		\item \textup{(Positive rates are sufficient.)} If $\gamma_i>0$ for every
		      $i$, there are constants $K\ge1$ and $\gamma>0$, depending only on $d$ and on
		      the rate vector $(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{d-1})$, such that for every
		      $H=H^\dagger\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, every nonzero traceless Hermitian $X$, and
		      all $t\ge s\ge0$,
		      \begin{equation}
			      \norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X)}_1
			      \le K e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\norm{X}_1.
			      \label{eq:q1-uniform-bound}
		      \end{equation}
		      The same bound with $K$ replaced by $2K$ holds for every traceless matrix
		      $X\in\M_d$, not necessarily Hermitian. In particular $\Dd_L$ is HIC.
		\item \textup{(Every edge is necessary.)} If the rates are only required to be
		      nonnegative and $\gamma_{i_0}=0$ for some $i_0\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}$, then
		      $\Dd_L$ is \emph{not} HIC: already the undriven generator $(H\equiv0)$ has two
		      orthogonal dark states whose traceless difference is a stationary fixed point.
	\end{enumerate}
	Thus, among nonnegative rate vectors, positivity of every edge is necessary and
	sufficient for HIC.
\end{theorem}

Part~(i) reuses the relaxed-control compactness scaffold developed for Q2 in
\sharedref{the Q2 context file}{sec:q2}; we restate only the definitions and
imported lemmas needed to keep the argument self-contained. The new content is
the algebraic obstruction: for the single lowering jump the jump-only algebra
$\Alg(\{L\})$ is not all of $\M_d$ (see \cref{rem:q1-wmt}), so the sufficient
hypothesis of \cite{Wolff2026Contractivity}~(d) fails and a fresh argument is
needed to exclude constant-norm relaxed trajectories.

\subsubsection*{Relaxed-control scaffold}

Work in the real normed space
$\mathsf V=\{X\in\M_d:X=X^\dagger,\ \tr X=0\}$ with the trace norm. For
$U\in\mathrm U(d)$ the interaction-frame dissipator of the single jump is
\begin{equation}
	\Dd_U(X)=U^\dagger\Dd_L(UXU^\dagger)U=\Dd_{U^\dagger LU}(X),
	\qquad
	\mathcal K=\{\Dd_U:U\in\mathrm U(d)\},\quad
	\mathcal C=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal K).
	\label{eq:q1-control-sets}
\end{equation}
Since $\mathrm U(d)$ is compact and $U\mapsto\Dd_U$ continuous, $\mathcal K$
and its convex hull $\mathcal C$ are compact. By Carath\'eodory's theorem every
$A\in\mathcal C$ has a finite representation
\begin{equation}
	A=\sum_{r=1}^R\alpha_r\Dd_{U_r}
	=\sum_{r=1}^R\Dd_{M_r},
	\qquad M_r=\sqrt{\alpha_r}\,U_r^\dagger LU_r,\quad
	\alpha_r>0,\ \sum_r\alpha_r=1,
	\label{eq:q1-caratheodory}
\end{equation}
the scalar weights being absorbed into the single jump; thus every relaxed
generator carries a canonical single-jump Lindblad extension with finitely many
weighted jumps $M_r$. We import three finite-dimensional facts, proved in
\sharedref{the Q2 context file}{sec:q2}.

\begin{lemma}[Relaxed-control compactness;
		\sharedref{Q2}{lem:q2-relaxed-compactness}]
	\label{lem:q1-relaxed-compactness}
	Fix $T>0$. If $A_n:[0,T]\to\mathcal C$ are measurable, the $X_n(0)$ lie in a
	compact set, and $X_n(t)=X_n(0)+\int_0^tA_n X_n$, then a subsequence has
	$A_n\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}A$ in $L^\infty$ with $A(t)\in\mathcal C$ a.e.\
	and $X_n\to X$ uniformly, where $X(t)=X(0)+\int_0^tAX$. Every relaxed trajectory
	obeys $\norm{X(t)}_1\le\norm{X(s)}_1$ for $t\ge s$, because each constant piece
	of a $\mathcal C$-valued control exponentiates through
	\eqref{eq:q1-caratheodory} to a completely positive, trace-preserving map.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Trace-norm directional derivative;
		\sharedref{Q2}{lem:q2-trace-directional}]
	\label{lem:q1-trace-directional}
	For Hermitian $X,Y$ with $P_0$ the orthogonal projection onto $\ker X$,
	\begin{equation}
		D_+\norm{\cdot}_1(X;Y)
		:=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{\norm{X+hY}_1-\norm{X}_1}{h}
		=\tr\!(\operatorname{sgn}(X)\,Y)+\norm{P_0YP_0}_1.
		\label{eq:q1-trace-directional}
	\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-inertia density lemma;
		\sharedref{Q2}{lem:q2-fixed-inertia}]
	\label{lem:q1-fixed-inertia}
	Let $X:I\to\mathsf V$ be differentiable at an interior point $t_0$ that is a
	density point of the set of times whose inertia equals that of $X(t_0)$. If
	$P_0$ is the projection onto $\ker X(t_0)$, then $P_0\dot X(t_0)P_0=0$.
\end{lemma}

\subsubsection*{The new algebraic obstruction}

The single lowering jump enters through one elementary fact.

\begin{lemma}[Invariant subspaces of a nilpotent with one-dimensional kernel]
	\label{lem:q1-nilpotent}
	If $N\in\M_d$ is nilpotent with $\dim\ker N=1$, then every nonzero
	$N$-invariant subspace $W$ contains $\ker N$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	The restriction $N|_W$ is nilpotent on the nonzero space $W$, hence singular,
	so some nonzero $w\in W$ lies in $\ker N$. As $\dim\ker N=1$, this forces
	$\ker N=\operatorname{span}\{w\}\subseteq W$.
\end{proof}

For \eqref{eq:q1-lowering} with all $\gamma_i>0$ we have $L\ket0=0$ and
$L\ket i=\sqrt{\gamma_i}\ket{i-1}\ne0$ for $i\ge1$, so $L$ is nilpotent
($L^d=0$) with $\ker L=\operatorname{span}\{\ket0\}$ one-dimensional. Any
unitary conjugate $N=U^\dagger LU$ shares both properties, with
$\dim\ker N=1$.

\begin{lemma}[No nonzero constant-norm relaxed trajectory]
	\label{lem:q1-no-constant}
	If all $\gamma_i>0$, there is no relaxed trajectory $\dot X=A(t)X$,
	$A(t)\in\mathcal C$, on a nontrivial interval with $X\ne0$ and $\norm{X(t)}_1$
	constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	Suppose such a trajectory exists. Only finitely many inertias occur, so one
	inertia stratum has positive measure; choose $t_0$ in the interior of the
	interval, a density point of that stratum, a Lebesgue point of $A$, and a point
	of differentiability with $\dot X=AX$. From the integral equation,
	$X(t_0+h)-X(t_0)=h\,A(t_0)X(t_0)+o(|h|)$ for signed $h\to0$. Put $X=X(t_0)$,
	$Y=A(t_0)X$, and let $P_\pm,P_0$ be the positive, negative, and zero spectral
	projections of $X$. Since $X$ is nonzero and traceless it has eigenvalues of
	both signs, so
	\begin{equation}
		P_+\ne0\quad\text{and}\quad P_-\ne0.
		\label{eq:q1-both-nonzero}
	\end{equation}
	By \cref{lem:q1-fixed-inertia} $P_0YP_0=0$, and constancy of $\norm{X(t)}_1$
	together with \cref{lem:q1-trace-directional} gives
	\begin{equation}
		0=D_+\norm{\cdot}_1(X;Y)
		=\tr\!(\operatorname{sgn}(X)\,Y)+\norm{P_0YP_0}_1
		=\tr\!(\operatorname{sgn}(X)\,Y).
		\label{eq:q1-directional-zero}
	\end{equation}

	Write $A(t_0)=\sum_r\Dd_{M_r}$ as in \eqref{eq:q1-caratheodory}, diagonalize
	$X=\sum_k\lambda_k\ketbra{e_k}{e_k}$, and set
	$s_k=\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_k)$. Expanding
	$\Dd_M(X)=MXM^\dagger-\tfrac12\{M^\dagger M,X\}$ in this eigenbasis yields the
	total-jump right-derivative identity
	\begin{equation}
		0=\tr\!(\operatorname{sgn}(X)\,Y)
		=-\sum_{r}\sum_{k,l}|\lambda_k|\,(1-s_ks_l)
		\big|\bra{e_l}M_r\ket{e_k}\big|^2,
		\label{eq:q1-total-derivative}
	\end{equation}
	which is the computation behind Proposition~19 of
	\cite{Wolff2026Contractivity}; because $A$ is only measurable, one must
	evaluate it once for the \emph{total} weighted-jump list $\{M_r\}$ rather than
	average the (nonlinear at singular $X$) derivatives of the separate
	$\Dd_{U_r}$.

	Every summand of \eqref{eq:q1-total-derivative} is nonnegative, since
	$1-s_ks_l\ge0$, and therefore vanishes. Fix $r$. For $\lambda_k>0$ and
	$\lambda_l\le0$ the weight $|\lambda_k|(1-s_ks_l)\ge|\lambda_k|>0$, so
	$\bra{e_l}M_r\ket{e_k}=0$; hence $M_r$ maps $V_+:=P_+\C^d$ into itself.
	Symmetrically $M_r$ maps $V_-:=P_-\C^d$ into itself. Thus $V_+$ and $V_-$ are
	invariant under every $M_r$, hence under $U_r^\dagger LU_r$.

	Now fix a single index $r$ and set $N=U_r^\dagger LU_r$, a unitary conjugate of
	$L$, so $N$ is nilpotent with $\dim\ker N=1$. By \eqref{eq:q1-both-nonzero}
	both $V_+$ and $V_-$ are nonzero, they are orthogonal as distinct spectral
	subspaces of the Hermitian $X$, and both are $N$-invariant. By
	\cref{lem:q1-nilpotent} each contains $\ker N$, so
	$\{0\}\ne\ker N\subseteq V_+\cap V_-=\{0\}$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Proof of \cref{thm:q1-main}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of part~\textup{(i)}]
	The argument is the interaction-frame iteration of
	\sharedref{the Q2 theorem}{thm:q2-uniform-hic}, applied to the single-jump
	control sets \eqref{eq:q1-control-sets} with \cref{lem:q1-no-constant} in place
	of its Q2 analogue. Passing to the interaction frame
	$X(t)=W(t)^\dagger\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X_0)W(t)$, with
	$\dot W=-iH(t)W$, turns each driven evolution into a control
	$\dot X=\Dd_{W(t)}(X)$ in $\mathcal K$; unitary conjugation preserves the trace
	norm, so it suffices to bound the larger relaxed class of $\mathcal C$-valued
	controls. Define the fixed-window factor
	\begin{equation}
		q_T=\sup\big\{\norm{X(T)}_1:\dot X=A(t)X,\ A(t)\in\mathcal C\ \text{a.e.},\
		\norm{X(0)}_1=1\big\}.
	\end{equation}
	By \cref{lem:q1-relaxed-compactness} the supremum is attained and $q_T\le1$;
	it is positive because a constant control has invertible exponential; and
	$q_T=1$ would produce a nonzero constant-norm relaxed trajectory, excluded by
	\cref{lem:q1-no-constant}. Hence $0<q_T<1$. Splitting $[s,t]$ into
	$\lfloor(t-s)/T\rfloor$ windows of length $T$ and a shorter remainder, the
	cocycle identity and trace-norm monotonicity give
	\eqref{eq:q1-uniform-bound} with the explicit constants
	\begin{equation}
		K=q_T^{-1}\ge1,\qquad \gamma=-\frac{\log q_T}{T}>0,
		\label{eq:q1-constants}
	\end{equation}
	for any fixed $T>0$; these depend only on $d$ and the rate vector through
	$\mathcal C$.

	For a general traceless $X\in\M_d$, write $X=X_1+iX_2$ with
	$X_1=\tfrac12(X+X^\dagger)$ and $X_2=\tfrac1{2i}(X-X^\dagger)$ both traceless
	Hermitian and $\norm{X_1}_1,\norm{X_2}_1\le\norm{X}_1$ (using
	$\norm{X^\dagger}_1=\norm{X}_1$). Since $\mathcal E^H_{t,s}$ is complex-linear,
	\begin{equation}
		\norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X)}_1
		\le\norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X_1)}_1+\norm{\mathcal E^H_{t,s}(X_2)}_1
		\le 2Ke^{-\gamma(t-s)}\norm{X}_1,
	\end{equation}
	the factor-two extension. The equivalence in \cref{def:q1-hic} converts the
	Hermitian bound to the stated bound for differences of states.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of part~\textup{(ii)}]
	If $\gamma_{i_0}=0$ then $L\ket{i_0}=\sqrt{\gamma_{i_0}}\ket{i_0-1}=0$, so both
	$\ket0$ and $\ket{i_0}$ lie in $\ker L$ and $\dim\ker L\ge2$. Any $\ket\psi$
	with $L\ket\psi=0$ gives $\Dd_L(\ketbra\psi\psi)=0$, so $\ketbra00$ and
	$\ketbra{i_0}{i_0}$ are two orthogonal dark states of the undriven generator.
	Their difference $\Delta=\ketbra00-\ketbra{i_0}{i_0}$ is a nonzero traceless
	Hermitian fixed point, so $\norm{\mathcal E^0_{t,0}(\Delta)}_1=\norm{\Delta}_1=2$
	for all $t\ge0$. No bound \eqref{eq:q1-contractivity} with $\gamma_H>0$ can hold
	for $H\equiv0$, so $\Dd_L$ is not even contractive for the zero drive, hence not
	HIC.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why a pointwise $C=1$ differential estimate fails]
	\label{rem:q1-pointwise}
	Even with all $\gamma_i>0$, the undriven flow yields no pointwise inequality
	$\partial_+\norm{X(t)}_1\le-c\,\norm{X(t)}_1$ with $c>0$. Take $d=3$,
	$\gamma_1=\gamma_2=1$, so $L=\ketbra01+\ketbra12$, and let
	$X=\ketbra00-\ketbra22$, which is singular with $\ker X=\operatorname{span}\{\ket1\}$.
	A short computation gives $\Dd_L(X)=\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,1)$, whence for
	$0<h<1$,
	\begin{equation}
		\norm{X+h\,\Dd_L(X)}_1
		=\norm{\operatorname{diag}(1,-h,-1+h)}_1
		=1+h+(1-h)=2=\norm{X}_1,
	\end{equation}
	so the right-derivative is zero. By \cref{lem:q1-trace-directional} the signed
	part contributes $\tr(\operatorname{sgn}(X)\Dd_L(X))=-1$ while the eigenvalue
	growing out of the kernel site $\ket1$ contributes
	$\norm{P_0\Dd_L(X)P_0}_1=+1$, and they cancel. The theorem holds because the
	compactness argument excludes an entire constant-norm trajectory, not because
	any instantaneous derivative is strictly negative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Position relative to Wolff--Malz--Trivedi]\label{rem:q1-wmt}
	The spectral sufficient criterion of \cite{Wolff2026Contractivity} already
	establishes HIC for several ladder families: uniform and angular-momentum
	weights through $d=5$, harmonic-oscillator weights through $d=3$, and the
	general three-level ladder $L=\ketbra01+\alpha\ketbra12$ for
	$\alpha\in(\sqrt3-\sqrt2,\sqrt3+\sqrt2)$. In the notation of
	\eqref{eq:q1-lowering}, the last interval is
	$\sqrt{\gamma_2/\gamma_1}\in(\sqrt3-\sqrt2,\sqrt3+\sqrt2)$.

	Their algebraic sufficient conditions do not cover the present single jump in
	general. Indeed,
	$\Alg(\{L\})=\operatorname{span}\{\id,L,\dots,L^{d-1}\}$ is the polynomial
	algebra of dimension $d$ generated by a regular nilpotent shift, never all of
	$\M_d$ for $d\ge2$; and the unital criterion does not apply because
	$\Dd_L(\id)=[L,L^\dagger]\ne0$. The necessary condition does hold, since
	$\Alg(\{L,L^\dagger\})=\M_d$ for positive rates. For $d=2$ the complete
	characterization of \cite{Wolff2026Contractivity} already yields HIC
	(\cref{rem:q1-correction}). The contribution of \cref{thm:q1-main} is to
	remove all remaining dimension and rate restrictions: it covers every
	positive rate vector in every finite dimension and provides one pair
	$(K,\gamma)$ uniform over all locally integrable Hermitian drives.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact formalization status]\label{rem:q1-lean}
	As in \sharedref{the Q2 formalization remark}{rem:hic-lean}, the analytic
	relaxed-control proof of \cref{thm:q1-main}(i) --- weak-$*$ control compactness,
	propagators with locally integrable coefficients, the trace-norm directional
	derivative, and degenerate Hermitian eigenvalue perturbation --- is not
	formalized and should be read as an informal result.
	\par\smallskip\noindent
	\leanstatus{Channel/\allowbreak Semigroup/\allowbreak WeightedLowering\allowbreak Contractivity}
	\par\smallskip\noindent
	The Lean kernel checks the new finite-dimensional obstruction. The module
	defines the weighted lowering endomorphism with arbitrary nonzero complex edge
	weights and proves:
	\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
		\item \nolinkurl{WeightedLowering.operator_pow_dimension_eq_zero},
		\item \nolinkurl{WeightedLowering.ker_operator},
		\item \nolinkurl{WeightedLowering.ground_mem_invariant}, and
		\item \nolinkurl{WeightedLowering.not_orthogonal_invariant}.
	\end{itemize}
	These declarations state that the shift is nilpotent, its kernel is exactly the
	ground-state line, every nonzero invariant subspace contains that line, and two
	nonzero invariant subspaces cannot be orthogonal.
	It does not formalize \eqref{eq:q1-total-derivative}, the relaxed-control
	scaffold, or the full HIC theorem. The formal file is therefore a
	machine-checked algebraic companion to the proof above, not a verification of
	\cref{thm:q1-main}.
\end{remark}
