%% Regression: a bracket under a traced row steps outside the row's closure
%% return (issue #5631).  Seven pictures, told apart by the order their
%% brackets render.  On a two-row ladder the lower row's return runs one
%% trace reach below the row; a south bracket on that row previously put
%% its arc between the row and the rail while its caption stood beyond the
%% rail, so the generated return split the annotation.  The bracket now
%% takes concentric order against the returns routed on the side it speaks
%% from: the rail keeps its pinned run, the band between the row and the
%% rail carries no annotation ink, the bar stands two clearances outside
%% the rail -- the turns that close the arc rise one clearance from the
%% bar, keeping one clearance of daylight between their tips and the rail
%% -- and the two paths never touch.
%%
%% The other six pictures pin the reach of that order.  The single-row
%% picture adds labelled physical legs through the return: the bar steps
%% past the leg tips and their label bands as well, so it never lands in
%% the label text beyond the rail.  The spanning picture selects glyphs
%% anchored on the open row whose `wires=' extent carries them onto the
%% closed one, so the rows a selection occupies -- not the row its anchors
%% name -- decide whether a return stands between the arc and the sites.
%% The sheared picture takes the same order on a plane frame, whose rows
%% are page-horizontal and whose returns the closure renderer transforms
%% through the frame, so the rail is a page ordinate and the support that
%% clears it is that ordinate in the hull's own axis.  The last picture
%% grows its legs from the picture's own physical policy and lengthens
%% them to meet a string routed below the row: a leg the picture rather
%% than the author asked for is an obstacle exactly as an authored one is,
%% so the bar clears the tips it carries past the rail.  The last two
%% pictures put a cluster on the closed row: its generated children carry
%% no address of their own, so a bracket over the carrier's name selects
%% only members that stand in no cell, and a bracket over the whole picture
%% selects those children beside ordinary sited glyphs.  A member reaches
%% the boundary row through the carrier it belongs to, and one that reaches
%% it through neither its own address nor a carrier is passed over rather
%% than allowed to overrule the members that do stand somewhere.
%%
%% Each expectation counts intersections of a probe segment against the
%% saved rail and bracket paths, so the layering is pinned structurally as
%% well as by the exact-toolchain pixel ledger.
